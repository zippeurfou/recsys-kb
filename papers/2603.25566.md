## Summary

User-generated content (UGC) videos often contain prior degradations like compression artifacts, making standard pixel-fidelity optimization inadequate. To address this, the authors propose a novel Mamba-based loss function that treats the reference video as a contextual guide rather than a pixel-perfect ground truth. This loss function, trained using a Siamese ranking strategy, is integrated into neural video codecs, enabling them to produce significantly better perceptual quality, with demonstrated 8.46% and 12.89% BD-rate improvements over baseline methods.

## Technical note

This paper proposes PT-Loss, a perceptual loss for learning-based user-generated-content (UGC) video transcoding that reframes the non-pristine reference as semantic context rather than a pixel-level ground truth, avoiding the fidelity-oriented RDO trap of replicating the reference's compression artifacts. The loss is a lightweight Selective Structured State-Space (Mamba) quality model that decouples spatial feature extraction from temporal selective scanning for linear O(L) complexity, replacing a prior Swin-Transformer design to cut training overhead while capturing long-range temporal dependencies (distinguishing temporal consistency from flickering). It is trained with a weakly-supervised Siamese ranking strategy to predict perceptual degradation from only reference and distorted clips, then integrated into the RDO of two neural codecs (autoencoder-based DCVC and INR-based HiNeRV). On BVI-UGC it delivers 8.46% and 12.89% BD-rate savings over the respective baselines, demonstrating model-agnostic generalization.

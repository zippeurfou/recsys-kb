## Summary

This paper proposes a framework taxonomy to navigate the complex design space of embedding retrieval systems, which involves balancing efficiency and effectiveness. It structures system design choices across four layers: Representation (handling semantic relevance and vector creation), Granularity (managing document chunking), Orchestration (combining retrieval steps), and Robustness (mitigating biases and degradation). By categorizing pitfalls and paradigms in these layers, the framework helps practitioners optimize performance in modern neural search systems.

## Technical note

This survey proposes a four-layer taxonomy for embedding retrieval system design that navigates the efficiency-effectiveness Pareto frontier. The Representation layer contrasts bi-encoders (scalable via MIPS but with a single-vector representation bottleneck), cross-encoders (highest accuracy, O(N) reranking cost), and hybrid late-interaction models (Poly-encoder, ColBERT MaxSim). The Granularity layer covers atomic vs hierarchical chunking, the Orchestration layer covers query fanout, agentic decomposition and multi-stage reranking, and the Robustness layer addresses domain generalization, lexical blind spots, and silent degradation from temporal drift.

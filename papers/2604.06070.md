## Summary

Extending language model context windows usually requires expensive long-context training, but this paper shows that capabilities can be transferred using logit-based knowledge distillation even when trained only on short data within a long context. The study demonstrates that this distillation effectively transfers positional information, tracing how positional signals propagate from the teacher model's layers to the student via the logit signals. Key findings include optimal RoPE scaling for distillation, the mechanism of positional information transfer, and identifying specific model parameters sensitive to long-context training.

## Technical note

This work shows long-context retrieval ability can transfer to a 1.1B student via logit-based knowledge distillation from a Llama-4 Scout teacher, even when training only on packed short-context documents (cross-document attention masked) within a 128k window. Finding 1: phase-wise RoPE base-theta scaling (10k in short-context phase, 500k in long-context phase) maximizes rotational spectrum utilization and beats fixed low/high theta; Finding 2: KD consistently beats cross-entropy on Needle-in-a-Haystack and RULER. A repeated-token probe (identical content at every position) isolates RoPE perturbations and traces how they propagate through query/key vectors, amplify across layers, and modulate the teacher's output logit distribution, which serves as an implicit positional distillation signal. Checkpoint comparison shows long-context extension drives dimension-specific (not position-specific) updates concentrated in high-index/low-frequency RoPE dimension pairs encoding long-range positions.

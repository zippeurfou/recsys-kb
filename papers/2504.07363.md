## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of incorporating large pre-trained language models (LMs) into generative recommendation systems. It proposes DMRec, a model-agnostic framework that bridges collaborative filtering spaces with LM semantic spaces by aligning their distributions through three distribution matching processes designed to preserve unique information and enhance shared understanding. Experiments show DMRec significantly improves recommendation performance across various generative methods compared to standard LM integration approaches.

## Technical note

Integrating pretrained LMs into VAE-style generative recommenders is hard because LM text outputs don't map to the probabilistic user-preference distributions that generative recsys estimate. DMRec is a model-agnostic, plug-and-play framework that models each user's preference as a Gaussian in a collaborative space (variational inference/ELBO) and a language space (a probabilistic meta-network over LM-generated profile embeddings), then aligns them via three cross-space distribution-matching strategies: GODM (Wasserstein optimal transport), CPDM (composite-prior JS divergence), and MDDM (mixing KL divergence tied to the information bottleneck). Applied to Mult-VAE, L-DiffRec, and CVGA on Amazon-Book, Yelp, and Steam, it lifts Recall/NDCG ~5-17% (MDDM usually best) and beats LM-enhanced baselines, helping most for sparse users.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently adapting Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex technical service tasks. The proposed framework tackles issues arising from ambiguous human demonstrations and the high cost of standard training by introducing three key innovations: Latent Logic Augmentation to better capture decision logic, Robust Noise Reduction using diverse ground truths to handle semantic diversity, and a Hybrid Reward mechanism combining an LLM judge with a lightweight reranker to reduce computational costs while maintaining alignment quality. These components together improve the stability, performance, and efficiency of deploying adapted LLM agents for technical services.

## Technical note

This paper proposes a lightweight adaptation framework for LLM-based technical service (Cloud) agents, tackling three obstacles: expert demonstrations lack explicit cognitive chains (causing myopic imitation under SFT), a single query admits multiple valid responses (single-ground-truth training causes knowledge collapse), and reliable RL reward signals are costly and prone to reward hacking. Latent Logic Augmentation enriches SFT data with backward-looking Decision Reasoning Augmentation (LLM-generated rationales for state-action pairs) and forward-looking Planning-Aware Trajectory Modeling (rewriting three-step future sequences into structured planning traces) under an MDP formulation. Robust Noise Reduction builds a Multiple Ground Truths dataset via dual-filtering with a Consistency Judge (92% human alignment) and a Utility Judge (83%), roughly doubling references from offline high-temperature exploration and online RL rollouts, evaluated with an Ensemble-Consistency Score. Lightweight Adaptation uses a Hybrid Reward Mechanism fusing an LLM judge with a lightweight relevance-based reranker to cut RL training cost while matching LLM-as-a-judge alignment, building on DAPO for stability.

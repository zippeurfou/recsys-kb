## Summary

1.  Recommender systems often suffer from popularity bias, disproportionately favoring popular items and misaligning recommendations with users' actual preferences for popular or niche content.
2.  This paper introduces SPREE, a novel method that uses "activation steering" at inference time to adaptively adjust model outputs based on each user's estimated personal bias towards popularity, aiming for better alignment rather than uniform debiasing.
3.  Experiments demonstrate that SPREE significantly improves user-level popularity alignment in recommendations while maintaining good recommendation quality across different datasets.

## Technical note

The work reframes popularity bias as misalignment between a user's historical popularity preference distribution p(s|u) and the recommender's induced distribution q(s|u), introducing Popularity Quantile Calibration as a calibration-error measurement grounded in measurement theory. It proposes SPREE, an inference-time method that identifies a popularity direction in the transformer's activation space and adaptively steers activations per user, varying both direction and magnitude based on each user's estimated bias. Unlike global debiasing or PopSteer (which steers a sparse-autoencoder latent at the user embedding for global effects), SPREE intervenes directly in the residual stream at an automatically selected block. Experiments across multiple datasets show it achieves the best alignment-performance trade-off without retraining.

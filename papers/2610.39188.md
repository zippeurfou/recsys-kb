## Summary

Latent Workspaces for Long-Sequence Recommendation":

The paper addresses the challenge of improving long-sequence recommendation by scaling both the length of user histories considered ("evidence scaling") and the depth of reasoning ("reasoning scaling"). It introduces the Latent Workspace Ranker (LWR), a novel model that fundamentally separates raw evidence (user history) from the state used for reasoning, allowing these two factors to be optimized independently. LWR outperforms existing methods by improving recommendation quality using long histories and deep reasoning, achieving faster performance gains and requiring significantly less computational cost compared to traditional approaches.

## Technical note

Meta's Latent Workspace Ranker (LWR) decouples evidence scaling (longer user histories) from reasoning scaling (more layers) in long-sequence ranking, where sequence encoders like HSTU push every added event through all layers and target-aware pooling or retrieval-based rankers select evidence only once. LWR keeps typed evidence (history, request/user context, matched events) in persistent external banks and propagates only a fixed-size candidate-local workspace (76 slots) that at each layer self-attends (Coordinate), forms 24 bounded queries per source (Query), cross-attends the banks (Reread), passes through a Cross/FM ranking interaction with dense features (Interact), and archives 4 condensate tokens for later layers, feeding an unchanged PLE multi-task head. Trained on 30-50B examples per surface on 128 H100s, at 10K history it improves normalized entropy by 0.19% over HSTU with 69.6% fewer forward GFLOP/request, leads all four compared families from 12K to 65K history, keeps improving with depth (0.41% NE over a 0.5x-8x depth sweep at fixed 10K history), and has the steepest compute-frontier exponent across 468 history-depth configurations (alpha 0.845 vs 0.558 for HSTU). On two billion-scale production surfaces it improves NE by 0.15-0.30% over HSTU and critical-task NE by 0.08-0.09% over the existing baseline.

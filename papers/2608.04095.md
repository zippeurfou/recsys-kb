## Summary

This paper introduces FinPerMA, a new benchmark designed to evaluate how well large language model (LLM) agents can maintain and update personalized user models over long periods in financial advising scenarios. Unlike existing benchmarks that rely on LLM-generated narratives for memory testing, FinPerMA uses theory-informed rules grounded in specific events to assess retention of both facts and preferences against a frozen baseline. The results show that even leading LLMs struggle significantly with this task, especially maintaining preference signals needed for personalization across multiple questions from different users.

## Technical note

Existing personalized-memory benchmarks test factual retention, not whether an agent revises its user model after a consequential event. FinPerMA builds 276 investor personas (demographic and financial marginals calibrated to Shenzhen Stock Exchange and AMAC retail-investor surveys, plus Big-Five traits, a 10-item risk-tolerance score and a 5-item literacy quiz, rejection-sampled so each of four Behavioral Investor Types holds >=20% of the corpus), samples 5-8 events per persona from 97 dated 2020-2026 macro/industry/personal events with >=4-week spacing, and runs a three-layer Impact Model -- deterministic ImpactConstraint, LLM-narrated reaction under that constraint, automated validation with retry -- so labels are auditable and the v8gold corpus is frozen before any memory system is scored. Evaluation is a 3x4 grid of dimensions (Memory Fidelity, Preference Reasoning, Context Adaptation) by checkpoints (zero-memory, in-timeline, post-distraction, and the headline Post-Shock), 2,994 questions total (2,494 six-option MCQs plus 500 open-ended graded by a three-way cross-vendor judge ensemble). Across seven frontier backbones persona history lifts accuracy 1.5-2.4x over the parametric floor, but no full-context run exceeds ~0.47 overall or ~39% MCQ (Qwen-3.8 leads at 46.9/38.7), so the benchmark is far from saturated. With Qwen3.7-Max fixed, plain retrieval beats purpose-built memory: bge_rag recovers 88.2% of the no-memory-to-full-context gap on 1.40k context tokens versus 12.8k for full context (~12x marginal efficiency) while Memobase, Mem0 and MemOS recover only 79.1%, 72.4% and 54.3%, and the full_context-minus-mem0 gap widens from 8.0 points in-timeline (53.4 vs 45.4) to 13.0 at Post-Shock (51.9 vs 38.9) -- summary/profile memory preserves facts but loses the preference signal needed after a shock.

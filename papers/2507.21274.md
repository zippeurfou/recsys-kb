## Summary

This paper introduces **LAAC**, an innovative method combining large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning for recommendation systems aiming to improve diversity and novelty while maintaining accuracy. LAAC uses LLMs as a reference policy to suggest novel items, but trains its own lightweight RL policy using system-specific data through bilevel actor-critic optimization, refining beyond simple LLM guidance. The approach features regularization to prevent over-reliance on potentially unreliable LLM suggestions by keeping critic estimates for unexplored items stable and close to established action values, leading to better performance across various metrics without expensive retraining of the heavy LLM models.

## Technical note

LAAC (LLM-guided Adversarial Actor Critic) improves diversity and novelty in recommendation by using an LLM as a reference policy that proposes novel items, while a lightweight policy refines those suggestions from system data. It casts training as a bilevel/minimax game between actor and critic (extending ATAC), where the critic selectively favors promising novel actions and the actor learns to surpass the LLM. To prevent overestimation of unreliable LLM suggestions, it adds regularization anchoring critic values for unexplored items near well-estimated dataset actions. On MovieLens it outperforms baselines in diversity, novelty, and accuracy, stays robust on imbalanced data, and avoids costly LLM fine-tuning.

## Summary

</think>
The paper addresses the challenge of deploying recommendation models directly on edge devices for real-time recommendations while maintaining personalization, but resource efficiency and accuracy. It proposes a framework called CHORD leverages mixed quantization and device-cloud collaboration to balance. This enables efficient personalized recommendations with reduced inference without full retraining. Experiments show effectiveness.

## Technical note

Deploying sequential rerankers on heterogeneous mobile devices needs compression, but uniform quantization ignores device-specific user interests and on-device finetuning is costly. CHORD uses channel-wise mixed-precision quantization driven by device-cloud collaboration: devices share a frozen randomly-initialized model, and the cloud uses hypernetwork-based sensitivity extractors to compute layer-, filter-, and element-level parameter saliency from each user's profile, generating a personalized quantization strategy. Inspired by the lottery ticket hypothesis, the per-channel strategy is encoded in just 2 bits per channel (vs 32-bit weights), enabling one forward-pass adaptation with no backpropagation or retraining. Experiments on three real-world datasets with SASRec and Caser backbones demonstrate improved accuracy, efficiency, and adaptivity.

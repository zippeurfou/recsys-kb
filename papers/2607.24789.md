## Summary

1.  The authors introduce NEXT, a video recommendation system using a Vision-Language Model that infers user intent after watching an item to suggest relevant follow-up videos, moving beyond simple similarity metrics.
2.  To make this work practical at scale, they developed and trained a specific model called NEXT-8B with three stages: enhanced perception learning, distribution-aligned supervised fine-tuning, and policy optimization for alignment.
3.  Deployment into a social media system showed significant production benefits (+0.53% watch time), demonstrating that their carefully trained compact model can effectively serve as a reasoning engine for targeted recommendations in real-world applications.

## Technical note

NEXT reframes short-video recommendation from Item-to-Item similarity to Item-to-Intent-to-Item reasoning: it infers a viewer's next intent from the just-watched video and retrieves concrete follow-ups, storing high-precision directed edges in a NEXT Knowledge Graph built from explicit continuation cues, VLM-generated intent-search queries, behavior pivots, and a constrained directional Swing collaborative signal (SID-prefix-partitioned, order-aware). Its engine NEXT-8B is an 8B vision-language model post-trained with Perception-Enhanced RL (a frozen text verifier rewards query-agnostic visual evidence), Distribution-Aligned SFT over real and synthetic DocVQA data, and last-mile GRPO; it reaches 97.28% ANLS on DocVQA (best single model, beating the 235B Qwen3-VL at about 30x smaller) and +3.3% next-intent logic quality via LLM-as-a-judge. Deployed as an additive offline-mined retrieval path (reusing the existing online ranker) on a roughly 100M-user platform, it lifts watch time +0.53% and distinct video exposure +0.51%, targeting popularity bias and narrative continuity. It is an extra retrieval path, explicitly not a separate or unified ranker.

## Summary

Large Language Models (LLMs) often produce inaccurate or hallucinated information, posing significant risks in critical domains requiring trust. To address this, the paper introduces DAVinCI, a novel framework combining claim attribution (linking statements to model components/sources) and verification (checking claim truth using reasoning and confidence). DAVinCI significantly improves the factual accuracy and reliability of LLM outputs across benchmark datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating these two processes for building more trustworthy AI systems.

## Technical note

DAVinCI is a two-stage framework that couples attribution and verification to improve factual reliability of LLM outputs. The attribution module scores claim-evidence relevance using either full-passage gold evidence or span-based extraction via a QA model, producing evidence for downstream checking. The verification module applies a transformer entailment classifier that labels each claim Supported/Refuted/Not-Enough-Info with a confidence score, then applies confidence recalibration: predictions below a threshold tau are downgraded to NEI to curb overconfident errors. Evaluated on FEVER and CLIMATE-FEVER, DAVinCI improves classification accuracy, attribution precision/recall/F1 by 5-20% over verification-only baselines. Ablations isolate contributions of evidence span selection, recalibration thresholds, and retrieval quality; a modular implementation is released.

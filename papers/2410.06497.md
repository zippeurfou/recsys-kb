## Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of running complex deep learning models for user representations in Meta's ad system, questioning if real-time inference is always necessary given strict SLAs. It introduces ERCache, an efficient caching framework that leverages triangular relationships between model complexity, embedding freshness, and service requirements to minimize unnecessary computations while ensuring reliability. After deployment supporting over 30 ranking models, ERCache has proven effective in saving computational resources without violating service SLA requirements.

## Technical note

Complex deep user-representation models are expensive to infer for every ad request under tight compute budgets and SLAs at Meta scale. Analyzing user access patterns, the authors find most user-model inferences cluster within short timeframes, exposing a triangular tradeoff between model complexity, embedding freshness, and SLAs. ERCache is a caching framework that splits caches into direct and failover types with per-model customized settings and eviction policies to reuse user representations while bounding staleness. Deployed at Meta for over six months, it supports 30+ ranking models, conserving compute while meeting SLA requirements.

## Summary

This paper introduces SelfDR, a framework designed to leverage large language models (LLMs) for recommendation. The core challenge addressed is the high computational cost associated with explicitly generating reasoning traces for LLM-based recommendations. SelfDR distills an LLM's internal reasoning process directly into its recommendations, bypassing the need for explicit trace generation. It achieves this by training a base LLM to generate relevant text rationales for its own predictions and then distilling those rationales back into the LLM for direct recommendations, maintaining efficiency while improving accuracy.

## Technical note

Meituan authors propose SelfDR to capture the accuracy benefit of reasoning-augmented LLM recommendation without paying its inference cost, keeping the deployed model in the direct-recommendation regime that emits a single candidate identifier token. Three stages, all on the same LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct backbone with no external LLM: (1) a Reasoner is trained with GRPO where the reward is the downstream next-item top-1 accuracy of a frozen recommender conditioned on the generated rationale, with anti-leakage prompting plus masking of any 3+ consecutive words overlapping the item title; (2) the rationale is concatenated into an instruction-tuned recommender input to form the teacher; (3) a student with identical parameters but no rationale is trained with offline reverse-KL distillation on pre-softmax logits over candidate character identifiers, mixed with cross-entropy through a dynamic weight alpha that depends on the teacher rank vs threshold k=5, the teacher-student rank difference through a sigmoid, and a penalty beta applied when the teacher underranks the student. Evaluated in a reranking setting over SASRec top-20 candidates (positive not guaranteed present) on Amazon Clothing, Amazon Home and MovieLens-1M. SelfDR is best on every metric on all three datasets, e.g. Clothing H_l@1 0.0132 vs 0.0114 for RecR1 and H_g@1 0.0118, ML1M H_l@1 0.0845 vs 0.0753 for SOFT and H_g@1 0.0735 vs 0.0575 for COT4Rec. The locally trained 8B Reasoner beats GPT-4o-mini, Claude-3-Haiku, DeepSeek-V3 and GPT-5-Chat as a rationale source (teacher H_g@1 0.552 vs 0.508 for the best external LLM on 2000 Clothing training samples), and a stronger general LLM does not imply a better distilled student. Inference is 109.46 ms/instance with 1 output token, roughly 6x faster than RecR1 (665.12 ms) and RecSAVER (623.38 ms), with 54.18 h total training. Ablations: distillation-only beats fixed alpha=0.5 and supervised-only (which overfits and falls below the pre-distillation model); gains hold with LightGCN and ComiRec candidate generators and transfer out-of-domain between Clothing and Home; best alpha_base 0.9, beta around 0.1. Caveat: public benchmarks only, no production A/B, and absolute hit rates are around 1 percent on the Amazon datasets.

## Summary

1.  This paper addresses inefficiencies and inaccuracies in agentic RAG systems like Search-R1 when handling complex questions, particularly due to repetitive information retrieval and poor contextualization.
2.  The authors propose two test-time modifications – a contextualization module for better information integration and a de-duplication module for retrieving more relevant documents – and evaluate their combination.
3.  Their best-performing combination, using GPT-4.1-mini for contextualization, significantly improved answer accuracy (5.6% EM increase) while also boosting retrieval efficiency (10.5% fewer turns) compared to the standard Search-R1 baseline.

## Technical note

The paper proposes training-free test-time modifications to the Search-R1 agentic RAG pipeline, which interleaves RL-trained reasoning with dense (E5) retrieval over Wikipedia but suffers from information forgetting (repetitive retrieval) and ineffective information extraction. A contextualization module prompts an external LLM to parse and reformulate retrieved passages into a concise representation injected into the reasoning chain and retained across turns, while a de-duplication module filters previously seen documents and returns the next most relevant novel ones; a hybrid applies both sequentially. Evaluated on HotpotQA and Natural Questions with exact-match, LLM-as-a-judge, and average-turn metrics, the best variant (GPT-4.1-mini contextualization) improved EM by 5.6% and reduced retrieval turns by 10.5% versus the Qwen2.5-7B Search-R1 baseline, without altering model architecture or training.

## Summary

This paper identifies the need for better user feedback mechanisms for generative AI systems, noting that while regulations require post-deployment feedback, there's a lack of guidance on designing effective ones. The authors conducted a collaborative study between academia and eBay to address this gap, evaluating current industry approaches and identifying issues like poor discoverability and confusing terminology. Based on their findings, they developed best-practice recommendations and designed a prototype feedback tool aiming to provide a positive, flexible experience for users and actionable, usable data for product teams.

## Technical note

Academic researchers and eBay study how GenAI products should collect post-deployment user feedback that consumers can use and product teams can act on, motivated by post-market monitoring rules (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF) and by OpenAI's report that a reward signal partly built from thumbs feedback contributed to GPT-4o's sycophancy. They inspected 40 popular GenAI products in March 2025, found feedback mechanisms in 32 (including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Amazon Rufus and six eBay features) and taxonomized them on 16 dimensions, then had 24 eBay experts rate 12 products against 11 Nielsen-style heuristics and ran moderated cognitive walkthroughs of an interactive Figma prototype with 13 eBay buyers and sellers. Common problems were hard-to-find entry points (hover, long-press or menus), generic bug-report forms that ignore GenAI issues such as bias or offensive output, little solicitation of positive feedback, missing or buried responsible-AI categories, jargon in preset options, web and mobile flows that differ, and little transparency about how feedback and the data sent with it are used. The recommendations call for high-contrast thumbs up/down below the output with a bold 'Share feedback' label and a separate 'Report a problem' path, six to eight optional multi-select presets ending in 'Other' (with merged responsible-AI options such as 'Offensive or biased' and 'Inappropriate or harmful') plus a broad free-text box, a thank-you snackbar with a short unsend window, a filled icon as submission status, and a brief privacy disclaimer next to Submit that links to a feedback-specific FAQ. The paper is an HCI and governance study with no model training, but it argues that binary votes give a shallow fine-tuning signal and that standardized templates make signals comparable across features at the cost of homogenizing user input, and eBay plans to base a new GenAI feedback survey template on the prototype.

## Summary

1.  Large Language Models (LLMs) waste computation on static prefilling, leading to inefficiency when reasoning over vast document collections.
2.  The authors introduce "Cartridges at Scale," which creates many small, reusable key-value caches representing individual documents or segments, dynamically rotating them between GPU and storage for efficient memory use while maintaining accuracy better than a single monolithic cache.
3.  This approach significantly outperforms the original idea of one large cartridge by improving performance with much larger document sets (over a million tokens) at similar token budgets, and when combined with retrieval selection, it achieves comparable or superior results to standard RAG using fewer prompt tokens.

## Technical note

Cartridges at Scale (CAS) extends the Cartridge method, which distills a document's context into a small trainable KV cache (10-100x compression) via a context-distillation objective on Self-Study synthetic QA pairs, keeping model weights frozen. The paper shows independently trained per-document cartridges suffer catastrophic interference at inference (collapsing to near-chance when mixed), and fixes this with mixed-visibility joint training that packs distractor cartridges via attention masks. A budget manager rotates hundreds of cartridges between GPU and persistent storage with optimizer offloading and prioritized pool rotation, scaling to million-token collections. CAS improves 10-31 points over a monolithic cartridge at comparable token budgets, and when paired with dense retrieval for cartridge selection matches or exceeds text RAG accuracy at 3-4x fewer prompt tokens.

## Summary

Current e-commerce search generative retrieval methods struggle with maintaining consistent query intent, primarily due to static product information limitations in Semantic IDs (SIDs) and inadequate training data for low-exposure products. To address this, the paper introduces ICEGR, an end-to-end framework designed to keep intent consistent throughout the generative retrieval training by using three integrated components: intent-aware SID construction, synthetic query-enhanced fine-tuning, and relevance-calibrated preference learning. Offline experiments and an A/B test on Baidu E-commerce Search demonstrate that ICEGR significantly improves standard retrieval metrics like Recall and NDCG, and crucially leads to higher Click-Through Rate (CTR), order volume, and Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) in real-world deployment.

## Technical note

Baidu E-commerce Search deploys ICEGR, a three-stage generative retrieval pipeline whose thesis is that query intent must be preserved at every stage, not just unified architecturally. Stage 1 Intent-Aware SID construction fine-tunes an 8B e-commerce embedding model with InfoNCE on query-product click pairs, propagates product vectors T=3 steps over a co-click graph (edge weight = sum over shared queries of sqrt(n_iq * n_jq), residual alpha_g=0.2), fuses a click-frequency-weighted query-intent profile through a confidence gate (lambda=1.0, Q_max=50), then quantizes 256-d fused representations with a 3-level RQ-KMeans at 1024 codes per level. Stage 2 SQE-SFT discards product-to-SID memorization tasks in favor of a single query-to-SID objective, synthesizing multi-granularity queries (product identity, category scope, series/variant, attribute-constrained) from parsed catalog semantics, pretraining on synthetic pairs then fine-tuning on real log queries. Stage 3 RCPO is a margin-adaptive DPO variant: preference pairs come from beam-search candidates screened by a 0.6B semantic relevance scorer and ordered by a Smoothed Business Preference (click 0.2, pay 0.3, GMV 0.5, category-level Bayesian smoothing kappa=10), with per-pair beta_eff = beta * clip(1 + log((2-delta_r)/(2+delta_r)), 0.2, 1.8) downweighting pairs that already have strong offline evidence, plus a length-normalized auxiliary SFT term. On ~3 months of logs (12.8M queries, 14.6M products) with a 0.5B backbone, ICEGR beats the production ProdGR baseline by +21.7% Recall@20, +26.6% MRR@20 and +26.6% NDCG@20; component decomposition credits SQE-SFT with 76.7% of the Recall@20 gain, RCPO 17.7%, IA-SID 5.6%, and popularity-stratified gains are largest for Low (+36.2%) and Tail (+21.2%) items. Backbone scaling from 0.5B to 7B lifts Recall@20 from 0.5043 to 0.5334 with no plateau, but 0.5B is what ships. A two-week A/B on 20% of live Baidu e-commerce traffic gave +3.52% CTR, +15.96% order volume, +7.53% GMV, +8.84% Show PV and +5.27% long-click rate, serving 2,200 peak QPS at 156 ms average latency on 67 NVIDIA L20 GPUs with beam size 50. Caveats: the only comparison point is Baidu's own ProdGR (other industrial GR systems are declared non-comparable), and removing the pair-specific beta collapses blinded human top-5 relevance from 0.87 to 0.58, showing the business reward is actively harmful without relevance calibration.

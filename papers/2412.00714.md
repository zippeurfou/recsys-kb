## Summary

This paper explores how recommendation systems are advancing beyond simple scaling of embedding tables, which traditionally plateaued performance. Inspired by Large Language Models (LLMs), they now utilize vastly expanded network parameters, up to thousands of billions, enabling significant improvements in relevance retrieval. The authors analyze the underlying scaling laws across different architectures and backbone models using Meta's generative recommendation model HSTU as a case study, demonstrating its effectiveness on complex user behavior tasks for the first time and offering future insights.

## Technical note

Empirically studies scaling laws for large recommendation models, motivated by Meta's HSTU generative recommender. The authors compare scalability of transformer backbones (HSTU, Llama, GPT, SASRec) as attention blocks increase, run ablations to locate the source of HSTU's scaling behavior, and enhance SASRec by porting in HSTU modules. They evaluate HSTU on complex behavior sequences with side information and, for the first time, on ranking tasks. Findings indicate HSTU-style dense-parameter scaling yields gains but struggles with intricate multi-behavior data, and they offer design guidance for scalable ranking models.

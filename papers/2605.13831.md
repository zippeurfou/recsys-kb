## Summary

This paper systematically studies training vision-language models (LVLMs) to handle very long contexts (up to 128K tokens). Their analysis shows that long-document Visual Question Answering (VQA) is more effective than OCR data for training, and they identify three key findings: balanced data lengths are best, retrieval-heavy data is crucial, and VQA data largely maintains short-context skills. Based on these insights, they introduce MMProLong, a model trained from Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a minimal token budget, which significantly improves long-document VQA performance and generalizes well to other long-context tasks.

## Technical note

The paper studies long-context continued pre-training (LongPT) for large vision-language models, extending Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 32K to 128K context with only a 5B-token budget. Ablations show long-document VQA is substantially more effective than OCR transcription, balanced sequence-length distributions beat target-length-focused data, and key-information retrieval is the primary bottleneck favoring retrieval-heavy mixtures. Pure long-document VQA largely preserves short-context ability, reducing the need to mix short data. The resulting MMProLong improves long-document VQA by 7.1%, generalizes to 256K/512K contexts without extra training, and transfers to webpage needle retrieval, vision-text compression, and long-video understanding.

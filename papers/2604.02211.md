## Summary

This paper surveys the evolution of video recommender systems, highlighting the limitations of traditional single-model approaches. It introduces multi-agent video recommenders (MAVRS) that use specialized agents for understanding, reasoning, and feedback, often powered by large language models (LLMs). The work provides a taxonomy of collaboration patterns, analyzes existing frameworks, and identifies key challenges and future research directions for this emerging field.

## Technical note

This survey traces the evolution of multi-agent video recommendation systems (MAVRS), bridging classical multi-agent reinforcement learning with foundation-model and conversational-AI paradigms toward LLM-powered architectures. It presents a taxonomy of collaborative patterns and analyzes coordination mechanisms across video domains from short-form clips to educational media, citing frameworks such as MMRF, MACRec, and Agent4Rec. It argues the video 'modality gap' necessitates agentic decomposition, where perception agents compress raw video into semantic summaries and reasoning agents perform logic-heavy personalization to sidestep single-model context limits. Open challenges highlighted include scalability, multimodal understanding, incentive alignment, and directions such as hybrid RL-LLM systems, lifelong personalization, and self-improving recommenders.

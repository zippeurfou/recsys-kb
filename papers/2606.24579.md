## Summary

This paper investigates how Large Language Models access different facts stored within their parameters across languages, finding that standard methods often miss localized information leading to inconsistent knowledge transfer.

Researchers explored cross-lingual prompting strategies and identified four key dimensions directly influencing retrieval performance from these prompts. Evaluating these on 17 diverse languages showed significant improvements in factual recall achieved through inference-time exploration without needing larger models.

The findings reveal that multilingual prompt exploration unlocks latent parametric knowledge more efficiently than just scaling a single language model, representing a better compute frontier and greatly enhancing cross-lingual consistency beyond simple accuracy gains.

## Technical note

This paper studies how to surface facts stored in LLM parameters that are hard to access in the query language, showing that reasoning through other languages can reach localized knowledge a monolingual path misses. It identifies four inherent dimensions of cross-lingual exploration that govern parametric-knowledge retrieval and evaluates them on multilingual factual benchmarks spanning 17 typologically diverse languages. Aggregating multiple linguistic reasoning paths significantly improves factual recall and cross-lingual consistency, beyond what accuracy gains alone would predict. The authors argue multilingual prompt exploration is an inference-time strategy that offers a better compute Pareto frontier than native-language scaling; no recommendation-systems relevance.

## Summary

This paper introduces MESH as a unified framework for large-scale heterogeneous retrieval systems, addressing an issue called "Scaling Bias" where model improvements benefit frequent content more than rare or fresh items.

MESH mitigates this bias through a modularized architecture with gated bias correction and partitions the feature space to protect sparse-item signals from interference during training.

The approach significantly improves scaling behaviour for less common content (14x better power-law exponent) and demonstrates positive real-world results (+5.5% fresh repins, 55% funnel improvement) while also boosting system throughput in production.

## Technical note

Identifies the 'Scaling Bias of Heterogeneity' in unified two-tower retrieval, where added model capacity disproportionately helps high-frequency evergreen content while fresh and long-tail items stay in a stagnant scaling regime (a rich-get-richer loop). MESH (Modularized Effective Scaling for Heterogeneity) partitions features into independent user/item/context sub-towers using DHEN-based signal amplifiers that build semantic super tokens, then applies a Residual Gated Bias Correction (RGBC) stage to give sparse items a protected gradient path; training uses in-batch negatives with frequency-based logit correction and ANN serving. Offline this yields a 14x improvement in the fresh-item power-law scaling exponent (beta_fresh 0.0059 to 0.0826). On Pinterest's billion-scale Related Pins, online A/B tests show +5.5% fresh-item impressions and +4.9% repins, +55% fresh warm-start funnel (CE) efficiency, +0.46% user retention, and a 2.87x serving throughput gain via TorchScript inter-op parallelism.

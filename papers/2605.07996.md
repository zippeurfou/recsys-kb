## Summary

This paper introduces "context-ordinal Nash equilibrium" as a way to analyze games without requiring precise numerical utilities, only ordinal preferences (rankings) of actions. The key challenge was redefining best-response using social choice theory to aggregate these ordinal preferences. The authors prove the existence of this generalized equilibrium under certain conditions and develop methods for computation and learning, demonstrating applicability to scenarios with only elicited preferences.

## Technical note

This work generalizes Nash equilibrium to settings where players provide only an ordinal ranking of their actions given co-players' joint actions, rather than numerical utilities. The key move is redefining best-response via social choice theory: under an opponent's mixed strategy, a population of rank votes proportional to the mixed strategy is aggregated by a voting rule (e.g., Borda) to select the best response. The resulting context-ordinal Nash equilibrium is shown to exist under mild conditions on aggregation rules, with accompanying notions of regularization, approximation, regret, complexity, and learning rules for computing equilibria. It is applied to elicited human preference data in agent-evaluation (Arcade) and ranked-choice leader-selection experiments.

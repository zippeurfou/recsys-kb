## Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of using user ultra-long sequences effectively in recommendation candidate retrieval. It introduces LongRetriever, a practical framework incorporating two novel techniques – "in-context training" and "multi-context retrieval" – designed to handle these long interaction histories specifically during retrieval while ensuring consistency with how models are trained. These innovations demonstrate statistically significant improvements through online A/B testing on an e-commerce platform, confirming their effectiveness in retrieving relevant candidate items from extensive user purchase or view history.

## Technical note

LongRetriever brings ultra-long user behavior sequences into the candidate-retrieval (matching) stage, where target-attention approaches are intractable due to billions of candidate items. It defines mutually exclusive item categories as interpretable interests and, per interest, hard-searches the lifelong sequence (SIM-style) for a category-matched subsequence, encoding it with a Transformer user tower to produce multiple interest-specific dense vectors under shared parameters. In-Context Training restructures mini-batches so subsequence and candidate share one category, preventing category-matching data leakage under the in-batch contrastive objective; Multi-Context Retrieval selects interests via a time-weighted Random-in-Top strategy and retrieves from per-category sub-indexes. Deployed on a billion-user e-commerce platform, it beat MIND with A/B gains including +1.70% orders per user and +1.33% UV CVR.

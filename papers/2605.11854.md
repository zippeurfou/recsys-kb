## Summary

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) face a training-inference discrepancy where standard fine-tuning is inefficient compared to their multi-step inference process. While trajectory-based self-distillation can accelerate inference, the authors investigated if these trajectories could also improve model knowledge directly. They found that standard fine-tuning on trajectories yields only minor gains, leading to the development of TABOM, a novel post-training framework that aligns training with the easy-to-hard inference trajectory using Boltzmann modeling and ranking objectives, which significantly improves performance in new domains and reduces catastrophic forgetting.

## Technical note

TABOM (Trajectory-Aligned optimization via Boltzmann Modeling) is a post-training framework for Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) that resolves the mismatch between NELBO-based SFT, which reconstructs randomly masked tokens in a single step, and inference, which follows a confidence-guided multi-step easy-to-hard denoising trajectory. The authors observe that prior trajectory-based self-distillation targets sampling-step compression and can even degrade full-diffusion decoding, and that naively fine-tuning on self-distilled trajectories with NELBO yields only marginal gains. TABOM instead models the inference unmasking preference as a Boltzmann distribution over per-token predictive entropy and derives a tractable pairwise ranking surrogate to align the model's certainty ordering with the observed decoding trajectory via KL minimization. It achieves substantial gains in new domains, expands the effective knowledge boundary, and mitigates catastrophic forgetting; a Trajectory Discrimination Score (TDS) verifies it reshapes the entropy landscape toward the easy-to-hard bias.

## Summary

This paper addresses two key limitations in multimodal retrieval: semantic drift (misalignment between text and visuals) and visual neglect (over-reliance on text). To solve these, the authors introduce SSA-ME, a framework using LMMs and visual experts to identify salient concepts and guide attention, along with a feature regeneration module. Extensive experiments show SSA-ME achieves state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating that incorporating subject-level modeling significantly improves multimodal retrieval quality.

## Technical note

SSA-ME improves unified multimodal retrieval with LMMs by adding subject-level modeling that sample-level contrastive learning overlooks, addressing semantic alignment deviation (mislocalized text-referred regions) and visual modality neglect (over-reliance on text). A Saliency-Guided Attention Alignment (SGA) module uses an LMM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with visual experts and tailored prompts to localize salient subjects that are both visually recognizable in the query image and semantically relevant to the positive sample, then distills this via a saliency-guided loss that aligns latent attention with meaningful regions. A Saliency-Driven Feature Regeneration (SDR) module recalibrates and re-fuses visual features from predicted saliency maps into the global embedding, boosting visual attention weight. Embeddings are extracted under the Explicit One-token Limitation (EOL) principle. SSA-ME achieves state-of-the-art results on the MMEB benchmark with qualitative interpretability analyses.

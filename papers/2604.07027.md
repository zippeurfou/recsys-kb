## Summary

Standard classification models often fail after deployment due to nonstationarity, even if they generalize well initially. To combat this, the paper reframes classification as time series prediction, conditioning the classifier on a sequence of historical examples beyond the training cutoff. It introduces a learned retrieval mechanism that selects relevant past examples via input-dependent queries, enabling the model to adapt to distribution shifts using historical data without retraining. Experiments show this improves robustness and scales predictably with VRAM as the historical sequence length increases.

## Technical note

This work reframes nonstationary classification as time-series prediction by conditioning a classifier on a long sequence of historical labeled examples that extends past the training cutoff. A learned discrete retrieval mechanism generates input-dependent queries that sample a small set of relevant historical items (via attention-style logits over cached low-dimensional keys), trained end-to-end with the classifier using a REINFORCE-style score-based gradient estimator with a greedy baseline. This keeps the full corpus off-device on an arbitrary filesystem, giving memory that scales predictably rather than with corpus size. On synthetic rotating-boundary and needle-in-haystack tasks and Amazon Electronics Reviews '23, it improves robustness to distribution shift; notably, retrieving only historical labels (not full features) is what mitigates degradation on out-of-distribution post-2014 data.

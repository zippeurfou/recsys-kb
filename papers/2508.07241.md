## Summary

1.  Existing recommender systems struggle with new ("cold-start") videos because they lack interaction history, making standard collaborative filtering less effective due to data sparsity, while purely content-based methods don't leverage user specificity enough.
2.  The authors propose SocRipple, a tailored two-stage framework designed specifically for distributing cold-start items on social graph platforms, aiming to balance exposure with relevance in this context.
3.  In the first stage, it uses the creator's connections for targeted initial recommendations; then, using stable user embeddings and early signals from those recommendations (the "ripple"), it performs a similarity search to find further relevant items, boosting distribution by +36% while maintaining engagement rates on new videos.

## Technical note

Meta's SocRipple is a two-stage retrieval framework for distributing cold-start videos on social-graph platforms. Stage 1 (Social Boost) seeds a new video to the creator's followers for high-precision initial exposure and logs early positive engagement. Stage 2 (Neighbor Expansion) uses stable pre-computed two-tower user embeddings and real-time KNN search to 'ripple' the item outward from early engagers to users with similar latent interests, trained with a sampled-softmax negative-log-likelihood objective. This cascade balances exploration (new-item discovery) against exploitation (personalized relevance) and, unlike static content-based or GNN methods, adapts to real-time signals. Large-scale experiments show +36% cold-start item distribution while maintaining engagement rate.

## Summary

This paper argues that standard model validation techniques relying on a train-test split are fundamentally flawed when applied to complex social systems. It presents formal impossibility proofs showing this common paradigm is often invalid for estimating real-world risks, including counterfactual and causal effects, which is crucial in AI development.

The issue arises from how modern data collection interacts with the assumptions of the train-test framework, particularly beyond basic prediction tasks like recommenders or large language models reasoning. The author demonstrates that even widely used benchmarks fail to adequately address these epistemic problems inherent in passive data gathering for complex social applications.

To tackle this lack of rigorous validation and understanding model reliability, the paper suggests remedies such as participatory data curation and open science practices, emphasizing the need for more transparent and trustworthy AI development processes specifically suited to social system complexities.

## Technical note

Nickel (FAIR at Meta) argues the train-test paradigm is invalid for risk estimation in complex social systems where data is collected passively rather than actively/i.i.d. The paper proves formal impossibility results: for widely-used inference settings (including recommender systems and LLM reasoning), no risk estimator -- counterfactual or causal -- can be validated with high probability under current data-collection practices. It illustrates the epistemic failure on the MovieLens benchmark and notes that neither naive scaling nor limited benchmarks resolve the issue. Proposed remedies are participatory data curation and open-science practices for trustworthy AI in social contexts.

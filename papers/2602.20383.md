## Summary

This paper addresses the issue that aggregating machine learning predictions of individual treatment effects (CATEs) to group levels can introduce systematic bias, leading to inaccurate estimates of the group-level average treatment effect (GATE). The authors develop a statistical framework to detect this group bias by comparing predicted CATE aggregates to the actual GATE from a randomized experiment. They propose a simple, closed-form shrinkage method to correct this bias and analyze its economic impact for personalized targeting, validating their approach on large-scale experimental data.

## Technical note

This paper shows that aggregating personalized CATE predictions to ex-post subgroups often fails to recover the group-average treatment effect (GATE), even under correct identification, due to non-collapsibility. It introduces a model-agnostic, asymptotically normal estimator and formal statistical test to detect group bias from randomized-experiment data, applicable to additive and relative effect scales. For mitigation it proposes a shrinkage-based bias correction that tunes debiasing to the signal-to-noise ratio of each group, deriving a closed-form oracle minimizer and feasible estimators. It analyzes the profit-targeting trade-off where correcting aggregate bias can degrade individualized targeting decisions, illustrated via off-policy evaluation on the Criteo Uplift dataset and A/B data from an online travel platform.

## Summary

Researchers developed Lingshu-Cell, a generative model for single-cell transcriptomics aiming to simulate cellular states and predict responses to changes, a core goal in virtual cell creation. This masked discrete diffusion model operates directly on gene expression data without needing prior gene selection, capturing complex relationships across nearly 18,000 genes. It accurately represents diverse cellular heterogeneity and demonstrates strong predictive power for gene expression changes due to cell identity or genetic perturbations, outperforming existing methods on benchmarks.

## Technical note

Lingshu-Cell is a masked discrete diffusion model for single-cell transcriptomics that explicitly learns the distribution of transcriptomic states to support conditional simulation, positioned as a generative cellular world model rather than a static representation learner. It operates in a discrete gene-expression token space compatible with sparse, non-sequential scRNA-seq data, using a masking-and-prediction objective that enables non-autoregressive bidirectional refinement across ~18,000 genes without prior gene selection or high-variability filtering. By jointly embedding cell-type or donor identity with perturbation context, it predicts whole-transcriptome expression changes for novel identity-perturbation combinations. It reproduces transcriptomic distributions, marker-gene patterns, and cell-subtype proportions across nine tissues and five species, and achieves leading performance on the Virtual Cell Challenge H1 genetic perturbation benchmark using only ~0.6M training cells plus cytokine-response prediction in human PBMCs.

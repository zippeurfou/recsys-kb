## Summary

Based on this paper:
1.  Semantic-ID (SID) generation represents items using sequences of shared semantic tokens, allowing generative models to recombine these tokens beyond existing item IDs.
2.  However, current SID-based models struggle significantly with cold-start recommendations for *new* items that contain unseen atomic tokens or require SID paths not previously observed; they can only rarely reach such entirely novel items based on the observed token and prefix information available at training time.
3.  This limitation arises because SID generation relies on a hierarchical structure where early tokens select broad semantic regions, but later tokens are still dependent on finer-grained context learned from data, making it inherently constrained rather than fully open-ended for truly unseen item composition.

## Technical note

Diagnoses whether semantic-ID (SID) based generative recommendation can inductively reach cold, newly emerging items under realistic temporal deployment. It introduces an absolute-time sliding-window protocol (replacing leaky leave-one-out), re-evaluates TIGER against SASRec, and analyzes failures with a token-level coldness taxonomy (all-token-seen / any-token-unseen / prefix-seen) plus oracle-prefix probing, reinterpreting autoregressive SID decoding as hierarchical semantic bucketing. TIGER's unseen Recall@20 is near-zero (cold ratios up to 0.94) and even all-token-seen cold items are often unreachable because RQ-VAE SIDs form ordered residual paths; hits concentrate in deep-prefix-supported regions. Of three diagnostic variants (TIGER-SID factorized tokenizer, TIGER-Scorer candidate scoring, TIGER-Edge cross-attention edge context), TIGER-Scorer recovers the most unseen performance, showing the autoregressive decoder rather than the encoder is the reachability bottleneck. A diagnostic study, not a new production pipeline.

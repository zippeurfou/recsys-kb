## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating large language models' genuine scientific reasoning capabilities, which is obscured by existing benchmarks that conflate retrieval, toolchains, and memorization. To solve this, the authors introduce DeR2, a controlled deep-research sandbox that decouples document-grounded evidence access from multi-step reasoning by varying the available information in four distinct regimes. Experiments on DeR2 reveal significant variation among state-of-the-art models, identifying issues like mode-switch fragility and structural concept misuse as potential limitations.

## Technical note

Current deep-research evaluations confound reasoning with retrieval/toolchain choices and are contaminated by parametric memorization and open-web volatility. DeR2 is a controlled sandbox that decouples evidence access from reasoning through four regimes (Instruction-only, Concepts, Related-only, Full-set), yielding interpretable gaps that separate retrieval loss from reasoning loss and enable fine-grained error attribution. A two-phase validation enforces parametric failure without evidence while ensuring oracle-concept solvability, and each instance ships a frozen 2023-2025 document library with expert concepts and validated rationales. Experiments reveal mode-switch fragility and structural concept misuse, where models name concepts but fail to execute them.

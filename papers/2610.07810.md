## Summary

1.  Airbnb faced challenges with their production filter-ranking system due to its reliance on expensive, hand-engineered features from pre-aggregated data.
2.  SIFT, a transformer-based model, addresses this by learning guest preferences directly from raw behavioral sequences, creating a unified representation that feeds multiple ranking tasks.
3.  This approach significantly improved performance (+51.9% and +62.8% PR-AUC offline) and engagement metrics online, while demonstrating impressive extensibility by rapidly adding support for new filter types with minimal changes.

## Technical note

Airbnb's SIFT replaces a production filter ranker (a two-layer MLP over hand-built, nightly pre-aggregated user features such as 30-day search-then-book counts) with a transformer that learns guest preferences from raw behavioral sequences. A single-layer, 4-head transformer (d=128) reads up to 384 chronological events (filtered searches, reservation requests, bookings, cancellations) in a PinnerFormer-style fixed-width token schema and emits a 32-d user embedding shared by three jointly trained head types: a booking head trained with BCE weighted by w=1+1/ln(pos+1) on the booked listing's rank, so bookings the filter plausibly caused count more; a multi-label engagement head over 31 amenity filters; and Frank & Hall ordinal heads for bedroom, bed and bathroom thresholds, with filters ranked by P(book) x P(engage). Training uses a rolling 365-day window with 7-day forward-attributed booking labels, Adam (beta2 0.98, eps 1e-9) with Transformer-style warmup and inverse-square-root decay, batch 512 and Horovod on 20 GPUs for about 12 hours, and zero-history guests get a learned cold-state embedding instead of a zero vector. Serving stays under 10 ms at over 1,000 QPS because a daily incremental job recomputes embeddings only for guests with new events into a key-value store and only the booking head runs per candidate filter, though same-day sessions (about 30 to 40% of bookings) then see stale embeddings. Offline, booking PR-AUC rises 51.9% (0.1388 to 0.2108) and amenity-engagement macro PR-AUC 62.8%, removing the sequence encoder costs 33.1% booking PR-AUC and the heads come out well calibrated; online A/B tests showed +20.0% recommended-filter clickers, +0.72% searchers with filters and +10.7%/+3.9%/+0.52% bathroom/bedroom/bed filter use with flat bookings, and a hotel-intent class added to the engagement head lifted uncancelled hotel bookings 3.8% and marketplace bookings 0.76%.

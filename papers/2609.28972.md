## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of recommendation across multiple countries in e-commerce, where disjoint ID spaces prevent effective knowledge transfer between markets. The authors propose CMRec, a generative recommendation framework inspired by code-switching, which explicitly injects cross-country knowledge by creating a shared semantic codebook and synthesizing mixed-country user behavior sequences with realistic constraints. Experiments show that CMRec significantly improves recommendation quality in data-sparse countries without harming performance in data-rich ones, leading to measurable increases in advertising revenue and orders.

## Technical note

Cross-country e-commerce deployments keep disjoint user and item ID spaces per market, so even generative recommenders with a shared token space transfer knowledge only through parameters, never through co-occurrence in training sequences. CMRec (Taobao/Alibaba) builds a shared RQ-VAE codebook (L=4) from item embeddings of a jointly trained two-tower i2i model (sampled softmax, cross-country negative pool) on Qwen-VL content vectors, then synthesizes code-mixed twin sequences by swapping 10% of items in 10% of samples for cross-country neighbors that pass both a token Hamming-distance filter (tau=1) and a dynamic side-info filter (price, audience, popularity; cosine >= 0.8), since the codebook is refreshed too rarely to capture those attributes. A context-aware loss weights each twin by a stop-gradient likelihood ratio P(y'|x)/(P(y|x)+P(y'|x)) under the original context, down-weighting implausible substitutions. On a 6-country industrial ads dataset (1B+ interactions) and Amazon M2, CMRec on a GenCDR backbone beats SASRec, HSTU, TIGER, REG4Rec, GenCDR and content-only or CF-only augmentation, with the largest gains in low-resource markets (up to +7.8% Recall@100), and a 10%-traffic online A/B across six countries delivered +1.77% ad revenue and +2.64% orders.

## Summary

1.  Recommender systems often inherit biases from observed data (like popularity bias), which current loss functions (e.g., BPR) don't adequately address, leading to poor recommendations for less popular items.
2.  To solve this, the authors introduce Cross Pairwise Ranking (CPR), a novel loss function that uses multiple observed interactions per sample to train the model, effectively removing the influence of data biases without needing to know the exposure mechanism.
3.  Theoretical analysis confirms CPR debiases the model, and experimental results show it outperforms existing debiasing methods in both recommendation quality/generalization and training efficiency.

## Technical note

Cross Pairwise Ranking (CPR) is a learning paradigm for unbiased recommendation from implicit feedback that removes exposure-driven biases (notably popularity bias) without estimating propensity scores. Assuming the propensity factorizes into user, item, and relevance components, CPR samples multiple observed interactions at once and forms the loss from a combination of their predictions so that the user/item propensity terms mathematically cancel, guaranteeing per-instance unbiased learning. Unlike inverse propensity scoring, it avoids high variance and the need to set propensities, and a dynamic hard-negative sampling scheme speeds convergence. Experiments on two benchmarks with multiple backbone models show CPR outperforms state-of-the-art debiasing methods in both generalization and training efficiency.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of real-time personalization recommendations. It proposes using simpler transformer models (those with a single self-attention layer) that effectively capture complex user preferences despite their reduced complexity compared to standard Transformers. The authors then developed an efficient algorithm specifically for optimizing recommendations based on these simple transformers, achieving near-optimal performance in sub-linear time and demonstrating its effectiveness on real-world datasets like Spotify's and Trivago's.

## Technical note

The paper studies transformer-based real-time personalization as a cardinality-constrained set/sequence optimization problem, proving it is NP-hard for general transformer objectives. It restricts attention to 'simple transformers' (a single self-attention layer) and shows they can represent variety effects, complementarity/substitution, and the Halo-MNL choice model, none expressible by pure embedding models. Exploiting the low non-negative rank of the softmax matrix, they give a two-phase retrieve-and-rank algorithm achieving (1-eps)-approximation in sub-linear amortized time. Experiments on Spotify playlists and Trivago sessions show simple transformers are 14.1% more accurate than non-attention models and only 2.5% below deeper transformers, beating beam search and kNN.

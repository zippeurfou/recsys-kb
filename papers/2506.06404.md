## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

The application scope of Large Language Models (LLMs) continues to expand, leading to increasing interest in personalized LLMs that align with human values. However, aligning these models with individual values raises significant safety concerns, as certain values may correlate with harmful information. In this paper, we identify specific safety risks associated with value-aligned LLMs and investigate the psychological principles behind these challenges. Our findings reveal two key insights. (1)...

## Technical note

This paper studies safety risks of value-aligned (personalized) LLMs, training a Llama-2 model on 154 Schwartz-value distributions and evaluating its tendency toward harmful behavior. It finds value-aligned LLMs are more prone to harmful outputs than non-fine-tuned or other fine-tuned models, and that harm arises because the models genuinely generate text following the aligned values (e.g., power), with significant value-safety correlations supported by psychological hypotheses. It proposes in-context alignment methods to improve safety; recsys relevance is only through personalization (best-effort, not a core recsys paper).

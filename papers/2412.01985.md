## Summary

This paper addresses the difficulty of implementing advanced feature interaction techniques in large-scale recommendation systems (like Pinterest's Homefeed) due to critical industry constraints such as model latency, GPU memory limits, and ensuring reproducibility. It details the specific challenges encountered at Pinterest when trying to improve their ranking model through better feature interactions and the strategies used to overcome these limitations while balancing performance gains against practical needs. The authors provide learning experiments that offer guidance for selecting effective feature interaction architectures under similar real-world constraints.

## Technical note

Shares practical lessons on improving feature interactions in Pinterest's Homefeed multi-task ranking model under industrial constraints (latency, GPU memory, reproducibility) that academic Criteo benchmarks ignore. The baseline uses four stacked full-rank DCNv2 layers plus a shared MLP over dense, sparse, and transformer-pooled user-sequence embeddings. The paper runs learning experiments to guide architecture and hyperparameter selection when trading off accuracy against memory/latency budgets. It cautions that higher-accuracy academic architectures (MaskNet, FinalMLP, GDCN, DHEN) often add prohibitive latency or memory in production.

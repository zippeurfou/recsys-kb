## Summary

The authors argue that the fixed, hand-crafted rotation structure in Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) for Transformers is an underutilized dimension for expressivity in sequential modeling. They propose SIREN-RoPE, a learnable encoding that replaces fixed rotations with a Sinusoidal Representation Network (SIREN) incorporating diverse signals like time and metadata. This approach demonstrates consistent performance improvements on a large-scale recommender system task with minimal overhead, suggesting the rotation space could be a fundamental enhancement for attention mechanisms.

## Technical note

SIREN-RoPE reframes the RoPE rotation manifold as a learnable, signal-conditioned expressive dimension of attention rather than a fixed positional scaffold. A dual-branch SIREN-DNN network maps continuous timestamps, cyclical temporal patterns, and categorical metadata into per-dimension rotation angles, capturing both periodic (diurnal/weekly) and aperiodic (recency-decay) structure directly in attention geometry. Per-dimension frequency scales and an ordinal-gate scalar lambda are jointly learned, replacing hand-crafted inverse-frequency constants while preserving RoPE's translational equivariance and recency monotonicity. Evaluated on a production-scale social news-feed dataset with a generative recommender as ranker, it consistently improves calibration (NE) and ranking (AUC) across three engagement tasks with ~0.2% extra parameters. Ablations show the rotation dimension carries signal complementary to and independent of the token embedding space.

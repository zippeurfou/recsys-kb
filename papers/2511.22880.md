## Summary

1.  Large Language Models (LLMs) using the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) method are increasingly common, but existing distributed serving systems struggle with efficiently handling many different "adapters" (LoRA models) simultaneously on shared base models due to variations in their complexity.
2.  The paper introduces LoRAServe, a system designed to overcome this challenge by dynamically placing and routing these diverse adapters across multiple GPUs, accounting for their varying computational demands (rank size).
3.  Evaluations show that LoRAServe significantly boosts performance, achieving up to twice the throughput and nine times lower first response time, while using up to half the number of GPUs needed to meet service level objectives compared to current state-of-the-art systems.

## Technical note

LoRAServe is a cluster orchestrator for serving hundreds of heterogeneous LoRA adapters over a shared base LLM, targeting the performance skew caused by co-batching adapters of different ranks. It analyzes production traces to characterize rank/traffic heterogeneity (heavy-tailed demand, skewed popularity) and shows co-serving low- and high-rank adapters inflates P95 TTFT (e.g., +84%). It performs rank-aware, workload-adaptive dynamic adapter placement to minimize on-server rank interference plus indexing schemes and GPUDirect RDMA over InfiniBand to fetch non-local adapters on demand. Against vLLM/S-LoRA it reports up to 2x higher throughput, up to 9x lower TTFT, and up to 50% fewer GPUs under SLO constraints.

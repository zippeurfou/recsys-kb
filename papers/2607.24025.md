## Summary

1.  Standard Transformer models perform poorly on recommendation tasks due to issues like embedding and attention collapse, even when adding more layers, limiting their effectiveness despite their complexity.
2.  This underperformance is caused by spectral collapse in recommendation data (high heterogeneity and long-tail), leading to a vicious cycle during training that restricts scaling potential with increased depth.
3.  To solve this, the paper introduces SpecFormer, a Spectral-Aware Transformer featuring learnable singular value smoothing, spectrum-softened attention, and spectral residual position encoding via Taylor expansion, which successfully mitigates these collapses and demonstrates superior performance, including strong scaling capabilities in real-world deployment.

## Technical note

Standard self-attention underperforms in CTR feature-interaction models and collapses at shallow depth because heterogeneous, long-tail recommendation embeddings suffer spectral collapse (a few singular values dominate), which the paper proves triggers a forward/backward vicious cycle that starves minor spectral directions. SpecFormer is a Spectral-Aware Transformer adding Learnable Spectral Softening (power-law reweighting of singular values), Spectrum-softened Attention (Q/K in the softened space, V kept in the original space), and a Taylor-expansion Spectral Residual Position Encoding. It beats DeepFM, AutoInt, RankMixer, and OneTrans on Criteo/Avazu/an industrial dataset and, unlike standard Transformers, raises attention effective rank and accuracy as layers stack. Deployed on an e-commerce ad platform, it lifted CTR +1.34% and orders +16.72% at only 5ms added latency.

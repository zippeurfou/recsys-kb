## Summary

A quality audit revealed significant data issues in three major multilingual speech datasets (Mozilla Common Voice 17.0, FLEURS, Vox Populi), particularly affecting under-resourced languages at the macro-level. These problems could mislead researchers and hinder progress. The authors recommend incorporating sociolinguistic awareness into dataset creation through proactive language planning to improve data reliability for future multilingual AI development.

## Technical note

This work is a data-quality audit of three widely used multilingual speech datasets (Mozilla Common Voice 17.0, FLEURS, VoxPopuli) rather than a recommender paper. Using quantitative metrics (SNR, VAD, utterance duration, word count) and native-speaker review across ~40 languages, it categorizes micro-level (programmatically fixable) and macro-level issues, finding macro-level problems concentrated in under-resourced, less-institutionalized languages. A case study of Taiwanese Southern Min (nan_tw) shows the subset is nearly unusable, and the authors argue for proactive language planning and sociolinguistic awareness in dataset creation to avoid misleading downstream evaluation.

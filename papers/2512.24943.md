## Summary

1.  Existing e-commerce relevance benchmarks are inadequate for fully evaluating modern language models (LLMs), lacking standardized complexity and evaluation metrics. This gap hinders comprehensive model assessment in the industry.
2.  To address this, researchers introduced RAIR, a new Chinese benchmark dataset incorporating three challenging subsets: a balanced general set, a difficult long-tail set, and a visual salience set requiring multimodal understanding.
3.  RAIR provides a standardized framework and rules for evaluation, and extensive testing on 14 models, including GPT-5, confirmed its difficulty, making it a valuable new benchmark for assessing LLM and Visual Language Model (VLM) performance in e-commerce relevance tasks.

## Technical note

RAIR is a large-scale Chinese benchmark for e-commerce search relevance built on a deterministic rule system that maps satisfaction across 16 attribute dimensions to a four-level (L1-L4) scale, turning subjective judgments into objective protocols. It offers three subsets: a stratified General subset, a Hard subset mining reasoning/knowledge/multi-attribute cases via rule-based retrieval plus LLM filtering, and a Visually Salient subset where visual evidence is strictly necessary. Evaluating 14 SOTA LLMs and VLMs shows the task is hard, with even GPT-5 reaching only 84.5% binary accuracy and 43.3% Macro-F1 on the General subset. The benchmark targets CoT reasoning, multimodal comprehension, and rule-adherence (instruction following) capabilities.

- Hierarchical Taxonomy (Sunburst Chart): The chart on the left illustrates the fine-grained nature of our assessment protocols. Each core dimension branches into specific discriminative rules to handle edge cases. For instance, the Brand dimension is not a monolithic label but is stratified into precise logic paths such as “Competitor Brand,” “Sub-brand,” or “Counterfeit Product.” This hierarchy ensures that models are evaluated on specific reasoning paths rather than vague semantic similarities
- Rule Distribution Analysis : The table on the right highlights the distribution of rule activations across the dataset. Visual and fundamental attributes, particularly Style and Category, constitute the dominant proportion, reflecting their role as the primary decision factors in user search behavior. However, the dataset maintains a substantial representation of complex, logic-heavy dimensions, such as Audience, Bundle/Set, and Color. This distribution ensures that while the benchmark captures common search needs, it retains a high density of intricate constraints required to rigorously test a model’s fine-grained reasoning capabilities
- Semantic Density (%): The proportion of queries containing sufficient multi-dimensional constraints to be categorized as ”Hard” evaluation targets
- Naturalness Rate (%): The proportion of queries representing coherent, human-like search intent, distinguishing natural search behavior from raw product titles or SEO keyword stacking
- Hard Query Filtering: We prioritized Recall to ensure comprehensiveness. The relatively high recall (82.1%) ensures that we capture a wide spectrum of potential boundary cases, minimizing the risk of missing valuable hard samples. Although precision is lower (71.4%), false positives are easily removed during human verification
- Visual Necessity Filtering: We prioritized Precision to enhance efficiency. Given the massive scale of e-commerce logs, our goal was to rapidly extract a high-quality subset of visual-dependent samples. The higher precision (79.6%) allows annotators to focus on valid candidates, significantly reducing wasted effort on non-visual samples
- Rule Extraction: This stage required a balance of both metrics to ensure accurate rule grounding, achieving $>$75% in both precision and recall (arXiv:2512.24943)
- Group-based Drafting: The experts were divided into small working groups, with four members per group. Each group was assigned specific attributes (e.g., Brand, Category) to draft the initial mapping rules from attribute satisfaction to relevance levels (L1–L4)
- Intra-group Consensus: Within each group, members debated boundary cases until a unanimous consensus was reached on the drafted rules
- Adversarial Cross-Validation: Once a group finalized their draft, the rules were exchanged with another group for blind review. The reviewing group acted as “adversaries,” specifically looking for ambiguities, edge cases, or logical loopholes in the definitions
- Three-Round Iteration: This cycle of drafting, consensus-building, and cross-examination was repeated for three rounds. The rules were finalized only after stabilizing across all teams, ensuring that the final protocol is both robust and reproducible
- Relevance Assessment (Figure 9): This prompt instructs models to adhere to our 16-dimension intent taxonomy with a Chain-of-Thought mechanism, requiring explicit reasoning about attribute mismatches before assigning L1–L4 labels. We also instruct models to treat high-CTR items as auxiliary context to mitigate popularity bias
- Hard Query Mining (Figure 11): This prompt categorizes queries by cognitive complexity, guiding models to distinguish between Knowledge-Dependent intents (e.g., Domain Jargon) and Reasoning-Dependent needs (e.g., Solution Seeking) to filter trivial samples
- Visual Necessity Assessment (Figure 12): This prompt performs a counterfactual check: determining whether visual information provides decisive value over text alone, ensuring evaluation targets cases where multimodal integration is strictly necessary
- Domain Jargon (DJ): In the first case, the user queries for a “Capybara plush toy.” The relevant item is titled “Official Water Pig…” (a common nickname for Capybara in specific cultural contexts). A model lacking this specific external knowledge graph would fail to link the nickname “Water Pig” to the scientific name “Capybara,” erroneously predicting a mismatch
- Entity Relationship (ER): In the second case, the user explicitly seeks an “Estée Lauder Alternative” (a substitute). The retrieved item is the original Estée Lauder serum. While semantically highly similar, the relational intent is diametrically opposed (Substitute vs. Target). The model must understand the logical relationship implied by “Alternative” to correctly identify this as a mismatch (L2), rather than being misled by keyword overlap
- Solution Seeking (SS): The query “Birthday Gift for Girlfriend” contains no concrete product keywords. The model must infer a usage scenario-to-product mapping, recognizing that a “Jade Bracelet” functions as a valid gift option (L4), despite the lack of textual overlap
- Question Answering (QA): For the query “What to do about poor bedroom soundproofing,” the candidate item is a “Cervical Pillow.” While both relate to the broad topic of sleep quality, the pillow does not functionally address the user’s specific question about noise isolation. The model must distinguish between a topical association and a functional solution to correctly label this as Irrelevant (L1)
- Negative Constraints (NC): The query “Silicone-Free Shampoo” imposes a logical negation. The model must perform a strict exclusion check, verifying that the item attributes explicitly satisfy the “non-existence” of silicone, preventing hallucinated matches
- Overlap in Hard Subset (Textual): The Tri-Model Intent Verification (Eq. 2) utilized Qwen3-235B-Instruct alongside Llama3-70B and DeepSeek-R1 to filter candidate queries via majority voting. In our experiments (Table 5), the Qwen3 family (including the 235B variant) is evaluated on this very subset
- Overlap in Visual Subset (Multimodal): The VLM Ensemble (Eq. 3) for the Visually Salient subset employed Qwen2.5-VL-72B as a verifier. Subsequently, this model appears as an evaluation target in Table 4
- Task Orthogonality: There is a fundamental cognitive distinction between the construction tasks and the evaluation task
- Ensemble Dilution: As described in Eq. 2 and Eq. 3, our pipeline relies on consensus mechanisms (e.g., majority voting) involving diverse model architectures (e.g., Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek). A sample is retained only if it aligns with the consensus, effectively ”diluting” the specific idiosyncrasies or hallucinations of any single model (including Qwen)

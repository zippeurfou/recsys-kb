## Summary

This paper introduces "Dialogue to Discovery" (D2D), a conversational product search framework designed to overcome the limitations of small screens during online shopping. The core challenge is that traditional interfaces can lead to user frustration or poor matches when navigating too slowly or making recommendations without clear preference knowledge.

D2D dynamically uses product attributes within conversations to efficiently gather user preferences before suggesting items, preventing premature recommendations and focusing discovery on the desired target. By adaptively asking informative questions and timing suggestions carefully, it improves both the speed of finding the right item and the overall quality of matches during search interactions.

The authors evaluated D2D using simulations and a user study against existing methods, showing significant improvements in accuracy (up to 30% better), reductions in session abandonment rates (as low as 6-16% less drop-offs), and shorter conversation times compared to standard approaches.

## Technical note

D2D is an attribute-aware conversational product search framework that decides at each turn whether to recommend items or ask a clarifying question, using confidence intervals over LLM-estimated attribute-value preferences (AVP) and their uncertainty (APU). Initial retrieval uses Sentence-BERT cosine similarity to restrict processing to a top-200 candidate set; item scores combine query relevance with summed attribute preferences, and recommendation is triggered when the 'top overlapping item' set is small enough. Attribute selection ranks by APU times an ordering-aware cumulative Shannon entropy (ACE) to pick the most discriminative attributes. Evaluation uses three Amazon-Reviews-derived datasets with a novel four-factor utilitarian patience model (recommendation relevance, inattentiveness, complexity, informativeness) simulating session abandonment. D2D improves success rate 22-30%, cuts abandonment ~12%, and reduces output tokens up to 64% versus a Full-LLM baseline.

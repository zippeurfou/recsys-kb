## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Personalized text generation requires a unique ability of large language models (LLMs) to learn from context that they often do not encounter during their standard training. One way to encourage LLMs to better use personalized context for generating outputs that better align with the user's expectations is to instruct them to reason over the user's past preferences, background knowledge, or writing style. ## Technical note

Personalized long-form text generation requires LLMs to reason over a user's implicit preferences, background, and writing style, but human reasoning-path annotations are costly. Google proposes REST-PG (Reasoning-Enhanced Self-Training), which uses Gemma 7B to synthesize reasoning paths from input/output/user-profile, does SFT to teach a Gemma 2B generator to emit reasoning then response in one pass, and applies Expectation-Maximization reinforced self-training (ReST-EM style) that samples m=32 outputs, scores them with an LLM reward model, and retrains on high-reward trajectories over T=3 iterations, using Contriever RAG (k=5) for personalized context. On the LongLaMP benchmark (four tasks) REST-PG improves average performance by 14.5% over SFT and 6.5% over reasoning-free self-training; starting each iteration from the base checkpoint beats continuing from SFT (which reaches only 96% of base-start performance).

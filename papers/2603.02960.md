## Summary

1.  The paper examines Large Language Models (LLMs) acting as epistemic agents, which autonomously shape our knowledge environment by curating information and providing advice, replacing traditional search methods.
2.  It argues that the power of these agents necessitates a fundamental shift in evaluation and governance because poorly aligned ones risk causing unreliable information (epistemic drift) and eroding human critical thinking (cognitive deskilling), making their alignment with human epistemic norms crucial.
3.  To ensure beneficial AI integration, the paper proposes a framework centered on building trust through aligning AI with human epistemic goals and strengthening socio-epistemic infrastructure, requiring technically verifiable knowledge and promoting epistemically virtuous AI behaviors.

## Technical note

This position paper argues that LLMs are becoming epistemic agents that autonomously curate information and shape the shared knowledge environment, replacing search and generating specialized advice. It warns that poorly aligned agents risk epistemic drift (unreliable information) and cognitive deskilling (eroded human critical thinking), especially amid multi-agent informational interdependencies. The authors propose a normative framework for epistemic trust built on three verifiable properties, demonstrable competence, falsifiability, and epistemically virtuous behavior, plus alignment with human epistemic goals. They call for socio-technical infrastructure such as technical provenance systems and 'knowledge sanctuaries' to protect human resilience.

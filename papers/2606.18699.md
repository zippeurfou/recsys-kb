## Summary

This paper introduces **TW-LegalBench**, a new benchmark designed specifically to evaluate large language models (LLMs) on their understanding and application of Taiwanese law, addressing a gap in current legal AI evaluation tools focused mainly on other jurisdictions.

**TW-LegalBench** consists of three parts: over 16,000 multiple-choice questions from professional exams, 117 open-ended essay questions with official rubrics, and more than 14,000 legal judgment prediction instances based on crime categories. It rigorously assessed thirteen LLMs using metrics like accuracy for objective questions, decomposed scoring for essays, and correctness for predicted judgments.

The results demonstrated that while top-performing models achieved near-human levels (exceeding the lawyer passing rate of 11%) by answering factual exam questions, their performance was significantly lower when required to meet judge/prosecutor standards or demonstrate precise legal citation ability within judgment predictions.

## Technical note

TW-LegalBench is a Taiwanese-law LLM benchmark with three task types: 16,493 statute-annotated multiple-choice questions across 18 professional domains and 43 law types (2020-2024), 117 open-ended bar-exam essay questions scored via a decomposed LLM-as-judge framework calibrated to official rubric points, and 14,325 criminal legal-judgment-prediction cases across 107 crime categories with a balanced 535-case test split. Thirteen LLMs are evaluated using accuracy for MCQs, rubric-point-based judging for essays, and sentencing/statute-citation metrics for LJP. Top models exceed the lawyer passing threshold (~11%) but fall short of the judge/prosecutor threshold (1-2%), and while verdict-type and sentence prediction are reasonable, models struggle to cite exact legal articles, indicating reliable legal text generation remains hard even when qualification-exam scores approach human level.

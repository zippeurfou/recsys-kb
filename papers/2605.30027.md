## Summary

1.  Existing multimodal document retrieval systems struggle due to the complexity of diverse document elements (tables, figures, layout) and rely on dense visual embeddings and supervised rerankers that have limitations (semantic loss, poor generalization, and inadequate evaluation).
2.  To overcome these challenges, the authors propose DocRetriever, a plug-and-play framework featuring a layout-aware sparse embedding technique for effective hybrid encoding (without OCR) and a generalizable reranker using reasoning and optimized sampling, along with a new benchmark called MultiDocR.
3.  Experiments show DocRetriever outperforms state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving retrieval accuracy and providing a more comprehensive evaluation suite for multimodal document retrieval.

## Technical note

DocRetriever is a plug-and-play framework for multimodal document retrieval that augments VLM-based dense retrievers with layout-aware sparse embeddings derived directly from the LM-head logit distribution (via lemmatization, stopword filtering, SPLADE-style ReLU/log-saturation, and top-256 selection), fusing dense and sparse scores with weight 0.8 for OCR-free hybrid retrieval that gains ~3% NDCG@10. Its generalizable reranker uses Reinforced In-Context Learning: reasoning-augmented demonstrations are autonomously synthesized with contrastive verification over positive/hard-negative pairs and multi-model consensus (98.3% audited precision), and pertinent demos are retrieved via joint query-document (dual-alignment) similarity, scoring relevance from the VLM's Yes/No token probabilities without fine-tuning. It also introduces MultiDocR, a benchmark spanning 10 domains and 7 query categories with paraphrases and five-level relevance annotations.

## Summary

Generative recommendation (GeneRec) models, while powerful, inherit and often worsen popularity bias due to imbalanced semantic tokenization and training procedures favoring popular items. To address this, the authors propose CRAB, a post-hoc debiasing strategy that rebalances the codebook by splitting over-represented tokens while preserving their semantic structure. Experiments show CRAB effectively reduces popularity bias and improves recommendation performance on real-world datasets.

## Technical note

CRAB is a post-hoc debiasing method for generative recommendation (GeneRec) that attributes popularity bias to over-popular tokens produced by imbalanced residual-quantization codebooks (RQ-KMeans/RQ-VAE) rather than only model-level bias. Stage one splits identified over-popular tokens into M new tokens via a regularized K-means that redistributes child tokens while preserving hierarchical tree structure and balancing token popularity scores. Stage two adds a tree-structure-aware regularizer enforcing embedding consistency among sibling tokens, trained with LoRA adapters on a Qwen2-0.5B backbone (MiniOneRec). Experiments on Office and Industrial datasets show CRAB reduces MGU@10 by ~16.5% and DGU@10 by ~14.8% versus MOR while preserving HR/NDCG, at roughly 1/10 the training cost of reweighting or D2LR baselines.

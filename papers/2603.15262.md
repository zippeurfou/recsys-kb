## Summary

Modern e-commerce search struggles with complex user intents, facing a trade-off between LLM reasoning capabilities (which ignore real-time data) and speed (from deep search agents incompatible with fast response times). To resolve this conflict, the paper introduces EASP (Environment-Aware Search Planning), featuring a "Probe-then-Plan" mechanism. This system uses a lightweight retrieval probe to expose real-time inventory and retrieval state, allowing a Planner to diagnose gaps and generate grounded, executable plans. EASP improves search relevance and business metrics (UCVR, GMV) through offline training and online deployment, successfully deployed at JD.com.

## Technical note

EASP reformulates industrial e-commerce query planning as a grounded single-step reasoning process to resolve a 'blindness-latency' dilemma between blind query rewriters and slow ReAct-style agents. Its Probe-then-Plan mechanism first runs a lightweight Retrieval Probe (core retrieval only, ~75% lower latency) to expose a retrieval snapshot, letting a Planner diagnose failure states (recall failure, precision failure, entity drift, attribute misalignment) and emit a grounded plan. Training uses SFT on execution-validated teacher (DeepSeek-R1) plans then GRPO alignment with a conversion reward gated by a hard relevance threshold; a Qwen3-0.6B complexity router sends ~80% simple queries down a fast path. On JD.com A/B tests EASP lifted UCVR +0.89% and GMV +0.57% overall (+4.10%/+2.59% on triggered complex traffic) with p75 latency 20ms and p99 under 700ms, and is deployed in JD's AI-Search.

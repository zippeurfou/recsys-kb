## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of news recommendation, where traditional pointwise methods fail to capture crucial item comparisons while pairwise/listwise methods are computationally expensive or perform poorly. The authors propose a novel framework integrating pointwise relevance prediction with pairwise comparisons using pretrained language models, aiming for efficiency and improved performance. They provide theoretical guarantees for their approach and demonstrate its effectiveness through extensive experiments on standard datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art methods.

## Technical note

GLIMPSE is a PLM-based news recommendation framework that combines pointwise relevance prediction and pairwise preference comparison in a scalable way. A generative model is multi-task fine-tuned to answer both a relevance question (is this item suitable?) and a preference question (which of two items is better?), casting both as text generation with pre-defined target words and converting pairwise token logits to probabilities via the Bradley-Terry model. At inference it initializes the ranking with pointwise scores then applies right-to-left (RTL) adjacent-swap passes using pairwise comparisons, needing only n-1 comparisons per pass (O(K) vs O(K^2)). A Markov-chain analysis derives testable conditions under which RTL aggregation provably improves a given ranking metric, and experiments outperform state-of-the-art on the MIND and Adressa datasets.

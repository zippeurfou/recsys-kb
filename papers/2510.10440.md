## Summary

1.  The paper systematically investigates if weighting observed interactions (like clicks) improves recommender system performance using matrix factorization on implicit feedback data.
2.  Surprisingly, it found that training with unweighted data performs equally well or even better than weighted data, especially when using large models.
3.  However, weighting is still beneficial for smaller capacity models and under certain regularization schemes, and the paper provides computationally efficient algorithms to train these specific weighted matrix factorization approaches.

## Technical note

This paper systematically studies interaction weighting in matrix factorization for implicit-feedback top-N recommendation, challenging the conventional wisdom that upweighting observed positives always helps. Across weighting schemes and MF variants (WMF, asymmetric/autoencoder-style AWMF, full-rank EASE-like models) it finds that large unweighted linear models often match or beat weighted counterparts on nDCG/Recall, while weighting mainly helps low-capacity models under specific regularization. Using Kronecker-product vectorization the authors derive closed-form solutions (paired with conjugate gradient) for weighted objectives previously thought intractable, resolving an open question. The work maps the interplay of weighting, regularization, and model capacity, showing weighting benefits are more circumstantial than believed.

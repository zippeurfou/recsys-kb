## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Recent breakthroughs in large language models (LLMs) have fundamentally shifted recommender systems from discriminative to generative paradigms, where user behavior modeling is achieved by generating target items conditioned on historical interactions. Yet current generative recommenders still suffer from two core limitations: the lack of high-quality negative modeling and the reliance on implicit rewards. ## Technical note

ReRe (Reinforced Preference Optimization for Recommendation) (Taobao/Alibaba) applies reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to LLM-based generative recommenders, fixing insufficient negative modeling and reliance on implicit rewards. It uses constrained beam search to improve sampling efficiency and diversify on-policy hard negatives (avoiding invalid/repetitive items in the narrow item space), and augments rule-based accuracy rewards with auxiliary ranking rewards for finer-grained ranking supervision. On three real-world datasets ReRe consistently outperforms traditional and LLM-based recommenders in ranking, generalizing across backbone families and scales, and the paper systematically studies the RLVR design space (generation, sampling, reward, optimizer).

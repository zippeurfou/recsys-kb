## Summary

This paper introduces a framework for multi-task models to learn cross-task relationships by approximating the joint distribution of task labels using focused pairwise interactions. The key advantage is improved performance via transfer learning and better information extraction, achieved without the complex modeling of the entire joint distribution. The framework was successfully tested and deployed within YouTube's recommendation systems, demonstrating significant gains in accuracy and user satisfaction metrics across different applications.

## Technical note

Standard multi-task rankers output only per-task marginals and throw away the joint structure of the labels, so YouTube adds targeted pairwise auxiliary heads that approximate the joint label distribution with at most O(N^2) heads instead of O(2^N). For two unconditional tasks it adds a cross-label head P(X and Y), and for a task trained conditionally on another (save given click, via label masking) it adds the unconditional head P(Y), so the model outputs E[X], E[Y], and E[XY] and with them the pairwise covariance and full pairwise joint; heads attach without stop-gradient to the shared body (preferred) or to the top hidden layers of the two parent heads. A two-phase live protocol first trains the heads without serving them to test transfer learning, then feeds their predictions as features to the downstream RL learned policy function to test environment modeling. On Notifications, 10 open/dismiss by visit cross-label heads raised direct-head AUC 3% to 8% and gave +0.17% DAU, -1.05% opt-outs, -7.21% sends, and +7.67% CTR; on Homepage, unconditioned and cross heads lifted valued watch time 0.05% and cut low-quality impressions 0.16% to 0.24%, and passing 11 heads to the RL ranker cut them another 0.64%; on Watch Next, 4 unconditioned heads gave 0.2% to 2.6% AUC gains and +0.19% Watch Next valued watch time. Sending cross-task gradients straight into the shared base beat routing them through variant towers by about 0.6% to 4% AUC on sparse, imbalanced tasks, and raising loss weights or duplicating heads gave no comparable gain because a cross head concentrates gradient on examples where both tasks are positive.

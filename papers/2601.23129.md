## Summary

1.  Existing methods for measuring the usefulness of grounding documents in RAG systems lack specificity or rely on costly human annotations, often ignoring model capabilities.
2.  This paper introduces GroGU, a novel reference-free and model-specific metric that quantifies document utility by analyzing the downstream LLM's confidence (entropy) in its generated answers.
3.  Applied to train a query rewriter for RAG using Direct Preference Optimization, GroGU significantly improved system performance, achieving up to 18.2 point gains in Mean Reciprocal Rank and 9.4 point gains in answer accuracy.

## Technical note

GroGU is a reference-free, model-specific metric for the utility of grounding documents in RAG, defined via the downstream LLM's generation confidence using a modified token-entropy calculation, requiring no gold-answer annotations. The authors argue retriever relevance scores are inadequate because they are LLM-agnostic (the same document has different utility for different models, and irrelevant context can help), whereas GroGU captures these nuances while still tracking relevance. Using GroGU to mine preference pairs for Direct Preference Optimization of a RAG query-rewriter yields up to 18.2-point MRR and 9.4-point answer-accuracy gains across sparse and dense retrievers.

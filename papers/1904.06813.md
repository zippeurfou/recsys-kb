## Summary

Traditional recommender systems often rank items individually without considering interactions between items or user-specific preferences, leading to sub-optimal results. This paper proposes a personalized re-ranking model that uses a transformer architecture with self-attention to consider the entire item list and user-specific preferences, improving upon existing ranking features. Experimental results on offline benchmarks and online systems show that this re-ranking model significantly improves recommendation performance.

## Technical note

PRM is a personalized re-ranking model that refines the initial list from any base ranker by modeling mutual influence between items with a Transformer self-attention encoder, replacing RNN-based re-rankers (DLCM, GlobalRerank) whose signal decays with distance and that cannot parallelize. It injects a pretrained per-user 'personalized vector' (hidden layer of a separately trained CTR model) plus learnable position embeddings to learn user-specific item-pair interactions. On Yahoo LETOR and a released e-commerce dataset it beats DLCM (e.g., +1.7% MAP over DLCM, larger gains on harder low-CTR data), and online A/B tests show higher CTR/PV and a +6.29% GMV lift from the personalization module. Attention visualizations confirm the model captures category- and price-based item relationships and position bias.

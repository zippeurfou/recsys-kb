## Summary

This paper introduces USD (User-Intent-Driven Sampling), a framework designed to improve large-scale homepage recommendations by addressing exposure bias issues where non-clicks are often misinterpreted. The core of USD consists of two main parts: first, it filters out invalid negative samples based on user intent; second, it implements dual debiasing to correct both exposure and click biases simultaneously. Online experiments on the Taobao homepage demonstrated that USD significantly boosts user engagement metrics by large percentages across different marketing sections.

## Technical note

USD targets large-scale homepage recommendation where non-clicks are pseudo-negatives (inattention) and some clicks are pseudo-positives (users clicking only to reach a marketing portal). It combines a user-intent-driven negative sampler that keeps only users who visited the marketing portal as confident samples, with a Transformer-based User Intent Extraction Module that predicts portal- and block-click intent probabilities. A causal dual-debiasing module then reweights the CTR loss via inverse propensity scoring using those intent estimates to correct exposure and click bias jointly. On Taobao's 1.45B-sample dataset it beats ESMM/DCMT/ESCM2 baselines on GAUC and delivers +35.4% and +14.5% UCTR in online A/B tests on two marketing blocks.

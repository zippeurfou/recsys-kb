## Summary

The paper argues that certain recommendation problems, like playlist continuation, can effectively be treated as ad-hoc retrieval tasks. To demonstrate this, the authors built a solution for the Spotify challenge using standard ad-hoc retrieval models, treating playlist titles as queries and employing feedback mechanisms. Their approach combined retrieval methods with collaborative filtering and nearest neighbor search, ultimately ranking highly (7th place) in the competition, showing the effectiveness of retrieval techniques for music recommendation.

## Technical note

This work argues recommendation problems like Spotify's automatic playlist continuation can be cast as ad-hoc retrieval and solves the RecSys 2018 Challenge with standard IR tools. It builds pseudo-documents (playlists as documents with tracks as terms) and uses pseudo-relevance feedback as a collaborative-filtering analog to expand tracks, treats playlist titles as queries against track meta-descriptions for content-based candidates, and adds a nearest-neighbor search over word2vec-learned track embeddings. Candidates from all three sources are fused and re-ranked with a LambdaMART learning-to-rank model. The approach finished 7th of 112 teams (main track) and 5th of 31 (creative track), showing retrieval methods are effective for music recommendation.

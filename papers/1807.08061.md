## Summary

This paper argues that certain recommendation problems, like playlist continuation, can effectively be treated as ad-hoc retrieval tasks. The authors demonstrate this by entering the RecSys 2018 Spotify challenge, using standard ad-hoc retrieval models, pseudo-relevance feedback, and content-based retrieval techniques derived from the playlist title. Their solution, supplemented by track embeddings and a learning-to-rank model, achieved competitive results, ranking 7th overall and 5th for creative tracks.

## Technical note

Argues that recommendation can be cast as ad-hoc retrieval and demonstrates this on the RecSys 2018 Spotify playlist-continuation challenge using standard IR tools. Three candidate generators are combined: pseudo-relevance-feedback query expansion (RM1, treating tracks as terms and playlists as documents) acting like collaborative filtering; BM25 over pseudo-documents of track meta-descriptions and parent-playlist titles for content-based matching; and nearest-neighbor search over track embeddings learned by word2vec CBOW plus metapath2vec heterogeneous-graph walks. A LambdaMART learning-to-rank model re-ranks the union of candidates. PRF query expansion was the strongest single source, and the combined system finished 7th of 112 (main) and 5th of 31 (creative), showing competitive recommendation from off-the-shelf retrieval methods.

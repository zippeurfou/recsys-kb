## Summary

This paper presents a method to fine-tune a large language model (LLM) recommender to generate personalized explanations for its video suggestions. The key requirements for these explanations were faithfulness to the recommended shows and ensuring they were strictly non-harmful. Experiments on a real-world dataset showed that the fine-tuned model significantly increased the success rate of generating explanations meeting both criteria, outperforming a standard recommender and the non-finetuned frontier model, while maintaining its original recommendation quality. This demonstrates that LLM recommenders can be adapted to handle complex tasks like explanation generation without sacrificing their core recommendation abilities.

## Technical note

Netflix fine-tunes its in-house recommender LLM to additionally generate personalized reference-style explanations for its own recommendations (of the form 'Watch this if you enjoyed the shared element in this show you already watched'), avoiding the cost and latency of a frontier-model call in the member-facing pipeline. Two quality requirements govern the task: faithfulness, meaning the highlighted common element genuinely holds for both titles, and harmlessness split into bias and offensiveness, where the production bar is explicitly stricter than frontier RLHF safety -- their example is that suggesting only 'smart' people would enjoy a political show passes a frontier safety judge but fails here. Reward modeling uses two LoRA judges on the same open-source 8B backbone, deliberately not the generator's own backbone, SFT on human-annotated labels plus annotator rationales then GRPO on labels only, with 80/10/10 splits held near 70% PASS to discourage reward hacking; the tuned harmlessness judge hits 0.939 catch-a-FAIL versus 0.778 for a frontier judge and 0.978 for the untuned 8B base (which is useless at 0.625 accuracy because it flags any vivid wording), while the faithfulness judge needs FAIL-any@8 test-time scaling to move from 0.822 greedy to 0.911 recall. The generator is warmed up by SFT on 952 frontier-generated explanations that passed all three human checks (857/95 split, LoRA deliberately small at r=16 alpha=32 to preserve RL output diversity), then trained with constrained GRPO: maximize E[R_fact] subject to E[R_c] >= tau_c, solved primal-dual with penalized reward R_fact - sum_c lambda_c (1 - R_c) and projected ascent lambda_c <- max(0, lambda_c + eta(tau_c - E[R_c])), thresholds set just below what the warm-up already achieved. The Constrained-joint variant, which constrains the per-sample conjunction E[R_bias * R_off] >= 0.98 rather than batch-level rates, wins: all-three-criteria PASS climbs from 0.649 to 0.956 under their own judges and 0.677 to 0.931 under an independent frontier judge, beating scalarized 0.4/0.3/0.3 weighting (0.910), Constrained-bias (0.932) and Constrained-harmful (0.948), while a prompted frontier generator scores only 0.652, essentially matching the untuned baseline. No regression on the day job: internal retrieval nMRR moves +0.0006 to +0.0009 and MMLU, HellaSwag, CommonsenseQA, MUSR and ARC are flat or marginally up across all recipes. Caveats: evaluation is judge-based offline with no member-facing A/B or engagement lift, only one explanation style is covered, no member data was used (prompts are title pairs replayed from the production selection pipeline over catalog metadata), and the authors note post-training does not add real show knowledge -- continued pretraining would be the deeper fix.

## Summary

This paper introduces Bifocal Diffusion Language Models as a solution to the conflict between bidirectional context access, which improves generation quality but sacrifices inference efficiency via KV caching, and unidirectional causal attention, which is efficient but provides weaker right-side context. The core idea involves asymmetric bidirectional context using a novel method called R2LM (Right-to-Left Mamba), combining standard causal attention with an auxiliary reverse SSM block to supply compressed future information without breaking cacheability. Experiments show that this approach significantly boosts inference throughput in batch settings while improving generation quality compared to both purely autoregressive and fully bidirectional diffusion baselines.

## Technical note

Bifocal dLLMs resolve the dLLM dilemma that bidirectional attention gives strong quality but breaks prefix KV caching, while causal attention caches but loses right-side context. The principle of 'asymmetric bidirectional context' routes left context through cacheable causal attention and right context through a non-attention pathway. The instantiation, R2LM, attaches a lightweight reverse-direction Mamba SSM sidecar to a pretrained autoregressive backbone via forward hooks every k-th layer with zero-initialized contribution (bit-identical at init), supplying position-aware compressed right context across the full sequence without breaking cacheability. On Qwen3-1.7B continued-pretrained with 60B tokens under the absorbing-state MDLM objective, R2LM achieves 2.4x-12.9x higher throughput than bidirectional dLLMs and beats the causal baseline on most benchmarks; a frozen-backbone plug-in mode recovers 59% of the joint gain with 5B extra tokens.

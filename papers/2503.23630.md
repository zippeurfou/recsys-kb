## Summary

This paper addresses the issue of popularity bias in recommendation systems, where highly popular items dominate recommendations, potentially neglecting less known ones. The authors introduce an exposure-aware retrieval method that models the probability of an item being exposed during the ranking stage, allowing real-time adjustments to balance engagement and fairness. They validate this approach through online experiments, showing it significantly increases the diversity of recommended items while reducing the dominance of popular content, without harming user engagement levels.

## Technical note

Meta introduces an exposure-aware retrieval scoring method that mitigates popularity bias at the retrieval stage rather than only in ranking or training. It explicitly models P(exposure|item) and reframes candidate scoring as P(positive label | item, exposed), decoupling engagement likelihood from exposure bias. An auxiliary exposure-prediction task (trained with random negatives) is added to the retrieval loss, and at inference the score subtracts a weighted exposure logit (logit-space subtraction = probability-space division) with tunable gamma. Offline, gamma=0.65 gives the best positive/negative recall trade-off; online A/B tests on a production video recommender show a 25% increase in uniquely retrieved items and a 40% reduction in over-popular content while maintaining engagement, on GPU-accelerated infrastructure.

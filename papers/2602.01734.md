## Summary

Large language model training often suffers from unstable gradients and wasted computation due to sudden gradient explosions. The authors identified two key precursors to training collapse: a declining stable rank in weight matrices and increasing alignment between adjacent layer Jacobians. They propose MSign, an optimizer that periodically restores stable rank using matrix sign operations, preventing these instabilities with minimal computational overhead.

## Technical note

This paper studies sudden gradient-explosion failures in LLM pretraining and identifies two precursors: sharp decline in weight-matrix stable rank and rising alignment between adjacent-layer Jacobians. It proves these jointly cause total Jacobian norm to grow as (aM)^L in depth, triggering exponential gradient growth when aM>1. The proposed MSign optimizer periodically applies the matrix sign operation (UV^T from the SVD) to projection weights to equalize singular values and restore stable rank, then rescales to the original Frobenius norm. Across models from 5M to 3B parameters it prevents failures that break baselines, at under 7% compute overhead, with attention output projections being the critical target.

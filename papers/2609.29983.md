## Summary

This paper introduces Semantic IDs (SIDs) to represent catalog items, allowing generative recommenders to predict the next item autoregressively. Standard training using sparse, final SID rewards creates two problems: lack of learning signals when no rollout hits the target and incorrect credit assignment when multiple reasoning paths lead to the same SID reward. The authors propose "retrieval-grounded query attribution" where a frozen retriever evaluates each intermediate hypothesis; rollouts are rewarded for any top-K retrieved query matching the target, assigning credit to specific reasoning parts rather than just the final SID.

## Technical note

Reasoning-enhanced Semantic-ID recommenders trained with GRPO on an exact-match SID reward get almost no signal in large catalogs: if every rollout misses, the group advantage is zero, and rollouts with equal SID reward get identical credit regardless of their reasoning. Microsoft's method (built on SIDReasoner with Qwen3-1.7B and 3-level RQ-VAE SIDs) structures each trace into a history summary and 3-4 future-interest queries ahead of a trie-constrained SID, runs every interest against a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-4B retriever, and rewards a rollout if any query retrieves the target in the top 50; SID, trace-validity and retrieval rewards are group-normalized separately and routed at token level, with retrieval advantage applied only to the interest lines that hit (never the SID span) under a dual-clip PPO objective. On Amazon Video Games, Office and Industrial it improves Recall@10 by 15.5-39.6% and NDCG@10 by 18.5-61.5% over a matched SID+trace RL control (Games NDCG@10 0.0493 to 0.0796) and beats SIDReasoner, R2ec, TIGER, HSTU, LETTER and SASRec on all 12 metrics. SID rewards were constant in 69-81% of rollout groups and the retrieval channel reactivated up to ~38% of them; hit-query routing beat broadcast, interest-block routing and SID-prefix rewards, and an oracle shows interest-conditioned candidate-trie decoding could raise recall and ranking further.

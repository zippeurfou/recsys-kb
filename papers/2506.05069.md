## Summary

Using Interaction-of-Thought Reasoning to Enhance LLM Recommendation" in three sentences:

The authors recognize that standard recommendation approaches using Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with their natural reasoning strengths. They propose R2Rec, a novel framework that translates sequential user-item interactions into structured 'interaction-of-thoughts' prompts for the LLM, guiding it to perform stepwise reasoning based on implicit patterns in data history rather than direct item recommendation. Experiments demonstrate that R2Rec significantly improves recommendation accuracy (over 10% HitRatio@1) compared to classical and standard LLM-based baselines by teaching the model reasoning through interaction chains and refining its output with reinforcement learning using reward signals.

## Technical note

Applying LLM reasoning (test-time scaling / RL) to recommendation is ineffective because user feedback is implicit and there is no supervision for reasoning over interactions. R2Rec samples closed interaction chains (u0->i0->u1->i1->u0) from the user-item graph and converts them into structured 'interaction-of-thoughts' via a progressive, masked prompting strategy, then internalizes reasoning through a two-stage pipeline: SFT warm-start on annotated traces followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a reasoning-step reward plus a ranking-correctness reward. Using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B with LoRA on MovieLens, Amazon-Book, and Amazon-Electronics, R2Rec beats classical (SASRec, LightGCN) and LLM-based (LLaRA, o4-mini) baselines by an average 10.48% HitRatio@1. Ablations show the interaction chain is the essential substrate (removing it drops HitRatio@1 on Electronics from 0.192 to 0.072) and SFT is critical for stable RL, and the learned reasoning transfers across domains.

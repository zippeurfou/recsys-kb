## Summary

Generative Recommendation systems using LLMs often use fixed-length term IDs and decoding tries that may poorly support beam search. This paper proposes BONSAI, an optimized framework designed to overcome these limitations by creating variable-length ID representations tailored to item metadata richness and controlling branching factors at shallow trie levels. Experiments show BONSAI improves performance significantly (up to 21.6% relative improvement) compared to state-of-the-art methods while demonstrating the crucial benefits of its proposed properties for autoregressive recommendation generation.

## Technical note

BONSAI co-designs textual (term) IDs and their decoding trie for LLM-based generative recommendation, arguing that constrained beam-search success depends on the trie structure, not just term semantics. Motivated by two properties, adaptive/variable ID length and constrained branching factor (especially at shallow levels), it filters informative terms from item metadata with Qwen3-32B and builds the trie by recursive greedy minimum set cover, then trains a Qwen3-1.7B backbone with SFT plus GRPO reinforcement learning that aligns the multiple valid root-to-leaf ID paths per item, backed by theoretical guarantees (Bellman-optimal trie, greedy set-cover approximation, variable depth beats fixed depth). On Amazon Beauty/Sports/Toys it achieves up to 21.6% relative gains over the strongest baseline GRLM (beating TIGER, HSTU, MiniOneRec, OneRec-Think, IDGenRec), and its minimum-cover reorganization transfers to other term-ID methods for consistent lifts.

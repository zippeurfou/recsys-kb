## Summary

This paper addresses the lack of global data on how cultures view Generative AI (GenAI). Through a large-scale survey across continents, it gathered diverse perspectives on the meaning of culture and how GenAI should represent its artifacts, concepts, and values. The study concludes by recommending participatory development, focusing on specific cultural dimensions like religion and tradition, and using a framework to identify and respect cultural "redlines" in GenAI applications.

## Technical note

The paper reports a large-scale multi-country survey (13 nations, n=5,629) gathering empirical evidence on global attitudes toward whether and how generative AI should represent cultures. It distills community-grounded working definitions of culture and, guided by UNESCO cultural-heritage frameworks, identifies which cultural categories populations find most important and sensitive, arguing current GenAI mirrors extractive Global-North dynamics that stereotype or erase non-Western cultures. It surfaces an inverse problem where developers lack anthropological training and firms lack standardized cultural-competency metrics. It concludes with recommendations for participatory development, prioritizing cultural dimensions beyond geography (e.g., religion and tradition), and a sensitivity framework for respecting cultural redlines.

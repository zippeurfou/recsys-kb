## Summary

This tutorial explores the evolution of spoken conversational agents towards voice-native LLMs, moving from cascaded ASR/NLU systems to end-to-end models. It addresses key challenges like adapting text LLMs to audio, achieving cross-modal alignment, and effective joint speech-text training. The paper reviews datasets, robustness (including accents), evaluation metrics, and compares system designs, while providing practical recipes and outlining open problems in privacy, safety, and evaluation for developers.

## Technical note

This is a tutorial paper tracing the evolution of spoken conversational agents from cascaded ASR/NLU/dialogue pipelines toward voice-native, end-to-end speech-language LLMs. It reviews probabilistic language-modeling history, LLM adaptation theory (population risk, transferability estimation), joint speech-text pre-training and post-alignment, and generative autoregressive models with joint speech-text tokenization for cross-modal understanding. It covers datasets, robustness to accents/dialects and fairness, evaluation metrics, and both open-domain and task-oriented dialogue systems. It concludes with open problems in privacy, safety, and evaluation, plus future directions like conversational-data foundation LLMs, end-to-end dialogue training, and situated/grounded dialogue.

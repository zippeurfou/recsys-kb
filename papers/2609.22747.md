## Summary

1.  Netflix researchers identified that raw engagement metrics (like views) in large recommender systems are ambiguous and conflate multiple factors, making performance understanding difficult for creators and developers.
2.  They proposed a novel counterfactual observability framework, viewing recommender evaluation as estimating what would have happened without specific content recommendations or model decisions.
3.  This framework provides a unified measurement approach, incorporating principles of bias reduction, relativity, and incrementality, and was successfully deployed across multiple Netflix recommendation stages to improve understanding and debugging.

## Technical note

Raw engagement signals conflate content quality, model behavior, presentation bias and audience reach, so Netflix frames recommender observability as counterfactual measurement: estimating what the recommender would have done, and what engagement would have followed, without a given item or model decision. The framework states three stakeholder-centered principles for content creators and model developers and provides bias-reduction, relativity and incrementality measurements (leave-one-out incrementality, a self-normalized Hajek irreplaceability estimator with propensities clipped to [1e-4, 0.9999]) for single-stage and cascading recommenders. Simulations show leave-one-out lift tracks simulated true CTR lift, and across 11 row-removal A/B tests on Netflix homepage rows the offline row-incrementality measurement reaches R^2 = 0.8212 against A/B ground truth, letting curators evaluate rows offline. A production monitoring case surfaced a label-misattribution bug through a sudden incrementality dip.

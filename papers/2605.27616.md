## Summary

This paper investigates how model architecture (like CNNs vs. attention-based Swin Transformers), model scale, and FP4 quantization-aware training (QAT) recipes interact to affect recall in brain tumor segmentation. It found that attention-based architectures are highly resilient to QAT recipe choices, while CNNs degrade significantly under certain recipes, especially at larger scales. Advanced QAT recipes are crucial for mitigating performance drops in CNNs at larger scales, but attention models like Swin Transformer remain robust regardless. The study concludes that the Swin Transformer is the recommended architecture for achieving reliable FP4-quantized anomaly segmentation due to its consistent performance across different scales and QAT methods.

## Technical note

A controlled study of the interaction between model architecture, scale, and NVFP4 quantization-aware training (QAT) recipe on recall-critical brain-tumor MRI segmentation. Three architectures (CNN, ViT, Swin) at three matched parameter scales (~530K, 3.7M, 13.7M) are trained under eight NVFP4 recipes combining 2D block-scaling, Random Hadamard Transform, and stochastic rounding, evaluated with threshold-free AUPRC under a BCE+Tversky loss. Swin is most FP4-robust, achieving the highest AUPRC at every scale and near-insensitivity to recipe, while low-capacity Swin suffers softmax attention discretization (head dim 16 gives logit SNR~4) that RHT and SR resolve, and large CNNs degrade under gradient-quantizing recipes that stochastic rounding largely recovers. Five-fold patient-level cross-validation confirms the Swin>CNN>ViT ranking. The 4M Swin with 2D+RHT+SR is recommended.

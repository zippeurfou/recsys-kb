## Summary

Efficient Model Exploration via Scaling-Law Prediction":

1.  Evaluating changes to large recommendation models is extremely time-consuming and resource-intensive due to the need for full-scale training runs.
2.  The paper introduces the Predictable Scaling (PS) framework, which uses empirically derived scaling laws to predict a model's full-scale performance based on smaller, cheaper experiments.
3.  PS demonstrates significant compute savings (up to 88% reduction) and effectively ranks potential model improvements, making large-scale exploration much more feasible without sacrificing accuracy.

## Technical note

Each candidate change (hypothesis) to a Meta ranking model needs a full training run costing thousands of GPU-hours, which caps how many ideas get tried. Predictable Scaling (PS) trains a scaled-down proxy built from composable scaling functions (hash-seeded data subsampling, embedding row and width shrink, importance-based feature pruning, uniform width scaling, depth truncation), measures the relative normalized-entropy gap between proxy hypothesis and proxy baseline, and maps it to the full-scale gap with one of three predictors: raw proxy, linear regression, or an Empirical Gaussian Process whose prior is learned from historical NE training curves and whose posterior variance rejects unreliable predictions; proxy configurations are chosen by cross-validated MAE on a benchmark corpus under a compute budget. On TS-1 (feed CTR+CVR, 14 held-out hypotheses) even the raw proxy has MAE 0.865 of the A/A noise floor and the EGP reaches 0.738 (-15%) with Spearman rho=1.0 among actionable hypotheses; on FM-1 (a ~35x larger multi-task foundation model, 7 hypotheses) the raw proxy is above the noise floor on CTR heads (2.15 and 1.75) and the EGP cuts MAE by 57-67%. Compute reductions are 88% (TS-1) and 79% (FM-1), roughly 5-8x cheaper per hypothesis.

- Per-model scaling function selection is essential. The best configuration differs across all four architectures (data_ds, hash_only, hash_data, hash_emb), and applying any single configuration universally would select a suboptimal proxy for at least three models
- Regularity violations are detectable. Configurations that violate (A3) (negative $\rho$) are identifiable on the benchmark corpus and would be excluded by [algorithm 1](#alg1), preventing their use on new hypotheses
- Architecture sensitivity. Models with tightly coupled feature interactions (DCN-v2’s cross-network, xDeepFM’s CIN) are harder to scale than models with simpler interaction structures (DLRM). The GP cannot fully compensate when the scaling function itself destroys the signal
- Embedding scaling is unreliable. ES produces poor rank preservation across all four public architectures, in contrast to its effectiveness on full scale models. This suggests ES is best suited for models with deep, redundant embedding representations rather than the relatively compact embeddings in these public benchmarks
- Data downsampling is scale-dependent. DD’s robustness on data ($\mathcal{O}(10^{10})$ examples) does not fully transfer to the smaller Criteo dataset, where $50\%$ downsampling may remove too much signal for architectures with complex feature interactions
- Linear predictor has narrow applicability. The linear predictor is effective only when the gap transfer function is approximately affine (DLRM), and is superseded by the GP on all architectures—supporting the GP as the default predictor choice (arXiv:2610.23453)

## Summary

*   This paper describes Apple Music's development of a multilingual semantic retrieval system to enhance search quality across hundreds of storefronts and millions of tracks, particularly crucial for handling misspelled or transliterated queries (common in tail searches).
*   The core solution is a large Siamese bi-encoder model fine-tuned from GTE-multilingual-base using specific curriculum learning and multi-task training methods.
*   This system demonstrably improves search effectiveness with significant offline gains (69% Hit@10 improvement) and strong online results (2.3% CR lift, 86% no-result rate reduction), especially benefiting tail queries without negatively impacting head searches.

## Technical note

Apple Music search over 150+ storefronts and a fast-growing multilingual catalog fails predominantly on recall for misspelled, transliterated, cross-lingual, and tail queries (83% of unique queries). ELISE is a 305M-parameter Siamese bi-encoder fine-tuned from GTE-multilingual-base with curriculum-scheduled multi-objective training over Query-Item and Query-Query tasks: stage 1 uses InfoNCE with in-batch plus uniform catalog negatives, stage 2 switches to a pairwise hinge on the hardest in-batch negative. Data strategies include storefront/entity-type capping, exact-match downsampling, synthetic misspelling augmentation, and injecting past engaged queries into item documents. It is served through hybrid dense (FAISS OPQ/IVF/HNSW/PQ) plus lexical retrieval, fused by linear score blending and quantile distribution matching so downstream rankers need no retraining. Offline it improves Hit@10 by +69% relative over the GTE baseline; a worldwide A/B test gives +2.28% conversion rate, -86% no-result rate, and tail-concentrated gains (+7.93% CR for tail vs +0.14% head) with <55ms added p95 latency and no per-storefront regressions; ablations show raw query logs beat adding five supplementary data sources and backbone choice was the highest-leverage decision.

## Summary

This paper addresses two major issues with standard collaborative filtering related video recommendations: poor semantic coherence (lack of meaningful topic consistency) and a strong bias towards popular items. The authors propose an innovative multi-objective retrieval framework that uses three key techniques to improve this balance – multi-task learning for optimizing both relevance and engagement, multimodal fusion for better content understanding via text/visual embeddings, and off-policy correction to reduce popularity bias.

Their evaluation on large-scale data shows significant success: the new system improves semantic relevance by increasing topic match rates from 51% to 63%, reduces recommendations of popular items effectively (-13.8%), while still achieving a modest +0.04% improvement in user engagement compared to YouTube's standard model, demonstrating practical scalability for real-world deployment.

## Technical note

Meta enhances a two-tower item-to-item retrieval model for related-video recommendation to jointly balance semantic relevance and engagement. It combines (a) multi-task learning that adds an auxiliary semantic-alignment task to co-engagement prediction (with a heavily weighted 10:1 or 100:1 ratio toward semantic alignment), (b) fusion of multimodal content features (textual metadata and visual embeddings), and (c) off-policy correction via inverse propensity weighting that down-weights popular training samples. Co-engagement is trained as binary classification with sampled softmax. A two-week live A/B test raised topic match rate from 51% to 63%, cut popular-video recommendations by 13.8%, and improved topline engagement by +0.04%.

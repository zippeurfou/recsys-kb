## Summary

This paper addresses challenges in online recruitment related to low-quality job descriptions and identifying suitable candidates for similar job pairs. It proposes two techniques: using LLMs with Chain-of-Thought prompts to improve job descriptions, and a category-aware Mixture of Experts (MoE) model to better match similar candidate-job pairs by learning distinguishable patterns. The method significantly improves recruitment metrics like AUC and GAUC, boosts conversion rates, and saves substantial costs through offline and online validation.

## Technical note

This work targets Person-Job Fit (PJF) in online recruitment with two additions: LLM-based data augmentation that rewrites the ~25% of low-quality, short job descriptions via chain-of-thought prompts anchored on interview-passing resumes, and a category-aware Mixture-of-Experts module whose gating network is driven by learned job/candidate category embeddings to disambiguate similar candidate-job pairs. A fine-grained bilateral historical-interaction encoder uses multi-head attention over three recruitment stages (resume evaluation, passed evaluation, passed interview), modeling internal and external interactions. The model is trained pairwise with BPR loss and regularization, and deliberately excludes sensitive/proxy features for fairness. Offline it beats CONFIT by 2.40% AUC and 7.46% GAUC on a real 8M-sample dataset; online A/B tests raise CTCVR by 19.4%.

## Summary

A Variational Stackelberg Game for Discrete Diffusion":

1.  Discrete diffusion models allow for "re-drafting" by correcting earlier tokens, but existing methods either fix tokens too early or use random substitutions. This paper introduces Variational Stackelberg Discrete Diffusion (VSDD) to learn a better corruption process specifically for training the denoiser to effectively re-draft.
2.  VSDD frames training as a leader-follower game: the leader defines a corruption process aiming to maximize denoiser improvement, while the follower optimizes denoising performance given that corruption process, with the leader's reward based on the actual improvement gained.
3.  Evaluated across different domains, VSDD significantly improves the quality of outputs compared to uniform or masked diffusion, enhancing molecular validity, reducing text perplexity, and improving offline playlist recommendation metrics.

## Technical note

Discrete diffusion can re-draft earlier tokens, but masked diffusion (MDLM) freezes tokens once unmasked, uniform D3PM substitutes at random, and learning the forward process jointly with the denoiser (FLDD, D3PM-Reinforce) collapses into near-deterministic, easy-to-reconstruct corruptions such as a period mapped to 'to' with probability 0.998. Spotify's VSDD parameterizes a Markov forward kernel Q_t = alpha_t I + (1 - alpha_t) M_t whose off-diagonal transitions are a softmax over time-conditioned bilinear similarities of the denoiser's normalized token embeddings, and trains it as a Stackelberg game in which the follower, a bidirectional transformer denoiser, minimizes the D3PM ELBO under the leader's stop-gradient process, while every 100 follower steps the leader scores K corruptions of a validation batch by the relative gain in a fixed uniform-reference ELBO after a one-step virtual follower update and updates the kernel with a REINFORCE score-function estimator using a leave-one-out baseline, running-variance normalization, and reward clipping. On ZINC SMILES it reaches 84.3% validity versus 58.7% for MDLM and 48.1% for uniform D3PM, and on TinyStories it cuts GPT-2 perplexity from 236.33 (uniform) to 106.71, close to MDLM's 103.26. On a proprietary Spotify set of about 44K listening histories, with each track hashed by LSH into a 3-token semantic ID (about 228K unique), infilling the last three tracks gives NDCG@3 0.108 and HitRate@3 0.246, best among five forward-process designs. Learned transitions correlate with embedding similarity (Spearman 0.70 on molecules, 0.74 on playlists), favor chemically sensible swaps such as halogen exchanges, and repair corruptions better (entity-swap exact repair 0.88 versus 0.36 for uniform, 86.1% repair of disrupted playlist transitions).

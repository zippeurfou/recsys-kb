## Summary

Researchers investigated how large language models (LLMs) like Gemma and Qwen generate verbal confidence scores. They found evidence that these models compute confidence automatically during answer generation, caching it for later retrieval, rather than calculating it just-in-time. Critically, the cached confidence scores represent a richer evaluation of answer quality than just token log-probabilities, indicating LLMs perform sophisticated self-evaluation, which has implications for understanding metacognition and calibration.

## Technical note

This mechanistic-interpretability study asks when and what verbal confidence is in LLMs, using Gemma 3 27B (plus Qwen 2.5 7B and Magistral 24B) on TriviaQA/BigMath/MMLU. Converging evidence from activation steering, patching, noising, and swap experiments shows confidence is computed automatically at the post-answer-newline (PANL) position at earlier layers (21-26) and later retrieved at the confidence-colon (CC) token (layers 30-36), supporting a cached-retrieval rather than just-in-time account. Attention blocking traces the pathway answer tokens -> PANL -> CC and rules out CC computing confidence de novo from question/answer tokens. Linear probing plus variance partitioning shows PANL/CC representations explain substantial variance beyond token log-probabilities (PANL L40 explains 38% vs ~10% for a 6-index logprob baseline), and the answer-colon position is causally inert, together indicating a second-order evaluation of question-answer fit rather than a fluency readout, with implications for calibration and hallucination detection.

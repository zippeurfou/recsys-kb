## Summary

1.  Deep search agents often inefficiently search too much or too little because their internal decision boundaries, determining when accumulated information is sufficient to answer, are misaligned due to outcome-centric training.
2.  The paper introduces DAS (Decision Boundary Alignment for Deep Search agents), a framework using causal intervention to diagnose boundary errors and preference optimization to align the agent's stopping policy by creating datasets from causal feedback.
3.  Experiments show decision boundary errors are common in state-of-the-art agents, and DAS effectively corrects them, significantly improving both the accuracy and efficiency of these search agents.

## Technical note

The paper targets inefficiency in deep search agents that over-search or under-search because their search decision boundary (when accumulated knowledge suffices to answer) is miscalibrated. It diagnoses boundary errors via causal intervention, comparing factual trajectories with counterfactual do(Answer)/do(Search) outcomes at each decision point. Decision Boundary Alignment (DAS) builds preference pairs from these causal signals and fine-tunes the policy with DPO (plus LoRA). Across NQ, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA, DAS simultaneously lowers over-search and under-search rates and improves exact-match accuracy while reducing inference time and average search queries.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of integrating generative retrieval into large-scale e-commerce search, proposing CQ-SID, a method that encodes products into hierarchical semantic cluster IDs to drastically reduce beam search complexity during recall.
2.  To train this generative recall effectively despite sparse rewards, the authors introduce EG-GRPO, an expert-guided reinforcement learning technique that aligns the generative model's output with downstream ranking goals.
3.  Offline and online evaluations demonstrate that CQ-SID significantly improves click-through rates and business metrics (GMV, UCTCVR), and the generative recall channel is now a major contributor to search results, validating a practical path for deploying generative retrieval in production.

## Technical note

CQ-SID positions generative retrieval as a recall-stage supplement rather than an end-to-end replacement in industrial e-commerce search, using RQ-VAE to encode items into hierarchical semantic clusters (Category-and-Query constrained Semantic IDs) instead of unique identifiers, which halves beam-search size. A four-stage progressive training pipeline (item-to-SID, query-to-SID, personalized user-query-to-SID, ranking-aligned refinement) on a lightweight Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone builds query-to-SID mapping. EG-GRPO (Expert-Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization) injects ground-truth samples into the policy-gradient group to stabilize sparse-reward RL and prevent the click-exposure trade-off collapse of standard GRPO. On TmallAPP logs it improves semantic/personalized click hitrate by 26.76%/11.11% over the RQ-VAE baseline while cutting beam size by more than half.

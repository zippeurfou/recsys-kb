## Summary

This paper tackles the challenge of optimizing sequential notification delivery, balancing message relevance (utility) and avoiding excessive notifications (user fatigue). It proposes using a Decision Transformer framework, which reframes policy learning as supervised prediction from trajectories, offering greater robustness and scalability than traditional offline RL approaches like CQL. Extensive experiments in production show that this DT-based system improves notification utility and session activity while reducing user fatigue compared to existing multi-objective agents at LinkedIn.

## Technical note

This LinkedIn paper reframes notification send/drop decision-making as return-conditioned sequence modeling with a Decision Transformer, replacing an unstable Conservative Q-Learning agent. It preserves a multi-dimensional reward vector (notification value, visits, adaptive volume penalty for fatigue) and learns state-dependent return-to-go via quantile (pinball-loss) regression, enabling prompt tuning at inference to trade off objectives. A production circular-buffer feature store on Venice/Samza supports transformer inference at 150K QPS. Offline it reaches 96.7% action accuracy, and online A/B testing yields +0.72% sessions with reduced notification volume and neutral CTR versus the CQL baseline, plus far greater training stability.

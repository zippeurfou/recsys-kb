## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of popularity bias in recommendation systems caused by unbalanced user-item interactions, which leads to overly popular recommendations. The authors analyze how this bias affects gradient optimization during training, specifically noting that gradients for popular items are stronger and misaligned with positive feedback. They propose a gradient-based framework that normalizes embeddings during training and adjusts them during testing to mitigate this bias, showing it works well across different models and datasets.

## Technical note

The paper analyzes popularity bias in implicit-feedback recommenders from a gradient perspective, showing that for popular items the overall embedding gradient is directionally dominated by (and larger in magnitude than) positive-interaction gradients, inflating their embedding norms and pushing them to rank high. Under BPR-style pairwise loss it attributes this to item popularity, user activity, and user conformity. The proposed model-agnostic framework normalizes user embeddings and records accumulated user/item gradients during training, then applies a gradient-based embedding adjustment at test time to rebalance positive and negative gradients. On two classic models and four datasets it outperforms state-of-the-art debiasing baselines while remaining interpretable and easy to integrate.

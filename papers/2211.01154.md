## Summary

Recommender systems often suffer from popularity bias due to unbalanced user-item interactions, leading them to recommend popular items even when they aren't the best fit for the user. The authors address this from a gradient perspective, identifying two key issues: popular items receive gradient directions closer to positive interactions and have larger gradient magnitudes than unpopular items. They propose a gradient-based framework that normalizes embeddings during training and adjusts them during testing to counteract this bias, finding it effective across various models and datasets.

## Technical note

Analyzes popularity bias in implicit-feedback recommenders from a gradient perspective, showing that popular items' embeddings are dominated by positive gradients—their overall gradient direction aligns with positive interactions and has far larger magnitude than for unpopular items—driven by item popularity, user activity, and user conformity. The proposed fix is model-agnostic and largely post-hoc: normalize user embeddings during training to curb high-activity users, then at inference subtract projections onto a 'popular direction' (mean popular-item embedding) from item embeddings and a conformity projection from user embeddings to recover genuine-interest representations. On four datasets (MovieLens-10M, Netflix, Adressa, Gowalla) with MF and LightGCN backbones and debiased test sets, it beats IPS, CausE, DICE, and MACR on Recall/HR/NDCG@20 while adding negligible runtime versus 2-3x for DICE/MACR.

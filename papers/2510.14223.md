## Summary

LinkedIn faces the challenge of efficiently retrieving relevant content (from outside a user's network) for its feed, starting from hundreds of millions of candidates while maintaining low latency and high throughput. To solve this, they fine-tuned a large causal language model (LLaMA 3) as a dual encoder, generating high-quality embeddings for users and content using only textual input, enabling effective similarity-based retrieval. This approach, along with prompt engineering techniques like feature quantization and optimized infrastructure, significantly improved member engagement, particularly benefiting newer users with fewer connections.

## Technical note

LinkedIn fine-tunes Meta's LLaMA 3 causal language model as a dual encoder to retrieve out-of-network Feed content, replacing a heterogeneous stack of inverted indices, collaborative filtering, and two-tower EBR sources. Member and post features are rendered into text prompts (with special tokens and an activity-history sequence of prior positive interactions) and the LLM generates dense user and item embeddings for similarity-based (EBR) retrieval optimizing daily unique Professional Interactors. A key finding is that quantizing numerical features in the prompt lets that information actually encode into the embeddings, improving retrieval-ranking alignment; ablations show using only positive-engagement history works best. With infrastructure for low-latency, high-QPS serving (2000 candidates from hundreds of millions), online A/B tests showed substantial engagement gains, especially for newer members with few connections.

## Summary

This paper uses a structural model on Netflix data to quantify the value of personalized recommendations separate from the content itself. It finds that replacing the current recommender system with simpler algorithms would significantly reduce user engagement by about 4-12% and decrease content diversity. The study shows that the main benefit of recommendations comes from effective targeting, particularly boosting engagement with mid-popularity content.

## Technical note

This paper builds a structural discrete-choice model on Netflix viewership to separate the value of personalized recommendations from the intrinsic value of the content. The model embeds a recommendation-induced utility 'bonus', low-rank user/good heterogeneity, and transformer-style attention over a user's viewing history to capture flexible state-dependent demand while scaling to two million users and ~7,000 goods without per-user parameters. Recommendation effects are identified from conditionally exogenous algorithmic exposure and validated against model-free diversion ratios from a randomized salience experiment (0.86 correlation out of sample). Counterfactuals show replacing the current RecSys with popularity-based or matrix-factorization algorithms reduces engagement by 12% and 4% respectively and cuts consumption diversity, and a decomposition finds most incremental consumption comes from effective targeting (41.9%), not mechanical exposure (6.8%), with mid-popularity goods benefiting most.

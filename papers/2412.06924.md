## Summary

This paper explores user history modeling using Transformers within deep learning recommendation models, noting that while effective they often have high latency costs. The authors compare different early fusion methods for incorporating candidate items and find that appending it as a separate item is more efficient than concatenation when combined with cross-attention. They then apply an amortized inference technique called M-FALCON to this method, demonstrating significantly reduced inference costs without sacrificing performance compared to standard non-amortized approaches on LinkedIn's platforms.

## Technical note

Addresses the high online serving cost of Transformer-based user history encoders in DLRM ranking models. The authors compare two early-fusion strategies for the candidate item, concatenating it to each history step (TransAct-style) versus appending it to the sequence with cross-attention (BST-style), and reformulate M-FALCON amortized inference for the append-with-cross-attention case. Appending matches concatenating on prediction accuracy across four public and two internal datasets while enabling amortization that shares history computation across candidates. Deployed on LinkedIn Feed and Ads, amortized inference cut latency ~30% (p90 +11% vs +56%) with a small engagement gain.

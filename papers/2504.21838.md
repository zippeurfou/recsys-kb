## Summary

Snapchat developed **Universal User Representations (UUM)** to create a general-purpose user model capturing behaviors across different app surfaces. This approach enriches existing domain-specific representations by encoding cross-domain trends without replacing them directly, overcoming limitations in traditional recommender system methods that hinder complex relationship discovery across the platform. Following successful A/B testing and offline validation, UUM representations were deployed into production to enhance ranking algorithms for various features like long-form videos and lenses, resulting in measurable improvements such as increased open rates and view times.

## Technical note

This industry paper from Snapchat describes Universal User Modeling (UUM), which learns general-purpose user embeddings that encode collaborative signals across multiple in-app surfaces rather than sharing per-surface representations post-hoc. It formulates UUM as a cross-domain sequential retrieval task: events from all domains are stitched chronologically, projected into a shared latent space, and processed by stacked multi-head self-attention with weighted pooling to produce a user embedding. Training uses a multi-task objective of next-event prediction (sampled softmax with in-batch negatives) plus next-event property prediction, with sliding-window subsequence augmentation to manage long, power-law sequences. After A/B testing, UUM launched in production, yielding gains such as +2.78% long-form video open rate and +19.2% video view time.

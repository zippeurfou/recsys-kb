## Summary

RecRSI presents a novel LLM-based research framework for discovering recommendation algorithms by structuring the process around explicit "mechanism hypotheses." These hypotheses link specific design changes to expected outcomes, organized into three components: MechSpace defines primitives and rules, Research Line develops and tests hypotheses, and Mechanism Feedback uses results to guide future experiments. This method significantly improves algorithm performance, achieving average relative gains of 8.48% in NDCG@5 and 6.88% in HR@5, and further demonstrating improvements over strong competitors in some cases.

## Technical note

LLM-agent discovery of recommendation algorithms struggles because mechanisms (representation, scoring, objective, update) interact and an experiment's outcome is only interpretable against the design it modified. RecRSI (Meituan) makes the mechanism hypothesis (reference design, proposed composition, rationale, expected observation) the unit of research: MechSpace uses an LLM generator and reviewer plus a deterministic assembler to derive implementable primitives and composition rules from the seed code (e.g. cosine-temperature scoring, pairwise margin loss added to BCE, focal BCE, recent-history fusion, label smoothing); Research Line's four roles (Mechanism Composer, Lineage Refiner, Falsification Designer, Frontier Architect) propose, implement and qualify candidates under a budget of 12 genuine train-eval runs; Mechanism Feedback records each outcome with its changed and unchanged mechanisms so later hypotheses can build on an earlier incumbent while using a failed contrast to change direction. From SASRec, MoRec, BERT4Rec and NGCF seeds on Amazon CDs, Electronics, Office and MovieLens-1M with GPT-5.6, RecRSI improves the seed in all 16 settings, averaging +8.48% NDCG@5 and +6.88% HR@5, and beats RecHarness, DeepEvolve, OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve and Self-EvolveRec by 2.60% and 2.20% on average (SASRec/CDs NDCG@5 0.3489 to 0.4329), while sitting on the quality-versus-API-cost Pareto frontier (+7.36% vs +4.68% mean test gain for RecHarness at USD 2.38 more per search). Ablations show explicit MechSpace only pays off with mechanism-level feedback (+0.61pp interaction), a continued 24-evaluation search beats two 12-evaluation restarts, the auto-generated space beats a human-designed one (0.4236 vs 0.3540 NDCG@5 on SASRec/CDs), and mechanism feedback picks better next candidates than raw or summarized history in 7 of 10 frozen decision points.

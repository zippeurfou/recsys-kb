## Summary

1.  Researchers introduced **MMSC**, a novel multi-modal self-supervised relational learning framework to infer complementary and substitutable product relationships from e-commerce data.
2.  It specifically addresses two major limitations: noisy user behavior data (like clicks) and sparse, long-tailed interaction patterns – problems existing GNN or content-based approaches don't fully handle.
3.  MMSC combines item metadata information with advanced denoising of user behaviors using LLM-augmented data, and integrates these representations hierarchically to achieve significantly better recommendation results (+26.1% & +39.2%) than previous methods and demonstrates effectiveness for new ("cold-start") items.

## Technical note

MMSC is a self-supervised multi-modal framework for inferring substitutable (co-viewed) and complementary (co-purchased) item relationships, targeting noisy user behavior and long-tailed sparsity. It fuses content representations from a frozen multimodal foundation model (BLIP-2) with task-specific multi-head attention, behavior-based representations from a meta-path encoder that denoises interactions, and a hierarchical aggregation across semantic and task levels. LLMs generate augmented training data to further denoise behaviors, and a multi-task objective jointly denoises and predicts the two relationship types. Across five datasets it beats baselines by 26.1% (substitutable) and 39.2% (complementary) and is effective on cold-start items.

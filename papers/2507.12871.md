## Summary

This paper introduces GMC, a novel **Generative Multi-Target Cross-Domain Recommendation** (**MTCDR**) method. Existing MTCDR approaches typically rely on domain-shared entities like users or items for knowledge transfer, but these can fail in non-overlapped domains and often require extensive pre-training data. The key innovation of GMC is its use of a generative sequence-to-sequence model with semantically quantized item identifiers as the core mechanism to integrate cross-domain information effectively across different recommendation targets.

## Technical note

GMC applies the generative paradigm to Multi-Target Cross-Domain Recommendation (MTCDR), removing the reliance on overlapping users/items or auxiliary pretraining data. It tokenizes each item's LLM text embedding into shared hierarchical discrete semantic IDs via a unified RQ-VAE, adding a domain-aware contrastive loss that pulls intra-domain quantized embeddings together. A single sequence-to-sequence model is then trained across all domains as a next-token (semantic ID) generation task, followed by domain-specific LoRA fine-tuning to capture per-domain characteristics while freezing the backbone. Constrained prefix-tree decoding ensures valid item generation. Across five public datasets GMC outperforms cross-domain and generative baselines, uniquely satisfying multi-domain, non-overlap, and no-auxiliary-data requirements simultaneously.

## Summary

This paper proposes using Large Language Models (LLMs) as a universal retriever for recommender systems, capable of handling multiple retrieval objectives. To achieve this, the authors introduce the Universal Retrieval Model (URM), which employs multi-query representation to model complex user-item relationships and matrix decomposition with probabilistic sampling to efficiently handle large candidate sets, ensuring fast response times. Applied to industrial data, URM outperformed specialized models on offline tests and significantly improved key online metrics by 3%.

## Technical note

This paper uses an LLM as a universal retriever (URM) that handles many retrieval objectives within one generative-retrieval model, avoiding the usual pattern of training separate task-specific retrieval models. Objectives are expressed as text sequences, letting one model cover an open-ended objective space and mitigating the multi-task seesaw phenomenon by scaling model size instead of hand-designing per-objective parameters. To model complex user-item relationships it introduces multi-query representation, and to scale to tens of millions of candidates with low latency it uses matrix decomposition (boosting learnability/discriminability/transferability) plus probabilistic sampling to cut computation. On industrial-scale data URM outperforms expert models specialized per objective offline, adaptively generates candidate sets within tens of milliseconds, and improves the core online advertising metric by 3%.

- Click Prediction Retrieval (CPR): Positive samples are click behaviors
- Retrieval for Scene A (RSA), Scene B (RSB), Scene C (RSC): Positive samples are the items exposed to users in $3$ scenarios with distribution shift
- Serendipity Retrieval (SR): Retrieve new items for users to mitigate the information cocoon effect. Positive samples are real click behaviors, whose categories have not appeared in user behaviors
- Long-term Retrieval (LR): Retrieve items based on long-term behaviors of users. Positive samples are the users’ real click behaviors, whose categories only appear in the long-term user behavior sequence
- Long-tail Item Retrieval (LIR):
- Purchase Prediction Retrieval (PPR): Positive samples are the items that the users have purchased
- Retrieval with Query (RQ): Given a query, retrieve the most relevant items that the user is most likely to be interested in. Positive samples are the user’s click behaviors under a certain query (arXiv:2502.03041)
- By changing the query to a certain context, we can inject external world knowledge into URM, allowing the results to get ready for potential upcoming events
- This also supports the combination of URM and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) technologies . Specifically, the LLM generates intermediate results in the form of text through reasoning, and then injects this text as context into URM, thereby producing the final set (arXiv:2502.03041)

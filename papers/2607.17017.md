## Summary

1.  **WHALE** addresses the challenge of combining non-sequence (user/item context) and sequence-based user behavior data in recommendation models by integrating two scalable architectures, Wukong and HSTU, into a unified framework.
2.  Its design keeps both backbone models active throughout each layer, using an attention mechanism for fusion where representations from Wukong (non-sequence features) query evidence from HSTU (sequence history).
3.  WHALE optimizes training and inference by deploying custom Triton kernels to handle the complex feature interactions efficiently, proving its practicality for industrial deployment through positive offline/experiment results and modest online serving throughput trade-offs.

## Technical note

Industrial rankers scale along two separate paths - Wukong for high-order non-sequence feature interaction and HSTU for long user-behavior sequences - and the usual way to combine them is a shallow hybrid that first compresses the history into a few summary embeddings and feeds them to the interaction model, which permanently forecloses fine-grained access to individual behavior events. WHALE (Meta) instead keeps both backbones active in every layer: each WHALE layer runs a Wukong module on the non-sequence branch (N x D), an HSTU module on the sequence branch (L x D), then a cross-branch attention fusion where the Wukong interaction representations are queries and the HSTU behavior representations are keys/values, followed by a Fusion MLP that concatenates A with H_w through a residual projection and a pre-norm SwiGLU FFN; the two branches keep separate propagation paths so the exchange is progressive across layers. Trained on 80B samples (4B eval) from a large short-form video platform on B200s and measured in normalized entropy, WHALE beats Wukong-only and HSTU-only baselines at matched 8/14/32 GFLOPs, and scales monotonically in sequence length (3k -> 15k), depth (2 -> 8 layers), and width (64 -> 512 dims). The fusion ablation is the key result: shallow-hybrid fusion costs 0.25% NE and replacing attention with average pooling over the sequence dimension costs 0.23% NE (vs 0.11% for dropping the fusion MLP and 0.08% for the SwiGLU FFN), on a platform where 0.05% NE is a noticeable win - i.e. compressing history into summaries is what hurts. Systems work (fused Triton cross-attention with shared K=V to cut memory traffic, shape-aware kernel selection for ~1.2x, 3->2 matmuls in the fusion, BF16, TorchInductor/AOTInductor) gives +30% training and +22% inference throughput; the 14-day online A/B lifted the primary metric +0.113% (0.03% is significant) with Metric 1 +0.824%, Metric 2 +1.820%, at a -5% inference QPS cost.

## Summary

Large language models (LLMs) struggle with recommendation tasks due to challenges in connecting textual reasoning with collaborative filtering and sparse user data. This paper introduces GREAM, an end-to-end framework designed to adapt LLMs into effective generative reasoning recommenders by addressing these core issues through specialized alignment, reasoning training, and optimization techniques. Experiments on multiple datasets show that GREAM consistently outperforms existing methods, demonstrating a practical way to build verifiable LLM-powered recommendation systems.

## Technical note

GREAM adapts a pre-trained LLM into a generative reasoning recommendation model through three components: Collaborative-Semantic Alignment that fuses item text into RQ-KMeans discrete item indices with alignment tasks, Reasoning Curriculum Activation that builds synthetic Chain-of-Thought data across a five-stage reasoning chain, and Sparse-Regularized Group Policy Optimization (SRPO). SRPO extends GRPO with a Residual-Sensitive Verifiable Reward and Bonus-Calibrated Group Advantage Estimation to stabilize RLVR post-training under the sparse, stochastic feedback typical of recommendation. The framework supports two inference modes: Direct Sequence Recommendation for low latency and Sequential Reasoning Recommendation that emits an interpretable reasoning chain first. Experiments on Beauty, Sports & Outdoors, and Instruments show consistent gains over generative and LLM baselines on Recall/NDCG and reasoning Pass@K metrics.

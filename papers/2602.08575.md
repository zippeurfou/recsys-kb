## Summary

Current generative retrieval (GR) methods for recommendations focus solely on next-token prediction, limiting their ability to capture nuanced user preferences. To address this, the paper introduces RankGR, a method incorporating listwise direct preference optimization. RankGR decomposes retrieval into two stages (Initial Assessment and Refined Scoring) optimized within a unified model to better understand user hierarchy and refine top candidates, achieving both high performance and real-time scalability.

## Technical note

The paper argues that next-token-prediction generative retrieval for recommendation fails to model the partial order of user preferences and lacks cross-effects between decoded candidates and the behavior sequence. It proposes RankGR, which decomposes retrieval into an Initial Assessment Phase (IAP) that adds listwise direct preference optimization via supervised contrastive learning over item-level reward signals (purchases, clicks) and a Refined Scoring Phase (RSP) with a lightweight module for deep candidate-sequence interaction beyond inner-product matching. Both phases are jointly optimized in a unified GR model with engineering optimizations for near-ten-thousand QPS serving. Deployed in Taobao's 'Guess You Like' section, RankGR yields a 1.08% increase in Item Page Views alongside offline gains on research and industrial datasets.

## Summary

This paper introduces HyperLLM, a framework designed to improve recommender systems by integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) with hyperbolic geometry. It addresses limitations of traditional Euclidean-based recommenders in capturing hierarchical information from text and semantic data. By using LLMs for multi-level tags and applying contrastive learning alongside a novel meta-optimized strategy, HyperLLM effectively extracts and utilizes this hierarchy, leading to significant performance gains exceeding 40% over existing methods combining both approaches.

## Technical note

Euclidean LLM-enhanced recommenders struggle to capture the hierarchical structure inherent in textual/semantic data, which hyperbolic geometry represents naturally. HyperLLM is a model-agnostic framework that extracts hierarchy two ways: structurally, LLMs generate multi-level parent-child tags whose tag-item and user-item interactions are jointly learned and aligned via hyperbolic contrastive learning; semantically, a meta-optimized two-phase strategy trains a Mixture-of-Experts (with frozen hyperbolic model) to transform LLM preference-summary embeddings into the hyperbolic collaborative space, using task-specific adaptive margin ranking losses. Across five hyperbolic backbones on Amazon Toys/Sports/Beauty it improves Recall/NDCG by over 40% at best, accelerates convergence and stabilizes training, and notably helps sparse long-tail users; semantic hierarchy contributes more than structural.

## Summary

Ranking models often rely on costly trial-and-error for refinement. This paper introduces MISO, a system that uses model internal states (like parameters, activations) to guide which components need optimization. The method automatically extracts and interprets these signals after each training cycle, suggesting specific edits to improve ranking performance while adapting to changing data or requirements over time.

## Technical note

Meta proposes MISO, a systems workflow that turns a trained ads-ranking model's internal states (parameters, activations, gradients, normalization statistics) into a prioritized shortlist of architectural edits, replacing expert trial-and-error and black-box AutoML for repeated local refinement inside a fixed model family. Three aggregation primitives map raw signals to actions: ranking-based neuron importance I_n = alpha * mean|a_n * grad_{a_n} L| + (1-alpha) * Delta-L under activation shuffling (drives selective scaling and pruning), alignment-based Align(l) = 1 - mean KL of pre/post-normalization distributions against N(0,1) (flags unstable normalization blocks for replacement), and comparison-based contrasts across models/layers/training stages (explains why nearby variants diverge). Evaluated on billion-example ads ranking workloads at 13x/20x/32x/50x scales, MISO delivers 2.0-2.5x the relative Normalized Entropy improvement of expert-driven tuning while needing only 3-12 training runs versus 50-92, an 84-94% reduction in exploration cost. A 50x-scale ablation shows ranking signals alone recover 62% of the full MISO NE gain, ranking+comparison 75%, and the full combination 100% at 95% params and 92% latency. The authors position it as post-hoc refinement of an already-usable baseline, not architecture discovery, and report aggregate operational measurements from a proprietary deployment rather than a reproducible benchmark.

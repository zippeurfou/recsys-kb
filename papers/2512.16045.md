## Summary

The paper introduces "Aria2," a complete system architecture for wearable egocentric contextual AI, designed to be always-on and power-efficient enough for all-day use. It argues that developing such wearable systems is complex due to stringent power constraints, requiring careful design across the entire system rather than focusing on single components. The work provides valuable lessons learned from its design and modeling, emphasizing the need for holistic system thinking to avoid power bottlenecks and enable future wearable AI personal assistants.

## Technical note

The paper presents the first complete system-architecture power and performance model (PnPSim) for an always-on wearable egocentric contextual AI device (Aria2), which must operate under ~200 mW to enable all-day (15h) use on a ~3 Wh battery. It models egocentric primitives (localization/SLAM, eye tracking, ASR, hand tracking) and explores whether to compute signals on-device (compressing sensor data) or offload them wirelessly. A key finding is that moving compute on-device is not always power-optimal (hand tracking saves 14%, ASR increases power 7%) and that no single component dominates system power, imposing an Amdahl's-law limit that requires holistic full-system optimization rather than component-level fixes.

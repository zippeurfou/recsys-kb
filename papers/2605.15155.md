## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles with providing enough fine-grained guidance for complex, long multi-turn conversations by LLM agents.
2.  **Solution:** The paper introduces SDAR (Self-Distilled Agentic Reinforcement Learning), which combines standard RL with a refined version of the On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) method. SDAR uses a "gate" mechanism to selectively strengthen positive feedback and soften negative feedback from the OPSD teacher, preventing instability.
3.  **Outcome:** This approach significantly improves agent performance on complex tasks compared to standard RL baselines (like GRPO) and avoids the instability issues of directly combining RL with OPSD, consistently outperforming simpler hybrid methods across different model sizes.

## Technical note

SDAR combines reinforcement learning with On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) for multi-turn LLM agent post-training, addressing OPSD's compounding multi-turn instability and asymmetric trust in skill-conditioned privileged guidance. It keeps GRPO as the primary RL backbone and treats OPSD as a gated auxiliary objective, mapping detached token-level teacher-student gaps through a sigmoid gate that strengthens distillation on teacher-endorsed positive-gap tokens and softly attenuates negative teacher rejections. This avoids the instability of naively adding OPSD to GRPO, since negative gaps may stem from imperfect skill retrieval rather than truly bad tokens. Across Qwen2.5/Qwen3 on ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA it improves over GRPO by up to +10.2% and beats hybrid RL-OPSD baselines across scales.

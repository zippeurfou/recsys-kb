## Summary

Recommender systems struggle with item discovery due to sparse historical data and the prevalence of long-tail or cold-start items. To address this, the paper introduces MSACL, a framework that enhances the classical Two-Tower model by incorporating multi-source semantic augmentations via contrastive learning. This method improves episode embeddings by leveraging positive correlations from various semantic sources, leading to effective recommendations for user exploration and even for newly released episodes with limited interaction data, as shown on a real podcast dataset.

## Technical note

MSACL extends the two-tower recommendation model with multi-source contrastive augmentation to improve podcast episode exploration and cold-start discovery under severe feature and interaction sparsity. For each episode it mines top-K semantically similar episodes via ANN (Annoy) over pretrained content-text and knowledge-graph embeddings, treating them as augmented positives (discovery item augmentation) and adding an NT-Xent contrastive regularization that connects augmented items to interacted positives. Training combines sampled-softmax cross-entropy with the contrastive loss. On a large real-world audio-streaming podcast dataset, MSACL improves explorative and cold-episode recommendation over feature-dropout and plain two-tower baselines, with knowledge-based augmentation especially helpful for cold items.

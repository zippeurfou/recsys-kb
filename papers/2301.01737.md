## Summary

1.  Podcast recommendation systems struggle with sparse user interaction data and the prevalence of long-tail items, hindering effective discovery of relevant episodes, especially new ones.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces the Multi-Source Augmentations Contrastive Learning (MSACL) framework, which enhances a standard Two-Tower recommendation model by incorporating information from multiple correlated semantic sources using contrastive learning to improve episode embeddings.
3.  Extensive experiments on a real-world podcast dataset demonstrate that MSACL significantly improves podcast discovery and is effective for recommending cold-start episodes with limited interaction data.

## Technical note

MSACL tackles episode/podcast exploration recommendation, where interaction and feature sparsity plus cold-start items limit a standard Two-Tower model. It augments scarce user-episode interactions with 'discovery' positive episodes retrieved via ANN over pretrained content (BERT) and knowledge-graph (DistMult) embeddings, then adds an NT-Xent contrastive regularizer that ties augmented-positive similarity to the user-item prediction, effectively injecting extra positive interactions. Trained with sampled softmax plus the contrastive loss on a real audio-streaming dataset, MSACL beats popularity and Two-Tower baselines (including feature-dropout TT-FD) by 4-9% across Recall/NDCG/MRR. Knowledge-graph augmentation combined with feature dropout works best and yields the largest gains on extremely cold episodes.

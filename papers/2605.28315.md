## Summary

Existing benchmarks like FLORES-200 struggle to differentiate performance across diverse, knowledge-intensive Chinese-English translation domains because translation systems score very similarly. To address this limitation, the paper introduces HardMTBench, a new diagnostic benchmark featuring 10,000 hand-crafted, domain-balanced test sentences designed to be inherently challenging. This new benchmark significantly widens the performance gap between translation systems and reveals domain-specific weaknesses that standard metrics miss, providing a more accurate assessment tool.

## Technical note

HardMTBench is a difficulty-aware diagnostic benchmark for bidirectional Chinese-English domain translation, motivated by FLORES-200 saturation where 22 systems cluster within a 7.87-point GEMBA range (std 2.29) on zh-en. It covers 12 knowledge-intensive domains (finance, healthcare, law, science, etc.) with 10,000 hand-curated pairs packaged as 20,000 directional items, built by a three-stage pipeline: closed-set domain reclassification, an LLM multi-signal judge over knowledge density, translation difficulty, terminology load, and reference correctness, and a hardness fusion rule with per-domain quotas over an 84,566-pair candidate pool. Across 22 systems spanning general LLMs, commercial engines, and specialized MT models, HardMTBench roughly doubles the cross-system GEMBA spread versus FLORES-200, induces visible rank reorderings especially in the mid and lower tier, and exposes domain-specific terminology and knowledge weaknesses. The authors argue GEMBA-DA and xCOMET-XXL surface different difficulty aspects and should be reported jointly.

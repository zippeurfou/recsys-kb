## Summary

This paper introduces a method for fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) using detailed human feedback. Annotators mark specific text spans in model responses as liked or disliked and explain their reasoning, which guides the model's rewriting process. The model sequentially rewrites disliked spans based on the feedback, learning from each step to create a chain of incremental improvements, ultimately leading to higher quality outputs.

## Technical note

This paper fine-tunes LLMs using fine-grained span-level human feedback rather than one-bit A/B preference labels. Annotators mark liked and disliked spans in a response and give rationales; the base model then rewrites disliked spans left-to-right into a chain of incremental improvements, and adjacent steps form preference pairs for direct alignment (DPO). This cumulative critique-guided revision (framed as a Lamarckian analogy) yields stronger models than standard A/B ranking or full contrastive rewrites, improving alignment with less training and better sample efficiency.

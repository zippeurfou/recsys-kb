## Summary

This paper addresses a gap between understanding user demand (intent) and making effective recommendation decisions when using Large Language Models (LLMs). Existing semantic-based recommenders capture local item transitions poorly, while LLMs lack the specific feedback needed for recommendations despite handling diverse interaction histories. The authors propose *Feedback-Grounded Policy Discovery*, distinguishing intent from policy knowledge.

Their solution introduces a framework that first induces user intent and then discovers recommendation policies based on their incremental utility over an intent-only baseline, evaluating candidates using outcome feedback not linguistic plausibility. This learned policy knowledge is transferred into two latent tokens within a lightweight Semantic-ID generator, allowing LLM-free online inference while retaining the benefits.

Experiments show this approach consistently improves recommendation results compared to baselines, achieving significant gains in revenue (+4.506%) and average dollars per visit (+4.621%) in large-scale online A/B tests.

## Technical note

SID-based generative recommenders such as TIGER and LETTER are supervised only by the target item, so demand understanding and decision making stay entangled; the authors name this the Understanding-Action Gap and argue that linguistically plausible LLM reasoning is not necessarily outcome-effective. The framework separates intent knowledge (what the user currently seeks, induced by an LLM intent agent) from policy knowledge (a recommendation direction plus a rejection boundary): a policy agent proposes K hypotheses from prefix-successor transitions, a shared executor scores each policy-conditioned prediction against an intent-only baseline using cosine similarity of Qwen3-Embedding-8B encodings as a dense reward (advantage), and a feedback agent turns group-wise comparisons into critiques that drive up to R evolution rounds; only policies with positive advantage become supervision (87.74% of Beauty training users qualify). Both teachers are used offline only and distilled into an Intent Token and a Policy Token prepended to the autoregressive SID decoding chain via dual-space relational distillation, which KL-matches first-order in-batch user similarity distributions and higher-order top-K2 neighborhood affinities instead of matching embedding coordinates. On Amazon Beauty/Toys/Sports it lifts TIGER Recall@5 from 0.0425 to 0.0491 and LETTER from 0.0447 to 0.0513 on Beauty; ablations show relational beats direct MSE distillation (R@5 0.0491 vs 0.0469), random policy selection is worse than advantage-validated selection (0.0470), and intent contributes most at SID level 1 (+2.23 pp R@10) while policy contributes at deeper levels (+0.37 pp at level 3). A 7-day A/B test in Kuaishou local-services advertising (13.25M users, 1.61M items, 5%/5% traffic, ~70M samples) gives +4.506% Revenue and +4.621% ADVV, with per-sample latency rising only from 0.023s to 0.032s versus 4.437s for direct LLM inference.

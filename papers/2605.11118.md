## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Personalized storefronts in large e-commerce marketplaces are often assembled from many independent components: static themes per page section ("placement"), retrieval systems to fetch eligible products per placement, and pointwise rankers to order content. While effective in optimizing for aggregate preferences, this paradigm is rigid and can limit personalization and semantic cohesion across the page. ## Technical note

This Instacart paper proposes a cascaded generative merchandising framework that replaces rigid storefronts (static placement themes + retrieval + pointwise rankers) with two generative tasks: placement-level theme generation and constrained per-placement keyword generation to power RAG-based product retrieval. Teacher-student fine-tuning makes it viable under production latency/cost, with fine-tuned student ablations approaching closed-weight LLM performance, and generative output is fused with traditional ranking models to preserve hybrid infrastructure. It adds AI-driven content evaluation and quality filtering for safe automated deployment, and online experiments show an estimated +2.7% lift in cart adds per page view over a strong baseline.

## Summary

1. Generative Recommendation (GR) using autoregressive token prediction suffers from "structural gaps," primarily losing item-level information during encoding with hierarchical IDs and experiencing semantic drift between training and inference due to inconsistent decoding paths.
2. The paper introduces BARGE, which addresses these issues by employing an Item Context-Aware Attention mechanism *during encoding* to preserve structure and uses Hierarchical Path Reranking combined with Dual-Path Decoding during *decoding* to prevent semantic drift from two angles.
3. Extensive offline experiments on benchmarks show BARGE improves recommendation performance significantly, while a large-scale online A/B test revealed practical benefits including higher click-through rates, more unique clicks, and increased reading time for users on the Tencent platform.

## Technical note

BARGE targets two structural gaps in generative recommendation over hierarchical RQ-VAE semantic IDs: loss of item-level structure when multi-token IDs are flattened, and semantic drift during hierarchical beam-search decoding. It adds Item Context-Aware Attention (ICA) to reinject item-level context at the encoder, Hierarchical Path Reranking (HPR) trained with symmetric InfoNCE to rerank decoding paths, and Dual-Path Decoding (DPD) using an orthogonal split-and-quantize VAE (OSQ-VAE) with OR-fusion across two complementary channels. It improves R@10 by +19.6% on Amazon Beauty and +8.8% on Sports over the strongest baseline, and an online A/B test on Tencent yields +0.60% CTR, +1.34% click UV, and +1.70% total reading time. It generates item identifiers autoregressively instead of scoring a candidate set, but remains a next-item generative recommender rather than an explicit retrieval-plus-ranking unification.

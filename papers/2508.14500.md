## Summary

This paper introduces DGenCTR, a novel two-stage generative framework using discrete diffusion specifically designed for click-through rate (CTR) prediction. It diverges from traditional sequence generation methods by focusing on understanding the data distribution at the sample level and effectively handling crucial cross-features between users and items that discriminative models often struggle with or require complex feature engineering for.

The core idea of DGenCTR involves first pre-training a generative model via discrete diffusion to learn item-user interactions, followed by a supervised fine-tuning stage tailored explicitly to predict CTR from the generated representations. This approach aims to alleviate difficulties faced by standard discriminative models in scenarios with scarce labeled data (label-scarce space).

Extensive offline experiments and real-world online A/B tests demonstrate that DGenCTR significantly improves CTR prediction performance compared to existing methods, validating its effectiveness as a universal generative paradigm for this task.

## Technical note

DGenCTR proposes a sample-level generative paradigm for CTR prediction, arguing that the autoregressive item-generation paradigm of sequential generative recommendation discards essential user-item cross-features and thus hurts CTR. It is a two-stage discrete-diffusion framework: a generative pre-training stage where a forward process progressively masks sample features and the model learns to reconstruct the clean features conditioned on behavior labels (modeling positive/negative sample distributions jointly), which respects the permutation invariance of tabular features better than autoregression. The second stage treats CTR as a special case of the denoising objective, enabling lossless direct transfer of all pre-trained parameters to supervised fine-tuning. This overcomes the discriminative binary-label bottleneck and representation collapse, and offline plus online A/B tests validate improvements over SOTA CTR models.

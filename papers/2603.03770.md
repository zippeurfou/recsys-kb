## Summary

1.  Large-scale recommender system pre-ranking often mixes diverse, heterogeneous candidate samples during training, leading to gradient conflicts where easy samples negatively impact the optimization of harder ones.
2.  This paper introduces Heterogeneity-Aware Adaptive Pre-ranking (HAP), a unified framework that separates easy and hard samples, using tailored optimization paths and allocating computational resources more efficiently (lightweight models for easy, stronger models for hard).
3.  HAP improves pre-ranking effectiveness and has been successfully deployed industrially with measurable positive impact on user engagement metrics, while also providing a large-scale dataset for further research into candidate heterogeneity.

## Technical note

HAP targets the pre-ranking stage of Toutiao's multi-stage cascade, where heterogeneous negatives (exposed, ranking, pre-ranking, and global random) cause gradient conflicts under BCE and InfoNCE, with hard negatives dominating the softmax gradient. Gradient-Harmonized Contrastive Learning (GHCL) splits negatives into hard and easy subgroups and computes separate InfoNCE terms to decouple cross-group gradient coupling, adding an auxiliary global BCE loss for click-free sessions. Difficulty-Aware Model Routing (DAMR) runs a lightweight DeepFFM model over all candidates and forwards only the hard candidates to an expressive attention-based model, allocating compute by difficulty. Deployed for 9 months in production, it yielded +0.4% app-usage duration and +3% CTR while cutting CPU cost ~6%, and the authors release the ToutiaoRec multi-stage dataset.

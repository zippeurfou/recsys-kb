## Summary

1.  The research addresses the challenge of recommending movies with insufficient user interaction data (the cold-start problem) on large entertainment platforms.
2.  It proposes using Large Language Models (LLMs) trained solely on movie metadata to predict their future popularity and identify potential hits before they gain traction.
3.  This LLM-based method was shown to be effective for forecasting new movies compared to standard baseline approaches, potentially useful within recommendation systems or as a tool for editorial promotion teams.

## Technical note

The paper tackles the item cold-start problem for movies on a large entertainment platform by using LLMs to forecast the future popularity of newly released titles from metadata alone (genre, synopsis, cast, crew, awards, mood, era), before interaction data accumulates. A benchmark of newly released movies (input) mapped to later-popular movies (labels) is built from production data, and LLMs in generative ranking mode with structured JSON output are compared against Random and a Popular Embedding (PE) baseline (BERT/Linq-Embed-Mistral/Llama embeddings ranked by cosine similarity to top-100 popular items). Llama 3.1 405B with the most information-rich prompt (MD V4, including top-5 cast awards) performs best, followed by Llama 3.3 70B, with larger models benefiting more from complex prompts while the 8B model degrades on longer prompts. Listwise ranking outperformed pairwise for this open-ended, no-shared-query task, and prompt refinements (JSON format, negative instructions) were key to reliable parsing; the method is proposed as a retrieval signal or an editorial promotion aid.

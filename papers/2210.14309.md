## Summary

Cross Decoupling Network (CDN) improves long-tail item recommendation without hurting overall quality by decoupling item-side memorization vs generalization via a mixture-of-experts and user-side training distributions via a regularized bilateral branch network, co-trained with a gamma-adapter. Validated on public benchmarks and deployed at Google.

## Technical note

Industrial recommenders suffer from long-tail item distributions where tail items receive little feedback, and the paper shows user-preference predictions are biased under this skew from two gaps (the item distribution and user preference given an item), while prior two-stage decoupling methods cause catastrophic forgetting that degrades overall quality. The authors propose the Cross Decoupling Network (CDN): on the item side it decouples memorization vs generalization through a mixture-of-experts, and on the user side it decouples training distributions through a regularized bilateral branch network, with a gamma-adapter that co-trains both branches and softly shifts attention toward tail items (avoiding two-stage forgetting). Over a two-tower backbone, CDN improves both tail and overall performance on MovieLens-1M and BookCrossing, outperforming SOTA long-tail methods, and a case study in Google's large-scale recommender shows offline and online quality gains at low serving cost.

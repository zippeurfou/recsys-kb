## Summary

Foundation model agents often suffer from compounding errors in complex tasks like persuasion. The authors identify semantic leakage during retrieval as a key cause for this issue. They introduce Taxonomic Strategy RAG (TS-RAG), which uses a categorical bottleneck to separate argument structure from topic content, significantly improving performance across different scenarios and providing better diagnostics.

## Technical note

TS-RAG replaces dense semantic retrieval with a discrete categorical bottleneck: live debate states are mapped to a K-dimensional probability vector over abstract logical vulnerabilities, destroying domain vocabulary so cosine-similarity retrieval fetches domain-agnostic exploitation blueprints rather than topically-similar noise. A turn-by-turn Debate State Representation (DSR) tracks committed claims and active flaws, forcing per-turn diagnosis instead of static blueprint injection. On held-out ChangeMyView domains, TS-RAG lifts persuasion win rates and acts as a capability bridge, letting a lightweight persuader beat a stronger opponent (70.5%->78.5%). Ablations show naive semantic RAG collapses the weak model (81.5%->65.0%) and that OP resistance constraints are required to avoid sycophantic evaluation collapse.

## Summary

Tsinghua/NTU analysis of three food-delivery store-recommendation datasets (Delivery Hero Stockholm and Singapore, Meituan Beijing) showing repeat orders make up 38.0% to 56.5% of orders, that historical situations (time, delivery location) drive repeat consumption while collaborative situations drive exploration, and that no single recommender wins on both slices; sequential models win on repeat, CF/situation-only models on exploration. Proposes RepRec (situation-similarity attention over previously ordered stores) and ExpRec (trigger inference over situation, history, situation-conditioned user and collaborative-user embeddings) plus an intent-aware ensemble, and argues that a single combined metric is dominated by the easy repeat task and hides exploration regressions. Offline only (HR@3/NDCG@3), no online test.

## Technical note

Problem: food-delivery store recommenders are trained and evaluated on one blended list although repeat orders (same store as a previous order) and exploration orders (any other order) have very different candidate spaces and drivers. Data: Delivery Hero Stockholm (7,900 users, 1,131 stores, 128,329 orders, 56.5% repeat), Delivery Hero Singapore (46,129 users, 7,296 stores, 791,088 orders, 42.3% repeat) and Meituan Beijing TRD (76,118 users, 3,169 stores, 839,011 orders, 38.0% repeat), after filtering to users with at least 10 (DHRD) or 5 (TRD) orders; at the 15th order 68.6%/55.1%/56.8% of users reorder from a previous store. Findings: Pearson correlation between situation similarity (date, hour, weekday, same delivery location) and store similarity is higher for repeat than exploration when computed over the user's own history, and higher for exploration than repeat when computed over the top-10 collaborative users' last-week interactions; sequential/repeat-aware models (DIN, SDIM, TSRec, RepeatNet) win the repeat slice while situation-only, FM/FinalMLP and serendipity SNPR win exploration, and sequential models still top the combined list because repeat is easy and frequent. Method: RepRec scores previously ordered stores with cosine situation-similarity attention over history (0.14M to 0.69M params); ExpRec fuses current-situation, GRU history, situation-conditioned user and collaborative-user embeddings via a softmax trigger-inference layer; an IntEL-style intent predictor and ensemble combines them. Results (HR@3, exploration slice, 1 positive vs 999 sampled new stores): 0.1315 / 0.2407 / 0.1463 vs best baselines 0.1098 / 0.2042 / 0.1211; repeat HR@3 0.7833 / 0.7092 / 0.7585; combined HR@3 0.5314 / 0.4124 / 0.4010; 16 of 18 improvements significant; offline only, no A/B test.

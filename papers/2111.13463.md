## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to preference elicitation in conversational recommender systems by generating questions about item usage, assuming users are more likely to know their intended use than specific item attributes. To facilitate this, the authors developed a multi-stage crowdsourcing protocol to create a high-quality, annotated dataset for training. They then designed and evaluated four different models (two template-based and two neural) capable of generating these usage-related questions effectively, even with limited training data, as confirmed by both automatic metrics and human evaluation.

## Technical note

The paper introduces usage-oriented preference elicitation for conversational recommender systems, generating questions about how a user intends to use an item (which users know better than specific attributes) rather than asking about attributes directly. The authors build a high-quality annotated dataset via a multi-stage crowdsourcing protocol to support training. They design and compare four question-generation models, two template-based and two neural sequence-to-sequence, and show these effectively generate usage-related questions even with limited training data, validated with both automatic metrics and human evaluation.

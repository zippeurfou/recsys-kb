## Summary

This paper proposes generating usage-related questions to elicit user preferences in conversational recommender systems, addressing the issue where users lack knowledge of item attributes. To enable this, the authors created a high-quality dataset using a multi-stage crowdsourcing protocol and developed four models—two template-based and two neural—to generate these questions. Both automatic and human evaluations confirmed the effectiveness of the models, even with limited training data, while also identifying their limitations.

## Technical note

Proposes eliciting user preferences in conversational recommenders through implicit, usage-related questions (about how/where an item is used) rather than explicit attribute questions that require domain expertise. The authors build a high-quality sentence-to-question dataset via a multi-stage crowdsourcing protocol (candidate sentence selection by POS/linguistic patterns, then question generation, validation, and paraphrase expansion), mining usage cues from item reviews. They develop four question-generation models: two template-based (n-gram patterns, optionally with an applicability classifier) and two neural text-to-text models fine-tuned from a pretrained seq2seq checkpoint (one taking heuristically selected sentences, one taking whole reviews). Automatic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, accuracy) and pointwise/pairwise human evaluation agree that the neural models outperform the strong template baseline even with limited training data.

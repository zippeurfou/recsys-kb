## Summary

This paper addresses a bandit problem where item recommendations (graph nodes) have payoffs similar to their neighbors. The key insight is that the regret grows slowly if measured against an "effective dimension" specific to the graph structure, which is typically small. The authors propose three algorithms that leverage this concept, enabling efficient learning even for large graphs, as demonstrated by experiments showing rapid preference estimation.

## Technical note

This paper studies a bandit problem where arm payoffs are smooth over an undirected graph, motivated by content-based recommendation and targeted advertising in social networks. Since a smooth graph function is a linear combination of graph-Laplacian eigenvectors with smallest eigenvalues, learning it becomes regression on those eigenvectors, and the authors introduce an 'effective dimension' that is small in real-world graphs and governs regret instead of the number of nodes. They propose three algorithms (spectral variants of LinUCB-style and Thompson-sampling approaches) whose cumulative regret scales linearly or sublinearly in the effective dimension. Experiments on content-recommendation data (e.g., Flixster) show a good estimator of user preferences over thousands of items can be learned from only tens of node evaluations.

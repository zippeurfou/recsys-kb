## Summary

DisaBench addresses the gap in evaluating disability harms in large language models, introducing a co-created taxonomy of twelve harm categories. It employs a taxonomy-driven methodology testing prompts across seven life domains, using a dataset of human-annotated responses revealing variations in harm rates and the limitations of standard safety evaluations. The framework, available open-source, aims to systematically identify and mitigate subtle disability harms previously missed by general benchmarks.

## Technical note

DisaBench is a participatory safety benchmark for disability-related LLM harms, built from a taxonomy of twelve harm categories co-created with people with disabilities and red-teaming experts. It pairs benign and adversarial prompts across seven life domains, yielding 175 prompts and 525 prompt-response pairs annotated by four evaluators with lived disability experience. Overt toxicity is rare; the dominant failure mode is stereotyping (42% of agreed-upon harmful responses), followed by harmful advice, pity, and quality-of-service degradation that toxicity classifiers miss. Findings show harm rates vary sharply by disability type, terminology-driven harm is culturally and temporally bound, and expert annotators systematically disagree in contested categories like pity, erasure, and denial of rights.

## Summary

RegionFed addresses the challenge of training effective search models across diverse, geographically distinct retail regions with different query patterns and preferences. Standard federated learning struggles with regional performance, while parameter-level personalization often fails on modern transformer models like T5. RegionFed introduces an architecture-robust framework operating entirely at the gradient level, dynamically detecting heterogeneity and applying appropriate, adaptive personalization per region without code changes. It achieves near-centralized performance (92.27%) with differential privacy, demonstrating significant effectiveness improvements compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

RegionFed is Walmart's federated-learning framework for regional retail search query understanding, motivated by cases where thongs means footwear in Australia and undergarments in the US. Its central claim is that personalization should happen at the gradient level, not the parameter level. The system is a three-layer hierarchy (global server theta, M regional coordinators theta_r, clients) where the single signal is the L2 conflict between a region's DP-noised gradient and the global gradient. That conflict diagnoses heterogeneity, routes each region to the cheapest sufficient strategy (Grad: theta_r = rho*(g_r - g); Interp: rho*(theta_r_local - theta); Meta: MAML-style rho*grad L_r(theta - eta*g)), and sets the personalization strength alpha_r = alpha_min + (1-alpha_min)*sigmoid((conflict - mu_r)/tau_r) with alpha_min = 0.5 and mu_r calibrated from the median at round 1; rho is tuned per region by golden section search, converging near 0.0344. Privacy comes from per-region clipping at C=1.0 plus Gaussian noise sigma_dp=4.0, giving epsilon approximately 0.60 at delta=1e-5 over T=50 rounds with q=0.1 under the moments accountant, and clipping conveniently normalizes the conflict metric. Evaluation spans Amazon ESCI (20K queries, 8 regions, 80 clients, Dirichlet 0.3) for three tasks (intent classification, spell correction, NER F1), Amazon Reviews, and LEAF-FEMNIST, over T5-Small, T5-3B, RoBERTa-Base and a CNN. RegionFed-Meta reaches 92.27 plus/minus 0.31 percent overall, within 1 sigma of the privacy-violating Centralized+Regional-Weighting bound (92.04 percent) and 12.09 pp above FedAvg (80.18 percent); FedProx is worse at 67.18 percent, and regional structure alone (Reg-Aware FedAvg 83.47 percent) accounts for only about a quarter of the gain. The most striking result is a negative one: SCAFFOLD, pFedMe, Ditto and APFL all collapse below 10 percent on every transformer tested and do so across E in {1,5,10,40}, while SCAFFOLD still reaches 79.52 percent on the FEMNIST CNN; controlled ablations pin the cause on T5's tied embeddings (untying recovers SCAFFOLD to 42.61 percent) and LayerNorm (freezing recovers 38.47 percent, both together 71.83 percent). RegionFed-Meta also degrades only 6.5 pp going from Dirichlet 1.0 to 0.1 versus FedAvg's 21.6 pp, and beats transformer-specific FedTP (91.54 percent) by 2.82 pp in regional robustness score while being 20 percent faster per round. Caveats: regions must be predefined (random misspecification costs 4.2 pp), the Dynamic router sometimes misroutes, and despite the retail framing all results are on public benchmarks with no Walmart production data or online deployment reported.

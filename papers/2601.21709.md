## Summary

The paper introduces TAPPA, a novel framework analyzing attention patterns in large language models (LLMs) temporally. TAPPA distinguishes predictable patterns (with clear regularities) from random ones, finding the predictability depends on the degree of query self-similarity over time. This analysis unifies previous observations and provides a mathematical basis, effectively guiding techniques like KV cache compression and model pruning to improve performance.

## Technical note

Prior observations of LLM attention patterns (retrieval heads, sink heads, diagonal traces) are fragmented and lack a unifying explanation. TAPPA analyzes attention formulations from a temporally continuous perspective, characterizing patterns as predictable (regular) or unpredictable (effectively random) based on the degree of query self-similarity over time. It gives a mathematical account of predictable patterns through the joint effect of queries, keys, and RoPE, including approximate diagonal shift-invariance. A simple q-similarity metric derived from TAPPA consistently improves KV cache compression and LLM pruning over baselines.

- High q-similarity without RoPE. In the second variant, we disable RoPE for this head by replacing the rotation matrices with identity, while keeping the original queries and keys unchanged. The attention map still exhibits a diagonal bias, reflecting the strong local similarity in the queries and keys. However, the diagonal becomes noticeably rough: it is broken into segments and is superposed with vertical streaks. This indicates that high q-similarity alone is sufficient to encourage local, near-diagonal attention, but it does not guarantee the smooth, globally shift-invariant diagonal pattern observed in the full model
- Perturbed q-dynamics with RoPE. In the third variant, we keep RoPE enabled but mildly perturb the temporal dynamics of the queries by randomly resampling their time indices within the same sequence. This reduces the average cosine similarity between consecutive queries from $0.99$ to $0.97$, while leaving the keys and RoPE parameters unchanged. The resulting attention map still contains a visible diagonal tendency, but it is now overlaid with many scattered, seemingly random activation spots.

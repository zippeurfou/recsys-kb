## Summary

1.  This meta-analysis investigates machine learning techniques for detecting cyber disinformation, finding most methods achieve impressive accuracy (over 80%).
2.  Across diverse ML approaches like SVMs, Random Forests, CNNs, and Transformers, the study reveals high effectiveness but significant variance within each model type.
3.  The results suggest no clear advantage between different classes of models for disinformation detection, recommending future research focus on replicating and developing existing effective methods at the ML level.

## Technical note

This work is a two-stage meta-analysis of ML-based cyber information influence (disinformation) detection, synthesizing 81 sampled detection techniques from the literature. It computes an overall meta-statistic for detection effectiveness and analyzes effectiveness by ML model subgroups (traditional ML like SVM/Random Forest/Naive Bayes versus deep learning like CNN/LSTM/transformers). The majority of techniques exceed 80% accuracy with a mean effectiveness of 79.18%, but subgroups show no statistically significant between-group differences and high within-group variance. The authors conclude the field is fragmented and recommend replication and ML-model-level standardized reporting.

## Summary

1.  Enterprise search struggles with acquiring large, high-quality labeled datasets for relevance. This paper proposes an alternative by fine-tuning small language models (SLMs) to act as efficient relevance labelers.
2.  The solution involves generating synthetic query-document pairs using a large language model (LLM), BM25 for retrieval, and a "teacher" LLM to assign scores, before distilling this data into a compact SLM.
3.  This distilled SLM achieves relevance labeling quality comparable to the teacher LLM, but significantly outperforms it in speed and cost-effectiveness, enabling efficient, scalable labeling for enterprise retrieval applications.

## Technical note

Addresses the lack of labeled data for enterprise-search relevance evaluation, where LLM-based labeling is accurate but too slow and costly at scale. The method synthesizes realistic enterprise queries from seed documents with an LLM, mines hard negatives via BM25, scores query-document pairs with a teacher LLM (GPT-4o), and distills these graded labels into a compact Phi-3.5-mini student labeler. The distilled SLM matches or exceeds the teacher's agreement with human judgments on a 923-pair human-annotated benchmark. It delivers roughly 17x higher throughput and 19x lower cost, enabling scalable offline ranking evaluation.

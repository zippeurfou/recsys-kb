## Summary

This paper presents a method to improve e-commerce search for superlative queries (e.g., "best", "most popular") by using LLMs to decompose them into structured "hints" representing different attributes during retrieval. This hint-based approach provides a 10.9 point improvement in MAP ranking compared to baseline methods. To address the high latency of direct LLM reranking, the method transfers the learned superlative interpretations to faster, lightweight models for efficient deployment.

## Technical note

The paper tackles e-commerce search for superlative queries (e.g. 'best') where relevance requires domain knowledge and comparison across attributes. It uses a large LLM (Claude) to decompose queries into structured 'hints' (reasoning, brands, features, reformulated queries) via chain-of-thought, generated offline/in-parallel with retrieval to avoid latency, then transfers this knowledge to lightweight pointwise re-rankers (Qwen2.5 0.5B/3B). Hint-augmentation lets small models surpass large listwise rankers, improving MAP by 10.9 points and MRR by 5.9 points over baselines. They also curate a 21k-query, 1.07M-pair superlative dataset from the Amazon Shopping Queries data.

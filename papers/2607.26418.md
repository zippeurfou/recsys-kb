## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently reranking item recommendations under fixed serving budgets by leveraging combinatorial selection principles. Existing autoregressive (AR) models capture inter-item dependencies but suffer from inherently sequential latency, while naive non-autoregressive (NAR) approaches sacrifice coordination and risk duplicates. The authors propose DIRECTOR, a novel system that maps items into a latent space to enable parallel generation of dynamic retrieval indices for all target positions simultaneously, combining the speed benefits of NAR with global dependency handling through training and inference techniques. Extensive experiments show DIRECTOR significantly outperforms strong baselines in both offline evaluations and large-scale industrial online settings.

## Technical note

DIRECTOR is a non-autoregressive, position-parallel reranker (Kuaishou) for the Generator-Evaluator paradigm that generates a matrix of request-conditioned continuous dynamic retrieval indices for all slate positions at once (via conditional VAE or conditional diffusion) instead of item-by-item AR decoding. Training uses entropy-regularized, capacity-constrained optimal transport to couple positions and expose candidate competition, while inference performs a single global hard matching (rectangular assignment) for complete duplicate-free slates; a prefix-anchored, telescoping credit-assignment scheme converts the opaque list-wise evaluator's scalar reward into position-specific signals. It beats strong AR/NAR baselines by about 2.8%-3.7% NDCG@6 on ML-1M, Amazon-Books, and RecFlow, and online at Kuaishou lifts Valid View +0.519% while cutting serving CPU by 66.7%. It targets the reranking stage only, not retrieval-plus-ranking unification.

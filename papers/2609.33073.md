## Summary

This paper explores algorithmic harms arising from incorporating generative models into recommendation systems. It argues that current harm frameworks don't fully cover these new systems and requires expansion to address updated harms and novel, *endogenous* harms specific to generative models. The authors propose an extended taxonomy and provide causal analysis to better understand and mitigate these potential negative effects.

## Technical note

This Pinterest position paper trains no model and argues that harm taxonomies built for classic ML need extending once recommender pipelines compose ML models with LLMs and VLMs in non-conversational subtasks. It formalizes users with partially observed sensitive attributes, a utility-maximizing system designer, and pull-oriented subtasks (query understanding and expansion, retrieval, ranking) versus push-oriented ones (tagging, data augmentation, summarization, captioning), then traces new causal drivers of representational and quality-of-service harms, including web-scale pretraining bias, RLHF-induced helpfulness that confidently infers unrevealed sensitive attributes, hallucinated items when corpus coverage is thin, and negative safety instructions that strip user-supplied attributes from expanded queries. It names a class of endogenous semantic-misalignment harms between inputs, designer prompts, and model priors, namely sanitization, hallucination, and superfluous pro-social injection, illustrated with GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo, and Llama-3-70B outputs such as a positive-tone captioning prompt turning a photo of used syringes into a clean-community slogan, menswear picks for a 25-year-old trader in cold-start fashion recommendation, and different travel advice for users named Ali and Michael. Proposed mitigations are separating user-supplied from output sensitive-attribute term sets via query-log analysis, harm-detector filtering, uncertainty-aware prompting, defining contested terms in prompts, screening out-of-scope inputs with a fallback to non-generative models, and judging (input, output) pairs jointly rather than outputs alone.

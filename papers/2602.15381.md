## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of automatically extracting humorous scenes from long cinematic videos, aiming to boost user engagement through better video previews and snackable content. It introduces an end-to-end system featuring novel scene segmentation using visual and textual cues, improved shot detection via guided triplet mining, and a multimodal humor tagging framework analyzing audio and text. Evaluations show the system significantly improves scene detection accuracy, achieves high F1 scores for humor detection, and successfully identifies most humorous clips from long text, demonstrating its potential for streamlining content creation workflows.

## Technical note

This paper presents an end-to-end pipeline for automatically extracting and ranking humorous scenes from long-form cinematic videos to produce snackable previews and boost streaming engagement. Scene segmentation fuses visual and textual (shot-caption) cues and groups shots into scenes using triplet loss with a guided triplet-mining strategy supervised by MovieNet-SSeg to improve intra-scene similarity and inter-scene discrimination. The shot encoder combines an X-CLIP cross-frame transformer with a DINO projection head, trained on ~80K triplets. Humor tagging uses a modified ColBERT architecture fusing audio prosody/laughter with context-punchline text understanding, plus a guardrail audio-tagging model to filter improper humor, and a heuristic humor-scoring mechanism for ranking. The system improves AP on OVSD by 18.3% and reaches F1 0.834 for humor detection, with curator evaluation confirming 87% funny-clip accuracy.

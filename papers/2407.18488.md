## Summary

Conventional conversational recommendation systems often rely on binary user feedback, limiting interpretability, and typically assume linear reward structures for contextual bandits. This paper addresses two key limitations: introducing a relative feedback approach using dueling bandits integrated with generalized linear models (GLM) to better capture nuanced user preferences during online conversations; the proposed algorithm, ConDuel, achieves strong performance gains compared to existing methods on both simulated and real-world data by directly modeling potentially non-linear reward functions. The authors further demonstrate that their framework can be extended to multinomial logit bandits with theoretical guarantees and promising experimental results.

## Technical note

The paper improves conversational recommender systems (CRS), which normally use conversational contextual bandits with explicit binary feedback on items or key-terms and assume linear rewards. It argues relative (pairwise) feedback is more informative and non-linear rewards are more realistic, and introduces ConDuel, a conversational dueling bandit algorithm operating under a generalized linear model (GLM) that queries exploratory key-term pairs and selects arm pairs via an uncertainty principle. The authors prove a sublinear regret upper bound and extend the method to multiple comparisons through the ConMNL algorithm for multinomial logit bandits. Experiments on synthetic and two real-world datasets validate ConDuel and ConMNL against baselines, including accelerated learning for cold-start users.

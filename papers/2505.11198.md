## Summary

This paper introduces a four-phase, customizable framework for music recommendations focusing on individual users' temporal listening contexts, aiming to improve both explainability and engagement.

The core methodology involves analyzing historical playback data from one Last.fm user over 15 years, representing their musical preferences using unique combinations of community tags and Spotify audio features at specific times.

By predicting audio feature preferences (like danceability) based on the relevant context of each listening moment, the framework identifies similar tracks to recommend music likely suited to the user's mood or situation.

## Technical note

Music recommendations are often black-box and hard to interpret from the listener's perspective, and generic ML approaches ignore an individual user's habits and listening context. The paper proposes a four-phase, customizable user-centric recommendation pipeline built from ~15 years (90,000+ playbacks, ~14,000 tracks) of a single Last.fm user, representing tracks by community-contributed Last.fm tags and Spotify audio features tied to temporal context (hour, day, etc.). Given the most relevant tags for a moment, a regression model predicts the preferred audio-feature values (a 'most-attractive fake track', here targeting danceability), then ranks real tracks by audio-feature similarity to that prediction. The framework is designed for explainability and engagement through inspectable per-phase steps; a fourth exploration/exploitation post-processing phase is proposed but not yet developed, and it is a proof-of-concept extensible to multiple targets and users.

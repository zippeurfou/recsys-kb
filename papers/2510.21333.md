## Summary

Current sequential recommendation systems often rely on correlations between items, which can lead to inaccurate recommendations due to spurious connections. To address this, the authors introduce CausalRec, a novel model that explicitly incorporates causal inference into the attention mechanism used for recommendations. By learning a causal graph and refining attention based on causal significance, CausalRec achieves significantly better performance, improving Hit Rate and NDCG metrics by over 7% on average compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

CausalRec integrates causal discovery into attention-based sequential recommendation to avoid spurious correlations from mere item co-occurrence (e.g., cable and phone case co-purchased only because both stem from buying a phone). A causal discovery block learns an item-level causal graph over behavior sequences by treating the transformer's final-layer representations as SCM samples and estimating structure from their covariance under a linear/equal-variance SCM, using NOTEARS-style continuous optimization with acyclicity and sparsity constraints; the authors give an identifiability theory. A CausalBooster then refines attention weights to amplify causally significant behaviors instead of filtering items out, preserving user interest. Across four datasets CausalRec improves HR and NDCG (reported ~4.7-7.2% HR, ~8.6% NDCG) over correlation-based baselines like SASRec.

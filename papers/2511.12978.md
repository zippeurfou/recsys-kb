## Summary

This paper introduces Cluster-based Concept Importance (CCI), a novel method using CLIP's own embeddings to identify and mask semantically coherent image patches, significantly improving the model's faithfulness (e.g., doubling deletion-AUC scores) and revealing errors aren't solely due to backgrounds. CCI, combined with GroundedSAM, automatically classifies CLIP predictions as foreground or background-driven, providing crucial diagnostics. The authors also present COVAR, a new benchmark systematically varying object and background conditions, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of eighteen CLIP variants to understand robustness factors.

## Technical note

The paper introduces Cluster-based Concept Importance (CCI), a training-free interpretability method for CLIP that groups patch embeddings into semantically coherent clusters via k-means, masks each cluster by attenuating its attention logits before softmax, and measures the resulting change in image-text similarity to attribute importance. CCI sets a new state of the art on faithfulness benchmarks (e.g. more than doubling deletion-AUC on MS COCO retrieval) and, combined with GroundedSAM, classifies predictions as foreground- or background-driven. They also propose COVAR, a benchmark that independently perturbs background, viewpoint, scale, and other factors, and use it to evaluate eighteen CLIP variants, showing many errors stem from fine-grained confusions rather than background reliance.

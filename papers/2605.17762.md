## Summary

1.  Amazon's music search system struggles with query variations (misspellings, transpositions) despite needing low latency, as current methods (like trigrams) are inefficient and lack semantic understanding.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces a new neural sparse retrieval system using precomputed embeddings and subword tokenization (max 3 chars), designed to learn surface-form robustness without relying on slow, explorative customer behavior feedback loops.
3.  This system achieves significantly better recall (91.4% vs 57.7%) at similar speed, demonstrating improved exploration efficiency and validating that its sparse training approach is the key driver of the performance gains.

## Technical note

This work adapts an inference-free learned sparse retrieval (SPLADE-style) architecture to industrial music search at Amazon Music, targeting robust fuzzy matching of misspelled, transposed, and phonetically varied queries under millisecond latency. It uses a domain-specific granular subword tokenization with a max 3-char token constraint to force learning of surface-form robustness over lexical memorization, and precomputes neural document expansions during offline indexing so online query encoding reduces to tokenization plus IDF weighting with effectively zero latency overhead, retaining standard inverted-index compatibility. On a 6M-document production corpus it reaches 91.4% recall@10 versus 57.7% for trigrams at comparable throughput, and simulating the High Confidence Index feedback loop shows +0.8% higher stabilized recall from improved exploration efficiency. Ablations attribute gains mainly to the sparse training pipeline, with modest domain-specific pretraining replacing large-scale general pretraining.

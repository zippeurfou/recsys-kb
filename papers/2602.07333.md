## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of creating a single, high-quality textual representation for users on large-scale platforms like LinkedIn, using data from diverse sources. The authors propose a Reinforcement Learning framework that synthesizes this representation by learning from implicit user engagement signals (like clicks and applies). This RL-based approach, which is label-free and enforces constraints, directly produces user representations compatible with LLMs and demonstrates significant improvements in downstream business metrics through extensive offline testing.

## Technical note

This LinkedIn paper casts unified member representation as a text-generation task, training a stochastic policy (a finetuned LLM) to synthesize a compact, human-interpretable textual synopsis from heterogeneous sources (profiles, professional records, search activity). It is optimized with reinforcement learning using implicit engagement signals (clicks, applies) as the primary reward via a frozen reward model, plus rule-based rewards enforcing format and length constraints, making it label-free. Optimization uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with group-relative baselines instead of PPO's learned critic, further stabilized by DAPO's asymmetric clipping and Dr. GRPO's length-bias correction. Compact text lives directly in the LLM token space, avoiding the re-embedding/index rebuild costs of dense vectors and the finetuning burden of Semantic IDs; representations are evaluated with an LLM-as-a-judge and downstream ranking metrics.

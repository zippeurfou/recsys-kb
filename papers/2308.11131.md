## Summary

This paper introduces ReLLa, a framework enhancing large language models (LLMs) for recommendation tasks. It addresses LLMs' difficulty understanding long user behavior sequences by combining retrieval methods with LLMs. ReLLa improves performance in both zero-shot (using semantic retrieval to filter test data) and few-shot settings (using retrieval for data augmentation during training), outperforming traditional models even with limited data.

## Technical note

ReLLa adapts a pure LLM for zero-shot and few-shot CTR-based recommendation and identifies the 'lifelong sequential behavior incomprehension' problem: LLMs fail to extract useful signal from long user behavior sequences (Vicuna-13B peaks at K=15) even well below the context limit. For zero-shot, it applies semantic user behavior retrieval (SUBR) to replace recently-truncated behaviors with the top-K behaviors semantically most relevant to the target item, improving input quality. For few-shot, retrieval-enhanced instruction tuning (ReiT) uses SUBR as data augmentation, training on a mix of original and retrieval-enhanced samples. On three public datasets, few-shot ReLLa with under 10% of the training data outperforms full-shot traditional CTR models such as DCNv2, DIN, and SIM.

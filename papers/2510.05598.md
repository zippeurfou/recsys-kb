Traditional recommenders often struggle with hallucinating items and under-recommending items, but miss item relationships. ## Technical note

Agent-based recommenders that use LLMs directly hallucinate non-existent items and cannot rank full catalogs, and they mostly re-model interaction signals that traditional recommenders already capture. AgentDR delegates full-ranking to traditional catalog-aware recommendation tools while the LLM (i) integrates multiple tool outputs based on personalized, per-user tool suitability stored in agent memory, and (ii) reasons over implicit substitute and complement item-item relationships grounded in the user's history. This grounding removes hallucination, scales to large catalogs, and injects relational world knowledge absent from ID-based models. On three public grocery datasets AgentDR achieves superior full-ranking performance, roughly a twofold average improvement over its underlying tools, and the paper introduces an LLM-based metric jointly measuring semantic alignment and ranking correctness.

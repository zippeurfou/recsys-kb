## Summary

This paper investigates using compact Semantic IDs (SIDs) as the sole item representation for reasoning rerankers, a departure from traditional methods using item metadata. The authors developed ReRankID, a testbed where models rank items based only on SIDs, and tested several post-training techniques to see if they could effectively elicit proper reranking behavior. They found that methods like supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning on ranking rewards significantly improve SID-only reranking performance, often outperforming models that read full text metadata, despite SIDs not being trained specifically for ranking purposes or containing behavioral signal. The best model ranks items well even with long histories, but its generated rationales often lack detailed item descriptions, suggesting post-training for reasoning itself could be beneficial.

## Technical note

Google builds ReRankID, a controlled testbed on Kuaishou short-video data with the OpenOneRec SID vocabulary, to ask whether an LLM can reason-then-rerank a supplied candidate slate when both history and candidates are rendered only as Semantic IDs (845 tokens vs 16,722 for matched captions at the 64-history/30-slate anchor). Before post-training, OpenOneRec-1.7B/8B-pro mostly copy the input order or loop (Identity 42%/21%, Loop 17%/37%) and sit far below text-reading GPT/Gemini/Claude references (NDCG@5 0.168 vs 0.256), and giving them captions does not fix this. Comparing SFT on Gemini hindsight traces, rejection-sampling FT, on-policy distillation and RLVR (GRPO with an NDCG@5 reward), all with LoRA, SFT removes the interface failures at both scales and RLVR at 8B adds a significant +0.040 to reach NDCG@5 0.277, on par with text LLMs reading about 20x longer prompts; the graded reward beats a binary Hit@1 reward by +0.045 at 8B. The 8B RLVR model beats random with zero history (+0.079, where the caption reader does not) and keeps its gains up to 256-item histories where the text reference degrades, but a Claude-judged paired rationale audit shows it wins trade-off arguments 50:0 yet loses item-level factual claims 0:66, so post-training elicits ranking from SIDs but not faithful item descriptions.

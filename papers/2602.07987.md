## Summary

Modern video recommendation systems often suffer from "familiarity bias," where popular, known content dominates recommendations, limiting user discovery. To combat this, researchers developed LAFB (Learning to Alleviate Familiarity Bias), a lightweight system designed to specifically reduce this bias in the ranking stage. LAFB models user familiarity with content and adjusts predictions to debias recommendations, leading to increased engagement with new videos and better exposure for emerging creators, as demonstrated through offline and online testing on YouTube.

## Technical note

The paper targets familiarity bias in video recommendation, where users rate familiar items higher so models over-predict for familiar content, reducing discovery and emerging-creator exposure. It proposes LAFB (Learning to Alleviate Familiarity Bias), a lightweight model-agnostic post-ranking framework that estimates a personalized debiasing factor E[score | familiarity features]—via empirical-mean bucketization for discrete features and a small MSE-trained network for continuous features—and divides the User Rating Prediction Score by it to decorrelate the score from familiarity. This adjustment flattens the score distribution and integrates without modifying the base model. Offline studies and online A/B on YouTube show reduced familiar watch-time share, higher Novel WT Share, and improved emerging-creator exposure while keeping overall watch time stable; LAFB is deployed in YouTube's post-ranking stage.

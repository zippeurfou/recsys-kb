## Summary

This paper introduces LIGE-GR, a novel framework transforming traditional itemwise recommender systems into generative ones, leveraging the LLM paradigm. It addresses the challenge of integrating sequence generation into mature recommenders by generalizing existing pointwise systems towards listwise optimization, thus maintaining compatibility and minimizing technical risk and organizational disruption. LIGE-GR was validated on social media video platforms, demonstrating small but significant improvements in user engagement metrics like time spent watching.

## Technical note

Meta's LIGE-GR upgrades a mature itemwise ranker into a listwise generative recommender in place rather than replacing the stack: it keeps the existing context-free multi-task ranking model and adds (1) a context-aware module, a 4-layer/4-head GPT-style causal transformer over the cached per-candidate representations that re-predicts the engagement heads conditioned on the items already placed in the list, (2) a listwise value model that weights each position's itemVM plus control-layer score by the predicted probability the user continues to that position, and (3) the Palette decoder, a beam search (b=6) whose Q-value adds a closed-form duration-aware future-value estimate to the accumulated list value; setting b=1, p_continue=1 and F=0 and swapping CA for CF recovers the incumbent greedy decoder exactly, giving config-level rollback and per-request latency fallback. The context-aware predictor lowers normalized entropy on every reported task (Continue -1.57%, Skip -0.74% on Instagram Reels; 15 of 17 tasks improved on Facebook Video). In seven-day A/B tests the b=1 base configuration lifted time spent +1.14% on Instagram Reels (likes +2.65%, video views +2.28%) and +0.72% on Facebook Video at roughly 10% extra inference cost relative to the context-free component and about 7% (Reels) / 2.2% (Facebook Video) added latency; on Reels the b=6 duration-aware configuration added a further +0.69% time spent, +1.82% video views and +2.93% likes over b=1 at about 20% CF-equivalent compute, and re-scoring only the top third of the roughly 100-candidate pool raised context-aware throughput 60-80%. Counterfactual paired-request logs (13,197 requests) show more topic and creator diversity (adjacent-video cosine similarity -3.68%, longest same-topic streak about -4%) with a small freshness regression (-0.66% videos under 72 hours old).

## Summary

Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to accurately understand long visual documents due to difficulty finding relevant evidence (evidence localization). To address this, the authors propose DocLens, a tool-augmented multi-agent framework that systematically narrows down from the full document to specific visual elements and relevant pages. By using a sampling-adjudication mechanism, DocLens generates reliable answers, achieving state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks and often outperforming human experts, particularly for visual-centric and unanswerable questions.

## Technical note

Tackles question answering over long visual documents where VLMs and vector retrieval fail at evidence localization (Gemini-2.5-Pro recalls only 68% of evidence pages), fueling hallucination. DocLens is a tool-augmented multi-agent framework whose Lens Module 'zooms in' with a Page Navigator (OCR-augmented page retrieval) and an Element Localizer (layout detection and cropping of tables/charts/figures), followed by a Reasoning Module that uses a sampling-adjudication process (Answer Sampler plus Adjudicator) to pick a reliable answer. Paired with Gemini-2.5-Pro it reaches 97.3% evidence-page recall and sets state of the art on MMLongBench-Doc and FinRAGBench-V, surpassing human experts and cutting hallucination especially on vision-centric and unanswerable queries.

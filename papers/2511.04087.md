## Summary

1.  E-commerce faces the challenge of accurately recommending relevant products based on user queries, which often involves leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for commonsense reasoning, but current methods are inefficient and costly due to high real-time inference and annotation needs.
2.  To address this, the authors propose E-CARE, an efficient framework that uses a "commonsense reasoning factor graph" to encode LLM reasoning patterns, allowing models to access this reasoning with just a single LLM forward pass per query during inference.
3.  Experiments show that E-CARE significantly improves recommendation accuracy (up to 12.1% gain in precision@5) while drastically boosting efficiency by eliminating the need for intensive LLM inference and associated human costs.

## Technical note

E-CARE injects LLM commonsense reasoning into e-commerce query-product recommendation without SFT, human annotation, or real-time LLM decoding at inference. A three-stage pipeline (LLM reasoning via need-utility prompts, node clustering, and LLM-uncertainty edge filtering) builds a reasoning factor graph from historical interactions; lightweight query/product adapters are then trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss over graph edges to predict factors for unseen items. At inference, cached adapter-predicted factors augment downstream bi-encoder/cross-encoder relevance models, giving LLM-level reasoning at retrieval speed. On the ESCI and WANDS benchmarks it improves search relevance Macro-F1 by up to 12.79% and app-recall Recall@5 by up to 12.1%.

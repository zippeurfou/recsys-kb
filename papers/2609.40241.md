## Summary

The paper investigates LLMs for recommendation reranking, highlighting a key tradeoff between quality and serving speed. It specifically evaluates Jev, a "System One Model," which treats reranking as a structured decision problem, comparing it to other rerankers across domains and candidate sizes. The findings show Jev maintains strong recommendation effectiveness while exhibiting much more gradual latency increase than some other LLM-based baselines, though its latency remains higher than specialized models.

## Technical note

This empirical study tests Jev, TypeSafe AI's hosted 'System One Model' that takes a decision state plus a choice question and returns typed probabilities, as a recommendation reranker, with the text of a user's 10 most recent items as the state, the candidates as the choices and the returned probabilities as ranking scores. On Amazon Reviews 2023 Movies and TV, Video Games and Books, each controlled candidate set holds the held-out next item plus K-1 top-ranked SASRec negatives (K from 20 to 200, restricted to users whose target is in SASRec's top 200), and Jev is compared with SASRec, DCNv2 and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct rerankers that score pointwise (yes/no token logits per candidate) or listwise (candidate-label logits in one pass). Jev matches or beats the best Qwen setup and degrades more slowly as K grows (Books MRR 0.346 vs 0.282 for pointwise Qwen at K=20, 0.140 vs 0.074 at K=200), while listwise Qwen is far cheaper than pointwise but collapses at large K, and the ID-based SASRec and DCNv2 answer in about 1 ms with generally lower quality. Jev's hosted-API median latency stays under roughly 1 s at K=200 against 6 to 15 s for pointwise Qwen run locally on an A800, which puts it on the empirical quality-latency frontier, though the authors note the latency comparison is not hardware-normalized and only one decision model and one 7B LLM were tested.

## Summary

This paper investigates the use of decision-oriented systems (TypeSafe Jev and OpenAI Decisions) for recommendation reranking, comparing them against standard LLM-based methods. The study finds that Jev often achieves a favorable balance between recommendation quality and response latency, performing competitively while being significantly faster. However, it concludes that specialized decision models do not inherently guarantee better recommendations or robustness, and their practical value depends heavily on specific implementation details and the application's efficiency needs.

## Technical note

The paper asks whether a decision-oriented model (TypeSafe AI's Jev, a 'System One Model' that returns typed probabilities over predefined choices instead of free text) offers a different quality-latency operating point for recommendation reranking than LLM rerankers. On Amazon Reviews 2023 (Movies and TV, Video Games, Books), SASRec retrieves candidates and hard candidate sets of K in {20,50,100,200} are built from the held-out item plus top-ranked SASRec negatives; Jev is compared with SASRec, DCNv2, and pointwise and listwise Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct rerankers on NDCG@10, HR@10, MRR and observed latency. Jev matches or exceeds the best Qwen configuration in NDCG@10 (highest on Video Games and Books), degrades gracefully with K, and its hosted-API latency stays in the low-second range at K=200 while pointwise Qwen latency grows rapidly; listwise Qwen is cheaper but drops sharply in quality at large K, and SASRec/DCNv2 remain far cheaper but less accurate. The authors stress that latency is not hardware-normalized (Jev is a hosted API) and that the result characterizes one model, not decision-oriented models in general.

## Summary

This paper argues that previous studies on long-context continual pre-training (LCCP) using small models and limited data don't adequately apply to large industrial models. To address this, the authors conducted a large-scale investigation on an 80B-parameter model, training it on 200B tokens and analyzing its learning dynamics through behavioral, probabilistic (perplexity), and mechanistic (attention) levels. Their findings show that industrial LCCP requires massive data (well beyond 150B tokens), traditional benchmarks can show "deceptive saturation" while intrinsic metrics like perplexity continue improving, and analyzing attention patterns, specifically retrieval heads, provides a reliable, low-resource way to monitor LCCP progress.

## Technical note

This paper presents the first systematic study of long-context continual pre-training (LCCP) learning dynamics on an industrial-grade model, the 80B-parameter sparse MoE Hunyuan-A13B, tracked over a 200B-token trajectory extending context from 32K to 64K. It analyzes dynamics hierarchically across behavioral (SFT probing), probabilistic (perplexity), and mechanistic (attention pattern) levels. Key findings: industrial LCCP needs massive data (saturation only past ~150B tokens, far beyond small-scale regimes); NIAH benchmarks show 'deceptive saturation' early while perplexity keeps improving and correlates better with downstream performance; and retrieval heads act as low-resource training monitors whose attention scores reliably track LCCP progress. The work delivers a monitoring framework, evaluation system, and mechanistic interpretation for industrial LCCP.

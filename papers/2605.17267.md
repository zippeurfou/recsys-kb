## Summary

Review-Augmented Generative Recommendation":

1.  Existing sequential recommendation (SR) and generative recommendation (GR) models focus solely on predicting the next item a user will interact with based on their sequence, ignoring valuable review feedback.
2.  To address this, the authors propose RAGR, a novel GR framework that incorporates review text by augmenting the user sequence with review IDs alongside item IDs, allowing reviews to directly influence item recommendations through the generative model.
3.  RAGR uses a special alignment strategy to ensure the model focuses on predicting items rather than reviews, and experiments show it significantly outperforms existing GR methods across various metrics.

## Technical note

RAGR (Review-Augmented Generative Recommendation) brings post-interaction review feedback directly into the generative recommendation sequence rather than treating reviews as auxiliary side information, arguing that item-only sequences capture what a user chose but not why. Its Review-Augmented User Sequence Modeling interleaves item semantic IDs and review semantic IDs chronologically into a mixed behavioral-semantic sequence, letting review context participate in autoregressive next-token generation via the GR unified tokenizer. To keep the recommendation objective intact, an Item-Centric Task Generation Alignment strategy based on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) teaches the model to prefer item SIDs over review SIDs at prediction positions. Experiments on three real-world datasets show consistent gains across multiple GR backbones, with ablations confirming both review augmentation and task alignment are necessary.

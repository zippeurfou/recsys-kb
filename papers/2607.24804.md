## Summary

This paper introduces Bumblebee, an innovative recommendation system architecture designed for large-scale applications. It bridges two separate development tracks—sequence modeling for personalization and feature interaction methods—which were previously not effectively combined in existing models like LightGBM or SAS Rec. Bumblebee uses stackable "blocks" that interleave sequence layers with attention-based feature crossing, allowing continuous mixing of different modalities throughout the network to improve predictive performance through enriched representations and residual connections.

## Technical note

Sequence modeling (HSTU, SIM, TWIN) and feature-interaction modeling (DHEN, DCN, DeepFM) evolved as separate tracks, so non-sequence context is injected either before or after a deep attention tower and fusion is deferred to the last layer. Bumblebee (Meta) defines a self-contained block running a three-stage micro-pipeline -- personalization (sequence reweighting: a Hadamard gate w_u over the user interaction history predicted from user features), encoding (long-sequence self-attention plus target-aware filtered attention over a pre-filtered UIH), and integration (cross-attention fusing sequence with non-sequence embeddings, then DHEN-style feature crossing) -- and stacks these blocks so each emits a joint representation and ordinary residuals become cross-modal pathways. Two engineering details matter: the reweighting module is conditioned on user-only features (candidate features bypassed and aggregated per user) to avoid train/serve candidate-count and distribution mismatch and to keep item scores independent, and sequence lengths are shape-bucketed (32, 64, ..., 4096) to cut compiled kernels from O(N) to O(10). Trained on 81B samples (155.2M users and 50.8M items per hourly timestep over 72 timesteps) at 3.804T parameters on 64 B200 GPUs, Bumblebee beats DLRM by -1.387% consumption NE, -1.002% engagement NE and -1.005% RMSE, versus -0.66%/-0.46% for SIM and -0.69%/-0.40% for HSTU. Component ablation puts the integration stage on top -- feature crossing +0.36% NE, cross-attention +0.31%, cross-module residual flow +0.25% (zero extra parameters) -- ahead of sequence reweighting and target attention (+0.15% each) and self-attention (+0.05%); a parameter- and FLOP-matched 'Sequential' megablock with identical components is 0.20% NE worse, isolating interleaving itself as the driver, at the cost of 7-10% lower training throughput.

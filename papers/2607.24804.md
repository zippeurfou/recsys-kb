## Summary

Recommendation systems have evolved significantly, facing limitations from separating sequence modeling approaches (like sequential personalization) and feature interaction methods.

This paper introduces **Bumblebee**, an innovative architecture designed to bridge this gap by combining both directions into interleaved building blocks called "bumblebees".

Each bumblebee block integrates multiple techniques—sequence processing, attention mechanisms, and feature crossing—and produces a unified representation from different data modalities. These representations are passed between blocks via residual connections, allowing repeated mixing of information types.

The architecture improves performance consistently across various tasks on large-scale industrial datasets by promoting early and continuous modality interaction compared to deep stacking alone. The paper demonstrates that its interleaved design is the key factor driving these improvements for future recommendation systems.

## Technical note

Bumblebee is an industrial ranking architecture that interleaves sequence modeling and feature interaction (historically two separate tracks: HSTU/SIM/TWIN vs DHEN/DCN/DeepFM) into stackable self-contained blocks, each a micro-pipeline of user-context sequence reweighting, HSTU self-attention over user interaction history, SIM-style filtered target attention, pooled multi-head cross-attention (PMA), and DHEN feature crossing, so every block emits a joint sequence-plus-feature representation. Interleaving turns ordinary residual connections into zero-parameter cross-module pathways (+0.25% NE) and blocks can be specialized (full/lite/long-sequence). On an 81B-sample industrial dataset (3.8T params, B200 GPUs) it beats DLRM, SIM, and HSTU across 26 classification and 4 regression tasks (e.g. -1.39% consumption NE, -1.0% engagement NE vs best baseline) at about 7-10% lower training throughput; a same-component Sequential megablock is 0.20% NE worse, isolating interleaving as the driver. It is a ranking model, not a unified retrieval-plus-ranking pipeline.

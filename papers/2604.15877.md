## Summary

This paper introduces the "Experience Compression Spectrum," a framework positioning LLM agent experience (memory, skills, rules) along a continuum of increasing compression to manage long-term interactions more efficiently. Current systems operate at fixed, low compression levels, lacking the ability to adaptively use higher compression techniques (the "missing diagonal"), and the authors identify several interconnected challenges specific to each compression level. The paper highlights the need for specialized, full-spectrum agent systems that address the entire knowledge lifecycle across compression levels, proposing design principles and identifying key open research problems.

## Technical note

This position paper proposes the Experience Compression Spectrum, unifying LLM-agent memory, skills, and rules as points on a single axis of increasing compression (5-20x episodic memory, 50-500x procedural skills, 1000x+ declarative rules) that reduces context consumption, retrieval latency, and compute. A citation analysis of 1,136 references across 22 papers shows a cross-community citation rate below 1%, evidencing that the memory and skill communities independently solve shared sub-problems. Mapping 20+ systems reveals every system operates at a fixed compression level with none supporting adaptive cross-level compression, a gap termed the 'missing diagonal'. It draws on cognitive-science analogs (Complementary Learning Systems, ACT-R, Fitts & Posner) and argues for full-spectrum, bidirectional, lifecycle-aware agent learning at the scaffold level.

## Summary

Large transformer models demonstrate impressive few-shot performance on unseen tasks, but decentralized policy deployment in cooperative Multi-Agent RL often struggles with coordination and reward assignment mismatches. To address this, the paper introduces MAICC, which trains a centralized embedding model to represent fine-grained trajectories and approximates it in decentralized agents. MAICC retrieves relevant past trajectories as context for decision-making and uses a hybrid utility score for team credit assignment, enabling faster adaptation to unseen tasks in benchmarks like SMAC and LBF.

## Technical note

Tackles fast adaptation in cooperative multi-agent RL under Dec-POMDPs, where decentralized execution causes partial observability and credit-assignment issues that break single-agent in-context RL methods. MAICC trains a centralized trajectory embedding model that distills team-level information (via KL) into decentralized embedding models, which retrieve relevant multi-agent trajectories (via MIPS/cosine similarity) as in-context examples for a shared causal-transformer decision model. A selective memory mechanism with exponential time decay balances offline data and an online replay buffer, and a hybrid utility score combining global and predicted individual returns improves credit assignment and mitigates the lazy-agent problem. On LBF and SMAC v1/v2 benchmarks it adapts to unseen tasks faster than ICRL and multi-task MARL baselines, with a regret bound provided.

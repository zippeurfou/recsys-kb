## Summary

1.  Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) often suffers from coordination problems due to decentralized policies, leading to inefficient adaptation and poor task alignment.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces MAICC, which trains a centralized embedding model to represent trajectories and uses decentralized models to approximate this. These models retrieve relevant past trajectories as context and combine them with the agents' current experiences to inform decisions, while also using a memory mechanism and hybrid utility score for better coordination and credit assignment.
3.  Extensive experiments on standard MARL benchmarks show that MAICC significantly improves adaptation speed compared to existing methods, enabling faster coordination on unseen tasks.

## Technical note

MAICC (Multi-Agent In-Context Coordination via Decentralized Memory Retrieval) brings in-context reinforcement learning to cooperative multi-agent RL under Dec-POMDPs, targeting fast adaptation to unseen tasks without parameter updates. It trains a centralized embedding model to capture fine-grained trajectory representations and distills it into decentralized embedding models that retrieve relevant past trajectories as in-context examples for each agent. A test-time memory mechanism balances an online replay buffer against offline multi-task data, and a hybrid utility score combining individual- and team-level returns addresses credit assignment and the lazy-agent problem. On LBF and SMAC v1/v2 benchmarks, MAICC adapts to unseen cooperative tasks significantly faster than prior ICRL and multi-task MARL baselines.

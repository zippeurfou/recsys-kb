## Summary

Structured skills enhance exploration in agent RL, but current methods struggle to autonomously internalize these skills. The paper introduces SKILLC (SkillC), which converts the contrast between skill-injected and skill-free rollouts into a direct learning signal during policy updates. Experiments show SKILLC significantly outperforms existing internalization methods and remains competitive with skill-augmented approaches, demonstrating effective autonomous skill learning without relying on external skills during training.

## Technical note

SkillC targets skill internalization in long-horizon agentic RL, where structured skill prompts are used only during training and withdrawn so the final policy acts autonomously. It identifies internalization blindness: under standard GRPO all rollouts for a task share one prompt condition, so the gradient cannot distinguish skill-dependent from autonomous success. Its Contrastive Skill Credit Assignment (CSCA) samples paired skill-injected and skill-free rollouts within the same policy update and injects their task-level contrast via a dual-stream advantage estimator that preserves global ranking while applying a one-sided correction toward skill-free success. A smoothed validation-level signal drives an adaptive curriculum over attribution strength, rollout allocation, and monotonic active-set pruning. On ALFWorld and WebShop, SkillC beats the strongest skill-internalization baseline by 5.5% and 4.4% without runtime skill access, though gains are signal-quality dependent (strong contrasts yield up to +33.4% on Pick2, weak ceiling-effect tasks regress).

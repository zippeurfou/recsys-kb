## Summary

This paper introduces a method to improve generative recommendation models by directly incorporating structured human knowledge ("human priors" like item taxonomies) into their end-to-end training. The approach uses lightweight adapter heads conditioned on these priors to guide the model, helping it disentangle user intent along understandable axes (e.g., interaction types) and model complex interactions between different prior types. Experiments show that this backbone-agnostic framework significantly enhances recommendation accuracy and beyond-accuracy objectives like diversity, and allows models to better utilize longer context and larger sizes.

## Technical note

This paper integrates structured human priors (item taxonomies, temporal patterns, behavioral and graph signals) directly into end-to-end training of generative recommenders instead of applying them as decoupled post-hoc ranking adjustments. It adds lightweight, prior-conditioned adapter heads inspired by efficient LLM multi-head decoding that guide the backbone to disentangle multi-faceted user intent along human-understandable axes (interaction types, long- vs short-term interest). A hierarchical composition strategy models complex interactions across different prior types, and the framework is backbone-agnostic (works with HSTU, HLLM). Experiments on three large-scale datasets show gains in both accuracy and beyond-accuracy objectives like diversity and personalization, and better utilization of longer context and larger model sizes.

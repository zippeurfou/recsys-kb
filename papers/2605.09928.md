## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of balancing high compression ratios with the speed and resource efficiency needed for industrial data compression systems.
2.  It introduces OpenZL, which uses a novel "graph model" of compression (a directed acyclic graph of modular codecs) and provides a self-describing format with a universal decoder, enabling rapid development of application-specific compressors.
3.  OpenZL achieves superior compression ratios and speeds compared to standard general-purpose compressors and is significantly faster than ratio-focused deep-learning methods, while also drastically reducing development timelines for internal deployments.

## Technical note

OpenZL frames compression as a directed acyclic graph of modular codecs (the graph model), producing a self-describing wire format that any configuration of a single universal decoder can decompress. This lets teams build application-specific compressors with minimal code and no deployment lag, cutting development from months to days while keeping maintenance low via a limited standard component set. Experiments show OpenZL beats state-of-the-art general-purpose compressors (LZ77/Zstd variants) on ratio and speed across real datasets, and is competitive on ratio with deep-learning compressors while being orders of magnitude faster. Meta's internal deployments report consistent size and/or speed gains; the paper is off-topic for recommendation beyond generic data/ML-pipeline compression.

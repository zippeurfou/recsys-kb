## Summary

Perch 2.0, a large-scale supervised bioacoustics model, lacks marine mammal data in its training despite having state-of-the-art performance on other benchmarks. To address this, the authors evaluated its ability to perform few-shot transfer learning for marine mammal and underwater audio classification tasks. Their results show that Perch 2.0 embeddings significantly outperformed other pretrained models across multiple underwater tasks, making it highly recommended for developing linear classifiers with minimal labeled data.

## Technical note

The paper evaluates whether Perch 2.0, a supervised bird-centric bioacoustics foundation model (EfficientNet-B3 trained on log-mel spectrograms with self-distillation and a source-prediction self-supervised loss), transfers to marine mammal and underwater audio despite containing almost no marine training data. Using linear probing on frozen embeddings in a k-shot (k in 4-32) logistic-regression setup, it benchmarks Perch 2.0 against multispecies whale, Perch 1.0, SurfPerch, AVES-bio, BirdAVES, and BirdNET on NOAA PIPAN, ReefSet, and DCLDE 2026 tasks. Perch 2.0 embeddings consistently outperform alternatives on most few-shot tasks measured by one-vs-all ROC-AUC, making them recommended for building marine classifiers via agile-modeling and vector-search workflows.

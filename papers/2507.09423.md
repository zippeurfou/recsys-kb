## Summary

This paper addresses the item cold-start problem in recommender systems (RS), where new items struggle for visibility due to RS being primarily user-centric. The authors propose shifting focus to an "item-centric" approach, identifying optimal users for new items rather than finding the best item for existing users. They introduce a Bayesian model within an exploration system that predicts user satisfaction and assesses item quality balance, leading to significantly improved cold-start targeting and exploration efficiency in online evaluations.

## Technical note

The paper reframes item cold-start as an item-centric problem (finding the best users for a new item) rather than the usual user-centric one, arguing user-centric systems mis-target nascent content in large item spaces. It adds a lightweight filter after ranking that models each item's intrinsic satisfaction rate with a Beta posterior (conjugate to Bernoulli) and removes an item from a user's candidate list when the predicted user-item satisfaction falls more than two posterior standard deviations below the item's mean. Deployed in a large short-video exploration system, a user-corpus co-diverted experiment shows large satisfaction gains, a 20% reduction in exploration impressions, and a 10% larger recommendable corpus.

## Summary

Current LLM-based information-seeking agents excel on standard benchmarks but critically rely on search-engine-indexed knowledge, ignoring the vital but unindexed information problem (UIS).

To study this gap, the authors introduce the UIS-QA benchmark, showing that even top agents perform drastically worse on unindexed queries than on indexed ones, highlighting the severity of the UIS challenge.

They then propose UIS-Digger, a multi-agent system designed to actively browse and parse unindexed sources. Despite using a relatively small model, UIS-Digger significantly outperforms larger models on the new benchmark, demonstrating the feasibility and importance of directly interacting with unindexed information for robust agents.

## Technical note

The paper identifies Unindexed Information Seeking (UIS), where vital content is not captured by search-engine crawlers (dynamic pages, embedded files, overlooked content), and shows state-of-the-art agents drop from ~70 on GAIA to ~24.55 on the new UIS-QA benchmark of 110 expert-annotated QA pairs. UIS-Digger is a multi-agent system with a dual-mode browser that switches between visual (screenshot) and textual modes, plus file readers and parallel tool execution to actively parse unindexed sources. Its ~30B backbone is trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for cold start followed by rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) to bootstrap UIS capability. It reaches 27.27% on UIS-QA, surpassing systems built on larger models such as O3 and GPT-4.1. The work formalizes indexed vs. unindexed information as a set-difference over crawlable pages.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of unreliable visual embeddings for e-commerce item recommendations, caused by cluttered product images containing promotional overlays. The proposed solution, Text-Guided Q-Former (TGQ-Former), uses structured metadata as guidance to selectively extract relevant visual information while ignoring spurious cues. Experiments show that TGQ-Former significantly improves retrieval robustness, achieving a 6.04% average increase in Hit Rate@100 compared to strong baselines on real-world datasets.

## Technical note

TGQ-Former (Text-Guided Q-Former) is a multimodal representation-learning framework for e-commerce item-to-item retrieval that uses structured metadata to steer visual token extraction, countering spurious cues from promotional overlays in poster-style product images. Its Hybrid-Query Connector splits queries into metadata-anchored semantic queries that focus on product-relevant regions and exploratory learnable queries that capture complementary visual evidence beyond the text. A lightweight reliability-aware Dual-Gated Vector Modulation calibrates the two streams, gating the metadata-anchored stream by an image-metadata agreement signal and the exploratory stream by an item-specific visual reliability signal, while a Redundancy-Reduction Regularizer keeps the streams complementary under a contrastive retrieval objective. On large real-world datasets with full-pool retrieval it improves Hit Rate@100 by 6.04% on average over strong connector baselines and end-to-end MLLMs.

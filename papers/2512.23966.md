## Summary

Researchers introduced LongCat ZigZag Attention (LoZA), a sparse attention mechanism designed to significantly speed up existing full-attention models, especially those handling very long input sequences (like processing up to 1 million tokens). LoZA achieves this efficiency improvement with minimal additional computational cost, benefiting both the initial processing phase and the generation phase of language models. By integrating LoZA into the LongCat-Flash model during training, they created LongCat-Flash-Exp, demonstrating LoZA's ability to enable efficient long-term reasoning and complex tasks involving long input sequences.

## Technical note

LongCat ZigZag Attention (LoZA) is a scheme to convert existing full-attention LLMs into sparse versions cheaply during mid-training, targeting long-context prefill (RAG) and decode (tool-integrated reasoning) speedups. It attaches a learnable per-layer factor to each Multi-head Latent Attention module, calibrates by freezing all other weights to rank layer importance, then sparsifies the lowest-ranked 50% of layers into streaming sparse attention (sink plus local blocks) and trains to close the gap, mirroring the lottery-ticket rewind procedure. Layer-level (not head-level) sparsity is chosen for balanced compute across ranks and simpler kernels. Applied to LongCat-Flash with YaRN, the resulting LongCat-Flash-Exp handles 1M tokens, preserves quality, and achieves >50% prefill speedup and ~90% decode-kernel cost reduction at 128K.

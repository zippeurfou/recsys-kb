## Summary

This paper compares generative retrieval (GR) and dense retrieval (DR) for candidate generation in online retail. While GR generally provides better *best relevant item value*, the study finds that popularity is a major factor driving this advantage. Crucially, adding a static item value prior to a DR reranking step reverses GR's advantage while maintaining its recall, suggesting a method to balance their performance.

## Technical note

Shopify revisits generative retrieval (GR, semantic-ID decoding with constrained beam search) versus dense retrieval (DR, cosine over the same jointly trained T5 encoder in the TIGER/LIGER setup) as candidate generators for e-commerce sequential recommendation on Amazon Toys 2014/2023 and an internal dataset. DR wins exact-target Recall@50 (0.228 vs 0.167 on 2014) and value-weighted vNDCG@50, while GR wins conditional best-relevant-item value (BV); decomposing GR's BV lead shows popularity accounts for 49.8% (2014) and 65.4% (2023) of it. A training-free static item-value prior (popularity, Wilson rating and brand percentiles, alpha=0.5 blend) applied as a single rerank of DR's top-200 flips the BV gap to +0.023/+0.006 in DR's favor, gives the best vNDCG@50 (0.240/0.175) and keeps Recall@50 above GR. GR retrieves fewer cold targets (0 of 296 vs 47 for DR), co-purchase substitute credit does not establish a GR edge beyond a random-credit control, and on an industrial set with a human-calibrated LLM judge a 14B Qwen3 SID-LLM scores NDCG@50 0.453 vs 0.474 for a 47x smaller GTE dense encoder and 0.530 for DR+prior.

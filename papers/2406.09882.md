## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of recommending content while preventing users from consuming harmful material, considering how user interests evolve over time. The authors develop models and algorithms to find optimal recommendation policies that balance maximizing click-through rates (CTR) with mitigating harm. Their experiments show these policies successfully achieve both goals, outperforming standard baselines in a realistic movie recommendation setting.

## Technical note

Models the interplay between recommendations, evolving user interests, and consumption of harmful (off-platform) content, seeking policies that trade off maximizing CTR against mitigating harm under a multinomial-logit choice model. A key theoretical result is that with static preferences top-k maximizes CTR while simultaneously minimizing harm, so harm mitigation only becomes meaningful when user preference (attraction) dynamics are modeled; the authors further prove that naively penalizing harm without dynamics has no effect and that alternating optimization can be arbitrarily suboptimal. They characterize the steady-state user profile via the Banach fixed-point theorem and compute policy gradients through the implicit function theorem since the objective has no closed form. On a semi-synthetic MovieLens setting with IMDB-based harmful labels, the gradient-based policies outperform baselines at jointly maximizing CTR and reducing harm, by up to 77%.

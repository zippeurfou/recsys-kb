## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of providing effective recommendations in business, focusing on link prediction within user-item graphs. To overcome limitations faced by traditional holistic graph learning methods as data volume grows, it introduces MCGCL (Multi-label Classification Graph Contrastive Learning), which combines a standard graph task with an auxiliary subtask designed to learn homogeneous user-user or item-item relationships specifically via contrastive learning. The model's effectiveness was validated through experiments on real Amazon Reviews datasets for multi-label classification tasks against state-of-the-art methods.

## Technical note

Bipartite-graph recommenders framed as link prediction struggle with data sparsity and with multi-label (rather than binary) interactions when forming sub-view relationships. MCGCL applies graph contrastive learning in a two-task, three-stage scheme: a main task learns representations over the holistic user-item bipartite graph, and a subtask constructs homogeneous user-user and item-item subgraphs from those representations to capture hard samples, before a final integration stage merges both views. Graphs are augmented by edge addition/removal. On Amazon Reviews it outperforms state-of-the-art methods on both multi-label and binary-label classification.

## Summary

This study investigates whether Large Language Models (LLMs) actively use internal confidence estimates to regulate their behavior. Through a four-phase abstention paradigm, it demonstrates that LLMs implicitly use confidence thresholds to decide when to answer or abstain, with confidence being a dominant predictor far outweighing other factors. Causal evidence confirms that manipulating internal confidence signals directly shifts abstention rates, showing models can systematically control their responses based on these estimates, mirroring biological metacognition and essential for developing reliable autonomous agents.

## Technical note

This paper provides causal evidence that LLMs use internal confidence signals to decide whether to answer or abstain, via a four-phase multiple-choice paradigm on a 4-way SimpleQA variant across GPT-4o, Gemma 3 27B, DeepSeek-V3, and Qwen3-Next-80B. Phase 1 measures temperature-scaled log-probability confidence and separately elicited verbal confidence; Phase 2 fits a logistic model recovering an implicit threshold and policy temperature governing abstention. Phase 3 uses activation steering of high/low-confidence residual-stream vectors in Gemma 3, with parallel mediation analysis showing confidence redistribution (not policy shift) is the primary mechanism. Verbal confidence independently predicts abstention despite lower AUROC discrimination, and activation decoding at the pre-answer token indicates both observable measures are lossy readouts of a richer multidimensional internal confidence representation.

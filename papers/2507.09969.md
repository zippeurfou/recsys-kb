## Summary

This paper addresses the high computational cost of using graph-based re-ranking in large-scale production recommendation systems. The authors propose a non-parametric strategy that performs graph convolution for efficient item ranking solely at test time. This approach leverages structural knowledge from user-item interaction graphs to improve ranking quality significantly (8.1% average) while adding minimal extra overhead during inference.

## Technical note

The paper studies how to inject user-item graph knowledge into the ranking (CTR) stage without the prohibitive message-passing cost of training a graph encoder. It first shows a graph encoder substituted into DCNv2 improves ranking but adds ~480-1260% training/testing overhead. The proposed non-parametric graph convolution operates only at test time: build normalized user-user and item-item co-interaction similarity matrices, retrieve top-n_k similar users/items, query the trained ranker on the n_k^2 constructed pairs, and aggregate predictions with a pairwise-adjusted weighting. Across seven CTR baselines and four benchmarks it yields ~8.1% average NDCG/Recall gains with ~0.5% extra compute, with larger gains on sparse datasets.

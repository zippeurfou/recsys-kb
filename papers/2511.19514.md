## Summary

Current LLM-based recommender systems face two main issues: they lack automated discovery of effective reasoning patterns and inefficiently transfer reasoning structure, either costing too much or losing step-by-step logic. To address these, the paper introduces SCoTER, a unified framework combining automated pattern discovery (using GVM) with structure-preserving transfer (using a specialized architecture). SCoTER effectively transfers complex LLM reasoning chains into efficient models, validated by significant performance improvements (up to 11.59%) and real-world deployment showing a 2.14% GMV lift while eliminating online LLM inference costs.

## Technical note

SCoTER transfers structured LLM chain-of-thought reasoning into efficient recommenders while eliminating online LLM inference. Its Generate-Validate-Mine (GVM) pipeline automatically discovers effective reasoning patterns: an LLM generates diverse reasoning chains, chains are validated by Recall@20 via semantic retrieval to the item catalog, and K-means clustering plus LLM synthesis mines an optimal CoT template. A teacher-student structured distillation produces stepwise reasoning embedding matrices stored offline, then an order-preserving fusion adapter integrates them into a backbone without collapsing the stepwise structure. It improves four benchmarks (up to 11.59%) and delivered a 2.14% GMV lift on Tencent's advertising platform.

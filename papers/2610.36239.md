## Summary

This paper introduces BehaveRec, a recommendation model that integrates three distinct signals: recent session interactions, long-term behavioral history (up to 90 days), and community signals derived from user-user graphs. The authors empirically investigate *when* incorporating this richer history beyond short-term sessions actually improves recommendation performance, finding a clear dose-response relationship. They conclude that long-term history benefits are significant mainly for users with rich interaction histories, providing a practical deployment threshold due to its efficiency despite combining complex signals.

## Technical note

Session-only sequential recommenders give identical predictions to users with identical short sessions; BehaveRec (Walmart) asks when 90-day history and community signals actually help, predicting the next of 2,371 LLM-derived intent clusters. It fuses a 4-layer causal session transformer, a bidirectional User History Encoder with a [USER] token over up to 100 history events, and a GraphSAGE aggregation over a user-user graph whose top-20 neighbors come from IVF ANN search on cosine similarity of the learned history embeddings (not item co-occurrence), via a learned ThreeWayGate, trained with label-smoothed cross-entropy plus 0.1x InfoNCE session-history alignment; the model has 5.67M parameters, trains on one A100 in under two days and adds <3ms P99 online. On 137M sequences from 12.6M users it reaches R@5 0.7201 vs 0.6975 for SASRec (+3.2%, 95% CI [0.0219,0.0233]), with the history encoder providing 83% of the gain, the graph +0.0038, and a larger SASRec (6.46M params) still 2.5% behind. Stratifying by 90-day history length shows a dose-response curve: -0.002 R@5 for users with 1-5 events, parity at 6-10, +0.004 at 11-20 and +0.017 at 101+, yielding a practical ~20-interaction deployment threshold; on sparse-history Amazon CDs/Electronics (avg 14-17 events) SASRec remains better, and full item-level MovieLens is mixed.

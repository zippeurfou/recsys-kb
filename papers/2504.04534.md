## Summary

This study provides a multi-dimensional empirical comparison of 17 large language models on text summarization tasks across seven diverse datasets (PubMed, BillSum, etc.). It evaluates performance using metrics like factual consistency, semantic similarity, and human-like quality at different output lengths. The findings reveal significant differences between models and datasets, identifying trade-offs between accuracy/factualness (better with shorter outputs) and perceived quality/fluency (better with longer outputs), while also highlighting efficiency considerations for practical applications.

## Technical note

The paper benchmarks 17 commercial and open-source LLMs on text summarization across seven datasets and three output lengths (50/100/150 tokens) using a weighted multi-dimensional framework combining factual consistency (SummaC), semantic similarity (BERTScore), lexical overlap (ROUGE), and LLM-based human-like judgments plus efficiency and cost. It finds a sharp tension between factual consistency (best at 50 tokens) and perceived quality (best at 150 tokens), with deepseek-v3 dominating factual consistency (SummaC 0.68) but slow, and Gemini flash models leading efficiency/cost. Technical domains (BigPatent, PubMed) yield much lower factual consistency than conversational data (SAMSum). gpt-3.5-turbo has the best balanced weighted rank overall.

## Summary

Live streaming platforms face challenges with existing recommendation algorithms being computationally limited (low FLOPs) and struggling to capture dynamic content changes or incorporate user-streamer interaction signals.

To overcome these limitations, researchers introduced SSRLive, which uses generative techniques guided by **dynamic semantic IDs (SIDs)** instead of static ones, better representing the live room's changing nature through an encoder-decoder design leveraging multimodal information.

SSRLive combines a generative module for dynamic SIDs with a discriminative module that integrates these SIDs and user features/interactions to perform multi-task predictions. Online tests showed significant improvements in watch time (+3.38%), GMV (+0.72%), follower growth (+3.12%), and interaction volume (+2.92%) compared to existing static SID-based algorithms, demonstrating its effectiveness and business value through real-world deployment serving hundreds of millions of users.

## Technical note

SSRLive is a hybrid generative-discriminative architecture for live-streaming recommendation that addresses two limits of applying generative recsys to live content: static semantic IDs cannot reflect rapidly changing live-room content, and generative pipelines omit user-streamer interaction signals. The generative module uses an encoder-decoder to produce both static SIDs (from the streamer's static multimodal information) and dynamic SIDs (reflecting real-time live-room changes), leveraging multimodal information. The discriminative module treats SIDs as auxiliary information rather than for retrieval, using per-task learnable queries to extract task-specific representations from SIDs and user features, fuses them with live-room and cross features, and runs multi-task prediction. Motivated by low FLOPs/MFU of conventional DLRMs, SSRLive is deployed to hundreds of millions of users with online A/B gains of +3.38% watch time, +0.72% GMV, +3.12% follower growth, and +2.92% interaction volume.

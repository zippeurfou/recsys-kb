## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Recent developments in Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) allow large language models (LLMs) to function as implicit ranking models by maximizing the margin between preferred and non-preferred responses. In practice, user feedback on such lists typically involves identifying a few relevant items in context rather than providing detailed pairwise comparisons for every possible item pair. ## Technical note

IRPO (In-context Ranking Preference Optimization) extends DPO to directly optimize LLMs from in-context ranked lists where users select a few relevant items rather than exhaustive pairwise comparisons. It uses a Plackett-Luce-inspired positional preference model capturing both item relevance and position, and introduces a differentiable objective via positional aggregation of pairwise item preferences to optimize discrete, non-differentiable ranking metrics. Theoretical analysis shows IRPO automatically emphasizes items with greater model-vs-reference disagreement and links its gradient to an unbiased, reduced-variance importance-sampling estimator; empirically it outperforms standard DPO on ranking performance.

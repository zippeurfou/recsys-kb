## Summary

This paper argues that Information Retrieval is evolving towards autonomous agentic workflows involving multi-step Reason-Act-Observe loops. It identifies that errors in these systems can cascade, leading to unreliable outcomes despite fluent endpoint responses, a phenomenon termed "deceptive fluency." To achieve reliable agentic systems, the authors propose focusing on the integrity of the entire process (trajectory) and implementing verification gates and systematic abstention under uncertainty, rather than just endpoint accuracy.

## Technical note

This position paper argues that IR is shifting from document ranking to autonomous agentic workflows in multi-step Reason-Act-Observe loops, where minor early errors cascade (the Snowball Effect) and linguistic fluency masks functional misalignment (the 'Fluency Trap'). It presents a process-oriented taxonomy of agentic hallucinations across four stages (planning, retrieval, reasoning, execution/API synthesis). It advocates measuring trajectory integrity and causal attribution over endpoint accuracy, proposing per-interaction-unit (c,a,o) verification gates: solvability classifiers at planning, stepwise attribution at reasoning, and state-diff/provenance dry-runs at execution. Proposed reliability metrics include First-Error Position, abstention precision/recall, Rollback Recovery Rate, and weakest-link reliability, with calibrated cost-sensitive abstention and reliability-aligned RL reward (e.g., SPA-RL credit for justified abstention).

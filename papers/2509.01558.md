## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Uplift modeling is a key technique for promotion optimization in recommender systems, but standard methods typically fail to account for interference, where treating one item affects the outcomes of others. This violation of the Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption (SUTVA) leads to suboptimal policies in real-world marketplaces. ## Technical note

Booking bridges evaluation to learning for uplift modeling under clustered network interference (SUTVA violations from cannibalization) in marketplace promotions. They repurpose the Additive Inverse Propensity Weighting (AddIPW) estimator, whose weights scale linearly rather than exponentially with cluster size, as a differentiable gradient-based learning objective and integrate it with response-transformation techniques to directly optimize economic outcomes like incremental profit. Simulations show the interference-aware approach significantly outperforms interference-naive methods, with gains growing as interference strengthens, and that profit-centric strategies best identify high-impact interventions.

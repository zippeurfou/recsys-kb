## Summary

Traditional recommendation systems using Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with effectively incorporating negative samples to optimize user preferences due to high computational costs from large numbers of negatives and ignoring their varying informativeness. To overcome these challenges, researchers propose NAPO (\textbf{N}egative-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{P}reference \textbf{O}ptimization), which introduces two key techniques: expanding the negative sample pool with "in-batch negative sharing" and adjusting model updates based on negative confidence via "dynamic reward margin adjustment". Extensive experiments show that NAPO significantly improves recommendation accuracy while effectively reducing popularity bias compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

NAPO improves preference optimization for LLM-based recommenders, where decoding many negatives per prompt is costly and fixed reward margins ignore negative informativeness. Built on SimPO (reference-free DPO), it introduces in-batch negative sharing that reuses computed log-probabilities across sequences (filtered by SASRec-based sequence-embedding similarity) to expand the negative pool without extra memory. It also adds a dynamic reward margin that scales each negative's update by a lightweight sequential recommender's confidence, amplifying reliable negatives and softening uncertain ones. On Goodreads, LastFM, and Steam it improves recommendation accuracy by ~13% while reducing popularity bias, without increasing compute.

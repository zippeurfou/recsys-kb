## Summary

This paper introduces C-MIG, a new retrieval-augmented generation framework for clinical diagnosis reasoning using large language models. It addresses limitations of existing methods by using an information gain-based approach estimated from two complementary views (retrieved-document and document-refinement) to guide both retrieval and refinement, overcoming issues with binary exact-match rewards. C-MIG also features an improved multi-subquery retrieval strategy and demonstrates superior performance on medical benchmarks compared to other RAG-RL methods and state-of-the-art general LLMs for clinical tasks.

## Technical note

C-MIG is a multi-view information-gain retrieval-augmented generation framework for clinical diagnosis reasoning that replaces brittle exact-match binary rewards used in RAG-RL. It formulates information gain as a unified process reward from two complementary views—the incremental benefit of newly retrieved documents over the prior turn (what to retrieve) and the contribution of the refined summary over the raw query (how to refine)—computed under a frozen reference model to eliminate reward drift from policy distribution shift. Rewards are shaped via truncated-tanh (soft) and batch-rank (hard) mappings over multi-turn think-search-evidence-refine-diagnosis rollouts, and a multi-subquery retrieval strategy decomposes colloquial patient inquiries into standardized subqueries to improve recall. The mechanism needs no additional reward model and adds minimal overhead. On four medical benchmarks C-MIG achieves the best in-domain and out-of-domain performance among RAG-RL methods, surpassing the concurrent IGPO by 6.71 points in-domain and beating state-of-the-art general LLMs on clinical diagnosis.

## Summary

Current regulatory frameworks designed for predictive AI often assume outdated constraints that don't fully apply when governing generative AI systems like GANs or large language models (LLMs). The paper identifies four unique aspects of generative AI—its generality, evaluation difficulties, new legal concerns, and distributed value chain—that require distinct policy approaches compared to predictive AI. Consequently, the authors recommend specific regulatory adjustments for policymakers to effectively manage these novel AI systems while leveraging existing frameworks where possible.

## Technical note

This is an AI-policy position paper, not a recsys method paper. It argues that regulatory tools built for task-specific/predictive AI encode assumptions (identifiable datasets, well-defined tasks, single regulatory targets) that break down for general-purpose/generative AI. It identifies four distinguishing aspects of GPAI: generality/adaptability, evaluation difficulty, new legal concerns shifting stakeholders, and a distributed value chain. It recommends regulating use cases and impact rather than technical specs, using technical proxies sparingly, and governing the entire risk chain with structural constraints rather than the model alone.

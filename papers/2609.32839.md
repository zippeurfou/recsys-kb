## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of measuring user preference for short videos using imperfect behavioral data like watch time, which is often confounded by video length and other factors. The authors propose the Factorized Latent Value Model (FLVM), treating observed behaviors as noisy signals of a latent preference state and isolating true user preference from measurement artifacts. FLVM significantly improves user value metrics, like enjoyment on YouTube Shorts, by providing a more accurate latent preference score for ranking recommendations.

## Technical note

On short-form video, watch signals measure preference poorly because raw watch time favors long videos and completion ratio favors short ones, so YouTube's FLVM treats heterogeneous feedback (watch-time buckets, completion, like, dislike, skip, shares, survey-style satisfaction) as noisy measurements of a three-factor latent value z covering consumption, active engagement, and valence, inferred by a supervised Gaussian variational bottleneck with a KL penalty to N(0, I). Each head's logit adds a stop-gradient baseline logit, from a restricted path that sees only video duration, user propensity, and session context and is trained by its own anchoring loss, to a positive-gain decoder over a hard-sparse-masked subset of z, so the latent path learns only the residual value advantage. The routing masks, positive gains, and sign-anchored valence labels give the factors stable meaning, with liked and explicitly negative impressions 1.7 to 2.0 SD apart on the valence factor across three independently trained models. At serving time a score built from sigmoid(z_s) and softplus(z_p) replaces the YouTube Shorts production multi-task pre-scorer; offline it lowers watch-time RMSE from 24.47 to 23.35 and raises Like PR-AUC from 0.2522 to 0.3190 and Dislike PR-AUC from 0.0038 to 0.0054, while overall Completion PR-AUC dips from 0.6898 to 0.6722 but improves on videos over 60s, and the two paths are complementary (Like PR-AUC 0.186 baseline-only and 0.072 latent-only versus 0.319 combined). A 14-day A/B test lifted the primary viewer-enjoyment metric by 2.67% with neutral guardrails, plus a more than 2% lift in early engagement for fresh, cold-start videos.

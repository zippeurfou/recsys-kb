## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of using Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate hard negative examples for improving retrieval systems trained with contrastive learning. Standard hard negative mining has inherent limitations, but LLM-based synthesis can provide more targeted negatives yet risks degrading performance if not carefully aligned. The authors propose CausalNeg, which tackles this issue by explicitly constructing counterfactual perturbations based on query information needs and preventing shortcut biases during training through entropy maximization.

## Technical note

CausalNeg addresses why naively adding LLM-generated hard negatives to contrastive retrieval training often degrades performance, formalizing the cause as a generative-discriminative gap: LLM generation optimizes fluency while contrastive learning needs strategic relevance violations at the decision boundary. It identifies two failure modes: discriminative-agnostic generation, where the LLM lacks a model of query information needs and produces generic or topic-drifted text; and source-dependent shortcuts, where distributional artifacts let the model separate negatives by origin, causing gradient drift. The method uses CoT-guided counterfactual perturbation that decomposes why a document satisfies a query into explicit information requirements then surgically violates individual ones, plus query-view entropy maximization that minimizes mutual information between source identity and similarity scores. Across four benchmarks CausalNeg beats mining-only and naive-generation baselines.

## Summary

Current benchmarks poorly evaluate language agents' vulnerability to adversarial search results (misinformation appearing prominently), leaving mitigation strategies largely untested. The authors introduce the Synthetic Web Benchmark, a controlled, programmable environment with reliable information and the ability to inject high-plausibility misinformation into specific search ranks. Evaluating six frontier models against this benchmark revealed significant failures, showing accuracy collapse and inability to resist manipulation, establishing a new baseline for testing search robustness.

## Technical note

The Synthetic Web Benchmark is a procedurally generated 'mini-Internet' of thousands of hyperlinked articles with ground-truth credibility, bias, and factuality labels plus process-level interaction traces, built to causally isolate agents' vulnerability to adversarial search ranking. Injecting a single high-plausibility misinformation article at rank 0 causes catastrophic accuracy collapse in six frontier models (e.g., GPT-5 from 65.1% to 18.2%) even with unlimited access to truthful sources. Tool traces expose three failure modes: minimal search escalation, poor multi-source synthesis, and severe overconfidence when wrong. Contamination filtering blocks pretraining leakage, and the testbed evaluates RAG safeguards under adversarial ranking.

## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

For multi-stage recommenders in industry, a user request would first trigger a simple and efficient retriever module that selects and ranks a list of relevant items, then the recommender calls a slower but more sophisticated reranking model that refines the item list exposure to the user. To consistently optimize the two-stage retrieval reranking framework, most efforts have focused on learning reranker-aware retrievers. ## Technical note

Kuaishou's DNR (Denoising Neural Reranker) makes the reranker retriever-aware by treating reranking as a noise-reduction problem on the previous-stage retriever scores, which they empirically and theoretically show are informative but noisy. DNR is an adversarial framework pairing a denoising reranker with a noise-generation module, extending score-error minimization with three objectives: a denoising objective aligning noisy retriever scores with user feedback, an adversarial retriever-score generation objective for better exploration, and a distribution regularization term matching generated to real score distributions. Experiments on three public datasets and an industrial recommender validate DNR's effectiveness.

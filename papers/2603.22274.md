## Summary

The authors developed Felis, an open-source toolkit designed for high-throughput absolute binding free energy (ABFE) calculations, pairing it with a data-driven force field called ByteFF. They demonstrated that Felis performs comparably to state-of-the-art relative binding free energy (RBFE) methods on diverse datasets, including a challenging one with highly charged ligands and cofactors, all achieved without custom modifications. This positions Felis as a viable, ready-to-use computational tool for structure-based drug design.

## Technical note

This paper presents Felis (Free Energy of Ligand-protein InteractionS), an open-source, automated, scalable toolkit for high-throughput absolute binding free energy (ABFE) calculations, which avoid the scaffold-similarity assumption of relative binding free energy (RBFE) by scoring each ligand independently via an alchemical thermodynamic cycle (decoupling in solvent, coupling in the binding site, plus a Boresch-style restraint correction). Paired with the data-driven ByteFF25 molecular-mechanics force field for ligands/cofactors (AMBER ff14SB for proteins), Felis achieves ranking performance comparable to state-of-the-art RBFE on a diverse benchmark of 43 protein targets and 857 ligands, and converges on a challenging KRAS(G12D) set with highly charged ligands and cofactor. Crucially, all predictions are strictly zero-shot, with no custom force-field modifications or alchemical lambda-schedule fine-tuning on benchmark systems. This addresses the historically small ABFE validation studies (e.g., FEP+ ABFE covered only eight targets), positioning Felis as a ready-to-use production tool for computer-aided structure-based drug design. This is a BioML/drug-discovery paper unrelated to recommender systems.

## Summary

Slate recommender systems present a challenge because while the final slate re-ranking step optimizes for item interactions, it can only handle a limited number of items due to hardware constraints. To address this, the paper introduces Slate-Aware Ranking (SAR), which improves the initial ranking stage by implicitly considering interactions *within* the slate. This provides a higher-quality candidate set to the re-ranking stage, ultimately boosting the overall relevance and diversity of the recommendation system without exceeding hardware limits.

## Technical note

SAR (Slate-Aware Ranking) brings modeling of cross-item mutual influence, usually confined to the re-ranking stage, into the ranking stage so the re-ranker receives a higher-quality candidate set. During training a slate encoder encodes the full recommended slate into a latent vector and a user encoder is aligned to it via an L2 similarity loss; at inference the slate is unavailable so the user-encoder output substitutes for it, avoiding train/serve inconsistency. Compared to privileged feature distillation (PFD), SAR reaches better accuracy with roughly half the training cost, and it integrates with multi-task backbones like PLE/MMoE. Offline on an industrial news dataset SAR_Attn gains +0.43% AUC over baseline; online A/B testing shows +0.9% page views, +0.745% stay time, and improved diversity (lower Gini).

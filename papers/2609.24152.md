## Summary

This paper introduces GradCIR, a methodology for improving composed image retrieval (CIR) in e-commerce by training on graded (4-level) relevance labels, unlike existing binary approaches. It uses a Vision-Language Model (VLM) like PaliGemma2 to automatically generate relevant image-text pairs and their corresponding relevance scores from raw catalog data. The system, deployed at scale by Walmart using this approach, achieves significantly better search performance, demonstrating up to an 8.5% lift in NDCG@10 compared to binary training, and outperforms standard zero-shot baselines on the FashionIQ benchmark.

## Technical note

Composed image retrieval (image plus modifier text) methods treat relevance as binary with a single positive target, a poor fit for catalogs where many items partially match. GradCIR uses Gemini-2.5-pro to generate similarity and modifier queries from Walmart catalog images and to label top-K candidates on a 4-level ordinal scale (Exact / NearExact / PartialMatch / Irrelevant) with no manual annotation, grows the data through a three-round relevance-feedback loop that mines hard negatives from the in-training retriever (3.5M graded pairs), and trains a PaliGemma2 (about 3B) early-fusion Siamese bi-encoder with LoRA, last-token pooling, Matryoshka embeddings and AngleLoss enforcing all six pairwise grade orderings. With backbone and data fixed, 4-level supervision beats binary by 4.9-5.9% NDCG@10 while a 3-level variant (merging NearExact and PartialMatch) gains little; the recipe lifts four other encoders by 24.3% Adj-R@5 (Exact) on average and reaches 0.6703 average recall on supervised FashionIQ. It is deployed with 256-d truncated embeddings as the retrieval backbone of Walmart visual search on partial traffic; note the internal test-set labels come from the same Gemini judge.

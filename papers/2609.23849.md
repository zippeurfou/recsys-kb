## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge in sequential recommendation where systems tend to favor popular items, sacrificing exposure to less common ones. The authors propose feature decorrelation as a solution to shape the representation space and reduce popularity bias. They introduce BT-SR, a model using Barlow Twins, demonstrating that this decorrelation suppresses the advantage popular items gain from shared low-rank directions, improving ranking quality across the tail and offering a control mechanism for accuracy-exposure trade-offs.

## Technical note

Dot-product sequential recommenders can concentrate scores on popular items through shared low-rank directions in the user-representation space. BT-SR augments SASRec-style next-item training with a Barlow Twins redundancy-reduction term, forming label-consistent positive pairs from user histories that share the same next-item target instead of synthetic corruptions, and introduces a bucket-based Alignment Concentration metric to diagnose how decorrelation suppresses popularity-aligned shared subspaces. On five public benchmarks BT-SR improves next-item ranking (e.g. ML-1M HR@1 +12.7% and HR@5 +6.5% over the best baseline), and the decorrelation weight acts as a control knob that reallocates accuracy between head and tail items, with dataset-dependent effects tied to temporal structure. The study is offline on public data only.

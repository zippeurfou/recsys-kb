## Summary

This paper introduces the **Combinatorial Rising Bandit** framework to address online learning problems where selecting a combination of base actions (super arms) provides stochastic rewards that also depend on past selections due to "rising" effects, like skill improvement from practice. The key challenge is modeling complex dependencies between rising components across multiple super arms simultaneously.

To solve this problem, the authors propose a new algorithm called **Combinatorial Rising Upper Confidence Bound** (CRUCB). This algorithm offers theoretical performance guarantees by establishing an upper bound on regret and proving it to be nearly optimal through matching lower bounds.

The effectiveness of their CRB framework and CRUCB algorithm is empirically validated not only in controlled synthetic environments but also applied successfully within realistic deep reinforcement learning systems.

## Technical note

The paper introduces the combinatorial rising bandit problem, where a learner selects a set of base arms (a super arm) whose per-arm rewards rise (improve) with repeated selection, motivated by settings like strengthening social influence and improving RL agents. Under additive super-arm reward and concave rising-outcome assumptions it characterizes the optimal policy and proposes CRUCB (Combinatorial Rising UCB), which estimates each arm's recent mean and slope to optimistically forecast future potential and picks the super arm maximizing it. The authors provide the first sub-linear regret lower bound for this rising setting and prove CRUCB's regret upper bound is close to it (parameterized by curvature c) without knowledge of c. Experiments on synthetic resource-allocation/network-routing tasks and deep-RL navigation show CRUCB outperforms rising and non-stationary combinatorial baselines, even when concavity is violated.

## Summary

AI search systems face challenges early in deployment due to dynamic, frequently updated product catalogs, making static fine-tuning or prompts ineffective. To address this, IGPO (Inventory-Grounded Policy-Level Optimization) separates the AI's decision-making policy from the constantly changing inventory data. It dynamically uses runtime inventory information to guide the search prompts online and learns from offline exploration to improve retrieval strategies, leading to a significant boost in click-through rates and a reduction in bad cases in a commercial deployment.

## Technical note

IGPO (Inventory-Grounded Policy-Level Optimization) targets an early-stage commercial smart-assistant AI search system at Huawei whose product catalog changes fast enough that items cannot be baked into prompts. The pipeline's Retrieval Planner and Selection LLMs stay frozen; the only optimized state is a store of natural-language Policy Guidelines, each tagged with a scene (category) and stage (ret or sel), that encode how to reason from inventory evidence and never assert that a specific item exists. Online, a probe retrieves top-k_p items by cosine embedding similarity and builds an inventory portrait from probe confidence (max, mean, sd of similarities), per-category probe density and field fill rates, fused with precomputed scene profiles of coverage gaps and recent changes; guidelines are fetched through an inverted index and ranked by query-guideline embedding similarity, with at most three injected per stage. Offline, N=5 stochastic rollouts per query are judged success/failure/uncertain by DeepSeek-V3 671B at temperature 0.3; mixed-outcome groups yield contrastive pairs directly, while all-failure groups enter an inventory-guided exploration loop (K_max=10 turns) that re-queries the Planner against still-unexplored portrait categories to separate a missed retrieval route from genuine absence. DeepSeek-V3 at temperature 0.7 then proposes JSON add/revise/merge/delete patches, gated by individual and combined replay with target win/loss, background loss-rate and 545-query regression thresholds; no patch gets individual human approval. On a 3,000-query test set evaluated four weeks later at a fresh snapshot, IGPO lifts Candidate Recall@30 from 0.691 to 0.847 and F1@8 from 0.643 to 0.816 while cutting false-no-inventory from 30.6 to 4.8 percent and false-match from 60.8 to 15.2 percent. Under a token-matched 120M budget it beats a GEPA-style optimizer (F1@8 0.817 vs 0.791, false match 15.6 vs 37.3 percent), and on a shared Qwen3-8B policy backbone it beats SFT (F1@8 0.781 vs 0.763) and SFT+GRPO (0.750 stage-specific, 0.699 shared reward). The exploration loop recovered routes for 112 of 124 inventory-supported misses (90.3 percent). A 14-day 50/50 A/B over roughly 100K users per arm gave +3.17 percent relative CTR (95 percent CI 1.9 to 4.5) and 38.9 percent fewer audited bad cases, concentrated in composite queries (+7.19 percent CTR, 47.9 percent bad-case reduction) and inventory-absence queries (68.1 percent). Serving cost is 247.3 added prompt tokens on average and 49.4 ms p50 / 185.8 ms p95 latency. Deployed since May 2026. Limitations: quality hinges on probe and metadata coverage, and maintenance evidence spans only a 31-day window at a 43 to 64 guideline scale.

## Summary

Airbnb faces challenges in personalizing recommendations for logged-out or first-time users with little search history. To overcome this cold-start problem while adhering to privacy regulations (especially regarding third-party cookies), they introduced Proximity Features, which clusters nearby users using geo-IP data and aggregates signals for anonymous groups without needing persistent IDs. This system demonstrably improves bookings, particularly for users lacking or having outdated individual features.

## Technical note

Solves cold-start personalization for logged-out and first-time Airbnb users under privacy constraints (GDPR, third-party-cookie deprecation) where user_id and cookie features are unavailable. Proximity Features group users by geo-IP into buckets of roughly 1,000 nearby users via a greedy two-phase adaptive clustering algorithm (dense-location IP-hash refinement plus sparse-location coarsening), producing a 'proximity key' that serves as a privacy-safe, k-anonymity-motivated analog to user_id and yields aggregated group-level features (short/long-term engagement, booking patterns, demographics) served as a 60ms soft dependency. Offline, proximity features alone reach Recall@5 of about 29-30% for destination prediction among low-activity users. Deployed on marketing landing pages and homepage AutoSuggest destination recommendation, online A/B tests show +2.0% first-time bookers on landing pages and +0.16% global / +0.33% first-time bookings on AutoSuggest, with the largest lifts among Never-Booked and Dormant cohorts and a reduction in popularity bias toward globally popular destinations.

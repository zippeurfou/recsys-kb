## Summary

1.  LinkedIn introduced the Feed Sequential Recommender (Feed-SR), a transformer-based model replacing their previous DCNv2 ranking system for personalizing the LinkedIn Feed while meeting strict production requirements.
2.  Feed-SR significantly improved user engagement (+2.10% time spent) compared to the old model, making it the primary system powering the core member experience on the Feed.
3.  The paper details the model's design choices, training, and serving optimizations that enabled its large-scale deployment, and also discusses why Feed-SR outperformed alternative sequential and LLM-based architectures in terms of both online metrics and production efficiency.

## Technical note

Feed SR is a transformer-based sequential ranking model that replaces a DCNv2 ranker for LinkedIn Feed at 1.2B-member scale, predicting action likelihoods (long dwell, like/comment/share) rather than pure next-item. It introduces production-driven modeling techniques: late fusion of candidate/context features, LLM-derived member-profile embeddings for sparse-history members, and within-session randomization to counter train-serve skew from intra-session label correlation. Serving relies on a disaggregated CPU/GPU stack, shared-context batching across hundreds of candidates per request, zero-copy Arrow-to-tensor transfer, and SRMIS, a custom Flash-Attention kernel with ~2x speedup over PyTorch SDPA. Online A/B tests report +2.10% time spent and +3.52% likes/comments/reshares. The authors also report why fine-tuned LLM-Rankers and TransAct-style encoders lost on the quality-latency-efficiency trade-off.

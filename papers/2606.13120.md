## Summary

This paper introduces EvoBrowseComp, a new benchmark designed to evaluate Search Agents effectively as world knowledge evolves. Existing benchmarks like BrowseComp rely on static data, making them vulnerable to contamination where models can score highly by memorizing facts rather than demonstrating true browsing competence.

EvoBrowseComp consists of 400 complex English and Chinese questions synthesized via live-web traversal using a sophisticated three-agent framework (QA synthesis, information filtering, high-level guidance). This automated system ensures the benchmark remains free from contamination and stays temporally fresh.

Regularly updating with EvoBrowseComp prevents data contamination and makes it suitable for assessing agents' ability to perform broad horizontal searches and integrate current knowledge meaningfully.

## Technical note

EvoBrowseComp is an auto-updatable benchmark of 400 English and 400 Chinese complex questions synthesized from live-web traversal to resist test-set contamination and parametric memorization. A three-agent pipeline (QA synthesis agent retrieving fresh knowledge, information filtering agent screening credibility and popularity, high-level guidance agent formalizing questions as reasoning graphs via projection/intersection/complement) removes logical shortcuts and redundancy. Because synthesis is fully automated, over-exposed questions can be retired and newly emerged facts swapped in without costly human curation. Even Claude-Opus-4.6 reaches only 44.8% accuracy with tools and 6.0% without, confirming the benchmark isolates genuine browsing and multi-hop reasoning from static recall.

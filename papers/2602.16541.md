## Summary

This paper introduces novel position-based click models specifically designed for complex carousel interfaces, unlike traditional single-list models. The authors propose three models, including the first position-based model (OEPBM) that uses observed eye-tracking data. Their findings indicate that gradient-based optimization yields better results, and while the OEPBM performs best in predicting clicks, they also highlight that accurately modeling user examination patterns is crucial and distinct from click prediction alone.

## Technical note

This paper studies position-based click models for carousel (multi-list, swipeable) recommender interfaces, where most existing click models assume a single ranked list. It proposes three novel carousel position-based models, including the Observed Examination Position-Based Model (OEPBM), the first position-based model without latent variables that replaces latent examination with observed examination signals derived from eye-tracking data. The authors provide a general implementation supporting expectation-maximization, maximum likelihood estimation, and gradient-based optimization, and find gradient-based optimization consistently yields better click likelihoods. OEPBM achieves the strongest click prediction and the examination patterns most aligned with real user behavior. A key finding is that strong click fit does not imply realistic examination modeling, exposing a fundamental limitation of click-only models in complex interfaces.

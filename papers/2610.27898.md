## Summary

Current SID-based generative recommendation systems lack sufficient stepwise personalization and suffer from imbalanced candidate item branches, leading to potential errors and favoring popular items. To address this, the paper proposes SPAR, a framework that enhances personalization at each decoding step by retrieving relevant user evidence and balances the interaction history across different branches using the RQ-PIB module. Experiments on real-world data demonstrate SPAR significantly improves click-through rate, retains target prefixes better, and balances branch traffic, leading to improved user engagement metrics in online A/B testing.

## Technical note

SID-based generative recommenders make a sequence of prefix-conditioned branch decisions, but use a static user representation and suffer from popular SID paths dominating sibling branches (top 1% of paths carry 47-71% of traffic depending on depth); SPAR (Alibaba Youku) adds stepwise personalization and prefix-balanced SIDs. The PATH Decoder (Qwen3-0.6B decoder-only backbone over recent SID history) compresses one-, two- and three-token-prefix views of the long-term history into Q-Former memory banks, reads them with prefix-conditioned cross-attention at every decoding step, and fuses User and Event tokens before each level's softmax; RQ-PIB runs residual K-Means then greedily moves items out of overloaded child branches under a per-parent-prefix load bound with minimum extra reconstruction distortion; a final stage mixes SFT with DPO on pairs built from beam candidates scored by a frozen ranker. Offline on Youku homepage logs, SPAR beats OneRec (strongest baseline) by 2.66% average HR@10/20/50/100 (HR@10 0.4831 vs 0.4648), with RQ-PIB worth +3.55pp average over RQ-KMeans and DPO adding +0.78pp; it raises target-prefix retention 5.79pp and cuts the traffic-weighted largest child-branch share 32.84%. A 7-day 10%-traffic online A/B test improved watch time per user +3.95% and play conversion rate +3.38%.

## Summary

This paper identifies a significant "validity gap" in health AI benchmarks, finding they fail to accurately represent the real-world clinical scenarios they are meant to evaluate. The authors analyzed over 18,000 consumer health queries across existing benchmarks and discovered a lack of critical elements like raw clinical data (e.g., lab results, imaging), safety-critical issues (e.g., suicide), and vulnerable populations. The study concludes that benchmarks must adopt standardized query profiling to better align AI evaluation with actual clinical practice needs.

## Technical note

This cross-sectional study identifies a validity gap in consumer-facing health LLM benchmarks, which report aggregate scores but rarely characterize the queries they contain. The authors apply a standardized 16-field Query Profile taxonomy spanning three dimensions (context, topic, intent) to 18,707 consumer health queries across six public benchmarks (HealthSearchQA, MashQA, MedRedQA, HealthBench, GoogleFitbit sleep/fitness), grouped into three generations. They surface four systematic blind spots: pediatric/older-adult queries are <11% despite being >half of clinical encounters (globally significant conditions like malaria and TB largely absent), chronic-disease management (5.5%) and preventive care (2.7%) are underrepresented, clinical document interpretation (labs 5.2%, raw artifacts 0.6%) is scarcely tested, and behavioral-health crisis scenarios (self-harm/suicidal ideation) are <0.7% and nearly absent from conversational benchmarks. They propose routine query profiling, analogous to CONSORT reporting for clinical trials, and release open-source tagging tools; the framework deliberately avoids ICD/SNOMED ontologies which presuppose confirmed diagnoses.

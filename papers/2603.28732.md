## Summary

1.  Current robotic mapping systems create "first-person" maps limited by the robot's own sensors and abilities, often missing aspects of the environment like hidden objects or articulating parts.
2.  This paper introduces Pandora, using egocentric vision data (like a human wearing Project Aria glasses exploring) to automatically learn detailed 3D models of articulated objects and integrate them into comprehensive scene graphs.
3.  By employing simple heuristics, Pandora achieves high-quality articulated object models and scene understanding, significantly enhancing a robot's ability to perform complex tasks like retrieving concealed items, demonstrated with Boston Dynamics Spot.

## Technical note

Pandora builds articulated 3D scene graphs directly from egocentric RGB-D-style recordings captured while a human wearing Project Aria glasses naturally interacts with a scene, transferring articulation knowledge to a deployable robot. Using simple heuristics over observation sequences (hand pose, RGB, implicit depth, SE(3) camera pose), it estimates per-part geometry (point clouds) and joint parameters—articulation type (prismatic/revolute), axis, and pivot—achieving quality comparable to state-of-the-art depth- or learning-based methods. These articulation models are integrated into hierarchical scene graphs that encode object dynamics and object-container relationships, addressing blind spots of robot-centric ('first-person') mapping. The representation is validated by deploying a Boston Dynamics Spot to retrieve concealed target items using only the scene graph as input.

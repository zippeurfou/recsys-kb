## Summary

Speculative decoding speeds up language model inference but often suffers from verification being a computational bottleneck, particularly with long inputs and MoE models. This paper addresses this by proposing a sparse verification framework specifically for the speculative decoding stage, identifying structured redundancies across attention, FFN, and MoE components. The framework further reduces computation via retrieval reuse, and extensive experiments show it achieves good efficiency-accuracy trade-offs while maintaining stable model performance.

## Technical note

In speculative decoding the parallel verification stage becomes the compute bottleneck, especially for long-context inputs and MoE models. The paper systematically studies sparsification of the verification stage and finds structured redundancy across attention, FFN, MoE, and layers. It proposes a training-free sparse verification framework combining importance-based sparse attention for multi-token verification, inter-layer retrieval reuse of block selections, low-activation FFN channel pruning, and adaptive skipping of low-contribution experts. Experiments on summarization, QA, and math reasoning show favorable efficiency-accuracy trade-offs with stable acceptance length.

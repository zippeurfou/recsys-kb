## Summary

This paper introduces novel mechanisms for fairly assigning credit to multiple creators in AI systems when their intellectual property appears together in the context window, generating content like code or news. The proposed approach uses the "least core" concept from cooperative game theory to distribute credit in a way that minimizes the risk of any subset of creators feeling significantly under-rewarded. The authors developed algorithms to approximate this least core solution, demonstrating that their methods can achieve this fair credit distribution using exponentially fewer calls to large language models compared to alternative approaches.

## Technical note

This paper proposes incentive-aligned mechanisms for in-context credit assignment: fairly distributing credit among creators whose intellectual property appears together in an LLM's context window when generating content like code or news. It adopts the least core solution concept from cooperative game theory, which distributes value as stably as possible so no subset of creators is significantly under-compensated relative to what they could generate alone. The authors develop approximation algorithms for the least core using novel constraint-seeding and constraint-separation routines. On a web-retrieval credit-assignment task, their methods approximate the least core with orders-of-magnitude fewer LLM calls than alternative attribution approaches.

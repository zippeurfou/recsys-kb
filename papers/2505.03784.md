## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of measuring insulin resistance, a precursor to type 2 diabetes, which is difficult due to expensive current methods. It presents a novel model using readily available wearable data (like activity patterns) combined with common blood biomarkers (including HOMA-IR). The results show this integrated deep learning approach significantly outperforms individual data sources and demonstrates high accuracy in identifying at-risk individuals, paving the way for early detection via digital health tools.

## Technical note

This health study predicts insulin resistance (ground-truthed by HOMA-IR) from consumer-wearable time series (resting heart rate, HRV, steps, sleep), demographics, and routine blood biomarkers, using a cohort of 1,165 diverse US participants. Time-series features are embedded via a masked autoencoder and fed to tree-based models (XGBoost), with a direct-regression variant for interpretability; extensive ablation across 25 feature sets, six time windows, and four learners (600 combinations) is evaluated with 5-fold cross-validation. Combining wearables, demographics, and blood biomarkers outperforms any single source (R2=0.5, AUROC=0.80, 76% sensitivity, 84% specificity at HOMA-IR>2.9), with 93% sensitivity in obese/sedentary subgroups, and results reproduce on an independent 72-person cohort. The predicted HOMA-IR is integrated into an LLM agent that contextualizes results and gives personalized metabolic-health recommendations.

Existing video recommender systems rely primarily on user-defined metadata or on low-level visual and acoustic signals extracted by specialised encoders. These low-level features describe what appears on the screen but miss deeper semantics such as intent, humour, and world knowledge that make clips resonate with viewers. ## Technical note

Proposes a recommendation-system-agnostic, zero-finetuning framework that injects high-level semantics into video recommenders by prompting an off-the-shelf MLLM to summarize each clip into a rich natural-language description (capturing intent, humor, world knowledge), then encoding that text with a SOTA text encoder as input features to standard collaborative, content-based, and generative recommenders. On MicroLens-100K (TikTok-style videos), MLLM-generated captions consistently outperform conventional video, audio, and metadata features across five representative models, with joint video+audio captions most robust. First systematic reproducible study of open-weight MLLMs as on-the-fly knowledge extractors for video recommendation (Spotify).

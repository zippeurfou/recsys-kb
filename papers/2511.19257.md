## Summary

Multimodal medical retrieval-augmented generation (MMed-RAG) systems, used for tasks like report generation, face new security risks from visual input manipulations, which were previously unexplored. To address this, researchers developed Medusa, a novel black-box attack framework designed to create adversarial visual perturbations that can transfer across different models and steal control of the retrieval process towards malicious text. Experiments show Medusa achieves over 90% attack success rates on various medical models and remains effective against existing defenses, revealing critical vulnerabilities and underscoring the need for better robustness testing in medical AI.

## Technical note

Medusa is the first framework for cross-modal transferable black-box adversarial attacks on multimodal medical RAG (MMed-RAG) systems. It formulates a perturbation-optimization problem that uses a multi-positive InfoNCE loss to pull an adversarial image embedding toward malicious target text and away from correct reports, hijacking retrieval and steering generation. Transferability is boosted by a surrogate-model ensemble, invariant risk minimization, and a dual-loop optimization for cross-model consistency. Across report generation and disease diagnosis over three retrievers and two generators, Medusa exceeds a 90% attack success rate and remains robust to four mainstream defenses, exposing critical vulnerabilities in medical RAG.

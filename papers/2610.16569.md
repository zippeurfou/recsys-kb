## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of providing high-quality relevance labels for e-commerce search systems, which traditionally requires expensive human judgment. It proposes a framework using large language models (LLMs) to automate relevance judgment effectively, but notes their high inference costs. The key contribution is distilling the performance of these LLM-based "teacher" systems into compact "student" multimodal models, which achieve comparable accuracy but significantly improve speed, cost-efficiency, and scalability for relevance prediction in production.

## Technical note

Human relevance labels for e-commerce query-item pairs are accurate but slow and expensive, and GPT-class LLM judges that match them are too costly to run at production throughput. eBay builds a 4-level (Bad/Fair/Good/Excellent) LLM-as-a-judge pipeline with a structured, iteratively refined respond-then-explain prompt on GPT-4.1 (83.7% accuracy vs a 3-judge human set), uses it to label 483K balanced query-item pairs with labels plus explanations, and black-box-distills them by SFT into Gemma-3-4B/12B/27B multimodal students (frozen vision encoder, LLaMA-Factory, 900px images) that take only a lightweight prompt. The 12B student reaches 81.59% accuracy on Test 1 vs 83.68% for the teacher and beats it on the independent Test 2 (65.24% vs 64.38%), while a text-only ModernBERT-large baseline lags at 74.06%/57.08%; ablations show explanations, images and data scale/diversity each help. Gemma-3-12B-IT-RLV in FP8 keeps accuracy within 0.4 points and serves 271.5 req/s label-only on one H100, chosen as the Pareto sweet spot over GPT-4.1 on cost and latency.

## Summary

This paper addresses the issue that optimizing e-commerce search rankings solely for user engagement (like clicks) can introduce biases, favoring popular or price-focused items over those with true semantic relevance. To solve this, the authors introduce a production-scale multi-task ranking system that explicitly balances relevance and engagement through a unified value model. They achieve this by using fine-tuned LLMs to efficiently generate high-quality ordinal relevance labels (irrelevant, moderately relevant, highly relevant) for vast datasets, enabling effective supervision for their model. Both offline metrics (like NDCG) and online A/B tests show that their approach significantly improves semantic relevance while maintaining core engagement objectives.

## Technical note

A production e-commerce search ranker adds semantic relevance as a first-class objective alongside CTR, add-to-cart, and conversion heads on a shared-bottom multi-task DNN. Relevance is modeled with an ordinal head predicting cumulative probabilities p(r>=1) and p(r>=2) over three graded levels, whose expected grade feeds a unified value function S = a*CTR + b*ATCR + c*CVR + (1-a-b-c)*s_rel for tunable relevance-engagement trade-offs. Supervision comes from a fine-tuned gpt-4o-mini generating ordinal labels for 100M+ query-item pairs (89% 3-class accuracy, 98% within-1) after auditing 600k human annotations against engagement signals and a Query-to-Taxonomy model. Offline the ordinal formulation gives the best relevance NDCG@10 without hurting engagement AUC/NDCG, and online A/B tests show +1.16% ATCR, +1.10% CVR, +0.50% GOV. LLMs are used only offline, avoiding online inference cost.

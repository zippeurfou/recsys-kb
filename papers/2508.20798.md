Unbiased learning to rank (ULTR), which aims to learn unbiased ranking models from biased user behavior logs, plays an important role in Web search. Previous research on ULTR has studied a variety of biases in users' clicks, such as position bias, presentation bias, and outlier bias. ## Technical note

Introduces the user-aware ULTR problem: existing unbiased learning-to-rank methods assume an average user and become biased when users differ in query preferences and examination propensities, which a causal graph shows creates an extra backdoor path from examination to click. Proposes a user-aware inverse-propensity-score estimator that models per-query user distributions and aggregates user-weighted examination probabilities into propensities, proven unbiased under mild assumptions and with lower variance than a naive per-impression user-dependent propensity. Experiments on two semi-synthetic datasets and one real dataset confirm superiority under personalized bias, especially with sparse logs.

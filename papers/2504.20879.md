## Summary

This paper argues that Chatbot Arena's leaderboard rankings for AI models are potentially distorted due to undisclosed private testing. Major providers like Meta, Google, and OpenAI use internal testing (private LLMs) before public releases, allowing them to select the best-performing scores. This advantage stems from data access asymmetry, as proprietary models receive significantly more evaluation opportunities than open-weight models, leading to biased results and overfitting to the Arena's specific characteristics rather than reflecting general model quality.

## Technical note

Chatbot Arena is the de facto leaderboard for ranking generative AI models via crowd pairwise preferences aggregated with a Bradley-Terry/Elo model, but the paper argues its rankings are systematically distorted. Undisclosed private testing lets a few providers evaluate many variants pre-release and retract low scores, so selective disclosure of the best score induces upward selection bias in Arena scores. The authors document 27 private Llama-4 variants tested by Meta and show proprietary models are sampled in more battles and deprecated less, giving providers like Google and OpenAI ~19-20% each of all Arena data versus ~30% for 83 open-weight models combined. This data-access asymmetry yields large gains (up to 112% relative on ArenaHard) and drives overfitting to Arena-specific dynamics rather than general quality; the paper proposes reforms for fairer benchmarking.

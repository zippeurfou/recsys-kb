## Summary

Late-interaction retrieval models using hard maximum similarity (MaxSim) pooling can be structurally biased during training due to extreme gradient concentration. This study shows MaxSim concentrates gradients more than alternatives like Top-k or softmax, leading to potential instability. The research highlights a tradeoff where MaxSim's sparsity improves early discrimination but increases sensitivity to document length, causing degradation faster than smoother pooling methods. These findings suggest the need for principled alternatives to hard max pooling in multi-vector retrieval systems.

## Technical note

This paper is a mechanistic study of pooling operators in late-interaction (multi-vector) retrieval, comparing hard MaxSim against Top-k averaging and temperature-controlled softmax pooling under in-batch InfoNCE training. Using a controlled synthetic environment with a shared concept dictionary, the authors measure patch-level gradient concentration via a Gini coefficient over per-patch gradient norms and find MaxSim maintains Gini ~0.78 versus ~0.45 (Top-4) and ~0.18 (softmax). MaxSim degrades more sharply than milder smoothing as document length M grows, exposing a sparsity-robustness tradeoff. Real-world corroboration on ColQwen2.5 + ViDoRe reproduces the ordering and shows 'spike hijacking' where injected distractor patches capture ~83% of token argmax selections and collapse recall, while larger k does not restore robustness.

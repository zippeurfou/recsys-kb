## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of instruction-guided recommendation where conflicting user interaction history can override current requests. The authors highlight that standard supervision from historical events doesn't work well due to their potentially misleading nature and the complex impact they have on item rankings. They introduce AIMS, a novel training method that uses a reference model to simulate the effect of removing specific misleading history events, creating asymmetric margin targets to improve recommendations without altering the input during inference.

## Technical note

In instruction-guided generative recommendation, an LLM decodes 3-token semantic IDs (2,048 codes per level) from a natural-language request plus the user's recent interaction history, and history events that conflict with the request can override it. An audit of frozen SFT checkpoints finds Influence-Utility Misalignment, where the JS-divergence influence of masking an event tracks the size of its effect on the target (Spearman 0.300 for Llama-3.1-8B, 0.167 for Gemma-3-12B) but not its sign (0.045, 0.002), and finds that deletions which raise the target score still shrink its margin over the rank-11 boundary competitor for 11.8% and 16.7% of requests. AIMS uses the frozen SFT model to test single-event history deletions on initially correct training requests, keeps a deletion only if it raises both the target score and the target-competitor margin by more than 1e-4 nats, caches the largest such margin as a counterfactual target, and trains a student on the complete history with cross-entropy plus an asymmetric competitor-suppression hinge whose gradient flows only through the competitor score (the target score is detached), so inference is unchanged. Across six backbones (Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Gemma-3-4B/12B, Qwen3-8B/14B) on an industrial content-platform dataset, Qilin and KuaiSearch-Lite, AIMS has the best Recall@10 and NDCG@10 in every setting, improving Recall@10 by 4.0 to 10.9% relative over matched continued CE training and beating CFT, LETTER (RG), LTRGR and S-DPO. Gains grow under instruction-history conflict (+0.558 and +0.874 points Recall@10 for Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-3-12B, with the observed-violation rate falling from 29.0% and 25.4% to 21.6% and 18.3%), the chosen deletions remove a constraint-violating event 80.9% and 83.8% of the time against 64.0% and 67.2% by chance, competitor-only gradient routing beats target-only and symmetric routing, and history dropout does not reproduce the gains.

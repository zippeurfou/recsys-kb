## Summary

Online advertising faces difficulties predicting conversions long after clicks due to delayed feedback (the "two-clock" problem). The proposed TWICE framework addresses this by splitting conversion prediction into two parts: an unseen head estimating target-window probability and a seen tail modeling delay distribution. It effectively learns both components using complementary data streams from recent clicks ("click clock") and longer-term outcomes ("conversion clock"), resulting in more accurate long-horizon predictions without needing complex historical computations during serving, as shown by improved experimental metrics.

## Technical note

Long-horizon post-click conversion prediction suffers delayed feedback: fresh click labels are only partially observed while conversions keep arriving from mixed historical click cohorts. TWICE factorizes long-window CVR into a target-window conversion probability (trained on click-clock records via a current-status likelihood over a short base observation window) and a grouped elapsed-delay CDF (trained only on conversion-clock arrivals), with stop-gradient keeping the two branches separate. Its arrival-conditioned temporal-convolution objective weights each observed conversion delay by fixed historical cohort pCVR mass, correcting traffic-driven cohort-mixing bias without replaying click features, and serving yields monotone horizon predictions with no historical lookup or convolution. TWICE beats DFM/FSIW/ES-DFM/ULC/DDFM/IF-DFM/MISS on Criteo and industrial ads (recovering up to 90.4% of the logloss headroom); in a Kwai A/B test it raised expected revenue +2.486%, revenue +1.858%, conversions +2.061% and improved PCOC calibration from 0.961 to 0.990.

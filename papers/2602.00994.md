## Summary

Current Agentic Reinforcement Learning (ARL) methods often train reasoning and tool-use together within a single model, implicitly assuming this improves performance. However, this paper finds that reasoning and tool-use capabilities often interfere during training due to conflicting gradient directions, undermining this assumption. To resolve this, they introduce LEAS to quantify the interference and propose DART, a framework that decouples the parameter updates for reasoning and tool-use, leading to significantly improved performance (6.35% average improvement over baselines).

## Technical note

The paper shows that jointly training reasoning and tool-use in a single agentic-RL model causes interference from conflicting gradient directions rather than the assumed synergy. It introduces LEAS to quantify this interference and proposes DART, which decouples parameter updates for reasoning versus tool-use (via separate LoRA ranks on one backbone) to prevent gradient conflict. Because DART shares a single backbone, switching between capabilities only toggles active LoRA ranks in O(1) and keeps the KV-cache valid, avoiding O(L^2) history re-encoding of a two-agent setup. Across multi-hop QA and text-to-SQL benchmarks, DART yields a 6.35% average improvement over baselines.

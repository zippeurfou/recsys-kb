## Summary

This paper addresses challenges in e-commerce ad recommendation caused by ultra-long user behavior sequences, which are difficult to manage efficiently during training or serve quickly for prediction. Existing methods often sacrifice fine-grained detail using search/cluster compression or rely on less effective lightweight attention mechanisms. The authors introduce TM20K, a system combining full transformer models with knowledge distillation; specifically, they train a teacher model heavily on the full sequence and then use it to guide student models that efficiently merge tokens while maintaining performance, achieving significant business metric improvements (ADSS +1.036%) without increasing training or serving costs compared to existing state-of-the-art models.

## Technical note

ByteDance extends the e-commerce behavior sequence in its ad CVR model from 5K to 20K tokens without paying the cost: naively going to 20K raised training time 3.5x, GPU memory +49G and serving latency ~6.3x. Attention analysis on a trained 20K full-attention (FA) model motivates three rule-based token merges applied only to the student -- LITM (merge consecutive tokens sharing a product ID within a positional gap T=3, since same-ID tokens get near-identical attention), PATM (segment-wise merge factors, e.g. [1,2,3,4] over [1-1K, 1K-5K, 5K-10K, 10K-20K], because the most recent 10% of tokens carry ~half the attention mass), and LPTM (halve the sequence by summing adjacent token pairs every 2 layers of the 6-layer transformer, since upper layers have sharper attention entropy); LITM/PATM run on CPU over raw features before embedding lookup, LPTM is a GPU tensor op. A one-time teacher keeps all 20K tokens (AUC +0.26%, LogLoss -0.55% vs the 5K baseline) but is never served -- throughput collapses 88K->11K -- and its cached logits train a separate student distillation tower with lambda=50 alongside the main tower. The student compresses average length 8.8K->1.8K (P90 20K->2.6K), scoring +0.15% AUC alone at 83K throughput (only -5% vs the 5K baseline), and +0.22% AUC with KD, recovering ~85% of the teacher's gain; ablations show standalone LITM/PATM/LPTM cost 0.02/0.06/0.03% AUC (0.11% combined) while LITM ~2x and PATM ~3x throughput over the 20K full baseline. Online A/B: ADSS +1.036%, ADVV +0.780% at +5.6% serving latency (vs +0.881%/+0.515% without KD); QK-Norm was required to stop late-training divergence (-0.45% AUC without it).

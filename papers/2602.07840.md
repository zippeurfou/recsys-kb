## Summary

1.  **Problem/Solution:** Large search systems face a challenge bridging human oversight (slow, nuanced) with high-volume AI evaluation (fast, resource-intensive). The paper introduces SAGE, a framework designed to translate high-quality human judgment into scalable evaluation signals for AI systems.
2.  **Mechanism:** SAGE operates via a bidirectional calibration loop involving evolving policies, precedents, and an LLM judge, transforming subjective relevance judgments into a detailed, human-level agreement rubric. It further uses teacher-student distillation to make these high-quality judgments accessible at a much lower cost (92x reduction).
3.  **Impact:** Applied in LinkedIn Search, SAGE enabled policy-driven model development and online oversight, effectively detecting subtle performance regressions invisible to standard metrics. This deployment led to a measurable increase in daily active users (+0.25%).

## Technical note

SAGE operationalizes human product judgment for search relevance via a Policy-Precedent-Judge triad with a bidirectional calibration loop, where an LLM surrogate judge, a natural-language policy, and a small expert-curated precedent set co-evolve to expose ambiguities. Relevance is decomposed into orthogonal attributes (Title, Seniority, Company) scored on a graded 0-4 scale rather than pairwise ranking, enabling explicit quality thresholds and auditable failure attribution. A calibrated GPT-o3 teacher (linear-weighted Cohen's kappa 0.77) is distilled via full-parameter supervised instruction tuning into an 8B decoder-only student at 92x lower cost, supporting >10^7 daily labels and >10^4 QPS. Deployed in LinkedIn Search, it detected regressions invisible to engagement metrics and drove a 0.25% DAU lift.

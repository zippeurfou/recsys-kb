## Summary

This paper addresses the limitations of traditional ItemIDs and purely content-based Semantic IDs (SIDs) in recommendation systems. It proposes MMQ-v2, a framework that tackles the unique challenges of user-item interaction data (skewed, diverse, noisy) by adaptively aligning, denoising, and amplifying multimodal information. The key innovations are an alignment aware of information richness and a dynamic router applying different weights to SIDs, resulting in ADA-SID, which significantly improves recommendation performance.

## Technical note

ADC-SID (the MMQ-v2 framework) improves Semantic ID learning by adaptively denoising collaborative information, addressing that user-item interactions are skewed so collaborative signal quality differs sharply between popular and long-tail items. It identifies two failure modes: indiscriminate behavior-content alignment lets collaborative noise from long-tail items corrupt content representations, and equal-weight behavioral SIDs let noise drown out the few informative SIDs. The solution has an Adaptive Behavior-Content Alignment controller that scales alignment strength by the reliability of pre-trained item embeddings, and a Dynamic Behavioral Weighting mechanism that learns importance scores so downstream models can suppress noisy SIDs. Experiments on public and industrial datasets show gains in both generative retrieval and discriminative ranking tasks.

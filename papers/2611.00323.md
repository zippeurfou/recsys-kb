## Summary

1.  Generative recommendation faces a "cold-start" challenge when suggesting new creators to follow, limited by a component called Semantic IDs (SID) which acts as a bottleneck.
2.  The paper proposes Atomic Creator Tokens (ACT), assigning each creator its own unique token in a learned vocabulary, thereby eliminating SID collisions and allowing gradient flow during training.
3.  ACT significantly improves creator-level recall and reduces latency compared to SIDs, even when the SID system is expanded, and shows directional positive results in real-world A/B tests.

## Technical note

Meta, unconnected-creator recommendation (suggest creators a user does not follow, from hundreds of thousands of head creators, given a short trail of opens / dwells / follows on previously suggested creators). Problem: the OneRec/TIGER recipe quantizes a frozen GNN creator embedding with RQ-KMeans into a many-to-one Semantic ID, so colliding creators are resolved by a popularity tie-break that caps creator-level recall (a collision tax of 10-27 Recall@15 points; resolution efficiency rho = creator / cluster recall as low as 0.47 at 3.79 creators per SID) and favors already-famous creators, while the frozen quantizer blocks gradients to the creator representation and forces codebook rebuilds for new creators. Method: Atomic Creator Tokens give every candidate creator its own vocabulary token in a Llama-3.2-3B backbone (up to 30Y creators; a 10Y x 3072 bf16 table adds about 0.6 GB), warm up the new rows on creator descriptions and co-engagement with the backbone frozen, then LoRA-SFT (r=64) end-to-end with next-token cross-entropy over creator tokens; inference is a single decode step and a direct top-k over creator logits with an O(1) lookup, no codebook join or popularity rerank. Results in a controlled production comparison where only the token layer varies: creator-level Recall@15 43.41% vs 23.58% for SID at 10Y (+19.8 points, 1.84x), Recall@200 79.3% vs 47.6%, NDCG@200 0.343 vs 0.187, still +8.7 points when the SID codebook is enlarged 64x at 30Y, and about 2.7x lower per-user latency since SID decodes a five-token block per beam; effective recall (coverage x conditional recall) rises monotonically with vocabulary size. On KuaiRand-27K (top-50k creators, about 331 samples per token) ACT Recall@15 is 2.763% vs 1.859% for SID and it is the only method above global popularity (2.351%), but on KuaiRand-1K (5-34 samples per token) it loses to heuristics, motivating a routed design with ACT for the head and conventional retrieval for the tail; GNN warm-start helps marginally, an in-batch contrastive loss hurts, DPO / GRPO / SDPO post-training did not beat SFT, and a four-arm live A/B was directionally positive on every metric (+0.66% product engagement vs the classic ranker, p about 0.05).

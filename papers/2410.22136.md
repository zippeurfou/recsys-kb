## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the difficulty sequential recommendation systems have predicting new ("cold-start") items with few user interactions.
2.  It introduces SimRec, which improves recommendations by incorporating item similarity information into predictions using a modified loss function, without changing the underlying model structure or adding training complexity.
3.  SimRec significantly boosts performance on rare items (occurring less than 10 times) compared to existing methods like SASRec and performs well across different data sparsity levels, offering an efficient solution for handling unseen or infrequently seen items even during training.

## Technical note

Proposes SimRec to mitigate the cold-start problem in sequential recommendation, where most items (e.g., 91% of Amazon Beauty) appear fewer than 10 times. SimRec incorporates a per-item similarity distribution, derived from text-embedding similarity between items, into a customized loss so the model learns from abundant items similar to rare ones, even items unseen in interactions. It requires no architecture change and no extra trainable parameters, preserving SASRec's inference cost. It achieves up to 78% higher HR@10 than SASRec on rare items and outperforms baselines on sparse datasets while staying on par on dense ones.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of determining an optimal experiment duration in online testing, which is heavily influenced by the choice of assumed effect size (AES). It proposes two data-driven methods—an hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Model and a utility theory-based approach—to automatically estimate or select AES more effectively than traditional domain-knowledge based practices for large-scale experimentation. The study demonstrates that these new approaches significantly outperform existing baseline strategies in terms of both simulated and real-world performance metrics, improving the accuracy and efficiency of online experiments by better balancing their cost with decision-making precision.

## Technical note

The paper studies data-driven selection of the assumed effect size (AES), which critically determines A/B test duration but is traditionally set by domain experts, impractical for services running tens of thousands of experiments. It offers two methods: a three-layer Gaussian mixture model (fit with an EM-type algorithm) that accounts for cross-experiment heteroskedasticity and estimates the expected true effect size among positive experiments; and a utility-theory approach that picks the optimal AES by balancing experiment cost against decision-making precision. Both are compared against baselines on simulated and real Amazon data, demonstrating superior performance. This is described as the first work on data-driven effect-size recommendation for large-scale online experimentation.

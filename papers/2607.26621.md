## Summary

1.  Current foundation recommendation models (FRMs) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation are inefficient due to high inference latency from producing lengthy rationales and rigid templates that don't adapt well to diverse user interests.
2.  WhisperRec proposes an efficient "Latent-Reason-then-Answer" paradigm, compressing explicit CoT reasoning into learnable latent tokens during a multi-stage alignment process, thus avoiding the bottleneck of autoregressive text generation while retaining crucial decision information.
3.  This approach, combined with Multi-View Adaptive CoT for diverse supervision and curriculum-based post-training, significantly improves recommendation accuracy (measured by SID@64) compared to explicit-CoT methods on both public and industrial-scale datasets, achieving over ten times faster inference speed.

## Technical note

Explicit Chain-of-Thought in LLM-backed foundation recommendation models (the Think-then-Answer paradigm of OneReason) overthinks easy requests, drifts toward dominant historical interests, and pays a heavy autoregressive rationale-generation cost at serving time. WhisperRec instead uses Latent-Reason-then-Answer: a teacher builds Multi-View Adaptive CoT supervision from three complementary prompts, Exploration (intent hypotheses with no candidate or target), Evaluation (candidate-conditioned match judgment plus a [0,1] confidence), and Attribution (evidence chain behind an observed conversion), with a per-instance Low/High difficulty estimate so reasoning budget tracks decision difficulty rather than being uniform. A three-stage Latent Reasoning Alignment distills those rationales into K=3 learnable latent tokens on a Qwen3.5-0.8B backbone whose vocabulary is extended with three-level Res-Kmeans semantic IDs: single-view rationale warm-up with a view control token placed after the shared latent slots, multi-view training with length-normalized per-rationale losses, then recommendation-oriented context alignment predicting the target SID; post-training mixes latent-reasoning SFT and plain SFT 1:1 under a high -> medium -> low user-activity curriculum. On the Kuaishou LLM-Rec public benchmark and a 7-day Kuaishou local-service industrial set, WhisperRec-Merge is best on all metrics (public SID@64 0.0239 / ID@64 0.0742; industrial SID@64 0.1993 / ID@64 0.5088), improving SID@64 by 17.44% over OneReason+CoT-Think and 9.33% over No-Think, with LLM-as-a-judge scores confirming MV-ACoT rationale quality (avg 4.35 for Evaluation vs 3.14 for OneReason-CoT). Since no rationale is decoded online, latency matches the no-CoT model (3.17s / 274.3 QPS vs 55.59s / 15.7 QPS for explicit CoT, over 10x throughput), while latent tokens still reconstruct CoT semantics at 0.788-0.804 cosine similarity, raise three-level codebook retrieval Hit@L1 from 0.354 to 0.401, and degrade MMLU less than explicit-CoT training.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of predicting conversion rates (CVR) online while protecting user privacy and meeting advertiser requirements. It proposes a novel framework using aggregated batch-level gradients instead of sample-level data during collaborative training, significantly reducing privacy risks by not sharing sensitive information directly with the platform.

The system further enhances efficiency through parameter-efficient fine-tuning combined with gradient compression techniques. To ensure accuracy despite privacy measures applied to labels (like differential privacy perturbations), it incorporates de-biasing methods that adjust model training appropriately.

Experimental results on industrial datasets show this approach achieves competitive ROCAUC performance, reduces communication overhead, and successfully balances high prediction accuracy with robust privacy guarantees for both users and advertisers.

## Technical note

This paper proposes a privacy-preserving framework for training large conversion-rate (CVR) models in a data clean room where the advertising platform holds features and the advertiser holds conversion labels. Unlike standard split learning, it exchanges only batch-level aggregated gradients (computed in the clean room from summed per-sample terms with very large batches) instead of sample-level gradients, preventing label leakage via gradient-matching attacks. It further adds label differential privacy with de-biasing to preserve accuracy under label perturbation, and reduces communication cost via adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning plus gradient compression. On industrial datasets it achieves competitive ROC-AUC while cutting communication overhead and satisfying advertiser and user privacy requirements.

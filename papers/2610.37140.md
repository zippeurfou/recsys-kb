## Summary

1.  Existing Transformer models for CTR prediction using standard index-based or token-level causal attention showed limited effectiveness, often matching a model with no positional encoding (NoPE).
2.  The paper proposes TRIP (Temporal Rank Interaction Transformer), which combines target-aware rank-time positional encoding to represent a user's historical behaviors relative to the current item, and temporal-group interaction modeling to capture relevant past events.
3.  TRIP significantly improves CTR prediction performance, achieving up to a 4.45% relative gain in AUC on four public benchmarks, by better aligning positional encoding with the task's needs and modeling interactions within specific time-related groups.

## Technical note

Tencent observes that on Transformer-based unified CTR models (OneTrans, HyFormer, MixFormer) none of six standard positional encodings (learned/sinusoidal APE, rank RoPE, T-RoPE, TO-RoPE, CAPE) beats no positional encoding on KuaiRand, while bidirectional attention within target-relative temporal groups beats both token-level causal and global bidirectional attention. TRIP combines Factorized Rank Encoding (low-rank additive ordinal embeddings with 3.8-21.6x fewer positional parameters than learned APE) with Target-Relative Time Encoding (coarse-to-fine elapsed time to the prediction timestamp, as density-corrected learnable attention potentials in TRIP-B or rotations in TRIP-R), and Temporal Group-Causal Interaction, which partitions the history into log-spaced target-relative groups with intra-group bidirectional and inter-group causal attention, a masked-item reconstruction auxiliary loss, and mean-pooled group summaries that shorten later layers. Across KuaiRec, KuaiRand, TAAC and TaobaoAd, TRIP-B beats eleven baselines on AUC with an average 1.47% and up to 4.45% relative gain (TAAC AUC 0.6337 vs 0.6067 for OneTrans); FRE+TRTE alone transfers to three backbones with up to 4.20% AUC gain over the best alternative encoding, and group-summary compression both raises AUC and cuts per-step training time by 6.3-32.8%.

## Summary

1.  Online advertising auto-bidding systems need to balance budget delivery and cost-per-action reduction, especially across different campaigns and changing market conditions.
2.  Pinterest developed RLBP, a hybrid system using reinforcement learning (a meta-controller) to daily select optimal campaign-specific parameters for its existing PID controllers, rather than using fixed parameters.
3.  RLBP achieved significant, statistically verified reductions in effective cost-per-click (eCPC) without harming revenue, demonstrating the successful application of RL to enhance a production bidding system.

## Technical note

Pinterest's auto-bidding pacing uses PID controllers whose fixed gains cannot fit hundreds of thousands of heterogeneous campaigns or the shifting cost-vs-delivery priority over a campaign's lifetime; RLBP keeps the PID bidder and uses RL only as a daily meta-controller that picks per-campaign multipliers for the proportional and integral gains from {1-a, 1, 1+a} (9 actions), written to a lookup table so no model runs at serving time. A multi-head Q-network (deployed with gamma=0, i.e. a contextual bandit) predicts budget-utilization and CPC outcomes separately using relative-lift rewards normalized against the production baseline, fuses them at inference with a weight beta(d) that decays as remaining campaign days d shrink (linear schedule chosen), and samples from a top-K softmax policy; training data come from a reserved uniformly random exploration slice and policies are screened offline with IPS. Offline, relative-lift rewards give +2.77% revenue lift on the final day and -3.02% CPC earlier vs +0.08% / -2.17% for absolute rewards. A 7-day online A/B test showed a statistically significant -0.25% eCPC with flat revenue (+0.43% revenue on lifetime-ended campaigns, up to -0.90% eCPC on high-budget campaigns), and two months of post-rollout holdout comparison showed -0.72% to -0.98% eCPC with +0.66% CTR.

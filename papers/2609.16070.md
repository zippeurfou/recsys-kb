## Summary

This paper introduces **NarraLite**, a framework for generative recommendation designed specifically for episodic content like TV series or movies, where recommendations must follow narrative progression rather than just match user preferences. NarraLite tackles efficiency challenges by combining **Progressive Spectral Compression** to reduce large visual inputs while preserving key information needed for story transitions, and **Latent Narrative Reasoning** to perform implicit story understanding without generating explicit text rationales. The authors develop a user-agnostic multimodal benchmark for short drama continuation and demonstrate through extensive experiments that NarraLite significantly improves recommendation accuracy, coherence, and robustness while achieving a better balance between performance and computational efficiency.

## Technical note

Tencent's NarraLite reframes generative recommendation for episodic short-form drama: the target segment is fixed by narrative progression rather than user preference, making the task user-agnostic story continuation, and the two bottlenecks are redundant long visual context and the autoregressive decoding cost of explicit chain-of-thought rationales. Progressive Spectral Compression conditions on a gated pooled text vector q_i, applies a 2D DCT to each frame's patch features, gates the frequency spectrum with sigma(MLP_f(q_i)) before an inverse DCT so text can adaptively preserve coarse scene structure or fine detail, then scores patch relevance against q_i and selects tokens with straight-through Gumbel Top-K -- top-K_a on the anchor frame and, for later frames, a larger candidate pool re-filtered by a cross-frame cosine diversity score so the same scene is not reselected. Latent Narrative Reasoning replaces generated rationales with a mixture-of-experts bank of input-independent learnable latent tokens: a lightweight router over the mean-pooled multimodal context selects K_r experts via straight-through Gumbel Top-K (deterministic at inference), the tokens are appended to the prefix [T; V^c; R], and a training-only InfoNCE objective aligns the mean-pooled final hidden states at those latent positions with a frozen teacher's encoding of the ground-truth continuation text. Total loss is L_SID + lambda * L_NCE, with SIDs produced by three-level RQ-KMeans (codebooks 512/256/256) over multimodal segment embeddings and inference via trie-constrained beam search returning 10 candidates. On Qwen-3.5-2B across 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with K_v=30 visual tokens and K_r=2 latent tokens, NarraLite leads a self-built 250k-pair benchmark (241,545 train pairs; UGC, PGC and OOD test splits where OOD isolates concluding episodes) at H@10 0.3933 on Eval and 0.3090 on OOD, versus 0.3583/0.2910 for LatentR3, 0.3458/0.2578 for TIGER, 0.3379/0.2487 for LETTER, 0.3483/0.2596 for the multimodal EARec and 0.2569/0.2127 for dense retrieval; SASRec, GRU4Rec and BERT4Rec trail far behind at H@10 ~0.11-0.14, showing preference-sequence models transfer poorly to narrative continuation. On their proposed Overlap-Penalized Semantic Coherence metric, which discounts predictions that merely reuse visually overlapping footage, NarraLite scores 0.5904 Eval and 0.5506 OOD versus 0.5587/0.5051 for LatentR3. Ablations remove each component (w/o LNR hurts most on OOD, w/o PSC most on UGC), K_v peaks at 30 and K_r at 2, and NarraLite strictly dominates all fixed-resolution full-frame variants in the accuracy-throughput plane. Caveats: offline only on an unreleased self-constructed benchmark with no online A/B, and the setting is deliberately user-agnostic, so this is a content-continuation model rather than a personalization model.

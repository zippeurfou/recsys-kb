## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Current multimodal language models (MLLMs) generating item descriptions for recommendation either ignore user feedback or require specific user data at generation time, limiting their adaptability and offline training potential.
2.  **Solution:** The proposed RecoReward method uses inferred behavior-derived rewards during *training* instead of inferring users at inference. It contrasts engagement signals from historical users (acting as proxies) against non-target users' observed affinities to guide the MLLM with a Recommender Affinity Score (RAS).
3.  **Results:** This approach improves recommendation performance significantly, both offline and online, while allowing for content-only description generation during user queries, thus preserving privacy and simplicity compared to methods needing user inputs at serving time.

## Technical note

MLLM-generated item descriptions used as recall-stage semantic features are normally produced content-only (no downstream signal) or user-conditioned (which turns a cacheable item representation into a per-user computation and requires serializing histories at inference). RecoReward (Kuaishou live-stream) instead injects user supervision only through the training reward: a frozen behavior-trained DSSM two-tower (user tower over history + description tower over generated text, trained with a behavior-weighted in-batch softmax where weights come from room entry / long view / follow / comment / like / gift) scores candidate descriptions, and the Recommender Affinity Score RAS_lambda(y;a) = (m_a^+ - lambda*m_a^-)^T v_y contrasts the mean embedding of the author's historically engaged target users against the mean of observational non-target users to strip out affinity shared broadly across active users. The behavioral analysis motivating this shows the proxy target center has cosine 0.7752 with the global user center and the non-target center 0.9255, while the corrected direction drops to 0.0960, lifting future-target-user ROC-AUC from 0.7603 to 0.8052 and the future-user margin from 0.1513 to 0.1616 (the two directions pick different descriptions for ~47.4% of authors). RAS plus a JSON-format term is used as a sequence-level reward for group-relative (GRPO-style) policy optimization; RecoReward-9B beats Qwen3.5-9B by 31.7-40.4% on all seven recall metrics (HR@10 0.9978% vs 0.7577%, MRR 0.5646% vs 0.4259%) and beats Gemini-3.1-Pro and GPT-5 descriptions. Ablations favor lambda=2, G=12 rollouts, and a surprisingly small reward user cap M=25 (larger M stabilizes the affinity estimate but washes out the target/non-target contrast); a one-week Kuaishou A/B gives +0.265% key-page effective-user penetration, +0.791% outflow exposure, +0.740% outflow users.

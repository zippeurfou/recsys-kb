## Summary

Existing translation benchmarks primarily measure semantic accuracy using metrics like BLEU, ignoring practical requirements like maintaining specific formats, glossaries, or registers. To address this gap, researchers introduced IFMTBench, a multilingual benchmark covering seven languages with diverse constraints and instruction types, evaluating both translation quality and constraint adherence through a unique scoring system. Evaluating 15 models revealed that instruction following improves more rapidly with model size than translation quality itself, and general instruction benchmarks don't strongly correlate with translation-specific performance.

## Technical note

IFMTBench is a benchmark for multilingual translation instruction following covering seven languages, with 4,506 single-constraint and 2,838 multi-constraint items across six constraint dimensions and five compositional patterns, with instructions issued in all seven languages. It partitions constraints into a gating subset verified by deterministic checkers (e.g., JSON schema validity, glossary term presence) and a continuous subset scored by a rubric-conditioned LLM judge, combined under a multiplicative rule that prevents soft scores from masking hard-constraint failures and resists reward hacking. It reports a two-dimensional view decoupling instruction following from translation quality measured by xCOMET-XXL. Evaluating 15 open- and closed-weight models shows instruction following scales with model size more sharply than translation quality (amplified an order of magnitude under multi-constraint composition), glossary and structured-format constraints dominate difficulty, and general instruction-following rankings correlate only weakly with translation behavior among top-tier models.

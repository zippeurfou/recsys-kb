## Summary

This paper reveals that in Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), the often-neglected scaling factor alpha plays a primary role in driving optimization effectively. Contrary to previous assumptions where it was treated merely as a complement to the learning rate, extensive empirical and theoretical analysis shows alpha significantly influences performance beyond standard hyperparameter adjustments.

The research uncovers three key findings: LoRA benefits from spectral suppression that smooths optimization but requires careful scaling; adjusting alpha accelerates convergence more effectively than just tuning the learning rate by amplifying task signal without exacerbating drift; and the optimal scaling factor follows a specific sublinear relationship, namely a square-root law with large coefficients, indicating existing rank-scaling heuristics are inadequate.

Based on these insights, the authors propose LoRA-alpha, a framework that restores alpha to its principal optimization role. This minimalist approach simplifies hyperparameter tuning by enabling standard small learning rates and consistently improves performance across diverse tasks.

## Technical note

This paper isolates the role of the LoRA scaling factor alpha (in Delta W = (alpha/r) BA), showing it is the dominant driver of effective optimization rather than a mere complement to the learning rate. A Signal-Drift framework explains why alpha amplifies the task-aligned signal without increasing the bilinear-induced drift ratio, whereas the learning rate amplifies both, and shows LoRA's spectral suppression smooths the Hessian and leaves standard hyperparameters overly conservative. The optimal alpha follows a sublinear square-root law in rank with an unexpectedly large coefficient, exposing under-scaling in common alpha=r or 2r heuristics. The proposed LoRA-alpha restores alpha to a principled regime, letting practitioners reuse standard small full-fine-tuning learning rates and improving results across model scales (184M-12B) and supervised, contrastive, and RL training.

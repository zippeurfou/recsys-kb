## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of designing efficient optimizers for Large Language Models (LLMs) that require low memory and fast convergence. The authors propose a framework based on approximating the structured Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), showing how existing state-of-the-art methods can be viewed as specific solutions to this FIM approximation problem under the Frobenius norm. Building on these insights, they offer design guidelines leading to two new optimizers, Row and Column Scaled SGD (RACS) and Adaptive low-dimensional subspace estimation (Alice), which are demonstrated through experiments to converge faster than Adam or standard SGD with significantly less memory overhead, particularly highlighting Alice's performance improvement over Adam.

## Technical note

This paper (an LLM optimizer design work, not recsys) frames efficient optimizers through structured Fisher information matrix (FIM) approximation under the Frobenius norm, showing Adam, Shampoo, GaLore, SOAP and gradient whitening are special cases with different structural assumptions (diagonal, Kronecker, block-diagonal). It gives two design recommendations: choose structures balancing generality and efficiency, and apply a novel low-rank extension framework to make general-structure optimizers memory-efficient. From these it derives RACS (Row and Column Scaled SGD, with SGD-like memory) and Alice (a low-rank extension of Eigenspace Adam). On LLaMA pre-training up to 1B parameters, Alice achieves >2x faster convergence than Adam and RACS performs strongly at 1B with low memory overhead.

## Summary

Large recommendation systems face scaling challenges with traditional transformers due to high computational costs associated with long user histories and large candidate sets. LIME addresses this by introducing two innovations: "link embeddings" precompute user-item attention weights decoupling interactions, making inference largely independent of candidate size, and "LIME-XOR" linear attention reducing sequence length complexity from O(N²) to O(N). Experiments show LIME achieves performance near state-of-the-art models but offers significant speedups (up to 10x) and drastically reduces inference costs for large datasets and long sequences.

## Technical note

LIME resolves the expressiveness-versus-efficiency trade-off between two-tower models and cross-attention transformers for CTR ranking through two innovations. Low-rank 'link embeddings' act as a fixed set of learned intermediary tokens bridging user history and candidate items, letting the expensive query-key attention be precomputed and cached offline so inference cost becomes nearly independent of candidate-set size. LIME-XOR attention uses an XOR mask to factorize full self-attention into bidirectional linear attention between link embeddings and history, reducing user-history complexity from O(N^2) to O(N). On public and industrial data LIME reaches near-parity with state-of-the-art transformers like HSTU at roughly 10x lower latency, and in production improved user engagement with up to 38% source-rate gain while keeping cost flat in candidate count and history length.

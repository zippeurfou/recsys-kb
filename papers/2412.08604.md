## Summary

1.  Current sequential recommendation systems primarily rely on past interactions, often using approximated user preferences only during training, which limits their ability to adapt dynamically and avoid reinforcing outdated or incorrect assumptions.
2.  This paper introduces "Preference Discerning" as a new paradigm where generative models explicitly use natural language representations of user preferences within their context for real-time recommendations.
3.  To evaluate this approach, the authors created a novel benchmark highlighting dynamic adaptation needs, finding that existing methods struggle with evolving preferences; they then proposed Mender, demonstrating it achieves state-of-the-art performance in adapting recommendations to human preferences even those unseen during training.

## Technical note

The paper introduces 'preference discerning,' a paradigm that conditions a generative sequential recommender on natural-language user preferences supplied in its context so it can adapt dynamically instead of relying only on interaction history. Preferences are approximated by prompting an LLM over user reviews and item descriptions, then a recommender is trained to consume them. The proposed Mender extends TIGER: items are tokenized into semantic IDs via an RQ-VAE over Sentence-T5 embeddings, and a randomly initialized decoder generates those IDs while cross-attending to a FLAN-T5 encoder over the language preferences and history. A new five-axis benchmark (preference-based rec, sentiment following, fine/coarse steering, history consolidation) shows existing generative retrievers steer poorly, while Mender achieves state-of-the-art and follows unseen preferences.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of limited personalization in sequential recommendation systems by introducing a novel approach called "preference discerning." Instead of relying solely on historical interactions, it explicitly conditions generative models on user preferences derived from reviews and item data using Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors propose Mender, an LLM-enhanced multimodal model that significantly outperforms existing methods in accurately identifying user preferences across various scenarios, even when these preferences haven't been seen during training.

## Technical note

The paper introduces 'preference discerning', a paradigm that explicitly conditions a generative recommendation model on user preferences expressed in natural language within its context, rather than using LLM-approximated preferences only at training time. It builds a holistic benchmark spanning five axes (preference-based recommendation, sentiment following, fine- and coarse-grained steering, history consolidation) and finds current generative-retrieval methods adapt poorly to evolving preferences. Their method Mender (Multimodal Preference Discerner) is a two-stage approach: an LLM distills user/item data into concise preferences (preference approximation), then a sequential/generative-retrieval model is conditioned on those preferences (preference conditioning), fusing collaborative semantics with pre-trained language encoders. Mender achieves state-of-the-art on most benchmark axes and can be steered by preferences unseen during training.

## Summary

Generative Recommendation (GR) systems face quality issues due to accumulated errors from single-pass decoding without refinement. To address this, the paper introduces GRC, a novel framework extending decoding into a structured Generation-Reflection-Correction process. GRC uses a template for initial generation, followed by multi-granular reflection and reflection-guided correction, optimized via reinforcement learning. Experiments show GRC significantly outperforms existing methods (up to 15.74%) and demonstrates strong practical value in real-world systems, improving ad revenue by 1.79% with minimal latency overhead.

## Technical note

GRC extends single-pass generative recommendation into an explicit Generation-Reflection-Correction process in the semantic-token space to curb error accumulation from teacher-forced, one-pass autoregressive decoding of RQ-VAE tokens. A supervised reflection-correction template serializes three components into one trajectory: draft generation, structured reflection (token-level error localization of the first deviating position plus semantic-level category/brand consistency judgments), and reflection-guided correction—avoiding free-form natural-language critiques that are hard to align with discrete tokens and add latency. GRPO-based RL then optimizes full GRC trajectories with token- and trajectory-level rewards scoring both retrieval quality and error-localization/correction behavior. For serving, Entropy-Guided Reflection Scheduling uses reflection-time token entropy to allocate a fixed correction budget to high-uncertainty beams. It beats six baselines by up to 15.74% and lifts ad revenue 1.79% online.

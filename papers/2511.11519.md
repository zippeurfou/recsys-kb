## Summary

Existing agentic AI systems struggle to adapt their complex reasoning strategies (involving LLM calls, tools, and logic) dynamically at inference time based on past experiences, often being either too rigid or only able to modify the input text. To overcome this, the authors introduce EGuR, an Experience-Guided Reasoner that uses an LLM-based meta-strategy to dynamically generate complete, tailored reasoning strategies on-the-fly, incorporating feedback from past problem-solving experiences. EGuR operates with a 'Guide' for strategy generation and a 'Consolidator' for improvement, significantly boosting accuracy (up to 14%) while drastically reducing computational costs (up to 111x) on challenging benchmarks, with performance improving as the system gains more experience.

## Technical note

Addresses the inability of agentic AI systems to adapt their full computational procedure (prompts, sampling parameters, tools, control logic) from experience at inference time, since prior memory methods only steer textual input and flexible methods require offline optimization. EGuR uses an LLM-based meta-strategy that generates complete, ready-to-run strategies conditioned on the current problem and a structured memory: a Guide proposes multiple candidate strategies while a Consolidator integrates execution feedback to improve future generation, and successful strategies are cached and retrieved for reuse. Strategies are formalized as compositions of stateful processes enabling cost tracking and tracing. Across AIME 2025, 3-SAT, and three Big Bench Extra Hard tasks it improves accuracy by up to 14% while reducing compute up to 111x, with both improving as experience accumulates.

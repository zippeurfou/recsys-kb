## Summary

This study used YouTube data and AI analysis tools (APIs) on colonoscopy preparation videos because this medical topic is often disliked by patients. It aimed to automatically extract relevant medical information from the complex video content itself, assessing patient understanding and recommendations without manual review. The goal was to provide healthcare stakeholders with a scalable way to create effective educational videos for better patient engagement across many health conditions.

## Technical note

This study builds a YouTube video-analysis pipeline to surface understandable, medically-relevant colonoscopy-preparation videos for patient education. It collects video metadata via the YouTube Data API and multimodal features (OCR text, speech-to-text transcripts, label detection) via Google Cloud Video Intelligence, annotates 312 videos for medical-information level, PEMAT understandability, and recommendation, and trains a BiLSTM to extract UMLS medical terms (F=0.960, beating a CRF baseline at 0.927). Three logistic-regression classifiers then predict medical-information level, understandability, and overall recommendation; the recommendation classifier reaches 0.947 accuracy, with number of unique medical terms a significant predictor. It is a healthcare informatics / video-classification paper rather than a personalized recommender.

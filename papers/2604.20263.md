## Summary

Existing virtual cell models struggle with inaccurate, uninterpretable, and weakly aligned predictions for genetic perturbations. To address this, AROMA integrates textual evidence, graph-topology, and protein sequence features with a two-stage training strategy to model perturbation effects more reliably and interpretably. The authors also created new datasets and show AROMA outperforms existing methods, remains robust on unseen cell lines, and handles challenging scenarios, demonstrating the promise of multimodal, knowledge-driven modeling for virtual cell prediction.

## Technical note

AROMA predicts differential expression (Up/Down/Non-diff) of a target gene under a perturbation gene in a given cell line, grounding predictions in structured biological evidence. It builds a Gene-KG (STRING+CORUM, 18k nodes, 700k edges) and a Path-KG (GO+Reactome, 80k nodes, 400k edges) and the 498k-sample PerturbReason dataset (from PerturbQA, with DeepSeek-R1 label-consistent reasoning traces). Retrieval-augmented contextualization combines gene functional descriptions, BFS-extracted shortest regulatory paths, and cell-line descriptions. Multimodal interaction encoding uses two GAT encoders (pretrained with edge-prediction BCE loss) on k-hop subgraphs plus frozen ESM-2 protein-sequence embeddings, fused by cross-attention. Two-stage training (multimodal SFT then GRPO reinforcement learning) yields accurate, interpretable predictions with strong zero-shot generalization to unseen cell lines and long-tail genes.

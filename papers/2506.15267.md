## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Recommendation systems struggle with new items lacking user interaction history, hindering their ability to gain initial traction and provide feedback for creators.
2.  **Proposed Solution:** The authors introduce "Next-User Retrieval," which uses generative modeling (based on transformer architectures) to predict the most likely future users an item would appeal to by analyzing sequences of similar recent users.
3.  **Results:** Offline experiments and online A/B tests show that Next-User Retrieval significantly improves cold-start recommendations, leading to measurable increases in both Daily Active Users (DAU) and publications on Douyin.

## Technical note

Item cold-start is critical because early success determines whether new items and creators are retained, yet systems rely heavily on item IDs and historical interactions that cold items lack. Inspired by lookalike algorithms, Next-User Retrieval reframes cold-start as generative next-user modeling: a transformer captures unidirectional relationships among recently interacted users and generates the next user most likely to interact with an item. Item features are injected as prefix prompt embeddings to guide the generation. Offline experiments and online A/B tests on Douyin show gains of +0.0142% daily active users and +0.1144% publications, demonstrating practical scalability.

## Summary

1.  Recommendation models face significant computational challenges when processing lifelong user behaviors after clicks due to long sequence lengths.
2.  Traditional two-stage approaches lose effectiveness by ignoring full context, and integrating standard multimodal embeddings directly is also computationally costly for large recommendation systems (LRMs).
3.  DMGIN effectively addresses this by using Multimodal LLMs to group repetitive items in the post-click stream without extra computation, then analyzes these groups internally and their sequence externally with transformers, resulting in significantly improved CTR prediction accuracy while maintaining efficiency compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

DMGIN tackles lifelong post-click CTR prediction where two-stage retrieval loses full-sequence context and raw multimodal embeddings are too costly. It uses a CLIP-like dual-tower MLLM to embed shops once, K-means clusters them into interest groups, and reorganizes tens-of-thousands-long behavior sequences into hundreds of group units with negligible overhead. Information loss from grouping is mitigated with group-level interest statistics plus intra-group transformers for interest evolution, and stacked HSTU inter-group transformers over temporally ordered groups, followed by candidate-aware target attention. On industrial and Amazon data it beats DIN/SIM/TWIN/TWIN-V2 baselines, and A/B testing shows +4.7% CTR and +2.3% RPM.

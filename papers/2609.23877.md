## Summary

Modern music streaming platforms face a challenge in recommending new artists, as users prefer familiar favorites over undiscovered ones. This paper presents a decoupled architecture that precomputes personalized recommendations and their natural language LLM rationales offline, isolating the expensive LLM inference. Online experiments showed that providing these explanations significantly reduced the trust barrier for new content, leading to increased exploration and overall user engagement on discovery surfaces.

## Technical note

YouTube Music wants to drive artist discovery, but users hesitate to pick unknown artists and real-time LLM inference is too costly. The system moves LLM work into an asynchronous offline phase: it generates user taste clusters, has an LLM nominate undiscovered artists with personalized natural-language rationales, canonicalizes entities against a Knowledge Graph, and serves the precomputed pools online with sub-second latency. A Gemini 3.1 Pro LLM-as-a-judge scores taste-cluster cohesion and candidate alignment on 1-5 rubrics and, for scores below 3, emits rationales and alternative artists whose aggregated loss patterns (semantic mismatch, entity hallucination, language inconsistency) drive prompt tuning, raising the pass rate (score >3) from 64.8% to 74.4% of sampled clusters. A large-scale online A/B test shows a statistically significant +22.43% lift in engagement on discovery surfaces and an expanded eligible discovery pool.

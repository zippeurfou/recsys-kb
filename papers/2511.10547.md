## Summary

This paper addresses the issue of low diversity in text-to-image (T2I) models, which often produce homogeneous outputs despite improving quality. It introduces a novel human evaluation framework using templates and prompts to systematically assess diversity by focusing on specific concepts and their factors of variation. The framework includes a methodology for comparing models via human annotations and binomial tests, enabling the identification of diversity strengths and weaknesses across different categories.

## Technical note

Addresses the lack of standardized, unconflated diversity evaluation for text-to-image models, which often produce homogeneous outputs and whose diversity is confounded with fidelity by metrics like FID. It proposes a framework that specifies both the concept assessed and its factor of variation (e.g., apple/color), a human evaluation template that splits judgment into counting and count-comparison subtasks, a curated prompt set of 86 concept-factor pairs, and binomial tests for model comparison. Using 24,591 human annotations across five models it ranks automatic metrics, finding the Vendi Score reaches roughly 65% (up to 80%) agreement with human diversity judgments when using an appropriate representation space. It identifies Imagen 3 and Flux 1.1 as top for attribute diversity. (Domain: image generation evaluation.)

## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Existing LLM-based embedding methods often suffer from "reasoning collapse," where either the LLM stops generating useful reasoning (passive feature extraction issue) or the reasoning itself becomes irrelevant to the embedding goal (active reasoning for optimization issue), degrading retrieval performance.
2.  **Solution:** To address this, the authors propose CoFree, a two-stage framework. The first stage restores the LLM's reasoning ability while maintaining its representation power. The second stage uses dual rewards (one for reasoning quality, one for embedding relevance) during reinforcement learning to guide the optimization process effectively.
3.  **Results:** CoFree successfully integrates reasoning into the embedding learning task, transforming it into a reasoning-guided process. Experiments show CoFree outperforms existing baselines significantly across multiple datasets, achieving average absolute 2.8 nDCG@10 improvements, and demonstrating consistent gains in online real-world retrieval systems.

## Technical note

LLM text embedders that generate reasoning before encoding suffer 'reasoning collapse' when specialized to embedding objectives: reasoning generation degrades or becomes fluent but retrieval-irrelevant. CoFree (Collapse-Free Reasoning Embedding) first applies reference-guided SFT on teacher-generated (Qwen3-235B-A22B) query/document reasoning to restore reasoning while preserving the base embedder, then GRPO-style RL with an embedding-oriented reward plus a reasoning-oriented reward so the generated reasoning itself discriminates relevance. CoFree-4B improves average nDCG@10 by 2.8 absolute points over Qwen3-Embedding-4B across 22 MTEB and BRIGHT datasets, and reranker-judged reasoning discrimination accuracy rises from 51.3% after SFT to 64.0% after full RL (about 51% without the reasoning reward). Deployed as an additional recall channel in Taobao e-commerce search, an online A/B test shows consistent CTR/CVR/order gains across the top product categories; the authors release RTED, a 3.6M-instance reasoning-augmented retrieval dataset.

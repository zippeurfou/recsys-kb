## Summary

Self-Improving VLMs Can Regress on New Tasks":

Verifier-driven self-DPO (Discriminative Policy Optimization), common in improving visual-language models, assumes stronger verifiers always make better students. The paper demonstrates this assumption fails because verifier effectiveness is highly dependent on the specific task. Verifiers that score top candidates and successfully update a model for one task can actually cause regression when used for DPO training on a different task, sometimes even worse than random performance.

This failure occurs specifically when verifiers drop below an optimal "threshold" accuracy on a new task; their quality degrades enough to silently worsen student performance, despite the DPO loss decreasing. Crucially, within this failing regime, the damage is often more severe from highly confident but incorrect ("more accurate-but-still-wrong") verifiers than those performing nearly randomly.

The authors explain this using a variance theorem for progress-gated replay and suggest practical deployment guidelines: measure target-task verifier accuracy first (the operational message), rank verifiers by task-specific quality rather than size, and be aware of diminishing returns above basic thresholds as an indicator the verifier might not improve further.

## Technical note

This work shows the monotonicity assumption behind verifier-driven self-DPO (a stronger verifier yields a stronger student) fails because verifier quality is highly task-specific. On a four-rung Qwen2.5/3-VL verifier ladder, verifiers that are above-threshold on MathVista drop to 8-23% task-rubric accuracy on MMMU, where every tested verifier silently regresses the student by -3.4 to -10.9 pp even as DPO training loss keeps decreasing. Damage is confidence-inverted: a more accurate-but-still-wrong verifier causes larger regression than a near-random one, explained by a variance theorem for progress-gated replay amplifying confidently wrong preference pairs. The authors recommend measuring task-rubric accuracy before running the loop, ranking verifiers by target-task rubric quality rather than parameter count, and treating diminishing returns as a compute-budget cap.

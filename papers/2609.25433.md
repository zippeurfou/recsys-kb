## Summary

Modern recommender systems face bottlenecks when adding new prediction tasks, primarily due to expensive retraining and potential conflicts with existing tasks. To accelerate multi-task experimentation in production, the paper introduces the Lightweight Ranking Heads (Light Heads) framework, which allows for the dynamic injection of new tasks. This framework isolates new tasks through stop-gradients and stateless daily training, significantly reducing iteration cycles from weeks to days and enabling faster testing and deployment of ranking models.

## Technical note

Adding a new task to YouTube's large multi-task ranking models risks negative transfer, requires cold-starting the backbone, and stalls downstream reward and score-combination models that need uniform prediction spaces across dozens of concurrent experiment models. Light Heads define new shallow heads in a central configuration that is dynamically injected into all continuously trained ranking models at the main-head layer, with stop-gradients so the shared towers are unaffected and a stateless reset at the start of every training run so head quality is consistent across models (with fallback defaults if a head is missing). On YouTube Home and Watch Next, Light Heads reach near-parity with co-trained full heads on sparse tasks (E(Positive Sentiment) AUC 0.9553 vs 0.9557) and are slightly worse on dense ones (P(CTR) 0.772 vs 0.781), while removing stop-gradients degrades the full heads (P(CTR) AUC 0.7767 to 0.7704). The framework cut ranker-plus-downstream experiment time from 24 to 11 days (joint-experiment start from 17 to 5 days), and launched heads gave +0.03% top-line engagement with 0.40% fewer low-quality impressions (long-horizon reward head) and +13.83% primetime-specific engagement.

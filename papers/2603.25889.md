## Summary

1.  This paper presents a method using Siamese neural networks for personalized calibration in polarization-based eye tracking systems integrated into head-mounted displays.
2.  By learning relative gaze displacements from just 10 calibration frames (compared to traditional methods needing more), the approach significantly reduces the calibration burden, leveraging polarization-sensitive camera inputs.
3.  The results show this Siamese personalization outperforms standard linear calibration with fewer samples and achieves accuracy comparable to traditional methods, while polarization inputs further reduce error by up to 12% compared to near-infrared inputs.

## Technical note

The work benchmarks Siamese-network personalization for polarization-enabled eye tracking (PET) on a 338-subject dataset captured with a polarization-sensitive camera under 850 nm illumination. A shared-weight Siamese model predicts relative gaze displacements between a test image and anchor calibration images, then reconstructs absolute binocular gaze (yaw/pitch per eye), turning calibration into a few-shot problem that needs ~10x fewer samples than linear regression calibration. Polarization inputs (raw 0/45/90/135-degree channels or derived intensity/DoLP/AoLP features) reduce gaze error by up to 12% versus near-infrared inputs, which is especially useful when the iris is occluded and only sclera is visible. Combining Siamese predictions with a lightweight linear post-calibration gives further gains of ~13% at P50 over a linearly calibrated baseline.

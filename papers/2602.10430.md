## Summary

1.  Generative recommendation using policy-based RL often suffers from "model collapse" when trained on offline logs containing low-quality data.
2.  The paper introduces the "Divergence Theory of Repulsive Optimization," explaining that existing methods fail due to negative gradient updates causing instability, and proposes "Optimistic Distributionally Robust Optimization" (ODRO) to overcome this "curse of repulsion."
3.  To solve this, they developed DRPO, which effectively filters out low-quality data by acting as a hard filter, optimally recovering high-quality behaviors and achieving state-of-the-art results.

## Technical note

DRPO tackles model collapse in offline/off-policy RL for generative recommendation where Zipfian data is dominated by low-quality negative-advantage samples. The paper establishes a Divergence Theory of Repulsive Optimization proving that negative-advantage gradients on a Gaussian policy form an expansion mapping driving exponential logit/gradient explosion, and shows soft-weighting methods (AWR, AsymRe) are trapped in a convergence-denoising paradox (noise cloning vs. variance collapse). It reformulates target identification as an Optimistic (max-max) Distributionally Robust Optimization whose closed-form solution is a hard-thresholding (Top-kappa) distribution, implemented via a CVaR variational dual as truncated weighted regression with a variance-guided adaptive kappa curriculum. DRPO achieves SOTA on RecSim simulations, remains robust at 80% noise, and enables safe offline-to-online transition.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of finding diverse yet relevant results in nearest neighbor search (NNS), a problem common in applications like recommendation systems and RAG. It introduces novel "welfarist" formulations based on economic welfare functions, specifically Nash social welfare, to balance query-dependent relevance and diversity across different attributes. These formulations enable a flexible trade-off between the two objectives, and the authors developed efficient algorithms that build upon standard approximate nearest neighbor methods, demonstrating improved diversity without sacrificing relevance through extensive experiments.

## Technical note

The paper addresses balancing relevance and diversity in nearest neighbor search for applications like recommendation and RAG, where prior constraint-based methods force a fixed diversity quota. It introduces welfarist formulations from mathematical economics—principally Nash social welfare (geometric mean of per-attribute utilities) and the more general p-mean welfare—that adaptively trade off relevance and attribute diversity in a query-dependent way without a quota parameter. For the single-attribute setting it gives a greedy algorithm that can wrap any sublinear ANN method as a subroutine; for the multi-attribute setting it proves NP-hardness and provides a (1-1/e) approximation for maximizing log-NSW. Experiments on real and semi-synthetic datasets show substantially improved diversity while maintaining high relevance.

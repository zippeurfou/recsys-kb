## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of ranking diverse content types (like articles, videos, UGC) effectively in a large-scale unified feed like Google Discover. It introduces HA-MoE, a heterogeneity-adaptive architecture that splits incoming queries into distinct segments and routes them to specialized experts based on explicit context about the query's type or source. This system is further supported by LENS for monitoring expert performance across continuous retraining, demonstrating consistent improvements in offline ranking tests measured with DL-AUC and confirming effectiveness gains from online A/B testing metrics like feed activity and exploration.

## Technical note

Google Discover ranks one unified feed over heterogeneous open-web content (web articles, long- and short-form video, UGC, sports cards, generative-AI summaries) whose feature densities and engagement patterns differ sharply, so the production shared-MLP backbone with 11 task heads suffers negative transfer, seesaw between pInterest and pDisinterest objectives, and minority-type collapse. HA-MoE keeps a dense 4-expert multi-gate MoE (FFN experts, orthogonal init, N chosen from {2,4,8,16}) but injects explicit heterogeneity signals h (content_type, followed-creator status, shoppable, AI-card flags) twice: heterogeneity-aware gating g_t = Softmax(W_t[x||h] + b_t), and Heterogeneity-Driven Linear Modulation, a FiLM-style late-layer affine transform E_n_tilde = gamma_n(h) * E_n(x) + beta_n(h). Training combines pointwise BCE over the 11 heads with a pairwise RankNet loss over co-impressed positive/negative pairs in the same session, L = alpha*L_p + (1-alpha)*L_r, with GradNorm-style dynamic task weights, and model selection uses Dual-Level AUC = 0.8*Micro-AUC + 0.2*Macro-xAUC. On a 7-day ~10M-example holdout HA-MoE reaches 0.691 pInterest / 0.949 pDisinterest DL-AUC versus 0.679/0.939 for shared MLP and 0.686/0.934 for standard MMoE (which regresses pDisinterest), and narrows the cross-type xAUC gap between Web+ vs Vid- and Vid+ vs Web- from 0.141 to 0.060 by lifting the video pair from 0.830 to 0.900. A LENS observability layer slices gating activations into row-normalized content-by-expert matrices and scores cross-snapshot expert stability via Permutation-Invariant Expert Matching (per-expert task profiles compared by Jensen-Shannon divergence, aligned with the Hungarian algorithm); 1% online A/B tests yielded +0.22% DAU, +0.48% viewed impressions, +0.34% scroll depth and +0.54% diverse engagement rate at under 5% model-size and under 0.5% p50/p95 latency overhead.

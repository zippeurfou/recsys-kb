## Summary

This paper introduces a novel approach to using Large Language Models (LLMs) in ad recommendation systems, not as a primary ranker, but as a complementary predictor specifically forecasting likely advertisers based on user data. This fine-tuned LLM provides ancillary information that enhances conventional candidate generation and improves the performance of downstream ranking algorithms. The approach was validated in a large-scale production environment, showing significant offline improvements and measurable positive business impact, demonstrating how targeted LLM predictions can boost end-to-end ad recommendation effectiveness.

## Technical note

This Pinterest paper uses a fine-tuned open-source LLM in an ads system not as a ranker but as an ads-specific ancillary predictor that forecasts likely advertisers from user profiles and conversion histories, providing informative priors to conventional candidate generation and downstream ranking. It addresses core mismatches between LLM token semantics and ID-centric sparse ads features by defining an advertiser-prediction task with a conversion layer bridging advertiser names and IDs, plus a data-compilation and prompt recipe over on- and off-platform histories (past conversions, top advertisers/brands/categories/URLs, demographics). Predictions are injected two ways: advertiser-targeted candidate filtering in retrieval and featurized inputs to late-stage conversion models. Daily inference is restricted to a high-value user segment to respect latency and cost budgets. Developed in a large-scale production advertising stack, it yields consistent offline AUC gains and measurable online business impact, showing targeted LLM predictions complement collaborative and feature-engineered signals across retrieval and ranking.

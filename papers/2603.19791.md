## Summary

This paper introduces Narriva, a method for simulating user privacy decisions by creating text-based synthetic personas. Instead of relying solely on demographic attributes or raw data histories, Narriva generates personas grounded in actual past privacy behaviors from surveys, structured by privacy theory, offering better accuracy and token efficiency. Benchmarks show Narriva achieves high predictive accuracy (88%) and significant token savings (80-95%) while effectively representing population-level privacy behaviors.

## Technical note

Narriva (Narrative Personas for Privacy) generates concise text-based synthetic personas to simulate human privacy decisions, aiming to jointly balance four competing dimensions that prior methods trade off: predictiveness, human auditability, scalability (bounded context), and population representation. Instead of concatenating raw data-sharing histories (predictive but not scalable/auditable) or using demographic stereotypes (scalable but biased and non-predictive), Narriva compresses large-scale survey data (Pew PP1/W49/W127, ACM CHI CAuthN, PETS SPA) into human-readable narratives structured by privacy theories such as Privacy Calculus and Bounded Rationality, and iteratively refines a persona against a user's historical decisions. It achieves up to 87-88% individual predictive accuracy (6-17 pp over a non-personalized LLM baseline) with 80-95% prompt-token reduction versus in-context learning on raw examples, condensing histories up to 20x. Personas synthesized from one survey reproduce another study's aggregate distributions, though transferability degrades over ~decade-long temporal gaps.

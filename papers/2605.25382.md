## Summary

1.  The paper introduces AuthTrace, a new diagnostic benchmark designed to evaluate different evidence construction systems (like retrieval or memory) within single-author corpora, solving the problem of incompatible existing benchmarks.
2.  AuthTrace uses a single challenging corpus and query set, analyzing system performance across a gradient of evidence needed (fan-in), and provides detailed gold-standard evidence for evaluation.
3.  The key findings are: evidence recall strongly predicts answer quality, the fan-in gradient reveals distinct failure modes for different systems, and full-context prompting is ineffective, highlighting the essential role of proper evidence construction.

## Technical note

AuthTrace is a diagnostic benchmark for evidence construction built on thematically dense single-author Chinese prose corpora (860 works, 2,099 QA instances) where near-miss distractors share style and vocabulary with gold evidence. It introduces an evidence fan-in gradient—the number of distinct source documents required—as the primary diagnostic axis, grouping instances into single-doc, low multi-doc, and high multi-doc, and scores systems under a unified pack-level protocol of Evidence Recall, Evidence Precision, and Answer Correctness. Across eight systems (flat RAG, HippoRAG2, Mem0, EverMemOS, LLM-Wiki), evidence recall is the dominant predictor of correctness (r=0.96) while precision correlates negatively, showing failures stem from missing evidence rather than synthesis. Fan-in exposes paradigm-specific collapse: flat retrieval degrades 2-3x faster than thematic organization, yielding a crossover heuristic favoring graph retrieval at fan-in 1 and thematic indexing at fan-in >=2.

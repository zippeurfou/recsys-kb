## Summary

Multimodal recommender systems (MRS) combine different data types like text, images, and user behavior. Recent large language models (LLMs), with their advanced semantic reasoning capabilities beyond earlier language models, offer new ways to enhance these MRS. This survey explores LLM integration methods and proposes a taxonomy for understanding how they improve recommendations across various modalities, while also outlining evaluation approaches and suggesting future research directions.

## Technical note

This is a survey of large language models applied to multimodal recommender systems, organized around an LLM-centric taxonomy rather than the encoder-centric taxonomies of prior surveys. It categorizes work into LLM methods (hard/soft prompting, chain-of-thought and in-context reasoning prompts, fine-tuning and parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA/QLoRA, and data-type adaptation for tabular/behavioral inputs) and MRS-specific techniques (disentanglement, alignment, fusion). It highlights transferable techniques from adjacent RS domains and catalogs evaluation metrics (including NLG and LLM-based evaluators) and multimodal datasets. It points to open directions such as multimodal agents, tabular/numerical modality integration, and cost/scalability of black-box LLM APIs.

- Hit Rate (HR@K): Measures whether at least one relevant item appears in the top-K list()
- NDCG@K (Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain): Accounts for the position of relevant items in the ranked list ()
- MRR (Mean Reciprocal Rank): Evaluates the rank of the first relevant item for each user ()
- MAP (Mean Average Precision): Computes the average precision per user over the top-K results, less frequently used but useful in ranking-sensitive setups ()
- LogLoss (Binary Cross-Entropy): Captures the deviation between predicted probabilities and binary ground-truth labels (). Often paired with AUC to evaluate both confidence and ranking:
- AUC (Area Under the Curve): Measures the probability that a positive item is ranked above a negative one ()
- Group AUC (GAUC) () Computes AUC separately for each user (or group) and then averages the result, weighted by impression count:
- Relative Improvement (RelaImpr): Quantifies AUC improvement over a baseline, normalised by the AUC gap from random chance (0.5) ()
- Return: Total reward accumulated per recommendation episode 
- Trajectory Length: Duration of user engagement within a session 
- Average Reward: Mean reward per action, reflecting policy effectiveness 
- CLIPScore : A reference-free metric that uses CLIP’s image-text embeddings to assess the alignment between generated textual explanations and visual content, without relying on human-written references. Used in multimodal explanation generation tasks 
- Catalog Coverage (): represents the fraction of catalog items that appeared in at least one top-n recommendation list of the users in the test set. These metrics are commonly used in general or sequential RS to evaluate diversity and coverage, as in 
- Serendipity(): measures the average number of correct recommendations for each user that are not recommended by a popularity baseline
- Novelty(): computes the negative log of the relative item popularity, or self-information
- Fluency, Relevance, Diversity: Rated by human annotators, often via crowdsourcing. Some works also report automatic diversity metrics such as distinct-n 
- Polarisation: Captures user reactions or self-reported shifts in sentiment or preference 
- Popularity: Measures the distribution and frequency of tags across similar objects to detect spammy or overly generic labels 
- Practicality: Assesses informativeness and efficiency of tag sets via least effort (tag count per object) and facet coverage (semantic breadth) 
- Uniformity: Quantifies semantic redundancy among tags to detect divergence or noise in tagging behavior 
- Qualitative Journey Coherence: Involves manual inspection of extracted user journeys to assess coherence and interpretability 
- User Studies and A/B Tests: Used to compare variants of generated journeys, names, or recommendations 
- Domain-specific Metrics: For instance, Clinical Relevance Score (CRS) rates medical appropriateness on a 1–5 scale, while Coverage Rate (CR) measures the proportion of relevant diagnostic tests included 
- LLM-based Evaluation (Win/Tie/Lose, Advantage): Some works substitute human judges with LLMs like ChatGPT , comparing outputs from different systems in terms of detail, relevance, and conciseness. - Click-through Rate (CTR): Measures the proportion of users who click on a recommended item. It serves as a primary indicator of immediate recommendation effectiveness 
- Gross Merchandise Value (GMV): Represents the total monetary value of transactions resulting from the recommender system, reflecting its commercial impact 
- User Interaction Count (likes, shares, comments): Captures deeper forms of engagement beyond clicks, such as video play time, likes, and collections

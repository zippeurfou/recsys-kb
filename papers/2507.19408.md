## Summary

This paper investigates **predictive multiplicity**, where multiple valid medical ML models give different individual predictions despite fitting the data well. It finds that this issue, often hidden by standard metrics and arbitrary development choices, reveals patients whose diagnoses vary significantly between plausible models. The study shows that using small ensembles combined with abstention strategies can effectively reduce predictive multiplicity in practice, making reliable predictions more consistent even if accuracy isn't guaranteed to eliminate it entirely, especially when consensus is low.

## Technical note

The paper empirically studies predictive multiplicity in medical AI: models with statistically indistinguishable accuracy can give conflicting per-patient predictions. Training 1,500 models across five medical imaging datasets and four architectures, the authors use Clopper-Pearson intervals to define an empirical Rashomon set and an Adjusted Pairwise Prediction Agreement (APPA) metric for per-sample stability. They find validation performance fails to identify a uniquely optimal model, higher model capacity reduces multiplicity by improving accuracy, and ensembles with an abstention/deferral strategy improve both expected per-sample accuracy and stability. They argue multiplicity should be a core part of model evaluation in safety-critical domains.

## Summary

With growing emphasis on privacy regulations, machine unlearning has become increasingly critical in real-world applications such as social networks and recommender systems, many of which are naturally represented as graphs. However, existing graph unlearning methods often modify nodes or edges indiscriminately, overlooking their impact on fairness. ## Technical note

FROG (Fair Removal on Graphs) is the first framework to study and mitigate how graph unlearning alters graph topology and inadvertently amplifies unfairness (disparate impact) in GNNs, e.g., increasing same-gender link recommendations after edge deletions. It jointly optimizes graph structure and the model: rewiring the graph by adding edges to counter deletion-induced bias while removing redundant edges that hinder forgetting, and it is adaptable to any graph-based unlearning method. It also introduces a 'worst-case forget set' evaluation, and experiments on real-world datasets show more effective and fairer unlearning than baselines like GNNDelete and GNNCon.

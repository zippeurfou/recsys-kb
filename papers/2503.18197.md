## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of implementing machine unlearning for privacy-sensitive applications like social networks, which often use graph data. Existing methods focus solely on structural changes without considering fairness implications after removal. The authors propose FROG, a novel approach that simultaneously optimizes both the graph structure and model parameters to efficiently remove data while preserving fairness through targeted edge modifications.

## Technical note

The paper studies how graph unlearning (edge/node removal from trained GNNs) inadvertently increases network homophily and harms group fairness in link prediction. It proves that dyadic fairness (Δ_DP) is bounded by the homophily ratio, motivating structural intervention. FROG casts fair unlearning as a bi-level Stackelberg (leader-follower) game: a leader performs fair edge augmentation (adding inter-group edges) while a follower performs sparse structure unlearning (pruning redundant edges), jointly optimizing graph topology and model parameters with a plug-and-play unlearning loss. It also introduces a worst-case forget set to stress-test unlearning, and experiments on real social-network datasets show more effective and fairer unlearning than GNNDelete/GNNCon baselines.

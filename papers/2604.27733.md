## Summary

This paper addresses theoretical inconsistencies in popular LLM alignment methods like DPO, which use standard loss surrogates inconsistent with the models' continuous nature, leading to weak generalization guarantees. The authors propose Structure-Aware H-consistency, introducing a margin-based framework and a novel objective (SA-DPO) that adapts the margin based on response similarity to handle synonyms and difficult pairs. Finally, they analyze the trade-off between model capacity and consistency, showing that heavy-tailed loss families like Polynomial Hinge offer better consistency guarantees for capacity-limited models than standard losses.

## Technical note

This paper analyzes theoretical inconsistencies in LLM alignment methods like DPO, whose standard loss surrogates are not H-consistent with the models' continuous nature and thus offer weak generalization guarantees. It introduces Structure-Aware H-consistency, a margin-based framework and a new objective SA-DPO that adapts the margin based on response similarity to better handle synonyms and hard preference pairs, defining the structure-aware margin as a scaled cosine distance from BGE-Large-v1.5 embeddings and calibrating the scale so the average margin matches a tuned SimPO fixed margin. The analysis characterizes a trade-off between model capacity and consistency, showing heavy-tailed loss families such as Polynomial Hinge give better consistency guarantees than standard losses for capacity-limited hypothesis classes. Derivations decompose the estimation error into a surrogate excess-risk term plus a structure-aware approximation gap, providing formal grounding for the proposed objective.

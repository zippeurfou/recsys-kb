## Summary

Standard time series models predict numerical values, but decision-makers require more nuanced predictive advice (direction, reasoning, risk). Training LLMs for this advisory task is difficult because the quality of advice depends on future outcomes, unknown during prediction. This paper introduces Hindsight Preference Optimization (HPO), which retrospectively uses observed outcomes to have an LLM judge and rank candidate advisories, generating preference data for training without human annotation. Applying HPO to Vision-Language Models on financial data showed that a smaller model significantly outperformed a larger teacher model in both accuracy and advisory quality.

## Technical note

Hindsight Preference Optimization (HPO) trains LLMs for predictive advisory (directional forecasts with reasoning and risk management) where quality depends on outcomes unknown at prediction time. It supplies an LLM-as-a-Judge with ground-truth future outcomes so the judge can rank candidate advisories on reasoning, scenario calibration, and risk dimensions that scalar metrics miss, producing DPO preference pairs without human annotation. The pipeline first does SFT distilling a large teacher's hindsight-selected best candidates into a student VLM, then on-policy Hindsight DPO with an SFT anchor on chosen responses to prevent likelihood degradation. Applied to candlestick charts of S&P 500 equities (2013-2017 OHLC), a Qwen3-VL-4B student outperforms its 235B teacher on directional accuracy, top-1 scenario accuracy, and pairwise preference judged by Claude Opus 4.

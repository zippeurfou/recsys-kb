## Summary

Based on the provided abstract, here's a summary in 3 sentences:

1.  Standard global evaluation criteria for physical video consistency are inadequate because different Vision-Language Models (VLMs) perceive physics through unique internal representations due to varying training data and architectures.
2.  The paper introduces JudgeFit, an iterative method that identifies and refines the specific dimensions each VLM uses for its evaluations by prompting it with videos and clustering responses, then calibrating these dimensions against human ratings until reliable per-model taxonomies are achieved.
3.  Applying JudgeFit to various models revealed significantly different performance profiles based on their learned physics dimensions; importantly, the refined scores were more accurate than a global baseline method for every VLM tested, highlighting model-specific blind spots that aren't captured by overall rankings.

## Technical note

JudgeFit is a seed-and-refine pipeline that discovers a per-VLM evaluation taxonomy for judging physical consistency in generated videos, arguing the evaluation schema should be a property of the evaluator rather than a fixed global benchmark rubric. In the seed stage the target VLM enumerates physics errors in free-form text over a stratified annotation set and an LLM clusters these into an initial taxonomy grounded in the VLM's own perceptual vocabulary; in the refine stage a ridge-regression calibrator maps the VLM's per-dimension scores to human physical-commonsense ratings, diagnoses unreliable/redundant/missing dimensions, and an LLM editor proposes local edits (drop, redefine, merge, add, split) accepted only if they improve a selection objective rewarding both predictive and distinct dimensions. The loop converges within roughly two rounds, which matters because each candidate requires re-scoring every video with the expensive VLM. Applied to 16 VLMs across eight model families using VideoPhy-2 human ratings, refined taxonomies beat the global schema on held-out videos for every model (mean ~32% relative improvement) and reveal that overall standing does not predict competence on any particular physical rule.

## Summary

This paper argues that weight decay, often used but poorly understood in Collaborative Filtering (CF) training pipelines, primarily serves to encode popularity information into embedding vector magnitudes. The authors find tuning weight decay acts as a non-linear knob influencing whether the model prefers popular or unpopular items during training. Consequently, they propose PRISM, which pre-initializes these magnitudes with popularity data, eliminating the need for extensive weight decay tuning while improving performance and reducing training times.

## Technical note

The paper asks why weight decay is essential when training collaborative-filtering models with angle-based losses (e.g., DirectAU, SSM) that are magnitude-invariant. Through theoretical and empirical analysis it shows weight decay's true role is to encode item-popularity information into embedding magnitudes via asymmetric mini-batch gradient updates, and that removing it collapses angle-based losses below simple BPR. It proposes PRISM, a popularity-aware initialization of embedding magnitudes that pre-encodes this signal, eliminating weight-decay tuning. PRISM improves accuracy by up to 4.77%, cuts training time by 38.48%, and its strength parameter lets one modulate popularity bias toward unpopular items.

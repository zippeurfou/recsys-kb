## Summary

1.  Existing retrieval systems either use separate stages for candidate generation and ranking (often with LLMs) or decouple them, but both face challenges: the joint process suffers from poor credit assignment (difficulty knowing if failures are due to generation or ranking), while decoupling limits overall effectiveness due to feedback limitations.
2.  To address this, the authors propose F-GRPO, a unified framework that uses a single LLM pass to generate and rank candidates, factorizing the policy into generation and ranking phases while sharing the model backbone.
3.  F-GRPO optimizes these phases jointly using a combined reward and a novel two-phase sequence-level objective that assigns credit specifically to each phase, leading to improved top-ranked performance compared to previous methods across different benchmarks, all while maintaining inference compatibility with the underlying LLM.

## Technical note

F-GRPO tackles the coupled list-to-rank problem where an LLM generates a candidate slate and ranks it in a single autoregressive rollout, but sequence-level utility feedback conflates coverage and ordering. It factorizes the policy into candidate generation and ranking over a shared LLM backbone, trained end-to-end with an order-invariant coverage reward and a position-aware utility reward. Two-phase sequence-level credit assignment gives separate group-relative advantages to each phase, resolving the credit-assignment gap that destabilizes plain GRPO. On sequential recommendation (MovieLens, LastFM) and multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, MuSiQue) with Qwen3 models, it improves Recall@k and NDCG@k over GRPO, decoupled, and supervised baselines with no inference-time changes.

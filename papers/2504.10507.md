## Summary

This paper introduces PinRec, a novel generative retrieval model designed for large-scale recommendation systems. It addresses current limitations by enabling users to specify how to balance different business outcomes (like saves and clicks) and by generating diverse item recommendations. Experiments show PinRec successfully balances performance, diversity, and efficiency, marking a significant step in deploying generative models at Pinterest's scale.

## Technical note

PinRec is a unified generative retrieval model serving all of Pinterest's surfaces (home feed, search, related pins), pretrained on cross-surface user activity sequences then fine-tuned per surface on impression data. It uses outcome-conditioned generation to optimize surface-specific business metrics (e.g., saves for home feed, outbound clicks for search) by generating a sequence of Pin tokens conditioned on a target outcome, and follows the embedding-based generative-retrieval paradigm (continuous outputs retrieved via ANN) to avoid ID hallucination and the hourglass collapse of token-ID approaches. Multi-token autoregressive generation captures within-session intent shifts, yielding up to +71% recall gains, with serving optimizations for low-latency decoding at hundreds of millions of users. Deployed across three surfaces, it delivers gains such as +4% search saves, representing horizontal (cross-surface) rather than vertical (retrieval+rank) unification.

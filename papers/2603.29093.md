## Summary

1.  LLM-based autonomous agents often lack persistent procedural memory, needing to re-derive solutions for structurally similar tasks. To address this, the paper introduces **APEX-EM**, a non-parametric online learning framework that stores and reuses structured procedural plans and episodic experiences without modifying the underlying LLM weights.
2.  **APEX-EM** features three key components: a structured experience representation encoding detailed execution traces and quality scores; a PRGII workflow incorporating task verifiers for feedback; and a dual-outcome experience memory with advanced hybrid retrieval methods.
3.  Evaluated across diverse benchmarks (KGQAGen-10k, BigCodeBench, Humanity's Last Exam), **APEX-EM** demonstrated significant performance improvements (+48.3pp, +29.4pp, +22.8pp respectively) by effectively reusing past successes and failures, showing the importance of structured memory for autonomous learning.

## Technical note

APEX-EM is a non-parametric online-learning framework that lets frozen-backbone LLM agents accumulate, retrieve, and reuse structured procedural-episodic experience without weight updates, acting as an experience-replay buffer for agents. It stores each execution as a Procedural Knowledge Graph capturing planning steps, entity/schema discovery, generated artifacts, iteration history with error analysis, quality scores, and an abstract 'structural signature' (operation sequence) enabling cross-domain transfer across surface-dissimilar tasks. The PRGII workflow (Plan-Retrieve-Generate-Iterate-Ingest) uses Task Verifiers producing multi-dimensional rewards (correctness, completeness, efficiency, error category), with quality-gated commitment where successes act as positive in-context examples and failures as negative examples with structured annotations—the procedural analog of hindsight experience replay. Hybrid retrieval blends semantic search, structural-signature matching, and plan-graph traversal; against MemRL and baselines (RAG, Self-RAG, Mem0, MemP, Reflexion) on KGQAGen-10k, BigCodeBench, and Humanity's Last Exam, it improves KGQAGen accuracy by +48.3pp over no-memory.

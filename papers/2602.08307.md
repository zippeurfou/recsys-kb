## Summary

This paper addresses the limitation of prior Interaction-Grounded Learning (IGL) algorithms, which only solved single-step problems, by extending them to handle multi-step sequential decision-making in contextual episodic Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with personalized feedback. The authors achieve this by developing a computationally efficient algorithm with sublinear regret, which involves extending a specific reward-estimator construction from single-step to multi-step settings and designing an Inverse-Gap-Weighting (IGW) optimization method based on this estimator. They demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, showing it can learn personalized objectives from multi-turn user interactions.

## Technical note

The paper extends Interaction-Grounded Learning (IGL)—learning from indirect feedback generated by an unknown mechanism rather than explicit rewards—from single-step contextual bandits to multi-step contextual episodic MDPs with personalized feedback, motivated by multi-turn LLM deployments. It builds a reward estimator via reachable-state identification, inverse-kinematic learning of action posteriors under a uniform policy, and a Lipschitz reward estimator that generalizes a single-step bandit construction to the multi-step setting. Using this estimator it designs an Inverse-Gap-Weighting policy-learning algorithm with a provable O(T^{3/4}) sublinear regret guarantee. Experiments on a synthetic episodic MDP and a real user booking dataset show it learns personalized objectives from multi-turn interactions.

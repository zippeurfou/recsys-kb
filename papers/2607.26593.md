## Summary

This paper introduces ASARL, a framework designed for QQ search in online communities where queries are informal. It addresses challenges like contextual discrepancy and data scarcity faced by large language models through autonomous multi-agent systems (ReasonAgent, CriticAgent, GenAgent) that curate specialized social data via relevance labeling and augmentation. The system then uses this curated dataset with three-stage training – focusing on social patterns, behavioral alignment, and efficiency – to significantly improve search relevance metrics and user engagement both offline and online.

## Technical note

ASARL is a fully automated relevance-learning framework for Tencent's QQ social search, addressing informal community language, long-tail data scarcity, and behavior-driven dynamics. A three-agent LLM system curates data: ReasonAgent generates interpretable intent/attribute relevance labels and reasoning traces, CriticAgent validates consistency (about 21% correction rate) and detects underrepresented regions, and GenAgent synthesizes long-tail query-title pairs. Training is three-stage: Social Context Training (SFT with social-aware CoT plus label prompts), Preference-Guided Optimization (DPO using CTR/join-rate signals to build preference pairs), and Social Distillation into a compact BERT/RoBERTa student. On about 1.1M QQ pairs it lifts Macro-F1/NDCG/Accuracy over BERT, RoBERTa, and LLM baselines, and online A/B gains reach +2.69% CTR and +2.59% join rate (channel search) at 12M DAU.

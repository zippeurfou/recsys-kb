## Summary

This paper tackles Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR), aiming to model users' changing interests across different domains. Existing generative recommendation methods often ignore inter-domain collaborative correlations during tokenization and use slow decoding strategies hindering real-time deployment.

To solve these issues, the authors propose GenCDSR, a new framework featuring hybrid tokenization via multi-tower architectures with codebooks capturing both cross-domain commonalities and domain-specific distinctions. Additionally, they introduce serial-parallel decoding that leverages this hierarchical SID structure to partially parallelize generation, speeding up inference.

Experiments on three datasets show GenCDSR significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines by achieving an average accuracy improvement of 1.5 percent while also providing a substantial reduction in inference latency (85.1 percent).

## Technical note

Applying generative retrieval to cross-domain sequential recommendation breaks in two places: RQ-VAE tokenizers are either fully shared (blurring domain-specific structure) or per-domain (fragmenting shared semantics and dropping cross-domain collaborative signal), and token-by-token beam search over semantic IDs is too slow for real-time serving while fully parallel decoding (MTP, NEZHA) predicts positions independently and loses per-position accuracy. GenCDSR adds (a) cross-domain hybrid tokenization: a Stage-1 shared-specific module feeds a shared encoder and per-domain encoders into two independent L1-level residual quantizers whose quantized outputs are fused by a Gumbel-Softmax router, and the leftover residual goes to Stage-2 fully domain-specific RQ (L1=L2=2, 256 codes of 128 dims per level; item embeddings are mean-pooled LLaMA-7B / Qwen2.5-7B last-layer states); and (b) two-pass state-carry serial-parallel decoding: target SID slots are filled with placeholder tokens so one forward pass precomputes hidden states for all positions, Step 1 decodes the L1 tokens serially with an additive context state s_{l+1} = s_l + e_l, then a second forward decodes the remaining L2 domain-specific tokens in parallel conditioned on the carried state, with trie-constrained decoding guaranteeing valid item IDs. Training is unified cross-entropy over merged cross-domain sequences followed by frozen-backbone per-domain LoRA adapters (rank 8), instantiated on both T5 and Qwen3-0.6B. On Amazon Clothing-Sports, Electronics-Phone and Douban Book-Movie it beats the strongest baseline GenCDR (Phone H@5 0.0809 vs 0.0780, Sports H@5 0.5015 vs 0.4979, Electronics H@10 0.0677 vs 0.0648) for about +1.5% average accuracy, and outperforms MTP by 21.9% and NEZHA by 37.9% in accuracy while cutting generation latency 85.1% versus beam search (87.5% on T5, 82.7% on Qwen3-0.6B; e.g. 47.3 ms -> 6.4 ms on Clothing). Ablations show Stage-1 shared-specific tokenization matters more than Stage-2 fine-grained tokenization, a 2:2 codebook allocation is optimal at L=4, and the hybrid tokenizer attains higher feature fidelity (reconstruction of the original semantic embeddings) than either a single shared RQ-VAE or one RQ-VAE per domain.

## Summary

Enterprise LLM agents, while boosting productivity, risk leaking sensitive internal data due to their contextual integrity (CI) challenges. The CI-Work benchmark evaluates these agents across five information flows, revealing widespread privacy failures (15.8%-50.9% violation rates) and crucially, a counterintuitive trade-off where higher task performance often leads to more leakage. The study concludes that simply scaling model size is insufficient to fix this vulnerability, advocating for a shift towards context-centric agent architectures instead.

## Technical note

CI-Work is a Contextual-Integrity-grounded benchmark that simulates enterprise agent workflows across five organizational information-flow directions (upward, downward, lateral, diagonal, external), partitioning each dense-retrieval context into disjoint Essential and Sensitive entry sets. Cases are built via human-in-the-loop seed generation plus an automated self-iterative refinement loop that re-labels entries until intended and perceived categories agree, with GPT-5.2 used for generation and an LLM-as-a-Judge for evaluation. Three metrics (Leakage, Violation, Conveyance) quantify the privacy-utility trade-off, revealing violation rates of 15.8-50.9% and leakage up to 26.7% across frontier models. A key finding is an inverse-scaling effect: larger models and deeper reasoning often increase leakage rather than mitigating it. The authors argue safeguarding requires context-centric architectures rather than model-centric scaling.

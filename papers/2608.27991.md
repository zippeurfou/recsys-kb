## Summary

1.  Recommendation systems face challenges learning feature interactions due to the diverse and heterogeneous nature of their data (users, items, context, etc.).
2.  To address this, the paper introduces HubMixer, a novel parameter-efficient architecture that uses a three-step process (`induction`, `interaction`, `readout`) involving learnable latent hubs to organize and selectively mix features.
3.  HubMixer first summarizes input features into compact hubs, then performs interaction within these hub spaces, and finally allows each token to read from relevant hubs, effectively improving performance over state-of-the-art models on both offline and online recommendation tasks.

## Technical note

HubMixer is Kuaishou's feature-interaction backbone for the short-video blue-collar recruitment ranking system (a lead-generation surface over roughly 40M DAU). Instead of flat token-to-token mixing it uses an induction-interaction-readout block: H=16 learnable latent hubs augmented by an input-conditioned residual from a two-layer Swish MLP over the mean-pooled tokens; hub induction is cross-attention with hubs as queries over RMSNorm-ed feature tokens; hub interaction is self-attention among the hubs; token-conditioned readout is cross-attention with each token as query over the interacted hubs, injected back through a LayerScale residual, so cost scales as T*H rather than T^2. Trained end-to-end on over 1 billion samples with 32 tokens, 2 blocks, and four BCE heads (plc_click, effective_view, interact, resume_submit). Offline it reaches avg AUC 0.8256 with 142.4M params versus TokenMixer 0.8241/156.9M, RankMixer 0.8238/155.1M, DCNv2 0.8212, Wukong 0.8208 and AutoInt 0.8210, i.e. better with fewer parameters. Ablations: removing hub interaction drops avg AUC to 0.8232, replacing token-conditioned readout with pooled hub injection gives 0.8247, and H=32 buys only 0.8258 at 150.8M params so H=16 is the chosen operating point. Representation analysis shows layer-1 input-output cosine distance of 0.200 versus 0.105 for TokenMixer, plus higher linear-probing AUC on frozen token representations, arguing the gain is injected task-relevant signal rather than perturbation. A 7-day online A/B on 7.2% of production traffic improved resume submission conversion rate by a statistically significant 5.48% and HubMixer is fully deployed. Caveats: a single business surface, absolute offline AUC deltas are small (0.0015 over the best baseline), and no latency or throughput numbers are reported.

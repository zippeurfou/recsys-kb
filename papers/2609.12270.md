## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge in sequential recommendation systems where a retriever's initial ranked list might not cover relevant items in the short prefix actually consumed by downstream rankers. To improve recall within this limited shortlist without retraining the retriever, it introduces a generative verifier that scores candidate items based on the likelihood of their identifier tokens. This verifier is trained post-hoc using cross entropy and only scores the top K candidates from the retriever list, achieving consistent Recall@10 improvements across different models and datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness as a standalone output-side adaptation mechanism.

## Technical note

A Meta paper that improves first-stage recommendation retrieval by bolting a post-hoc generative verifier onto a frozen retriever, borrowing the draft-and-verify structure from speculative decoding without preserving any sampling distribution. The frozen drafter supplies a top-K pool and a query state d_t; a stop-gradient linear projector W_proj feeds that state into a two-layer, two-head causal Transformer of width 128 that scores each candidate as the autoregressive log-likelihood of its item tokens, instantiated as a 4-level residual-quantized semantic ID (256 entries per level) concatenated with a 4-head multiplicative hash of the item index (1024 buckets per head). Training is dense teacher-forced next-token cross entropy at every non-pad prefix position, so it needs no sampled negatives, no in-batch negatives and no candidate pool, unlike the InfoNCE used by the drafters themselves; inference fuses drafter and verifier ranks by weighted reciprocal rank fusion (kappa=60, validation-selected w) with drafter-order tail backfill. On Amazon Reviews Industrial and Office the verifier lifts Recall@10 by +6.2%/+9.9% for SASRec, +8.1%/+10.8% for GRU4Rec, +8.4%/+14.5% for NextItNet, and +2.6%/+4.6% on MiniOneRec's own top-100 beam pool. On YaMBDa music (50M/500M/5B interactions, 0.63M to 5.36M items, top-1000 pool) the lift grows with catalog size to +25.3%, +27.3% and +29.8% Recall@10 at 5B, which the authors attribute to catalog dilution. The identifier ablation flips with scale: hash-only beats semantic-only on YaMBDa-500M (SASRec 4.75% vs 4.38% R@10) while semantic-only wins all six small-catalog Amazon comparisons. A content control rules out simple feature injection, since giving the drafter the same quantized content codes does not help standalone (SASRec+C 3.47% vs plain ID 4.16%) yet the verifier still lifts +C by 23-51% and the content-only drafter by 247-289%; a never-heard versus re-listen split on YaMBDa-500M shows +12.7-14.7% R@10 on never-heard targets. Caveats: entirely offline on public benchmarks, a single seed (42), and no production A/B or serving-latency measurement despite the multi-stage-ranking motivation.

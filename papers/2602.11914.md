## Summary

This paper analyzes rank-order contests using shortlisting, where a transparent cut-off score pre-selects participants. It examines how contestants behave and compares two objectives: maximizing the highest individual performance or total performance, assuming full equilibrium knowledge. The key findings are that a winner-take-all format is optimal for both objectives, a shortlist of exactly two contestants maximizes individual performance, total performance is independent of shortlist size, and shortlisting increases the highest achievable individual performance by a factor of 4/3 compared to no pre-selection.

## Technical note

This is a contest-theory paper analyzing rank-order (all-pay) contests with shortlisting and public cut-off score disclosure, characterizing equilibrium effort for any prize structure and shortlist size. Under linear cost the optimal prize structure is always winner-take-all, proven by collapsing the prize vector into rank-wise marginal contributions in quantile space. For maximizing the highest individual performance the optimal shortlist size is exactly two contestants, derived via a beta-function asymptotic representation of truncated order statistics; for total performance the outcome is invariant to shortlist size. Shortlisting yields highest individual performance 4/3 times that attainable without it. It has essentially no recommender-systems technique content beyond a Kaggle framing.

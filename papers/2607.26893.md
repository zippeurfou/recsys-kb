## Summary

This paper introduces **DASH**, a new decision-aware user simulator for online advertising using large language models (LLMs). It addresses the limitations of current action-imitation simulators by incorporating users' *thinking processes* alongside their observable actions, drawing from diverse cross-domain interaction data. The system first creates detailed reasoning traces and then refines its predictions through reinforcement learning, validated to be more effective and diagnostic on real-world advertising datasets.

## Technical note

DASH is a decision-aware LLM user simulator for Tencent online advertising that jointly generates a user's thinking trace and predicts the behavioral action (skip/click/conversion/negative-feedback) from heterogeneous cross-domain histories, targeting fidelity and diagnostic value for offline evaluation. It uses a three-stage Context Engineering to SFT to RL pipeline: hierarchical context compression plus closed-loop prompt optimization fold million-token histories into a 32K budget, teacher (Qwen3.5-397B) trajectories are distilled into a Qwen3.5-35B student, and GRPO with a hybrid action reward plus a rubric-based thinking-quality reward (form/content/logic scored by an LLM judge) refines it. The small SFT+RL model reaches 62.15 Weighted-F1 and 92.01 thinking quality, beating much larger prompting baselines such as Kimi-K2.5 (1T), and ablations show Context Engineering has the largest impact. It is a simulator, not a retrieval or ranking model.

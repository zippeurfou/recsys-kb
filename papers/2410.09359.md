## Summary

This paper examines how downsampling can improve energy efficiency for recommender systems by optimizing dataset sizes during training. Through experiments on several movie and Amazon review datasets, it found that algorithms like FunkSVD and BiasedMF often maintain high recommendation quality even with significantly reduced data. Specifically, these algorithms achieved nearly identical performance ratings (nDCG@10) using as little as 50% of the full dataset size, suggesting substantial computational savings are possible without major loss in accuracy.

## Technical note

Training recommender models on huge datasets incurs large computational and carbon costs, raising the question of whether full datasets are necessary. The paper empirically downsamples training data (10% to 100%) on MovieLens 100K/1M/10M and Amazon Toys & Games, evaluating classic algorithms such as FunkSVD, BiasedMF, and KNN variants with LensKit and RecPack. On sparse, imbalanced data like Amazon Toys & Games, FunkSVD and BiasedMF retain nDCG@10 within ~13% of full-data performance even after a 50% training-data reduction. The authors conclude strategic dataset reduction can cut energy consumption without substantially hurting recommendation quality.

## Summary

This study investigates "entropy bias" – potential differences in information provided by LLMs for gender-specific questions. Using a new dataset derived from real-world business and health queries, they found no significant category-level bias across four domains with different models. However, substantial variations occurred at the individual question level between male- and female-associated prompts, often cancelling each other out; these differences were addressed by their debiasing method which merges responses for both genders.

## Technical note

The paper defines and studies gender entropy bias in LLMs, the discrepancy in the amount of information an LLM provides for semantically equivalent queries that differ only by (binary) gender, in four business/health recommendation domains (education, jobs, investment, health). It contributes RealWorldQuestioning, a HuggingFace benchmark of ~870 gender-attributed questions curated from real forum posts (Reddit, Quora, MarketWatch), and evaluates ChatGPT-3.5-turbo, ChatGPT-4-turbo, Llama3-8B, and DeepSeek-R1-7B using Shannon entropy, CTTR, and Maas lexical-richness metrics plus a ChatGPT-4o LLM-as-judge. Statistical t-tests show no significant category-level gender bias and rare question-level significance (1.12%-38.20%), but the LLM-as-judge selects one gender's response as more informative in 93-100% of pairs, usually favoring male responses (especially Llama3), indicating question-level disparities that cancel out in aggregate. The authors propose a simple model-agnostic debiasing method that iteratively merges the male and female responses.

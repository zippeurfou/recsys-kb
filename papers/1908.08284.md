## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of recommending similar items in session-based recommendation, finding that state-of-the-art models like STAMP and NARM underperform compared to basic collaborative filtering on certain datasets. The authors propose a two-stage method that enhances the initial recommendations by focusing on the most relevant items using session information and introduces Candidate Rank Embeddings to encode global item ranking during re-ranking. Experiments show significant improvements in recall and MRR on the Zalando similar-item dataset and demonstrate the method's effectiveness on other datasets, also improving next-click prediction.

## Technical note

This paper tackles session-based similar-item recommendation, observing that state-of-the-art models like STAMP and NARM can underperform simple collaborative filtering on certain datasets. It proposes a two-stage method: a first stage produces initial recommendations, then a second re-ranking stage refines the most relevant items using session information. A key contribution is Candidate Rank Embeddings, which encode each candidate's global item ranking position as an embedding fed into the re-ranker. On the Zalando similar-item dataset and other public datasets, the approach significantly improves Recall and MRR and also boosts next-click prediction.

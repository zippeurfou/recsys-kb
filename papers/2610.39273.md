## Summary

This paper introduces PFA, a sparse decoding framework designed to accelerate generative recommendation (GR) models that use long user behavioral sequences. It leverages the observation that attention progressively focuses on a small set of critical historical tokens and that their importance ranking remains stable across decoding steps. PFA initially performs full attention, then establishes a shared importance ranking and reorders the key-value cache for efficiency, reusing this ranking to progressively reduce the token budget in subsequent steps, significantly lowering computational overhead while maintaining or improving performance.

## Technical note

In long-sequence generative recommendation every beam attends to the full encoded history at each SID decoding step, so latency grows linearly with history length (4x history gives 2.46x latency); static compression (LONGER, Q-Former) drops step-specific semantics and per-beam dynamic top-k sparse attention pays for repeated selection and non-contiguous KV gathers (2.36x slower in practice). The authors show GR attention focuses progressively (from step 1, 10% of tokens carry 92.98% of the mass) and that token rankings stay consistent across steps (Spearman 0.729 vs step 1). Progressively Focused Attention (PFA) keeps full attention at step 0, at step 1 aggregates attention over beams weighted by normalized beam probabilities into one shared importance ranking, reorders the top-k KV entries into a contiguous cache shared by all beams, then geometrically shrinks the budget k_t = ceil(k r^(t-1)) for later steps, with step-aware KV projections to avoid representational homogenization; theoretical attention MACs fall 2.65x vs full and 1.99x vs dynamic top-k. PFA improves Recall@10 by 5.75% and NDCG@10 by 7.18% on average over the strongest baseline on two public benchmarks and an industrial dataset, gives a 1.45x speedup with +3.16% Recall@10 on the industrial data, and in a 7-day 10%-traffic A/B on an e-commerce ad platform that extends the sequence from 128 to 512 it lifts revenue +3.76%, CTR +0.34% and GMV +2.56% at 11 ms response time vs 15 ms for naive full 512-item attention.

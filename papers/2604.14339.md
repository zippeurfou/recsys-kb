## Summary

Large language models often struggle with long contexts because their accuracy relies heavily on specific information positions. To address this brittleness, the authors introduce RoPE-Perturbed Self-Distillation, a training method that deliberately shifts parts of the context within a sequence. This forces the model to learn consistent outputs regardless of position, improving robustness and performance on long-context tasks like the authors demonstrated on benchmark datasets.

## Technical note

RoPE-Perturbed Self-Distillation is a training regularizer that reduces long-context LLMs' brittle sensitivity to the absolute position of relevant evidence. It forms two views of each training sequence by adding a skip offset to the RoPE indices of a sampled contiguous suffix, leaving tokens and causal mask unchanged, then minimizes a KL divergence matching the perturbed-view next-token distribution to a stop-gradient standard view alongside the usual CLM loss. Because the index shift mainly disrupts high-frequency RoPE components, cross-view agreement pushes the model to rely on semantic content and coarse positional structure. On Llama-3-8B and Qwen-3-4B it gives up to 12.04% on RULER-64K and 2.71% on RULER-256K, plus improved YaRN-based length extrapolation, with comparable short-context performance.

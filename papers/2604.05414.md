## Summary

The paper investigates training deep learning models for rotation estimation. It finds that removing orthogonalization during training and applying SVD orthogonalization *only* at inference significantly improves performance. The key theoretical contribution is a detailed gradient analysis explaining *why* SVD harms training gradients (due to severe gradient distortion when the matrix is far from SO(3)) and *why* Gram-Schmidt is less effective, providing the foundation for training via 9D regression and using SVD only for inference.

## Technical note

This paper provides the theoretical foundation for training rotation networks with direct 9D regression while applying SVD orthogonalization only at inference. It derives the exact spectrum of the 3x3 SVD backward-pass Jacobian for SO(3) projection: rank 3 with nonzero singular values 2/(s_i+s_j), spectral norm 2/(s2+s3), and condition number (s1+s2)/(s2+s3), so gradients explode as s3->0 (early training when M is far from SO(3)). It shows even Taylor-stabilized SVD gradients trade magnitude for direction error, and that SVD backprop retains only 1/3 of gradient energy, discarding the 6D normal component encoding distance from SO(3). It further proves Gram-Schmidt's 6D Jacobian is asymmetric (one-directional column coupling), explaining why 9D beats 6D, while SVD remains the optimal coordinate-independent Frobenius projector at inference (~3x error reduction).

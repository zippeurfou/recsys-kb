## Summary

1.  Standard methods for complex reasoning (Chain-of-Thought prompting) are slow and costly, while fine-tuning smaller models often sacrifices interpretability and adds overhead.
2.  The paper introduces Prompt-Level Distillation (PLD), which transfers reasoning patterns from a large "Teacher" model into structured instructions for a smaller "Student" model's system prompt, eliminating the need for fine-tuning.
3.  PLD significantly improves the reasoning performance of compact models (e.g., Gemma-3 4B) to match large models, with minimal latency, and makes the reasoning process transparent and verifiable, ideal for regulated industries and edge computing.

## Technical note

Prompt-Level Distillation (PLD) is a non-parametric alternative to fine-tuning that transfers a teacher model's reasoning into a smaller student's system prompt, giving Chain-of-Thought-level accuracy at zero-shot inference latency without weight updates. A four-phase pipeline: supervised instruction extraction, where the teacher solves labeled examples and abstracts each rationale into a generalized entity-free instruction; clustering logic synthesis using DBSCAN over dense instruction embeddings (isolating noise, no fixed cluster count) to merge micro-rules into unified instructions; a closed-loop conflict-resolution phase that runs the student, isolates errors, and adversarially refines instructions; and zero-shot inference with the consolidated prompt. Evaluated on Gemma-3 4B and Mistral Small 3.1, it lifts StereoSet, Contract-NLI, and LogiQA scores to near-frontier levels while keeping decisions transparent and human-verifiable for regulated use cases.

## Summary

Standard knowledge distillation (KD) methods transfer limited scalar predictions from large Foundation Models (FMs) to compact Vertical Models (VMs), missing crucial intermediate knowledge and suffering from low transfer ratios. LoopFM addresses this by using historical representations derived from the FM's intermediate embeddings as input features for the VMs, allowing richer knowledge transfer without requiring real-time FM inference. This approach significantly improves the transfer ratio compared to standard KD, leading to substantial performance gains (+0.5% to +1.22%) and strong AUC improvements on both benchmark datasets and industrial-scale recommendation systems.

## Technical note

LoopFM (Learning frOm HistOrical RePresentations of FM) addresses the deteriorating transfer ratio of scalar knowledge distillation between trillion-parameter foundation models (FMs) and compact vertical models (VMs) by opening a high-bandwidth channel: it materializes historical FM intermediate embeddings as structured VM input features. Three modular stages extract concatenated layer activations, compress them with a co-trained (optionally Matryoshka) autoencoder plus INT4 quantization (4x storage reduction), and structure them by a grouping key (here user-keyed temporal sequences of past interactions), consumed by a VM sequence encoder alongside standard KD. Using only historical embeddings avoids real-time FM inference and architectural coupling at serving. Theory decomposes the information gain into temporal, cross-feature, and compression-loss terms with a transfer-ratio lower bound growing with the FM-VM feature gap. It roughly doubles the KD transfer ratio and delivers +0.5%, +1.03%, and +1.22% production conversion gains.

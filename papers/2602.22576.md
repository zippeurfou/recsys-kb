## Summary

Traditional RAG systems struggle with complex multi-step reasoning, and current RL training methods for agentic RAG are inefficient due to sparse rewards that ignore intermediate steps and failed samples.

To address this, the authors propose Search-P1, a path-centric reward shaping framework comprising Path-Centric Reward (using soft scoring for all steps) and Dual-Track Path Scoring (assessing self-consistency and reference alignment).

Experiments show that Search-P1 significantly outperforms baselines and Search-R1, achieving an average accuracy gain of 7.7 points across multiple QA benchmarks.

## Technical note

Search-P1 introduces path-centric reward shaping to train agentic RAG search agents, addressing sparse outcome rewards and low sample efficiency in Search-R1-style RL. It makes the implicit plan explicit in the trajectory and computes a Dual-Track Path Score: Track A rates self-consistency (whether the model executes its own stated plan) and Track B rates reference-alignment against an offline reference planner distilled via rejection sampling and LLM voting, using order-agnostic step matching; the path reward is the max of the two tracks. Soft outcome scoring assigns partial credit to incorrect trajectories via answer correctness and reasoning quality, converting zero-reward failures into training signal, and an efficiency ratio penalizes redundant steps to prevent reward hacking. It reports a 7.7-point average accuracy gain over baselines across public and internal advertising QA benchmarks.

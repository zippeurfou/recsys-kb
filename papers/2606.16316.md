## Summary

This paper introduces RL-Index, a new framework addressing the challenge of retrieving relevant knowledge when query-knowledge relationships require implicit reasoning (beyond simple matching). Instead of performing complex reasoning online during queries, which causes high latency, it shifts this process to an indexing stage. Using reinforcement learning and large language models, documents are augmented with explicit rationales during indexing that encode potential reasoning needed for future queries.

The key innovation involves employing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a standard inverted index structure, training the system using retrieval similarity as a reward signal from the BRIGHT benchmark to optimize these added rationales. This allows direct optimization of the indexing process itself for better downstream query performance without adding online reasoning overhead.

Experiments show RL-Index significantly improves both information retrieval accuracy and question answering performance on unseen queries by leveraging this learned rationale augmentation, while also drastically reducing online inference latency. The approach demonstrates robustness as a general plug-and-play strategy applicable across different retrieval systems.

## Technical note

RL-Index shifts retrieval reasoning from online query rewriting to offline index-side reasoning, augmenting documents with LLM-generated rationales that explicitly encode latent query-knowledge relationships beyond surface semantic or lexical matching. Framing this as an agentic indexing problem, it trains an open-source LLM augmenter with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using retrieval similarity (incremental document relevance) as a verifiable reward, directly optimizing indexing decisions for retrieval effectiveness. Because rationales are precomputed at indexing time, online inference latency is substantially reduced relative to query-rewriting approaches like TongSearch. On the BRIGHT benchmark it consistently improves retrieval (nDCG@10) and downstream QA, and the learned rationale augmentation generalizes across diverse retrievers and generators as a plug-and-play strategy that further compounds with query-side reasoning.

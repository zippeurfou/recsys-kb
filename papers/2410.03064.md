## Summary

This paper addresses limitations in standard latent variable collaborative filtering by tackling overfitting towards user/item identity and ignoring item geometry. The authors introduce a "generalization gap" concept specific to recommendation systems and propose loss functions based on geometric properties derived from item metadata, providing a theoretical upper bound for these models. They present a new algorithm called GeoCF that minimizes this geometric-aware loss function, demonstrating experimentally that it outperforms existing methods significantly across multiple standard and internal datasets.

## Technical note

Latent collaborative filtering (e.g., MultiVAE) is effective but lacks theoretical understanding of generalization and typically ignores item-metadata geometry. The paper formalizes a generalization gap for latent collaborative filtering and derives a geometry-dependent upper bound that yields new loss functions leveraging item-metadata structure. Minimizing this bound gives GeoCF, whose convergence rate scales as n^(-1/d*) with d* the intrinsic dimensionality induced by item embeddings, highlighting metadata's theoretical role. GeoCF outperforms existing baselines on MovieLens20M, Netflix, and two large internal datasets.

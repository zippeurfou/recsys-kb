## Summary

1.  The paper introduces LoRA-Squeeze, a novel technique for compressing LoRA modules, either post-hoc or dynamically during training, to improve performance and manage model size.
2.  LoRA-Squeeze works by first fine-tuning a model with a higher-rank LoRA module and then compressing this high-rank solution down to a lower target rank using randomized SVD.
3.  Experiments show that this compression, particularly when done post-hoc or gradually during training (rank annealing), produces smaller adapters that often outperform those trained directly at the low target rank, especially with limited fine-tuning steps.

## Technical note

LoRA-Squeeze compresses LoRA modules from a high source rank to a lower target rank by reconstructing the full weight-update matrix and applying Randomized SVD, decoupling the training rank from the deployment rank. Two variants are proposed: Post-Squeeze (a single offline compression after fine-tuning) and In-Squeeze (gradual in-tuning rank annealing), with a memory-efficient QR-based formulation that operates on an r_src x r_src matrix instead of the full delta. Across 13 text and 10 vision-language tasks on Gemma 3, over-parameterized fine-tuning then compression often outperforms directly training at the target rank, especially for very low ranks, and the gradual In-Squeeze annealing gives the best size-performance trade-off. The authors connect the effect to intrinsic task dimensionality and the lottery ticket hypothesis.

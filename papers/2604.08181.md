## Summary

Modern transformer-based recommenders capture short-term preferences but neglect stable long-term user tastes due to recency bias and computational issues with long sequences. To address this, the paper introduces Long-Term Embeddings (LTE), a fixed "high-inertia" contextual anchor providing stable long-term item representations, which helps bridge the gap between short-term attention and long-term preferences. Furthermore, the authors tackle a critical deployment issue (offline-online mismatch) by grounding the embeddings in a fixed semantic basis and explore causal language modeling strategies to avoid data leakage, evaluating two representations; online A/B tests showed significant improvements in user engagement and financial metrics when using LTE as a contextual prefix.

## Technical note

This Zalando paper proposes Long-Term Embeddings (LTE), a high-inertia contextual anchor that summarizes a user's distant history into a single vector to counter recency bias in SASRec-style causal-language-model recommenders without paying O(N^2) attention cost. To solve the point-in-time consistency problem of single-versioned feature stores (training-serving skew and rollback version mismatch), LTEs are constrained to a fixed semantic basis of static content-based (CLIP) item embeddings, giving temporal inertia and cross-version compatibility. The authors compare a heuristic average against an asymmetric autoencoder with a fixed decoder that learns behavioral affinities while preserving stability, and use a lagged sliding window to prevent CLM data leakage. Integrating LTE as a contextual prefix token yielded significant online A/B uplifts in engagement and financial metrics and measurably redistributed attention away from recent items.

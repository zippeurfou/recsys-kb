## Summary

Existing speech-to-video synthesis struggles with creating realistic two-person interactions, particularly capturing cross-individual dependencies and fine-grained reactive behaviors. To address this, the paper introduces InterDyad, a novel framework that first extracts identity-agnostic motion priors from reference videos and then uses a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to align conversational audio with these priors, dictating precise reaction timings. Additionally, InterDyad incorporates Role-aware Dyadic Gaussian Guidance (RoDG) to improve lip-syncing and spatial consistency, especially under head movement, and presents new evaluation metrics demonstrating its state-of-the-art performance in generating natural dyadic interactions.

## Technical note

InterDyad generates two-person (dyadic) speech-to-video interactions by querying intermediate visual motion guidance rather than relying on text prompts. A first-stage Interactivity Injector extracts identity-agnostic motion latents from reference videos and injects them as driving signals into a pretrained audio-conditional video-generation backbone, enabling explicit dyadic pose control without breaking audio-visual sync. A second stage uses a MetaQuery-based modality alignment with a multimodal LLM (MLLM) to distill linguistic intent from conversational audio and map speech into the learned motion space, dictating precise reaction timing. Role-aware Dyadic Gaussian Guidance (RoDG) applies a spatially-weighted Gaussian over the oral region to preserve lip-sync and identity under extreme head poses, and a dedicated evaluation suite with new spatio-temporal coordination metrics validates state-of-the-art dyadic interaction quality.

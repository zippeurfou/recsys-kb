## Technical note

PESO (Proximally rEgularized Single evolving lOra) (Amazon) is a continual-learning method for LoRA-based LLM generative recommenders that adapts to evolving users, items, and preferences. It argues recommendation differs from vision continual learning because outdated preferences can hurt, so it maintains a single evolving LoRA adapter regularized with a proximal term anchoring it to its most recent frozen state, balancing plasticity and stability rather than accumulating adapters. It uses a per-module softmax-KL proximal, is shown theoretically to give data-aware direction-wise guidance in the LoRA subspace, and empirically outperforms cumulative LoRA and single-evolving-adapter baselines on real-world datasets.

This aligns with prior work , suggesting that sequential updates naturally prioritize recent preference signals, whereas full retraining treats old and new data equally, diluting the signal of evolving interests
- Storage Complexity: PESO stores only one previous LoRA adapter, resulting in $O(1)$ storage complexity relative to the number of stages. In contrast, Cumulative LoRA grows linearly $O(T)$ as it must store all past adapters
- Computational Complexity: PESO adds only a lightweight quadratic/KL penalty to the loss. This requires no additional forward passes. In practice, we observed no measurable slowdown in training time compared to standard Single LoRA fine-tuning

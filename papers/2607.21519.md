## Summary

1.  Current recommender systems using large language models often rely on inefficient autoregressive generation methods that prioritize sequential order over capturing complex user preferences between items.
2.  To address this, researchers propose DLMRec, which adapts diffusion modeling principles to recommendation – specifically by introducing a specialized tokenizer and training strategy focused on inter-item relationships.
3.  DLMRec enhances recommendations through three key innovations: encoding collaborative signals into tokens (stochastic tokenizer), progressively learning item preferences during training (curriculum-driven), and combining iterative predictions for more consistent results (stability-aware voting).

## Technical note

DLMRec reframes LLM-based generative recommendation as discrete diffusion (iterative masked denoising) rather than autoregressive next-token prediction, arguing that bidirectional, whole-sequence refinement better fits the order-insensitive inter-item structure of user preferences. It introduces a collaborative-aware stochastic tokenizer (CAST) that quantizes multi-hop LightGCN embeddings into diffusion-compatible discrete tokens, a curriculum strategy alternating item-level and token-level masking with a hard-negative preference loss, and a stability-aware voting mechanism over refinement steps; it is built on LLaDA-8B with LoRA. On LastFM, MovieLens-1M, and Amazon-Beauty it beats the strongest LLM baseline (LLaRA) by ~6.75% Recall and ~5.19% NDCG, and a discrete-diffusion baseline by ~11.83% Recall. It does next-item prediction followed by nearest-neighbor retrieval over the item bank, not a unified retrieval-plus-ranking pipeline.

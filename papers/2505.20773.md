## Summary

1.  Collaborative filtering recommender systems struggle with "cold-start" items lacking user interaction data.
2.  While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer zero-shot solutions, they often produce inaccurate recommendations due to sparse metadata and potential knowledge gaps ("hallucinations").
3.  The proposed ColdRAG system dynamically builds a domain-specific knowledge graph from item attributes, uses an LLM to reason over it for cold-start candidates based on user history, and then prompts the LLM to generate final recommendations with verifiable evidence, significantly improving recall and NDCG metrics compared to existing baselines in experiments.

## Technical note

Item cold-start remains hard because collaborative filtering lacks interaction data and LLM recommenders depend on pre-filtered candidate lists and hallucinate under sparse metadata. ColdRAG dynamically constructs and incrementally updates a domain knowledge graph from raw item metadata (LLM-generated item profiles yielding typed entities and relations with embeddings), then performs query-aware LLM-guided multi-hop traversal over the graph to assemble a compact candidate set with evidence paths, removing the need for pre-filtered candidates. On Amazon Games/Toys/Office it improves Recall@10 over the strongest baselines by 78.2%, 26.8%, and 123.8% respectively, and it generalizes across GPT-4o-mini and Qwen2.5-32B backbones. It also cuts out-of-domain hallucination to 3.15% (vs 5-10% for other LLM baselines) with low variance across runs.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of managing diverse quantum computing backends by proposing the Quantum Device Management Interface (QDMI) as a standard interface for hardware abstraction and unified access.
2.  It presents a case study demonstrating how QDMI can be integrated with Amazon Braket, a cloud service offering various quantum hardware and software simulators, treating the entire cloud service as a single, unified device.
3.  This integration allows for standardized management of the entire task lifecycle (authentication, submission, retrieval) across Braket's diverse backends and paves the way for interoperable access from QDMI-compliant software.

## Technical note

This case study integrates the Quantum Device Management Interface (QDMI) with Amazon Braket, treating an entire multi-backend cloud service as a single logical device. QDMI provides a uniform hardware-abstraction boundary between the middle (compilation/scheduling) and backend layers of stacks like the Munich Quantum Software Stack, covering device discovery, capability queries, job execution, and lifecycle management. The implementation maps Braket's quantum-task lifecycle (authentication, verbatim-compiled circuit submission, queue/status queries, and S3 result retrieval) onto QDMI calls. The authors analyze abstraction mismatches from representing heterogeneous simulators and QPUs as one device and offer a generalizable blueprint for incorporating cloud-native execution models into QDMI-enabled HPC quantum software stacks. Not a recommender-systems paper.

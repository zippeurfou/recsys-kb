## Summary

This paper argues that current evaluations of AI output grounding, which treat support as a simple binary choice, are inadequate as they fail to capture the nuances of how AI reformulates source material. The authors propose a new, reader-centred taxonomy defining multiple support relations between generated text and sources, incorporating different syntactic and interpretive moves. This framework aims to improve benchmarking and user interfaces by communicating the *specific way* an AI's claim is supported, not just whether it is grounded.

## Technical note

This is a position/framework paper arguing that binary supported/unsupported groundedness labels for AI output obscure both syntactic moves (verbatim quote vs. paraphrase vs. rounding) and interpretive moves (deduction, induction, assumption-laden inference) that occur when models reformulate sources. It proposes a reader-centred taxonomy of support relations R(S,D) between a generated statement and source documents, synthesized from linguistics and philosophy of language (Toulmin's claim/grounds/warrant model, Gricean implicature, argumentation-support theory, ScholOnto). The authors sketch an operationalization path emphasizing discriminability and actionability, plus a benchmark built by enriching existing groundedness corpora with support-relation labels validated via inter-annotator agreement and LLM-as-judge. The goal is provenance interfaces communicating how a claim is supported, supporting critical reading rather than passive reliance.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenges of recommending short-form videos on platforms like e-commerce, which face unique issues such as position bias and duration bias due to their immersive feed UIs. The authors found that even with sufficient interaction data, overcoming these biases requires more effective approaches than standard supervised learning methods. They successfully demonstrated this by using a fine-tuned multimodal vision-language model for video retrieval, showing better performance in online experiments compared to conventional techniques.

## Technical note

This e-commerce paper addresses launching a new immersive short-form video feed where limited data, position bias, and watch-time-driven duration bias hurt conventional supervised recommenders. It builds a two-tower retrieval system on a fine-tuned CLIP vision-language model, embedding videos as averaged frame CLIP features and users as recency-weighted CLIP embeddings of recent product interactions in a shared semantic space, with few-shot adaptation from browse/search data. Evaluation uses NDCG with inverse propensity scoring, visual coherence, and an LVLM-as-a-judge (Qwen2.5-VL). The CLIP-based model beat a conventional two-tower VCG on bias skew and online metrics, raising videos watched >=50% by 50% and reducing popularity/duration skew, and was rolled out to production.

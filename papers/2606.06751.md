## Summary

This paper addresses the difficulty in diagnosing slowdowns during distributed ML training caused by synchronization delays appearing differently across nodes ("exposed delay"). Current dashboards using averages often misrepresent these issues while heavy full profilers are impractical. The authors introduce StageFrontier, an efficient always-on signal that accurately tracks stage durations and identifies where group-visible delays first originate, helping operators pinpoint problems without needing heavy profiling tools.

## Technical note

StageFrontier is an always-on, low-volume signal for diagnosing slowdowns in distributed ML training, where synchronization displaces symptoms (a data stall on one rank surfaces as backward wait on others). Each rank reports only a short ordered vector of coarse stage durations (data, forward, backward, callbacks, optimizer, residual) timed with CPU wall-clock, with no synchronized clocks and no kernel tracing. At each stage boundary it takes the cumulative time of the furthest-along rank; the increments of this frontier form an exact, additive (telescoping) decomposition of the step's exposed time and point to the stage and rank where group-visible delay first appears, acting as a profiler-router rather than a fix recommender. Because coarse signals cannot always separate cause from co-occurrence, it emits downgrade labels (co_critical, role_aware_needed, telemetry_limited) outside the homogeneous synchronous DDP model. A PyTorch implementation adds <0.2% throughput overhead through 128 ranks on Gloo/NCCL and recovers the same top-stage routing as PyTorch Profiler, HTA, and Nsight from a 0.11 MB summary instead of a 15.81 GB trace.

## Summary

This paper introduces a novel method using video visual saliency (attention-grabbing regions) and deep learning, specifically combining the HD2S saliency model with OpenFace facial action unit analysis, to predict viewer emotions more effectively than traditional methods. The key findings are that videos with multiple distinct salient areas tend to cause positive but calm feelings, while those with a single dominant salient region typically induce negative and highly aroused responses, suggesting specific emotional patterns linked directly to visual attention dynamics rather than just facial expressions or subjective reports. This approach offers computationally efficient insights into how video design influences emotion through natural viewing focus, providing an alternative for applications like content creation aiming for desired viewer reactions or experiences.

## Technical note

This study introduces a saliency-based approach to predicting viewer emotions from video stimuli, extracting two interpretable features (saliency area and number of salient regions) from HD2S saliency maps and relating them to emotion. Using the MAHNOB-HCI dataset (527 trials), it correlates these saliency features with self-reported valence/arousal (circumplex model) and with 18 facial action units from OpenFace via Pearson correlation and canonical correlation analysis (CCA). Key findings: videos with multiple salient regions tend to elicit high-valence low-arousal emotions, while a single dominant salient region tends to induce low-valence high-arousal responses (number of salient regions is significantly negatively correlated with arousal, p=0.0030). The work also surfaces a notable inconsistency between self-reported emotions and facial-expression-derived emotions, arguing for continuous rather than end-of-trial self-reporting and offering a computationally efficient, interpretable alternative for content creation.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of predicting multi-step user behavior trajectories by introducing a new task, User Behavior Trajectory Prediction (UBTP), which models global, listwise preferences across future actions, unlike traditional sequential recommendation. To tackle this, the authors propose Listwise Preference Diffusion Optimization (LPDO), a novel training framework based on diffusion models that uses a Plackett-Luce ranking likelihood and variational bounds to optimize structured preferences over entire item sequences. They evaluate LPDO on real-world datasets, demonstrating its superiority over existing methods and establishing a benchmark for structured preference learning with diffusion models.

## Technical note

This paper defines User Behavior Trajectory Prediction (UBTP), forecasting an ordered sequence of k future user actions rather than a single next item, to capture long-term listwise preferences that myopic next-item models miss. It proposes Listwise Preference Diffusion Optimization (LPDO), which injects a Plackett-Luce listwise ranking signal into the diffusion model's variational ELBO, deriving a tight lower bound so that at every denoising step the model favors true items over alternatives and captures inter-item ordering, overcoming the independent-token assumption of prior point-wise diffusion recommenders. Evaluation uses a new Sequential Match (SeqMatch) exact-trajectory metric plus perplexity. On four real-world benchmarks LPDO consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in precision and sequence coherence.

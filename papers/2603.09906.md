## Summary

The paper finds that LLMs can use reasoning to answer simple, single-hop factual questions, even when no complex logic is needed. Two key mechanisms explain this: a "computational buffer effect" where reasoning tokens enable hidden computations, and "factual priming" where generating related facts acts as a bridge to retrieve correct answers. However, this process carries a risk: hallucinated facts during reasoning can increase the likelihood of final answer hallucinations, and the authors show how identifying and prioritizing factual reasoning paths can improve model accuracy.

## Technical note

This study shows that enabling reasoning in hybrid ON/OFF LLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro, Qwen3-32B) expands parametric knowledge recall even on simple single-hop factual questions, measured by large pass@k coverage gains on SimpleQA-Verified and EntityQuestions. It isolates two mechanisms: a content-independent computational buffer effect, where reasoning tokens enable latent computation (evidenced by gains from filler traces), and content-dependent factual priming, a generative self-retrieval where recalling related facts bridges to the correct answer. Extracting facts from a trace and re-running with reasoning off but conditioned on those facts recovers most pass@k gains. A large-scale audit (per-fact verification via search-enabled Gemini-2.5-Flash) shows hallucinated intermediate facts increase final-answer hallucination. A test-time strategy that prioritizes hallucination-free reasoning trajectories improves accuracy.

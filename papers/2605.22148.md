## Summary

Self-evolving LLM agents face a bottleneck in lifecycle management of their skills, despite being able to accumulate knowledge. The paper introduces **Ratchet**, a single-agent system enabling a frozen LLM to self-manage its skill library by writing, retrieving, curating, and retiring its own natural language skills. Experiments show Ratchet significantly boosts performance (+0.328 rolling mean gain) compared to no-skill baselines over many rounds, demonstrating effective skill accumulation and preventing performance drift, with key mechanisms being skill retirement and a meta-skill authoring prior.

## Technical note

Ratchet is a single-agent loop in which a frozen LLM writes, retrieves, curates, and retires its own natural-language skills, addressing library drift—the silent degradation of a self-evolving skill library through unchecked growth, redundancy, or premature pruning. It integrates four hygiene mechanisms: outcome-driven retirement, a bounded active-cap, meta-skill authoring guidance, and pattern canonicalisation that collapses near-duplicate critic labels. A non-divergence proposition shows the bounded cap plus retirement threshold together prevent expected performance from drifting below the no-skills floor. On MBPP+ hard-100 with Claude Opus 4.7, Ratchet lifts held-out pass@1 from a 0.258 baseline to a late-window rolling mean of 0.584 (+0.328 gain vs +0.002 for the no-skill control), and transfers to SWE-bench Verified (+0.22 peak); ablations find retirement and the meta-skill authoring prior are the load-bearing components.

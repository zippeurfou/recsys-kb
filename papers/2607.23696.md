## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating vast numbers of ad creative options generated by AI. The core solution involves using a predictive model trained on historical A/B test data during an offline phase to rank and refine these generative candidates, specifically guiding their creation with an inference-time critic. This refined slate is then tested in an online adaptive experiment designed to efficiently identify top performers while minimizing traffic exposure to weaker options.

The results from several field experiments show that the best creative identified by this method significantly outperformed human-authored creatives (45.1%, 46.7%, and 36.2% engagement lifts). Crucially, despite the predictive model being inherently noisy for direct ranking, it effectively directs the generative process towards producing a high-quality slate suitable for online testing.

Therefore, the paper proposes an offline-to-online workflow as a key principle: use generative models with predictive guidance to create testable slates, rely on adaptive experiments for final selection at feasible scale, and leverage historical data prediction without needing perfect accuracy upfront.

## Technical note

Generative models make ad-creative generation cheap but shift the bottleneck to evaluation, where online tests can only afford a small slate. This deployed Meta workflow uses a Bradley-Terry ranking model (a LoRA-tuned Llama-3.2-1B value head trained on within-experiment A/B comparisons) as an inference-time critic that steers a frozen LLM through one TextGrad-style refinement step to build a high-recall candidate slate, then screens it with a Thompson-sampling batched adaptive experiment. The key principle is that the predictive model need not pick the winner (its top pick finished 43rd of 50) but should generate a slate that contains winners, measured by candidate-set recall (top-30 retains 83% of above-best winners). Across three large field experiments the best AI-refined creative beat the best human creative by 45.1%, 46.7%, and 36.2%, and Thompson sampling cut in-experiment regret 66% at slate size K=30.

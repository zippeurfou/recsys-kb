## Summary

This paper uses Airbnb as a case study to understand how guests make booking decisions based on host prices and other factors. It combines economic modeling with causal inference techniques to analyze this behavior and identify variations among different guests and listings. The ultimate goal is to apply these findings to improve Airbnb's marketplace by developing better pricing tools for hosts and more personalized experiences for guests, thereby balancing demand and supply while matching preferences effectively.

## Technical note

Combines economic demand modeling with causal inference to measure guest price sensitivity and preference heterogeneity in Airbnb's two-sided marketplace, where hosts (not the platform) set prices. Models guest choice with a logit demand model estimated via the Berry-Levinsohn-Pakes (BLP) approach, using differential listing-supply growth across geographies (conditional on realized demand) as an instrumental variable to isolate exogenous, supply-driven price variation and recover the price elasticity of demand. Estimates are validated and calibrated against carefully designed pricing A/B experiments, with an applied 'haircut' correcting a slight upward bias in observational estimates. A segment-level panel regression links how the guest-conversion mix shifts with price to per-segment elasticities, informing host pricing tools and guest personalization; the framework generalizes to C2C e-commerce marketplaces.

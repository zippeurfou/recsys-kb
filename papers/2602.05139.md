## Summary

1.  Classical bandit algorithms struggle in environments with hidden, time-varying states that bias rewards and complicate action selection.
2.  The paper introduces novel state-model-free bandit algorithms that use lagged features and coordinated exploration to implicitly track latent states and learn optimal policies without explicit modeling.
3.  These methods adapt efficiently to non-stationary rewards and outperform classical approaches, providing practical guidance for real-world applications with unobserved confounders.

## Technical note

The paper studies bandits whose rewards depend on an unobserved Markov state that evolves autonomously, so the optimal arm shifts while only past actions and rewards are seen. Rather than modeling the latent chain, it feeds LinUCB low-dimensional summaries: a lagged action-reward pair (LC-UCB) and probe 'fingerprints' from multiple arms' rewards (RP-UCB for parallel units, SP-UCB for single-unit). Adaptive variants refresh fingerprints via residual, margin, and staleness tests (AdaRP-UCB, AdaSP-UCB). In synthetic sweeps over state count, transition rate, noise, and horizon, these reduce dynamic regret versus classical, adversarial, and non-stationary baselines when summaries separate states and are refreshed often enough.

## Summary

Recommender systems historically relied on simple raw IDs for large-scale recommendations due to their efficiency in lookup and memorization. However, a shift towards using richer semantic information is occurring, where it's increasingly encapsulated into structured IDs rather than just used as auxiliary features. This evolution introduces "semantic planning," which predicts abstract user targets before selecting specific items, representing a potential future direction requiring new model designs, evaluations, and industry coordination efforts beyond traditional retrieval methods.

## Technical note

This position paper reads two decades of recommender-system evolution as a flow from raw IDs, to richer semantic information around IDs, to semantic IDs, framed by how systems utilize information under industrial-scale constraints. It explains why raw IDs dominated (stable operational anchors for lookup/logging/serving and high-fidelity item-specific memorization) and why semantic information is increasingly encapsulated into the identity layer via semantic IDs (a model-facing identity enabling within-domain generalization, cross-domain transfer, multimodal alignment, and search-recommendation unification while mitigating cold-start and embedding-table growth). It then proposes semantic planning as the next stage: the system first predicts a semantic target for the next exposure as an explicit multi-stakeholder decision over user, platform, and provider objectives, and only then instantiates that target as an item, offer, message, or generated creative. It argues this demands new evaluation beyond the Cranfield paradigm (planning resonance via user-simulation agents) and flags open challenges of ID stability, target grounding, and objective observability. The paper is conceptual with no experiments.

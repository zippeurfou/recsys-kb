## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of training a search retriever for Walmart's sponsored search, where user engagement signals, commonly used for supervision, are an imperfect proxy for semantic item relevance and can be influenced by factors beyond relevance. The authors propose a novel training framework that primarily uses graded semantic relevance labels as supervision, combining them with a retrieval system prior and engagement data applied only to semantically relevant items to refine preferences. This unified approach outperforms the existing system, improving both offline evaluation metrics and online performance through better average relevance and NDCG scores.

## Technical note

This Walmart paper builds a bi-encoder first-stage retriever for sponsored search that makes graded semantic relevance the primary supervision signal, using engagement only as a preference signal among already-relevant items, because ad engagement is structurally sparse and exposure-biased (auctions, bids, budgets, limited ad slots). It constructs a context-rich training target by combining three sources: 5-point graded relevance from a cascade of cross-encoder teacher models and human labels, a multi-channel retrieval prior score derived from rank position and cross-channel agreement of production retrievers, and debiased historical engagement applied only to relevant query-item pairs. The multi-channel prior doubles as hard-negative mining by surfacing top-ranked but irrelevant candidates. The unified framework outperforms the production system in offline metrics and online A/B tests, improving average relevance and NDCG.

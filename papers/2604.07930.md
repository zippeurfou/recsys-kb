## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Modern search systems rely on a fast first stage retriever to fetch relevant items from a massive catalog of items. Deployed search systems often use user engagement signals to supervise bi-encoder retriever training at scale, because these signals are continuously logged from real traffic and require no additional annotation effort. However, engagement is an imperfect proxy for semantic relevance. Items may receive interactions due to popularity, promotion, attractive visuals, titles, or price,...

## Technical note

This Walmart sponsored-search work argues engagement is a noisy and structurally sparse proxy for relevance (impressions gated by ad auctions, bids, budgets), so it makes semantic relevance the primary supervision for bi-encoder retrieval and uses engagement only as a preference signal among relevant items. It constructs a context-rich training target combining graded relevance labels from a cascade of cross-encoder teachers, a multi-channel retrieval prior score from production channels (also used to mine hard negatives), and engagement applied to relevant items. The unified-supervision retriever outperforms the production system in offline evaluation and online A/B tests.

## Summary

Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities, but explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches for recommendation models are inefficient due to verbose generation and rigid templates. The paper introduces OneLatent, a latent reasoning framework that compresses CoT traces into learnable tokens, significantly improving inference speed while retaining recommendation quality. Experiments on real datasets show OneLatent outperforms explicit CoT methods and traditional baselines, achieving substantial offline metric improvements and a significant online revenue lift.

## Technical note

Explicit Think-then-Answer CoT in LLM-based foundation recommendation models (e.g. OneReason) is slow and brittle; OneLatent (Kuaishou) instead inserts three learnable latent token IDs between the user context and the target 3-level Res-KMeans SID so the model does Latent-Reason-then-Answer with constant reasoning overhead. Supervision comes from MV-ACoT, teacher CoT traces from three views (Exploration, Evaluation with the positive item as privileged info, Attribution) with prompt-guided adaptive length (35% shorter on average); a Qwen3.5-0.8B FRM is trained via single-view then multi-view rationale alignment, SID-target context alignment, then curriculum SFT (high- to low-activity users) mixed 1:1 with latent-reasoning SFT. On Kuaishou local-services data OneLatent-Merge lifts SID@64 by 17.44% over OneReason-CoT Think and 9.33% over No-Think, and on the public Kuaishou LLM-Rec benchmark gains 12.21% SID@64 / 19.10% ID@64 over the strongest non-latent result, with 17.49x the QPS of explicit CoT and better MMLU retention; a 9B variant also beats size-matched baselines (SID@64 0.2008 vs 0.1857). Served as an asynchronous behavior-triggered retrieval channel with Redis-cached candidates, a 7-day 5%-traffic A/B test showed +2.566% impressions, +5.157% ad spend and an estimated +9.6% revenue, after which it was rolled out to full traffic.

## Summary

This paper addresses the **rich-get-richer bias** in large-scale content recommendation systems that disadvantages emerging creators. The authors developed a **detailed user-simulation framework** capturing creator diversity and behavioral trajectories over weeks, calibrated against real platform data. Using this simulator and a budgeted multi-armed bandit approach, they designed a **fairness policy** ("floor-protected") that significantly boosts the weekly view targets for small creators (approx. 90%) and increases their organic reach by about 33% per week, while maintaining high distribution stability.

## Technical note

Meta builds a grounded user-simulation framework for a billion-user video platform to evaluate interventions against the rich-get-richer (Matthew effect) dynamic that keeps emerging creators in a cold-start loop (little distribution, no engagement signal, conservative ranker prior under UCB-style scoring). The simulator models about 42K creators over 12 weeks with a population calibrated to real follower, engagement (EWTR, LTR, PSR) and breakout-rate distributions, coupled creator and content state machines (exploration, growth, graduation) and organic-reach compounding, and frames equitable views-per-video allocation as a weekly budgeted multi-armed bandit: a floor guaranteeing 90% of each creator's parity target (crediting organic views), then reward-greedy water-filling of the remainder with a small-creator reserve fraction rho, comparing VPV-only, parity-gap, engagement-weighted and hybrid rewards and step vs follower-scaled graduated targets. The deployed configuration (graduated target, rho=0.7, parity-gap reward) funds under-parity creators 100% of the time, grows small-creator organic reach about +33% per week, keeps 24-32% of budget reward-steered, and is the most stable option (funded-set Jaccard 0.819, Spearman 0.645, turnover 0.144). The paper also catalogs the Sim2Real gap (weekly vs hourly pacing, organic attribution, population drift, strategic creators) and proposes a holdout-based validation protocol comparing simulated and online outcomes.

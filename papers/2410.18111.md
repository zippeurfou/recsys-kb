## Summary

Large recommendation models (LRMs) in online advertising require massive datasets for training due to their scale and continuous adaptation needs.

This paper addresses the challenge of data efficiency, as handling such huge volumes impacts computational costs and slows down research velocity.

It proposes actionable principles and frameworks derived from optimizing Google's largest LRM systems, focusing on accelerating data convergence and optimally balancing data volume with model size.

## Technical note

Google's Ads CTR large recommendation models train on hundreds of billions of examples, so data volume dominates compute cost and R&D velocity. The paper frames a 'data convergence' point and shows post-convergence dataset pruning, negative downsampling with inverse-rate importance reweighting, and continuous downsampling that persists into online training all accelerate convergence at a small constant accuracy cost. Continuous distillation, where a larger teacher keeps distilling into the student through online training, reaches convergence with about 35% less data than fixed-cutover distillation. An iso-compute analysis reveals a Chinchilla-like loss valley, giving an optimal joint choice of model size and sampling rate for a fixed compute budget.

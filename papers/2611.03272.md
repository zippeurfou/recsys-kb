## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the problem of recommendation systems using generative models (specifically SID generation) and tackles the issue of "popularity bias" that reduces diversity during training with on-policy distillation (OPD).
2.  The proposed solution, HERO, uses two specialized teacher models (one for popular, one for unpopular items) and routes training samples to the appropriate teacher based on their ground-truth item's popularity. It also modifies the student rollout to explicitly include SIDs for unpopular items during training.
3.  Offline evaluations on datasets and real-world deployment via an online A/B test showed that HERO effectively improves recommendation diversity while maintaining performance, achieving a significant 3.412% increase in Gross Merchandise Value (GMV).

## Technical note

On-policy distillation (OPD) gives SID-based generative recommenders dense token-level supervision from a teacher along student-generated prefixes, but it amplifies popularity bias round by round: the teacher favors head-item tokens, the updated student generates them more often, and tail paths receive little supervision (on 1688, OPD's head-item share exceeds SFT and GRPO even as hit rate rises). HERO (Taobao/1688) trains two teachers from scratch, one on head targets and one on tail targets (each with a 10% sample of the other group), routes each example to the teacher matching its ground-truth item's popularity group, and matches the student's full next-token distributions to that teacher by forward KL on the J trajectories of a trie-constrained beam search under the current student; a diversity-aware rollout adds ground-truth cross-entropy for every example and tail-teacher KL along the ground-truth SID path for tail targets, all at unit weight, with only the student used online. On 1688 (6-layer PrefixLM, 4-level SIDs with 512-entry codebooks, Beam-240) and Amazon Beauty, Pet and MovieLens-1M (Qwen3-Embedding-8B RQ codes, Beam-20), HERO attains the best overall and tail hit rates and the demand-to-recommendation ratio closest to 1 against SASRec, TIGER, SFT, GT-KD, off-policy KD, GKD, MOPD, SID-GRPO, Ghost-AUO and BEAR (1688 Click@240 0.5819 vs 0.5786 for GKD, DTR* 0.392 vs 0.715). Ablations show target-aware routing beats either single teacher and a fixed 50/50 mixture, tail-reweighted SFT cannot match it, and denser teacher feedback shifts generations toward head items unless corrected. A 15-day user-level A/B test in the 1688 'Guess What You Like' module raised GMV 3.412% (p=0.0104, CI [0.76%, 6.07%]) over the existing generative recommender with higher tail exposure, clicks and CTR and no latency change.

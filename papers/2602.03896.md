## Summary

This paper systematically compares two methods for gradient estimation in Poisson-distributed latent variable models: Exponential Arrival Time (EAT) simulation and Gumbel-SoftMax (GSM) relaxation. The authors introduce a modification to EAT that guarantees unbiased first moments and reduces second-moment bias, while also evaluating the distributional fidelity and performance of both methods on tasks involving Poisson VAEs and latent neural connectivity inference from spike trains. Their findings indicate that the modified EAT method offers superior overall performance and robustness for standard Poisson models compared to GSM, but only GSM generalizes effectively to non-Poisson or under-dispersed distributions, providing practical recommendations for researchers working with such models.

## Technical note

This paper gives the first systematic comparison of two gradient estimators for Poisson-distributed latent variable models: Exponential Arrival Time (EAT) simulation and Gumbel-SoftMax (GSM) relaxation. Its main contribution is a modified EAT that theoretically guarantees an unbiased first moment (matching the firing rate) and reduces second-moment bias; the cubic variant (EAT_cubic) achieves ~7x lower Wasserstein-1 distance to the true Poisson at high rates. Modified EAT performs best on Poisson VAEs and POGLM spike-train connectivity inference (also Negative Binomial), while only GSM generalizes to arbitrary non-Poisson/under-dispersed distributions.

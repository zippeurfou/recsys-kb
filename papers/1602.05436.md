## Summary

This paper introduces a new method for learning Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs), used for diverse product recommendations, by factorizing their kernel matrix into a low-rank form. This factorization significantly speeds up both the learning process (nearly 10x faster) and the prediction of recommendations (up to 20x faster), especially for large catalogs. Despite the reduced complexity, the approach achieves predictive performance comparable to traditional DPP methods and often outperforms other recommendation techniques.

## Technical note

The paper learns Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) for diverse product recommendation by factorizing the DPP kernel matrix L into a low-rank form instead of learning a full-rank kernel. This low-rank parameterization makes learning nearly an order of magnitude faster and prediction up to 20x faster for large item catalogs, where full-rank DPPs become intractable. DPPs jointly model set quality and diversity, favoring complementary rather than redundant recommendations (e.g., laptop plus cover, not two laptops). Across several real-world product datasets, the low-rank method matches or exceeds full-rank DPP predictive performance and outperforms competing recommendation methods.

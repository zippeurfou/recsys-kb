## Summary

Deep Recommender Model (DLRM) inference is a major AI workload at Meta, but its performance is often limited by inefficient random memory accesses during embedding lookups. This paper introduces strategies to optimize these lookups on individual cores and an automatic framework for mapping tables unevenly across multiple SoC cores based on their access patterns. Their approach demonstrates significant speed-ups (up to 20x) compared to standard methods, especially in unbalanced scenarios, proving better independence from the specific query distribution than existing baselines like Huawei Ascend or Nvidia A100 compilers.

## Technical note

This paper optimizes Deep Learning Recommender Model (DLRM) inference, whose bottleneck is the many random memory accesses in embedding-layer lookups (>79% of Meta's AI workload). It proposes four per-core strategies for efficient embedding-table lookup plus an automatic framework that maps tables asymmetrically across the multiple cores of a SoC based on access patterns. Evaluated on Huawei Ascend accelerators against the default compiler (with high-level Nvidia A100 comparisons), it achieves 1.5x-6.5x speedups on real workloads and over 20x on extremely unbalanced distributions, while being far less sensitive to the query distribution than baselines.

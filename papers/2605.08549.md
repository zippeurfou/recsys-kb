## Technical note

This paper evaluates whether LLMs can recover 'developmental cognition' signal from text, grounded in Kegan's constructive-developmental theory of meaning-making. It introduces the Developmental Sentence Completion Test (DSCT), a 20-item self-administered instrument, and tests three response regimes: simulated personas, real human respondents, and default model-generated answers. Frontier models recover simulator-intended labels with high accuracy on synthetic personas, human-LLM agreement is only fair on real DSCT text (stronger within-neighborhood than exact), and unconditioned model responses show stable stage-like differences across families with larger/newer models rating higher -- suggesting the core constraint for stage-aware conversational AI is availability of developmental signal, not classifier accuracy alone.

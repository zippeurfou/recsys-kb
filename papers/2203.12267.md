## Summary

This paper introduces PEAR, a novel personalized re-ranking model for recommendation systems. Unlike traditional methods that score items individually, PEAR explicitly models how items interact with each other within a list using a contextualized transformer. It captures both item-level and feature-level interactions, considers user context from the initial list and history, and includes a list-level satisfaction task during training, leading to better re-ranking performance than existing methods.

## Technical note

PEAR is a personalized re-ranking model for the final stage of the matching-ranking-re-ranking pipeline, built on a contextualized transformer. Unlike prior re-rankers (DLCM, PRM, SetRank) that only encode the initial ranking list, PEAR jointly models feature-level and item-level interactions and uses merged sequence-to-sequence cross-attention to condition on the user's historical clicked list as well as the initial list. Training combines an item-level click prediction (binary cross-entropy) with an auxiliary list-level classification task via a CLS token, in a multi-task objective. On MicroVideo-1.7M and a Huawei production news dataset, PEAR outperforms baselines on gAUC@K and nDCG@K, with performance improving as historical sequence length grows to 128.

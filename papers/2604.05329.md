## Summary

Generative Recommendation systems using high-granularity Semantic IDs (SIDs) face two major problems: inefficient training due to redundant tokens and unstable performance, stemming from the "Semantic Dilution Effect".

The authors propose STAMP, a dual-end optimization framework addressing both issues. STAMP uses Semantic Adaptive Pruning (SAP) to filter out redundant tokens during processing, cleaning up the input sequences, and Multi-step Auxiliary Prediction (MAP) to strengthen long-range dependencies by adding denser output supervision.

Experiments show STAMP significantly improves training efficiency (up to 1.38x speedup) and reduces VRAM usage (up to 54.7%) while maintaining or even improving recommendation performance across different architectures.

## Technical note

STAMP is a dual-end optimization framework for Semantic-ID generative recommendation (SID-GR) that attributes both training inefficiency and unstable, non-monotonic accuracy to a shared 'Semantic Dilution Effect': high-granularity SIDs expand sequences with redundant tokens (feature-level and sequence-level) while supervision remains sparse (signal only from the final target item, with joint-probability chain-rule error accumulation). On the input side, Semantic Adaptive Pruning (SAP) scores token utility by the product of semantic saliency (L1-norm feature magnitude) and attention centrality, then order-preservingly compresses sequences at a designated layer (with a protected recent window for decoder-only models). On the output side, Multi-step Auxiliary Prediction (MAP) adds a multi-token objective to densify supervision and strengthen long-range dependency capture. On Amazon and an industrial dataset over T5 and Qwen backbones, STAMP yields 1.23-1.38x speedup and 17.2-54.7% VRAM reduction while maintaining or improving recommendation performance.

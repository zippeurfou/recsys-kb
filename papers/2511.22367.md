## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of continual learning for large language models (LLMs), where models must learn many new tasks without forgetting old ones. The authors tackle two key issues: selecting which task sequences to rehearse and integrating new knowledge effectively. They propose Surprise-prioritised Replay (SuRe) to select surprising sequences for rehearsal and a dual-learner system with LoRA adapters and exponential moving average (EMA) for knowledge consolidation. Combining SuRe with the dual-learner significantly improves performance, especially in settings with many tasks, demonstrating that surprise-based selection and slow-weight consolidation are effective strategies for mitigating forgetting.

## Technical note

SuRe targets catastrophic forgetting in continual fine-tuning of LLMs by decomposing forgetting into a selection term (what to rehearse) and an integration term (how to consolidate), proving an additive bound over these. For selection it proposes Surprise-prioritised Replay, storing the highest-NLL (most surprising) sequences, which reaches state-of-the-art in the large-number-of-tasks (LNT) setting. For integration it adds a dual-learner with fast and slow LoRA adapters merged via an exponential moving average, whose variance reduction is theoretically bounded. Combining SuRe with the EMA dual-learner yields up to +5 accuracy points over prior SOTA on LNT and remains robust under small buffers and reduced replay frequency.

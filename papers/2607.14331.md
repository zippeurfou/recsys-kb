## Summary

Here are three sentences summarizing the paper:

The authors address the challenge of incorporating long interaction histories into online ad ranking systems that require extremely fast CTR prediction (within milliseconds). They decouple history processing by using an offline transformer model trained on vast logs to generate compact, cached representations of user histories. These cached features are then combined with recent events during real-time serving by a lightweight model, achieving near-full-history performance gains (+2.77% CTR) without exceeding latency constraints or needing expensive recomputation at each request.

## Technical note

Yandex ad CTR system that makes long-history transformer ranking deployable by decoupling history encoding from real-time inference. A high-capacity 10-layer offline transformer (full causal attention, ALiBi) asynchronously encodes each user's full cross-surface interaction history (up to 8192 events) into a fixed-size vector cached in a feature store, while a lightweight 5-layer runtime transformer with windowed attention (receptive field 100) consumes the freshest events plus request context; the two are fused in a two-tower (DCNv2 + MLP) CTR head scored by inner product. The backbone is pre-trained autoregressively (ARGUS recipe) with a dual objective - click/feedback prediction (BCE) and next-item retrieval (sampled softmax with logQ correction, in-batch negatives) - then fine-tuned per surface with position debiasing. The split recovers 72-80% of the full-history quality ceiling; cached embeddings degrade slowly (0.38pp after 5 days) while a stale checkpoint costs 3.40pp after 6 months. Online A/B lifts the primary acceptance metric +2.77% (Search Ads) and +2.10% (YAN) with revenue gains (+2.26%/+0.43%) and no serving-latency increase.

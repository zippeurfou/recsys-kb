## Summary

This paper addresses the limitations of existing CTR models, which either lose important behavioral details during sequence aggregation or introduce noise when directly interacting all user behaviors with item features. To overcome this, the authors propose CDNet, a dual-view interaction network that models both fine-grained interactions between highly relevant user behaviors and item features, and coarse-grained interactions between the user's overall interest distribution and item features. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CDNet effectively balances capturing important behavioral details and holistic user interest, leading to improved CTR prediction accuracy.

## Technical note

CDNet (Core-Behaviors and Distributional-Compensation Dual-View Interaction Network) bridges user behavior sequences and contextual item features for CTR prediction, avoiding both information loss from pre-aggregating sequences into a single vector and the noise/quadratic cost of interacting every behavior with contextual features. A Fine-grained Core-Behaviors Module selects a small subset of behaviors highly relevant to the candidate item and interacts them at fine granularity with contextual features, sharply reducing the number of interacting behaviors. A complementary Coarse-grained Interest-Distribution Module models the similarity distribution of the entire history relative to the candidate item as a summary of global interest for coarse-grained interaction, preserving holistic context without reintroducing behavior noise. Fusing the two views captures fine behavioral detail while staying computationally efficient; offline and online experiments validate the design.

## Summary

Based on the abstract, here's a 3-sentence summary:

1.  Personalized LLMs interpret user emotions differently based solely on their internal memory of the user's profile (e.g., single mother vs. wealthy executive).
2.  Evaluations show systematic biases where models provide more accurate or favorable emotional interpretations for users with advantageous backgrounds.
3.  This research highlights a significant risk: incorporating long-term user memory into AI could inadvertently embed social hierarchies and reinforce inequalities in how emotions are understood and supported.

## Technical note

This study asks whether injecting long-term user memory (profiles) into LLMs biases their emotional reasoning. Evaluating 15 models on validated emotional-intelligence tests (STEU understanding, a modified STEM guidance task) with explicit advantaged/disadvantaged personas and 81 intersectional demographic personas, it finds user memory systematically shifts judgments (significant in 11/15 models), with advantaged profiles receiving more accurate interpretations and systematic biases across gender, religion, and age quantified via mixed-effects models and flip rate. As mitigation, the authors curate a preference dataset teaching models to acknowledge that persona details are irrelevant and apply DPO to Gemma-2-2B and Qwen-3-1.7B, which reduces the advantaged-vs-disadvantaged bias gap (even reversing its sign) while preserving or improving MMLU, at some cost to instruction following.

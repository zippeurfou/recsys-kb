## Technical note

Training LM-based conversational recommender systems (CRSs) is hard due to scarce public dialogue data, and LM user simulators often lack behavioral consistency, producing utterances no real user would generate. ICER generates preference-consistent dialogues in three stages: a behavior simulator grounded in collaborative-filtering user/item embeddings and choice models produces acceptance/rejection, example critiques, and preference-elicitation responses from sampled user embeddings; these are templatized into NL; then an LM refines them for naturalness. Soft attributes are handled via concept activation vectors, and the approach draws on dialogue inpainting. Using ICER on MovieLens 25M they release MD-DICER, a 100K-dialogue dataset with user/item embeddings, and rater evaluation shows the dialogues are more natural, fluent, factual, and consistent than templatized counterparts.

## Summary

Existing social recommendation models primarily focus on predicting individual items (blogs, products) and largely overlook the unique characteristics of communities like their dynamic nature and structural patterns within social networks. This paper introduces CASO, a novel model specifically designed for recommending entire communities by incorporating these aspects through three carefully crafted encoders: one focusing on global community structure via modularity maximization, another analyzing local user connections using closeness aggregation, and the third capturing individual item preferences with collaborative filtering.

Furthermore, CASO integrates mutual exclusion between its social structural signals and collaborative filtering signals to reduce redundancy in user embeddings. It also adds a community detection loss during optimization to ensure the learned community representations are sensitive to their overall structure. Extensive experiments across six real-world datasets show that CASO consistently outperforms nine strong baselines, demonstrating superior performance for community recommendation compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

CASO targets social community recommendation, which prior social recommenders handle poorly because communities are dynamic and defined by member structure rather than being static items. It learns user embeddings with three disentangled encoders: Social Modularity Maximization for global inter/intra-community structure, Social Closeness Aggregation for local neighborhood structure, and User-based Collaborative Encoding for user-community affiliations. A Feature Mutual Exclusion module minimizes empirical HSIC between social and collaborative signals to remove redundancy, and a KL-divergence community-detection loss yields community-aware representations. Across six real-world social networks CASO consistently beats nine strong baselines on community recommendation.

## Summary

Spotify developed an AI-driven system called Hypothesis-Driven Shelf Generation (HDSG) to automate creating personalized recommendation "shelves" like "More of What You Like". This system generates natural language hypotheses about shelf content, then uses generative AI and ranking models to independently optimize both the shelf theme generation and the specific songs/artists retrieved for that shelf. Offline evaluations show HDSG expands recommendations for long-tail tastes with varying engagement levels and performs competitively compared to existing shelves in some cases.

## Technical note

Spotify Home shelves (themed rows) are usually built from hand-crafted templates that cannot scale to the long tail of individual taste. This system reframes shelf creation as generative planning across four stages: a distilled compact LLM generates natural-language shelf hypotheses from a user profile, a separate fulfilment model performs constrained generative retrieval over Semantic IDs (per-content-type trie-constrained decoding) to ground each hypothesis in catalogue entities, and an LLM shelf-alignment stage selects final items and rewrites titles, before offline precomputed serving. Evaluated with two LLM-as-a-judge rubrics and uniform-random-exposure online tests, generative retrieval beats BM25/MiniLM/hybrid baselines on every dimension (overall 0.71 vs 0.56), alignment lifts overall shelf quality +78%, and served shelves are competitive (album pool +36%, episode +2%) while greatly expanding personalized supply. The distilled generator matches the frontier LLM (78.2% vs 78.3%). It decouples planning from fulfilment and lets shelves compete in the existing Home ranker rather than collapsing the cascade.

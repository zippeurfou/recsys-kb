## Summary

Benchmarking and Enhancing Small Language Models for Reasoning Evaluation":

1.  The paper introduces JudgeBoard, a new evaluation pipeline that directly queries small language models (SLMs) to judge answer correctness, unlike prior methods that required comparing answers.
2.  JudgeBoard evaluates SLMs on specific reasoning tasks using leaderboards, and the authors propose MAJ, a multi-agent framework using multiple SLMs with different reasoning styles to improve judgment accuracy.
3.  While SLMs struggle with the judging task compared to large models, the MAJ framework significantly improves their reliability, with smaller SLMs even outperforming larger ones on some benchmarks, suggesting multi-agent systems can achieve LLM-level judgment performance.

## Technical note

The paper introduces JudgeBoard, an evaluation pipeline that directly asks (small) language models to judge answer correctness on math and science/commonsense reasoning, rather than the indirect comparison-based LLM-as-a-judge setups, and ranks judges via accuracy and an Elo-style rating across five benchmarks. It proposes MAJ (Multi-Agent Judging), where multiple SLMs with distinct reasoning-profile system prompts deliberate collaboratively to approximate LLM-level judging. Isolated SLMs show a large judging gap versus LLMs, but MAJ substantially closes it; on MATH, MAJ with Qwen3-14B backbones beats the best single LLM by ~2%, and smaller-model ensembles sometimes exceed larger counterparts.

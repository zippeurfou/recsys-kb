## Summary

Uber Eats replaces its legacy Mini-LM search retriever with a fine-tuned two-tower gte-Qwen2-1.5B LLM embedding model, trained with two-stage InfoNCE + Triplet-NCE contrastive learning and served with Matryoshka representation learning plus INT8 quantization on an ANN index. It delivers large offline recall gains and positive online CVR/CoPSU lifts across six markets and three verticals (store/dish/grocery).

## Technical note

Uber Eats builds a unified multilingual, multi-vertical (store/dish/grocery) semantic search retrieval system by fine-tuning a two-tower gte-Qwen2-1.5B LLM encoder to replace a legacy Mini-LM baseline. Training is two-stage -- large-scale InfoNCE contrastive learning with in-batch negatives followed by a Triplet-NCE stage on LLM-labeled hard positives/negatives -- with JSON-structured inputs and EOS pooling found most effective, and Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) exposing nested {128..1536}-dim embeddings for latency/cost trade-offs. Offline, the final model lifts Store Recall@20 by +88% and Recall@200 by +31% over the Mini-LM baseline across six markets; a 256-dim INT8 MRL cut retains recall (R@200 0.668 vs 0.678 full-precision) while cutting index size to ~4% and index cost to ~15% of the 1536-dim FP32 vector. Online A/B tests show +0.44% intentful-search CVR, +0.54% CoPSU, and a -69% zero-result rate, with retrieval latency flat under INT8 quantization; serving uses a Lucene Plus ANN/HNSW index with a lightweight FFN neural distance head for micro re-ranking.

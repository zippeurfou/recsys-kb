## Summary

This paper introduces HotelQuEST, a new benchmark for agentic search focusing on both quality and efficiency, addressing the gap in existing evaluations that primarily prioritize quality and neglect the critical efficiency needed for real-world use. The benchmark features diverse hotel search queries, including complex ones with underspecified user preferences, for which the authors collected explicit clarifications to enable better evaluation. Their analysis reveals that while LLM-based agents achieve higher accuracy than traditional methods, they incur significantly higher costs due to inefficient tool calls and routing, highlighting substantial potential for cost-aware optimization in agentic search systems.

## Technical note

HotelQuEST is a benchmark of 214 handcrafted hotel search queries spanning simple to complex, many with underspecified preferences, that jointly evaluates answer quality (relevance, factuality) and efficiency (cost, token usage, latency)—a dimension prior agentic-search benchmarks ignore. To evaluate underspecified intent (e.g., ambiguous 'dog-friendly'), authors collect judge-only clarifications that make each annotator's implicit preferences explicit, plus complexity ratings and structured qualifier decompositions. Experiments show LLM-based agents beat lightweight retrievers on accuracy but at far higher cost due to redundant tool calls and routing that fails to match query complexity to model capability, and the paper establishes an efficiency upper bound. It motivates cost-aware agent design and adaptive fast/slow-thinking mode selection.

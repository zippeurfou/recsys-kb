## Summary

1.  Decision-making is difficult, especially with unstructured documents, and current AI tools often fail due to information overload or inaccurate preference capture.
2.  The paper introduces Decisive, an interactive system that uses document-grounded reasoning and Bayesian inference to actively learn user preferences through targeted tradeoff questions, grounding decisions in an objective option matrix.
3.  Decisive converges efficiently to personalized recommendations, significantly outperforming large language models and existing tools by boosting decision accuracy by up to 20%.

## Technical note

Decisive decouples decision support into objective document grounding and subjective preference modeling. An LLM extracts K per-scenario decision factors and scores each option on an 8-point ordinal scale (median-aggregated across GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude-4.5-Sonnet) to build an M x K Option-Scoring Matrix. User preferences are modeled as a uniform Dirichlet prior over weight vectors, approximated by P=500 particles updated via a sigmoid Bayesian rule on pairwise factor-preference answers. Questions are selected by maximizing Expected Information Gain over the decision distribution (entropy over argmax option) rather than over preferences, focusing elicitation on trade-offs that differentiate top options. On a curated Finance/Education/Hiring benchmark it reaches ~79-90% top-1 accuracy in 2-6 questions, beating fully-informed LLM baselines and DeLLMa/DecisionFlow, with zero LLM calls during interaction.

## Summary

This paper investigates the impact of periodic SID map updates on generative retrieval performance in dynamic e-commerce search environments. A key finding is that semantic drift from these updates significantly degrades retrieval quality (HitRate), especially at low beam sizes, though this degradation can be obscured by larger beam sizes generating redundant results. The second major finding reveals a strict monotonic relationship between the fraction of items requiring SID updates and the resulting HitRate drop. Crucially, the study advocates preserving the SID topology by freezing old SIDs and inserting new ones, which transforms even a basic warm-start strategy into a highly effective method, validated through extensive offline experiments and a positive online A/B test.

## Technical note

In Tmall/Taobao e-commerce search, a Qwen2.5-0.5B generative retriever generates 3-token SIDs (two RQ-VAE semantic levels trained with query-item contrastive loss, 8192x8192, plus a business-value rank token with hybrid collision-aware buckets); periodic refreshes of the SID-to-item map reassign old items and cause 'semantic drift' that invalidates learned decoding paths. Controlled experiments over 67 days of logs (~90M examples) show map updates cut old-item HitRate@1 by 41.78-62.05% but only 8.33-10.85% at beam 1000, so wide-beam offline evaluation masks the damage; HitRate falls strictly monotonically with the remapped fraction gamma (72.75% remap: HR@1 -42.66%), and even t=10 thresholded remapping is 12.4% below freezing. Under identical warm-start training, freezing old SIDs and inserting new items beats full remapping by +36.7% old-item HR@1 and +13.9% new-item HR@1, full remapping after warm-start still underperforms the frozen no-retrain model, and interpolating old/new checkpoints (model soup) never beats the pure new checkpoint. The resulting rule (freeze old SIDs, insert new items, warm-start weekly) yielded UCTCVR +0.29% and GMV +1.16% in a three-week 20%-traffic A/B test on TmallAPP.

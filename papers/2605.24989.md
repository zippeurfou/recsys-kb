## Summary

CTR prediction models face a challenge with sparse, uncertain data leading to unreliable predictions, while well-represented data yields confidence. Existing methods use fixed selection rules learned during training, offering no instance-specific adaptation. The paper proposes UTTSI, a training-free framework that dynamically allocates more compute to uncertain instances based on their prediction uncertainty, leading to significantly improved CTR predictions and confirming the effectiveness of selective test-time compute scaling.

## Technical note

UTTSI (Uncertainty-Triggered Test-Time Selective Inference) is a training-free, model-agnostic framework that scales CTR inference depth proportionally to per-instance uncertainty, addressing the training-inference asymmetry where sparse feature combinations yield unreliable predictions at serving. It estimates uncertainty from two signals—model-internal logit confidence and a frequency prior maintained via a probabilistic hashing structure—to separate epistemic uncertainty (sparse coverage) from aleatoric ambiguity near the decision boundary. Every instance undergoes adaptive feature filtering that removes embeddings with insufficient training support, and uncertain instances additionally receive a variable number of stochastic feature-path explorations aggregated by consistency-weighted ensembling, while confident instances bypass exploration. Average overhead is ~2.8x base cost with worst-case latency of a single forward pass under parallel serving, and it yields statistically significant gains over CTR baselines across four datasets, three backbones, and a seven-day online A/B test.

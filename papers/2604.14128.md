## Summary

Large language models represent rhetorical questions, asked to persuade rather than seek information, using multiple distinct linear patterns identified through linear probing. These patterns capture different cues, such as discourse-level stance or syntax-driven interrogative acts, rather than relying on a single shared representation. The study demonstrates that while rhetorical questions are detectable across different datasets, the specific way LLMs encode them varies significantly, highlighting the complexity of their internal representation.

## Technical note

This interpretability study probes how decoder-only LLMs (Qwen3-32B, Llama-3.3-70B) encode rhetorical vs information-seeking questions using three linear probes (training-free diffMean, logistic regression, linear SVM) on PCA-reduced (k=64) hidden states from two social-media datasets (RQ, SRAQ). Rhetorical signals emerge early and are most stably captured by last-token representations, reaching cross-dataset transfer AUROC ~0.7-0.8. Critically, transferability does not imply a single shared direction: probes trained on different datasets produce different rankings on the same corpus, with top-instance Jaccard overlap often below 0.2 and divergent Spearman orderings. Qualitatively, some probes capture discourse-level rhetorical stance while others capture localized syntax-driven interrogative acts, indicating rhetorical intent is encoded by multiple linear directions.

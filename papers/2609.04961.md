## Summary

E-commerce search often suffers from users and products having different vocabularies, especially when product titles are brief. Standard Doc2Query techniques, which are text-only, don't align well with business goals and ignore visual product attributes, leading to potentially ineffective search results. To address this, the paper introduces SAM-D2Q, a multimodal framework that combines vision-language understanding with reinforcement learning preference alignment, specifically designed for Boolean retrieval and commercial effectiveness, demonstrating significant improvements in both offline metrics and online business performance (GMV and Pay Count) when deployed.

## Technical note

AliExpress (Alibaba/Taobao) attacks vocabulary mismatch in Boolean inverted-index e-commerce retrieval with SAM-D2Q, a multimodal Doc2Query expander on a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone trained as a three-stage curriculum. The framing argument is that under Boolean matching a term's presence is binary, so TF re-weighting is worthless and only genuinely new terms add recall; Stage 1 pre-SFT therefore keeps only the 9M click-log pairs whose query tokens are not a subset of the product text (the information-gain constraint), which kills the summarization shortcut where the model just copies title tokens. Stage 2 builds 500k counterfactual samples from the complementary text-covered pairs by masking visual attribute values (color, material, pattern, neckline, sleeve style) identified via the platform's Category-Property-Value knowledge base while leaving the image intact, forcing the model to recover the target from pixels, and mixes them with information-gain-filtered human-labeled pairs. Stage 3 runs GRPO (64 rollouts per prompt, 80k item prompts, lr 1e-6, global batch 256, temperature 1.4, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3) on a gated composite utility U = I_gate * (lambda1 R_sem + lambda2 R_biz + lambda3 R_vis): R_sem comes from a Qwen3-VL relevance model trained on 3.5M human-annotated pairs, R_biz scores only the newly introduced terms by PV * CVR with Bayesian smoothing toward category means plus a global fallback for cold-start terms, R_vis indicates a new term hitting the category visual-attribute lexicon, and the binary safety gate zeroes the whole reward on low relevance so the model cannot farm popular-but-irrelevant terms. Offline on an 8.8M-item index with 30k stratified live queries, Top-3000 relevant items rise from 454.5 (titles only) to 594.1 (+30.7%) and Total Qual from 5,366M to 7,139M (+33.0%); intrinsic relevance goes 43.11% (text-only Qwen3-8B Doc2Query) to 70.42% (multimodal Stage-1 SFT) to 74.96% (full), while RL contributes mainly commercial Query-Value 0.969 to 0.981. A 21-day A/B on 4% of AliExpress traffic (June 8-28, 2025) delivered +3.38% GMV, +2.27% Pay Count, +2.98% UV Value, +0.25% CTR, +0.53% CVR and cut zero-result PV 17% from 2.07% to 1.72%; a post-rollout 14-day reverse-bucket holdout lost 2.80% GMV and 1.74% Pay Count. Deployment is offline indexing only (Top-100 generated queries per active product, no online model inference), inflating index size ~35% for +0.2% P99 recall latency, with commercial-weight refresh and GRPO realignment every two months. Caveats: only Pay Count and CVR cleared the platform's 95% confidence checks (other online numbers are directional), and offline relevance labels come from a frozen model judge rather than humans.

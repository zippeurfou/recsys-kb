## Summary

Explicit Sparsity for Scalable Recommendation":

1.  **Problem:** Large-scale recommendation systems face a performance bottleneck because dense neural network architectures (like deep MLPs) are ill-suited for inherently sparse user-item interaction data, often leading to diminishing returns or degradation despite increased model complexity.
2.  **Solution:** The paper proposes SSR (Explicit Sparsity for Scalable Recommendation), a novel architecture that explicitly incorporates sparsity. SSR uses a "filter-then-fuse" mechanism with two strategies (Static Random Filter and Iterative Competitive Sparse) to efficiently process inputs by filtering out low-utility connections before dense fusion.
3.  **Result:** Experiments on various datasets show SSR outperforms state-of-the-art baselines under similar computational budgets and crucially demonstrates superior scalability, providing continuous performance gains where dense models eventually plateau.

## Technical note

SSR (Explicit Sparsity for Scalable Recommendation) argues that dense MLP backbones mismatch the high-dimensional sparse nature of recommendation inputs; production CTR models show implicit sparsity where ~92% of weights are near-zero and 80% of weight mass lies in the top 4% of dimensions, making dense connectivity the scaling bottleneck. SSR shifts from implicit weight suppression to explicit signal filtering via a multi-view 'filter-then-fuse' mechanism, applying dimension-level sparse filtering before dense nonlinear fusion. It offers two strategies: a Static Random Filter (zero-FLOP fixed dimension subsets) and Iterative Competitive Sparse (ICS), a differentiable, sample-conditional dynamic filter using bio-inspired competition to retain high-response dimensions. On three public datasets and a billion-scale AliExpress dataset, SSR beats state-of-the-art baselines under matched compute and keeps improving where dense models saturate.

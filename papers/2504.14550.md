In multi-stakeholder recommender systems (RS), users and providers operate as two crucial and interdependent roles, whose interests must be well-balanced. Prior research, including our work BankFair, has demonstrated the importance of guaranteeing both provider fairness and user accuracy to meet their interests. ## Technical note

BankFair+ extends BankFair to jointly balance user accuracy, provider fairness, and user individual fairness in multi-stakeholder recommendation. It introduces a non-linear regret-theory function to model user satisfaction (capturing regret aversion) and formulates fair re-ranking as a regret-aware fuzzy programming problem solved by an online learning algorithm, replacing rigid linear programming that scales poorly with per-user constraints. On two real-world datasets (including KuaiRand-1K), BankFair+ outperforms baselines on individual fairness, user accuracy, and provider fairness simultaneously; the original BankFair left ~41% of users below the minimum accuracy threshold on high-average-accuracy days.

## Summary

The paper addresses two key limitations in using large multimodal language models (MLLMs) for deepfake detection (DFD): the lack of quality forgery knowledge and handling noisy information. To solve this, the authors propose VRAG-DFD, combining Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for dynamic, high-quality knowledge retrieval with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for critical reasoning. This framework is supported by two specialized datasets (Forensic Knowledge Database and Forensic Chain-of-Thought Dataset) and employs a three-stage training method. VRAG-DFD achieves state-of-the-art performance on DFD generalization tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach.

## Technical note

VRAG-DFD is an interpretable deepfake-detection framework that combines RAG and RL to overcome MLLMs' lack of forgery knowledge and susceptibility to noisy references. It builds two RAG-annotated datasets: a Forensic Knowledge Database (FKD) that annotates fake images using K highly-relevant retrieved case exemplars rather than single-image prompting, and a Forensic Chain-of-Thought (F-CoT) dataset for critical reasoning that cross-validates external retrieved evidence against internal visual analysis. A three-stage training curriculum (visual alignment on ViT/aligner/LLM, F-CoT SFT for critical thinking, then GRPO reinforcement learning) progressively cultivates the ability to balance conflicting evidence. It reduces hallucinations and achieves SOTA/competitive cross-dataset generalization on deepfake benchmarks with high interpretation fidelity.

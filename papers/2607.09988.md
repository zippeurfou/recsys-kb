## Summary

1.  Current recommendation systems struggle to effectively integrate diverse, unstructured contextual signals (like trends or events) into their rankings due to reliance on structured data and manual feature engineering.
2.  This paper introduces an LLM-powered agentic system for Connected TV content discovery that leverages large language models' reasoning capabilities to naturally process various heterogeneous information sources.
3.  The key contribution is the novel engineering approach combining flexible LLMs with traditional ML methods, particularly addressing inference latency, demonstrating how LLM technology can practically enhance recommendation systems despite their limitations in specific tasks.

## Technical note

Builds an LLM-powered agentic recommender for Connected TV content discovery that ingests heterogeneous unstructured context (trending events, breaking news, cross-surface activity) which traditional and unified-generative systems cannot reason over. An orchestrator coordinates three agents (a Llama-3-70B topic retrieval agent, a media retrieval+ranking agent, and a topic ranking agent) alongside traditional ML rankers (multi-task multi-label); MediaBrain, an LLM-native generative retrieval model (Llama-3.2-1B), maps natural-language prompts to media via 4-layer semantic IDs from RQ-kMeans (codebook 1024) using beam search plus prefix lookup. Engineering optimizations (per-component caching, cursor-anchored token persistence, vLLM fixes) yield a roughly 100x throughput speedup (2 to 200 QPS per A100) with neutral latency, and MediaBrain niche-entity relevance rises 0.15 to 0.50. It deliberately keeps the retrieval-then-ranking cascade and orchestrates hybrid LLM+ML components rather than unifying into a single generative model.

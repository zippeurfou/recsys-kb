## Summary

Standard alignment methods for language agents often prioritize task accuracy alone, neglecting crucial auxiliary objectives like tool-use efficiency needed for practical applications. This limitation motivated the development of ParetoPO, a two-stage multi-objective optimization framework designed to align large language models with competing goals such as high accuracy and efficient use of tools. Experiments on complex reasoning tasks demonstrate that ParetoPO successfully discovers better trade-offs between these objectives than traditional static or heuristic alignment methods.

## Technical note

ParetoPO is a two-stage multi-objective online RL framework for aligning tool-using LLM agents under competing objectives such as task accuracy and tool-use efficiency, addressing limits of fixed-weight scalarization (which misallocates effort across differently-scaled objectives and only recovers convex Pareto regions) and expensive per-objective gradient methods. Stage one uses hypervolume-guided dynamic scalarization to adapt reward weights in real time based on smoothed global Pareto-frontier progress, improving exploration of the objective space. Stage two replaces scalarized signals with Pareto-ranking-based advantage computation via non-dominated sorting, promoting nondominated trajectories through dominance-aware, action-level credit assignment toward Pareto-ascent stationary points. On mathematical reasoning and multi-hop QA, it discovers policies with superior accuracy-efficiency trade-offs (e.g., fewer tool calls) compared to static and heuristic baselines.

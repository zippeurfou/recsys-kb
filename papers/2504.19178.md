## Summary

This paper introduces Relative Contrastive Learning (RCL) for sequential recommendation. It addresses a limitation in existing Supervised Contrastive Learning approaches where the scarcity of true positive samples (sequences with the same target item) leads to insufficient contrastive signals.

To overcome this, RCL uses two tiers: strong positives derived from exact same-target sequences and weak positives selected based on similarity scores between different items. The core innovation is a weighted relative loss function that forces each sequence's representation closer to its strong positive targets than its weaker ones.

RCL improves upon state-of-the-art SR methods by significantly enhancing their performance, achieving an average improvement of 4.88% across five public and one private dataset using two different deep learning models.

## Technical note

The paper tackles the scarcity of same-target sequences that limits supervised contrastive learning (SCL) for sequential recommendation, where nearly half of sequences have no same-target partner. It proposes Relative Contrastive Learning (RCL), which also treats similar sequences (different target items but high Jaccard/Bigram similarity) as positives. A dual-tiered positive-sample selection module labels same-target sequences as strong positives and similar sequences as weak positives via a weighted sampling strategy over order-sensitive/insensitive similarity metrics. A weighted relative contrastive loss adds a boundary to InfoNCE so each sequence is pulled closer to its strong than its weak positives without over-repelling weak positives. Applied to SASRec and FMLP, RCL yields 4.88% average improvement over state-of-the-art methods on six datasets.

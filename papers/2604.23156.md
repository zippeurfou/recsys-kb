## Summary

Local service recommendations often struggle with ensuring suggestions are geographically feasible, a problem exacerbated by existing systems that tokenize locations without considering proximity. To address this, the paper introduces Pro-GEO, a novel recommendation system that integrates geographic constraints directly into its semantic tokenization using a geo-centroid coordinate system and a geo-rotary encoding mechanism. Pro-GEO effectively balances semantic and spatial signals, leading to significantly better recommendations, as demonstrated by a 45.60% reduction in average geographic clustering distance and a 1.87% improvement in Hit@50 on a large-scale dataset.

## Technical note

Pro-GEO is a proximity-aware geo-codebook for generative local-service recommendation, addressing that over 50% of POIs sharing a semantic ID are >40km apart because semantic variance dominates codebook clustering. It builds a two-layer semantic codebook via cosine-similarity RQ-Kmeans with orthogonal projection residuals, then adds a geo-centroid local coordinate system that anchors each SID cluster to its geographic centroid and converts global lat/long into polar coordinates to preserve fine-grained intra-cluster spatial relations. A Geo-RoPE mechanism encodes each POI's spatial offset as complex-valued orthogonal rotations applied to the codeword embedding, so spatially nearby POIs share similar embeddings without collapsing semantics. A fine-tuned LLM then generates candidates while excluding infeasible cross-city deliveries. On a large industrial dataset it cuts average geographic clustering distance by 45.60% without codebook collapse and improves Hit@50 by 1.87%.

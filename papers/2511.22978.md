## Summary

This paper introduces ShoppingComp, a new benchmark designed to rigorously test large language models (LLMs) used in e-commerce shopping agents. It evaluates three key capabilities: finding precise products, generating expert reports, and making safe decisions, focusing on real-world scenarios and verifiable outcomes. The benchmark reveals significant limitations in current LLMs, including serious errors like failing to identify unsafe products, even for top models like GPT-5 and Gemini, highlighting a major gap between research and practical deployment.

## Technical note

ShoppingComp is a benchmark of 145 instances and 558 scenarios curated by 35 experts to evaluate LLM-powered shopping agents on precise open-world product retrieval, expert-level comparative report generation, and safety-critical decision making. Unlike prior closed-catalog benchmarks it uses open-world purchasable products, multi-constraint goal-oriented queries, and verifiable rubrics with supporting evidence. Even frontier models score very low (e.g. ~17.8% GPT-5.2, ~15.8% Gemini-3-Pro), with errors concentrated in open-world information grounding, multi-constraint verification, reasoning over conflicting evidence, and risk-aware safety judgments, exposing a large research-to-deployment trust gap.

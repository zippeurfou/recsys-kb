## Summary

The paper addresses the lack of predictable scaling laws for Large Language Models (LLMs) used in recommendation systems, primarily due to noisy real-world data. It introduces a novel "curriculum" of high-quality synthetic data designed to overcome these data limitations. Experiments show that models trained on this synthetic data significantly outperform those using real data and exhibit robust, predictable power-law scaling, establishing a foundation for reliably enhancing LLM capabilities in recommendations.

## Technical note

This Meta paper argues that the absence of scaling laws for LLM recommenders is a data problem, not an algorithmic one: raw interaction logs carry position, popularity, and exposure bias that LLMs codify and amplify. It proposes a layered synthetic-data curriculum: Layer 1 grounds item-text alignment and collaborative-filtering association data, and Layer 2 generates position-debiased user-interaction histories via graph-based random walks (Node2Vec) over a CF graph, which have no intrinsic order. Standard sequential models (GRU4Rec, NARM, STAMP, SASRec) trained on this synthetic data beat real-data training (+130% recall@100 for SASRec), and continual pre-training on it yields the first robust power-law scaling (0.6B-8B params, 163B tokens) with L(D)=L_inf + A*D^-alpha. UIH data scales strongest (alpha ~0.45-0.59), CF next (~0.35), item-text weakest (~0.15), with asymmetric CF-to-UIH transfer reducing asymptotic UIH loss by 31%.

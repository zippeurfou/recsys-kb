## Summary

This paper introduces **Multi-Decoder OneRec**, a framework designed to manage multiple objectives in industrial recommender systems effectively. It achieves this by combining shared user representations and decoding mechanisms (General Decoder) with isolated objective-specific LoRA experts, addressing the fragmentation issues from separate retrieval routes while maintaining control over each goal like recall or watch time.

The system uses **specific training techniques** during pretraining: exposure-sample NTP updates the base model, target-filtered NTP trains individual expert decoders to understand their own objectives (like filtering), and KL-regularized policy optimization specifically develops a decoder focused on maximizing long-term engagement metrics such as watch-time. Different gradients are routed for each update type.

Evaluation shows **significant improvements**, demonstrating that Multi-Decoder OneRec outperforms its single-decoder baseline across various Recall@512 metrics under the same retrieval budget, and crucially validates this in production with positive A/B test results improving user engagement and retention. The authors also released a large-scale benchmark dataset (Kwai26) to facilitate further research into multi-objective recommendation systems.

## Technical note

Multi-Decoder OneRec is a controllable multi-objective generative retrieval framework (Kuaishou) that keeps a shared user-context encoder and General Decoder for the common Semantic-ID prior while attaching an isolated, gradient-routed LoRA expert (plus task BOS and SID-embedding residual) per objective. Event objectives train with target-filtered next-token prediction and the Watch-time expert with KL-regularized group-relative-reward policy optimization (L-GBPO); Multi-Decoder Constrained Beam Search (MD-CBS) assigns explicit per-route quotas and masks claimed SID prefixes to produce complementary, duplicate-free candidates under a fixed 512-item budget. It beats single-decoder OneRec by 1.69%-5.62% on four Recall@512 metrics and, in a Kwai Brazil A/B test, lifts usage time +0.37% and cold-start +2.09%; the authors release the billion-record Kwai26 benchmark. It is a candidate-generation (retrieval) model feeding downstream ranking and re-ranking, not a unified one-stage pipeline.

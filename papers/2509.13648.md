## Summary

This paper argues that sequential data augmentation, used to train generative recommendation models by creating synthetic user interaction sequences, is crucial but often poorly understood. Existing methods treat it inconsistently or simplistically without a clear framework for optimizing its effects on model generalization and performance.

Motivated by this gap, the authors conducted an analysis showing significant performance differences based on augmentation strategies. They then propose GenPAS, a principled framework that models augmentation as stochastic sampling over input-target pairs across three steps (sequence, target, input) to systematically control bias and unify different approaches.

Experiments demonstrate that GenPAS consistently produces better recommendation accuracy while requiring less training data and fewer parameters compared to standard augmentation techniques.

## Technical note

This paper systematically studies data augmentation (training-pair construction) in generative recommendation, showing augmentation choice can change NDCG@10 by up to 783.7%, often exceeding architecture effects. It analyzes Last-Target, Multi-Target, and Slide-Window strategies via their target distribution (KL to test) and input-target distribution (alignment vs discrimination), with theory bounding training-vs-test target TV distance by recency bias and sampling variance. It proposes GenPAS, a principled three-step stochastic sampling framework (sequence, target, input sampling with bias exponents alpha/beta/gamma) that unifies existing strategies as special cases and enables an efficient training-free parameter search. On SASRec and TIGER across benchmark and industrial data GenPAS gives 2.3-59% gains plus strong data and parameter efficiency.

## Summary

Modern vector retrieval systems often operate successfully in high dimensions, seemingly contradicting classical "curse of dimensionality" theories. This paper resolves this paradox by focusing on the concept of stability – ensuring small query changes don't drastically alter nearest neighbors. It provides theoretical guarantees and experimental validation for stability in three common retrieval scenarios (multi-vector, filtered, sparse search), demonstrating that specific metrics (like Chamfer distance) and conditions can preserve stability, thereby enabling robust and efficient search regardless of dimension.

## Technical note

This theoretical paper resolves the paradox that high-dimensional vector retrieval works despite the curse of dimensionality, using the notion of stability (small query perturbations do not radically change nearest neighbors), characterized via relative variance. It extends stability theory to three practical settings: multi-vector search, proving Chamfer distance preserves single-vector stability while average pooling can destroy it (justifying ColBERT); filtered search, proving sufficiently large mismatch penalties induce stability even when unfiltered search is unstable; and sparse search, formalizing concentration-of-importance and overlap-of-importance as sufficient stability conditions. Synthetic and real-embedding experiments confirm the theory, giving concrete model and system-design guidance.

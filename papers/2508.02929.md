## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of deploying large-scale recommender system models effectively, despite promising performance gains from scaling laws. It proposes the "Foundation-Expert Paradigm," which involves training a central model on diverse user data to learn general patterns and then efficiently transferring this knowledge via embeddings to lightweight, surface-specific expert models for adaptation with minimal overhead. To support this approach in production, the authors developed HyperCast infrastructure; their system is now deployed at Meta, serving billions of requests daily, demonstrating online improvements over previous methods while boosting developer velocity and efficiency.

## Technical note

This work operationalizes scaling laws for recommenders via a Foundation-Expert paradigm that decouples a central foundation model from many lightweight surface-specific expert models. The FM is trained on lifelong, cross-surface, multi-modal user data to capture generalizable knowledge, which is transferred to experts through target-aware embeddings so each expert adapts to its local distribution and objective with minimal overhead, addressing streaming data, distribution shift, and per-surface diversity. HyperCast, a production-grade system, re-engineers training, serving, logging, and iteration to support this decoupled design. Deployed at Meta serving tens of billions of requests daily, it improved online metrics over the prior one-stage system while boosting developer velocity and keeping infra efficiency.

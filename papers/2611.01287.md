## Summary

This paper introduces the "Dynamic Click Destinations (DCD)" framework, designed to personalize the post-ad-click landing experience for users to maximize Advertiser Value (AV). It addresses the limitations of static, one-size-fits-all landing page policies by showing that the optimal destination varies significantly across different contexts. Online experiments demonstrate that DCD, particularly a single-task policy model, significantly outperforms static routing by delivering tailored destinations per click based on value-weighted conversion outcomes.

## Technical note

After an offsite ad click the landing destination (web view vs the advertiser's installed app, landing page choice, native vs external surface) changes conversion outcomes, and a Meta holdout that flipped all eligible clicks from Web to App lost 1.05% Advertiser Value (value-weighted conversions) overall while gaining 0.1% on purchase-optimized ads, so Dynamic Click Destinations treats destination choice as a per-click contextual-bandit policy with heterogeneous treatment effects. Three DLRM-based generations were deployed: (1) from Web-landed clicks, predict whether the eventual conversion happens in App and route when that probability exceeds a threshold, noting that where the alternative destination is unreachable the bootstrap should instead randomly route a small traffic fraction to each destination to create exploration data; (2) two-task destination-conditioned CVR heads with the rule app_cvr > tau x web_cvr; (3) a single-task actor trained on A2C-inspired soft labels sigma(alpha x (logit_App - logit_Web)) from a larger destination-conditioned critic, directly targeting the relative advantage at the decision boundary. In 3-week A/B tests with 10% cells (>100B impressions and billions of clicks each) AV lifts over always-Web were +0.06%, +0.42% and +0.54% respectively. The single-task formulation also made offline Normalized Entropy deltas directionally agree with online AV deltas across candidate iterations, whereas the two-task model's offline wins frequently did not carry online, and the predicted destination ranked among the top-3 features when added to the eCVR ranking model.

## Summary

Modern recommender systems face challenges using ultra-long user sequences due to latency and memory, and existing summarization methods struggle with accurately capturing user preferences and retaining long-tail behaviors. To address this, the paper introduces HiSAC, a framework that identifies user-specific preference centers using hierarchical semantic IDs and sparse activation. HiSAC then employs soft-routing attention guided by these centers to aggregate historical data effectively, minimizing quantization errors. Evaluated on Taobao, HiSAC significantly compresses sequences, reduces costs, and improves click-through rates (CTR) in real-world deployment.

## Technical note

HiSAC (Hierarchical Sparse Activation Compression) compresses ultra-long user behavior sequences for production ranking by summarizing history into personalized interest centers. Items are encoded by a large multimodal encoder (text plus image) and discretized into multi-level semantic IDs via a Residual Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE), then organized into a global hierarchical semantic tree. A hierarchical voting mechanism sparsely activates user-specific 'interest-agents' at coarse and fine granularity by pruning and retaining the most representative tree branches. Soft-Routing Attention then aggregates historical behaviors in the learned semantic space, weighting each behavior by similarity to the agents to minimize quantization error and preserve rare long-tail preferences. Deployed on Taobao's 'Guess What You Like' homepage, HiSAC yields substantial sequence compression and serving-cost reduction with a consistent 1.65% online CTR uplift over prior compression baselines.

- The RQ-VAE encoder maps $e_{\text{mm}}$ into a latent vector $e\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, which is progressively quantized through $L$ residual codebook levels, each codebook containing a series of learnable codewords. At each level $l$, the quantized output $z^{(l)}$ is added to the residual from the previous level, enabling finer partitioning of the embedding space
- The quantized vector $\hat{z}$ (sum of residual quantizations) is passed to the decoder to reconstruct the original multimodal embedding $\hat{e}_{\text{mm}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$. This ensures that the quantization process preserves semantic fidelity
- SIM : A two-stage long-sequence modeling framework based on the GSU–ESU paradigm. It includes two variants: SIM-Hard and SIM-Soft. In SIM-Hard, the GSU stage performs retrieval using the item category as the matching key, whereas SIM-Soft relies on the cosine similarity between item embeddings. In the ESU stage, multi-head attention (MHA) is applied to perform cross-attention between the retrieved short sequence and the target item, enabling target-aware interaction modeling
- TWIN : In SIM, the retrieval objectives of the GSU and ESU stages are not fully aligned. TWIN addresses this by employing the same retrieval strategy (MHA-based cross-attention) and parameter settings in both stages, thereby enhancing the consistency and coherence of the overall model pipeline

## Summary

This paper examines how language models can assist in complex high-stakes decisions like emergency medical triage. It argues that effectively deploying these models requires understanding them as probabilistic decision systems rather than just prediction tools, similar to established clinical practices. The study demonstrates that capable language models adjust their recommendations based on explicit utility functions provided, revealing the potential impact of different decision objectives (e.g., minimizing missed emergencies or unnecessary escalations) on outcomes like treatment advice derived from clinical vignettes.

## Technical note

Not a recommender-systems paper - it is a Microsoft decision-analysis study of LLM emergency triage - but its core argument (a scoring model plus an unstated implicit utility equals an uncontrolled operating point) transfers directly to any ranking system with asymmetric error costs. Revisiting the Ramaswamy et al. (2026) ChatGPT Health stress test (576 primary cases, 64 emergencies; 1,248 in the expanded set), the authors separate probabilistic assessment from utility judgment by eliciting a probability of needing emergency care and the triage decision in two separate queries, then fitting a discrete-choice logistic model to recover the single break-even threshold - and hence the single false-negative:false-positive cost ratio - that best explains the model's own decisions given its own probabilities. All evaluated frontier models discriminate well (AUROC 0.95-0.99 primary, 0.88-0.94 expanded), so the deployed tool's 48% sensitivity at a ~10% false-alarm rate is mostly an extreme resource-prioritized operating point rather than a diagnostic-competence failure; the other model families default to 94-100% sensitivity at 12-28% false alarms, and recovered default cost ratios vary widely across model family and reasoning level. Plain-language cost-ratio prompts ('decision-analytic steering') move models along the ROC curve - GPT-5-mini prompted with 5:1 or 10:1 gains roughly 50% more correctly routed emergencies at no increase in unnecessary referrals (about 30% on the expanded set) - but steering fidelity depends on capability and inference-time reasoning (Claude Fable/Sonnet 5 shift in the right direction but with damped magnitude; small models with reasoning off barely move). Since most models are poorly calibrated despite good ranking, the recommended alternative is to fit a model-specific external threshold on a modest set of locally reviewed cases for the desired target cost ratio, and to always evaluate over the full trade-off curve rather than at a single default operating point.

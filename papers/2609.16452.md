## Summary

Facebook Marketplace introduced a system called PCap to improve search result diversity by adding user-level constraints during the candidate item retrieval stage. PCap works by modeling individual diversity preferences using Shannon entropy and segmenting users into buckets, then applying personalized category caps to limit diversity in certain areas. This framework, combined with an automated Parameter Tuning Sequence, was shown in large-scale online experiments to significantly enhance the user browsing experience by improving engagement metrics.

## Technical note

PCap is Meta's personalized capping framework that injects user-level diversity constraints into the retrieval stage of the Facebook Marketplace browse feed, rather than at ranking where prior industry work placed diversification. Items are grouped by Facebook Product Taxonomy (FPT) category, predicted by an image-text classifier over hundreds of categories. A user's diversity preference is the Shannon entropy of their clicked FPT distribution over a sliding window, min-max normalized with a smoothing constant l (set to 0 for full personalization) and bucketed into six roughly equal-sized, day-over-day stable buckets; entropy replaces the incumbent normalized Herfindahl-Hirschman Index because HHI ignores engagement volume (four all-distinct clicks and ten all-distinct clicks both score 1). The per-group cap equals the retrieval source fetch count times a tuned per-source multiplier f_k times a per-bucket personalization multiplier m_u, enforced both at indexer-shard scan time and at cross-source aggregation so multi-source recall and latency budgets survive. The m_u values were learned by Parameter Tuning Sequence, an in-house sequential range-narrowing online grid search chosen over Bayesian optimization or bandits because engagement metrics are noisy per arm and the QE infrastructure is built for fixed-allocation arms; it converged monotonically to relaxed caps for buckets 1-2, neutral for 3-4, tighter for 5-6. Phase 1 (uniform capping vs no capping) moved only Viewport Views +0.3088%, with PDP clicks +0.0313% and Message Listing Interactions -0.2604% both insignificant, at +8.6ms latency. Phase 2 (PCap vs uniform capping) gave statistically significant VPV +0.2243%, PDP clicks +0.2250% and Marketplace Sessions +0.1708%, MLI +0.1109% not significant, for only +0.8ms. Per-bucket pagination-feed HHI moved as intended: bucket-1 -1.0537%, bucket-2 -0.9195%, bucket-6 +0.2469%. The headline practical insight is that personalized diversity pays off only at the preference extremes. Caveats: no offline evaluation at all by design, no MMR or DPP comparison (argued systemically incompatible with sharded retrieval lacking global relevance scores), sub-1% effect sizes, and users below a click threshold are excluded entirely.

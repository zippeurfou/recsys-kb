## Summary

This paper proposes Spatio-Temporal Periodic Interest Modeling (STIM) for local-life service recommendations, addressing challenges like long sequence sparsity and strong spatio-temporal dependence by drawing an analogy to human memory's recency effect and cyclicality. STIM integrates three key components: a dynamic masking module based on the forgetting curve to extract recent and periodic features; a query-based mixture of experts approach that adaptively activates networks under different masks for collaborative modeling; and a hierarchical multi-interest network capturing deep semantic interactions. The method demonstrates significant improvements in recommendation performance through online A/B tests (achieving 1.54% GTV uplift) and has been deployed at scale serving hundreds of millions of users daily.

## Technical note

STIM (Spatio-Temporal periodic Interest Modeling) models long, sparse user behavior sequences for local-life service recommendation by analogy to the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve's recency and periodicity. After GSU compression, a dynamic masking module groups behaviors by hour/week and geohash6 location and applies exponential memory-decay-with-review weighting to extract recent and periodic spatiotemporal features. A query-based mixture-of-experts adaptively activates expert networks per mask to jointly model time, location, and items, and a hierarchical multi-interest network captures shallow-deep semantic interactions. Deployed at scale for hundreds of millions of daily users, it delivered a 1.54% GTV uplift in online A/B tests plus offline gains.

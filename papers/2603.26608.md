## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of coordination errors (specifically early triggers) in VR's gaze-and-pinch interaction. The authors propose two heuristics, Sticky (temporal buffer) and Magnetic (spatial field), to automatically correct selection failures. Their study found these heuristics significantly reduced errors and changed user behavior, with Magnetic enabling faster, less precise selections, while both methods reframed ambiguous failures as coordination issues.

## Technical note

This paper studies error correction for gaze-and-pinch selection in VR, focusing on coordination errors from timing misalignment between fast gaze and slower pinch: late triggers (gaze leaves target before pinch) and the underexplored early triggers (pinch before gaze arrives). It introduces two heuristics: Sticky selection, a temporal buffer keeping the gaze reticle stuck to a target briefly (for late triggers), and Magnetic selection, a spatial field that snaps the reticle to a target's center once touched (for early triggers). A within-subjects study (N=9) on the Samsung Galaxy XR against a baseline found throughput, accuracy, and selection time stayed stable while both heuristics significantly reduced errors. Magnetic induced an offloading effect where users traded precision for speed, and both heuristics reclassified ambiguous failures as explainable coordination errors, yielding design recommendations for selection heuristics that preserve cognitive agency.

## Summary

This paper addresses large-scale retrieval where novel items arrive quickly. Conventional methods using small encoders struggle with complex world knowledge needed for good results, while extreme meta-classification (using separate classifiers per item) is accurate but impractical for new items due to training limitations. The authors propose EMMETT and its specific implementation IRENE, which dynamically synthesizes classifiers on-the-fly for novel items based on existing ones, improving accuracy significantly even at scale.

## Technical note

EMMETT extends extreme classification (per-item one-vs-all classifiers atop a shared Siamese encoder) to zero-shot retrieval of continuously arriving novel items, addressing the gap that classifiers cannot be trained for items lacking click data. It has two modules: a classifier selector S that shortlists informative observed-item classifiers for a novel item, and a meta-classifier generator G that combines them into a synthesized meta-classifier. IRENE is a deployable instance: S uses ANNS (DiskANN, MIPS, K~=3) over frozen encoder representations, and G is a single-layer transformer with learnable type embeddings that fuses selected classifiers with the item embedding; the encoder and observed classifiers stay frozen and G trains with a weighted one-vs-all BCE where observed items are trained via their neighbors' classifiers to simulate novelty. A theoretical framework recasts zero-shot retrieval as calibrated binary classification, showing one-vs-all loss and classifier freezing reduce variance/overfitting for generalization. IRENE adds up to +15% Recall@10 over leading encoders with millisecond overhead and +4.2% ad CTR in a search-engine A/B test.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the problem that traditional large-scale targeting/recommendation systems optimized for simple predictions often fail when goals require incremental impact (e.g., marketing), because they target users who would have acted regardless.
2.  It introduces a decision-centric framework optimizing causal effects globally, combining a causal neural network using Transformers to estimate treatment impacts, Bayesian bandit methods for uncertainty-aware exploration, and constrained allocation via large-scale linear programming under one objective function.
3.  Evaluated through offline simulations and an online A/B test on LinkedIn's feed, the system demonstrated significant improvement (+7.2% lift) in a primary long-term-value metric, proving the feasibility of production-grade causal optimization while handling business constraints like sequential context and multi-outcome effects.

## Technical note

LinkedIn reframes marketing targeting and recommendation as decision-centric causal optimization: a Transformer-augmented DragonNet estimates individual treatment effects over marketing-touchpoint sequences with multi-outcome outcome embeddings, a Bayesian neural-bandit layer (last-layer linearized Laplace Thompson sampling) explores to keep positivity, and a dual-decomposition LP allocates sends under global budget, volume, and delivery constraints with an explicit no-send action for negative or non-incremental users. Offline, on the Open Bandit Dataset remapped to four business lines plus no-recommendation, incremental scores plus constrained optimization give the highest doubly-robust net return ($0.46 vs $0.39 propensity+constraints, $0.31 propensity+ranking) and route 9% of members to no-recommendation; multi-turn simulations over 200 rounds show the bandit variant overcoming injected training-data bias after ~50 updates; ablations use outcome AUROC and CausalML AUUC. Training data are built with randomized snapshot dates and non-overlapping context (60d), treatment (7d), and outcome (>=30d) windows. An eight-week 50/50 A/B test on LinkedIn Feed marketing traffic, with delivery and cost constrained to the BAU envelope after an early un-controlled test under-delivered, gave a +7.20% lift in the primary long-term-value metric (p=0.041, CI [0.31%,14.09%]).

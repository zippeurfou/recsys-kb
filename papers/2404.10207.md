## Summary

1. The paper introduces Hellinger-UCB, a new variant of the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm for stochastic bandit problems, designed to handle the cold start problem in recommender systems.
2. It leverages the squared Hellinger distance between observed and expected reward distributions to define an upper confidence bound, proving it achieves the theoretical performance lower bound for finite time horizons.
3. Numerical experiments show Hellinger-UCB outperforms standard UCB variants (like KL-UCB and UCB1) in terms of click-through rate (CTR), and highlights its potential benefit for cold starts through a financial app example, assuming certain conditions hold.

## Technical note

The paper proposes Hellinger-UCB, a new UCB variant for the stochastic multi-armed bandit that builds the confidence bound using the squared Hellinger distance instead of KL divergence. It proves the algorithm attains the theoretical (Lai-Robbins) regret lower bound and gives it a statistical interpretation, with finite-horizon numerical experiments comparing it to UCB1 and KL-UCB. As a real-world application it solves the cold-start problem for a financial app's content recommender, exploiting a convenient low-latency computation property. An online experiment shows Hellinger-UCB outperforms both KL-UCB and UCB1 in click-through rate.

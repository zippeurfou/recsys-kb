## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of effective IT support troubleshooting, which requires iterative evidence gathering to identify root causes from ambiguous user reports. It introduces DQA, a diagnostic question-answering framework designed to maintain persistent diagnostic state and aggregate evidence effectively across conversational turns. DQA outperforms standard multi-turn RAG systems, achieving a significantly higher success rate and reducing the number of troubleshooting turns needed.

## Technical note

DQA is a diagnostic question-answering framework for enterprise IT support that treats troubleshooting as progressive uncertainty reduction over latent root causes, addressing standard multi-turn RAG's lack of explicit diagnostic state. Its RAggG (Retrieval-Aggregated Generation) subroutine retrieves a large ticket neighborhood, clusters candidates by resolution-embedding root cause, and computes per-cluster evidence counts, compressing hundreds of near-duplicate tickets into <=5 aggregate signals for scalability over 100K+ repositories. A persistent structured diagnostic state holds a hypothesis-weight vector (recomputed each turn by re-retrieval/re-aggregation rather than probability propagation), symptoms, ranked KB articles, and candidate root-cause clusters; it conditions query rewriting and a policy over three actions (clarifying questions, investigative steps, resolution proposals). On 150 anonymized scenarios with replay evaluation, DQA reaches 78.7% success vs 41.3% for a multi-turn RAG baseline while cutting average turns from 8.4 to 3.9.

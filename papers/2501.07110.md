## Summary

This paper introduces MetaMMF, a novel micro-video recommendation framework utilizing meta-learning to dynamically adjust multimodal (visual, acoustic, textual) fusion parameters for each individual video during its representation learning process.

MetaMMF treats the fusion task as unique per micro-video and uses a meta-learner to parameterize an item-specific neural network based on extracted meta-information from its multimodal features, overcoming limitations of static fusion.

Additionally, MetaMMF improves training efficiency using canonical polyadic decomposition, and extensive experiments across benchmark datasets show significant performance gains over state-of-the-art models like MMGCN, LATTICE, and InvRL.

## Technical note

Static multimodal fusion assumes every micro-video combines visual/acoustic/textual modalities the same way, which under-fits the varied cross-modal relationships across items. MetaMMF treats each item's fusion as an independent task: a meta-information extractor (MLP) abstracts the item's multimodal features, and a meta fusion learner uses a task-shared 3-D tensor to generate item-specific fusion-network parameters dynamically. Canonical Polyadic decomposition compresses the 3-D tensors to reduce parameters and speed training. On three benchmarks MetaMMF surpasses MMGCN, LATTICE, and InvRL for multimodal representation learning.

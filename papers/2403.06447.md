## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Traditional recommender systems struggle with long-tail recommendations due to data sparsity, and while Large Language Models (LLMs) show promise, they often neglect specific collaborative user-item interaction data when making recommendations.
2.  **Solution:** The paper introduces CoRAL, a system that augments LLMs by directly incorporating relevant user-item interaction evidence retrieved from the dataset into the model's prompt. This retrieval is guided by a reinforcement learning-based policy to find the most relevant information efficiently.
3.  **Result:** Experiments demonstrate that CoRAL significantly improves LLM performance on recommendation tasks by aligning their reasoning with the specific collaborative patterns of the dataset, and shows how reinforcement learning can efficiently explore relevant collaborative evidence.

## Technical note

CoRAL addresses long-tail recommendation by aligning LLM reasoning with dataset-specific collaborative signals that pure semantic/content-based prompting ignores. It retrieves actual user-item interactions and injects them into the prompt as collaborative evidence ('collaborative prompting'), letting the LLM reason about shared and distinct preferences among users for a target item. Because prompt capacity is limited, CoRAL learns a retrieval policy to select the minimal-sufficient interaction set, formulating retrieval as a sequential decision-making problem solved with reinforcement learning to balance exploration and exploitation. To ease reward sparsity, the policy network is warm-started with user/item representations learned from abundant popular-item data via collaborative filtering. Experiments show CoRAL substantially improves LLM reasoning on specific long-tail recommendation tasks and explores collaborative information more efficiently.

## Summary

1. Existing uplift modeling techniques for e-commerce fail due to complexities like seller-level incentive cannibalization (competition between shops) and incentive-level cannibalization (noise from organic conversions). These issues violate standard assumptions, making ITE estimation unreliable in multi-seller environments.
2. To address this, the authors propose CanniUplift, a novel framework incorporating three specific techniques: Platform Global Alignment to handle cross-shop substitution effects, Redemption Decomposition Denoising to reduce noise caused by alternative rewards or organic conversions, and Treat-Attention modeling for user behavior interactions with offers.
3. Experiments on both synthetic data and large industrial datasets show CanniUplift significantly outperforms existing methods in ITE estimation accuracy (measured by wAUUC/wQINI). It was successfully deployed online, resulting in a 4.08% increase in incremental GMV and improved ROI for the platform.

## Technical note

Standard uplift models (DragonNet, EUEN, CFRNet, TARNet) assume SUTVA, which breaks in multi-seller e-commerce due to seller-level cannibalization (coupons shift GMV between shops) and incentive-level cannibalization (conversions without coupon redemption). CanniUplift adds to an EUEN baseline three components sharing a Transformer sequence encoder with a Treat-Attention (candidate-as-query) mechanism: Platform-level Global Alignment (PGA) that enforces a global GMV consistency constraint to suppress spurious cross-shop uplift, and Redemption-based Decomposition Denoising (RDD) that splits treated outcomes into redemption / non-redemption paths in an entire-space, Tweedie-loss framework. It beats all baselines on wAUUC/wQINI (industrial User AUUC 0.849 vs 0.794 EUEN) and RDD cuts over-attribution on non-redeemers by ~7%. Online A/B testing gave +4.08% platform incremental GMV, -2.45% marketing cost, and +6.69% ROI.

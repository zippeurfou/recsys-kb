## Summary

1.  Existing document expansion techniques like Doc2Query, while effective, often produce irrelevant queries or repetitive content using sequence-to-sequence models.
2.  The paper introduces Query Generation with Direct Preference Optimization (QGDPO), which refines these models by using a separate relevance model to score generated tokens and guiding DPO training with these relevance preferences.
3.  This approach significantly improves performance, eliminating 50% of irrelevant predictions compared to baselines and a filtering step removing an additional 14.61%, resulting in a deployed system on Walmart.com that boosts search relevance and user engagement.

## Technical note

Walmart's QGDPO improves Doc2Query document expansion for e-commerce lexical retrieval, where a seq2seq model generates queries to append to a product before indexing but often hallucinates unrelated tokens or repeats words already in the title. Walmart first fine-tunes T5-base on 64M exact-match product-query pairs from 16M products (lr 1e-4, 5 epochs), scores its generations with a three-class relevance model (exact, substitute, irrelevant, ESCI-style, trained on human judgments), and runs DPO on triplets of a product, a winning exact query and a losing irrelevant query (10M triplets, lr 1e-6, beta 0.1, since larger beta increased hallucination). At inference, top-5 beam search (beam 10, which beat top-k and top-k/top-p sampling) feeds a relevance filter set at 95% recall for the exact class, and a daily offline job indexes only the novel tokens into an extra Solr text field. On 1,000 human-labeled products, QGDPO raises exact predictions by 8.07% and cuts irrelevant ones by 49.87% vs Doc2Query, the filter takes that to +9.63% and -64.48% (another 14.61 points of irrelevant predictions removed), novel tokens per product dip only from 6.0 to 4.9, and a second DPO round overfits. Online, human relevance grading of top results showed NDCG@5 +0.93% and NDCG@10 +0.86%, a two-week A/B test showed search-session add-to-cart +0.36% (p=0.01) and GMV +0.17% (not significant), and a LoRA-tuned Mistral-7B Doc2Query beat T5-base by +2.00% exact and -22.84% irrelevant, helped by world knowledge on media and toy products.

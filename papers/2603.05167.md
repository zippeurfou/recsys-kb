## Summary

Benchmarking LLM Judges for Causal and Coverage Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning":

Researchers introduced C2-Faith, a benchmark designed to evaluate how well large language model (LLM) judges can assess two key aspects of chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning: causal faithfulness (logical step-by-step flow) and coverage faithfulness (inclusion of essential intermediate steps). To create this benchmark, they used controlled perturbations on a large dataset to generate examples with specific causal errors or missing coverage elements. Evaluating three frontier LLM judges across various tasks revealed that performance varies significantly by task, no single judge dominates all settings, error localization is particularly difficult, and coverage judgments tend to be overly optimistic, providing practical guidance for selecting appropriate judges.

## Technical note

C2-Faith is a diagnostic benchmark built from PRM800K that decomposes chain-of-thought faithfulness into causality (each step logically follows prior context) and coverage (essential intermediate inferences are present). It constructs examples via controlled perturbations: acausal single-step replacements at known positions and step deletions at controlled coverage fractions, enabling reference-based measurement. Three frontier LLM judges are evaluated on binary causal detection, causal step localization, and coverage scoring. Results show judge reliability is highly task-dependent with no dominant model, a large gap between detecting an error and localizing it, and systematic overestimation of reasoning completeness, arguing for more calibrated process-level evaluation before using LLM judges in RLHF or process-reward pipelines.

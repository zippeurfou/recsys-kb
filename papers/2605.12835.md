## Summary

PROMETHEUS is a framework designed to automate deep causal research by integrating diverse sources like text, data, and scientific models into structured "causal atlases," which are composed of interconnected local predictive-state models. These atlases, built as "sheaf-like" structures over a research substrate, allow users to navigate evidence, understand local causal claims, and identify tensions where overlapping regions fail to form a coherent global view. PROMETHEUS aims to move beyond simple text extraction, enabling rigorous causal exploration and counterfactual evaluation directly from the scientific literature and its underlying data/code.

## Technical note

Prometheus turns retrieved literature, filings, reviews, code, simulations, and data into 'causal atlases': sheaf-like families of local causal predictive-state models over an explicit cover of a research substrate. Each local region holds causal episodes, structured claim tables, predictive tests, support statistics, and provenance; restriction maps compare overlapping regions and gluing diagnostics expose agreement, drift, contradiction, and underdetermination rather than forcing a single global graph. A Claims Atlas interface supports navigation, and an evaluation program targets coverage, drift visibility, provenance, support aggregation, and rerun consistency. When a paper ships source data or code, Prometheus can evaluate counterfactuals against the scientific substrate and rebuild the sheaf world model around measured interventions.

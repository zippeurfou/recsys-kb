## Summary

This paper introduces a unified, model-agnostic downstream reward learning framework to optimize long-term user engagement in recommendation systems, tackling difficulties with sparse and delayed retention signals seen in traditional approaches.

The core contribution involves identifying early observable session-level behaviors that predict future retention by developing an offline screening process across diverse data sources.

Several generalizable downstream reward signals were created based on these identified patterns, successfully integrated into production ranking models despite engineering challenges, leading to significant improvements in engagement metrics confirmed through online A/B testing and deployment.

## Technical note

This Pinterest paper presents a unified, model-agnostic downstream-reward framework that optimizes long-term engagement and retention through early-observable proxy signals instead of sparse, delayed retention labels or full reinforcement learning. An offline screening pipeline (Random Forest with SHAP and user-prevalence normalization) selects session-level behaviors predictive of revisitation, yielding three reward families: deeper session engagement over Pin-to-Pin rabbit-hole trajectories, negative rewards for shallow closeups, and use-case adoption for novel interest clusters; rewards are added as heads to a multi-head ranker with serving weights tuned by HyperOPT. A DRv2 Ray-based dataloader computes reward labels on the fly (~10x faster iteration, 3 weeks to 2 days). Online A/B tests across Homefeed, Related Pins, Search, and Notifications show consistent lifts (e.g., +0.36% successful sessions, -0.40% unsuccessful sessions, +0.25% search fulfillment, plus WAU gains). Not a unified retrieval-plus-ranking pipeline.

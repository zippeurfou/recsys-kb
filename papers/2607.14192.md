## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing long-term user engagement in mature recommender systems. It proposes a model-agnostic framework for identifying and utilizing downstream reward signals derived from early observable session behaviors that predict future retention. The authors developed this system, deployed it across multiple Pinterest platforms, and demonstrated significant improvements through online A/B tests.

## Technical note

Pinterest framework for optimizing long-term engagement and retention via model-agnostic 'downstream rewards' (DR): dense, early-observable proxy signals that substitute for sparse, delayed retention labels, avoiding RL/REINFORCE-style policy gradients. An offline screening pipeline (Random Forest with SHAP and correlation analysis, plus a session-level Markov chain) identifies behaviors predictive of revisitation, motivating three reward families - deeper-session engagement (discounted cumulative downstream actions over the Pin-to-Pin rabbit-hole trajectory), negative rewards (a shallow-closeup penalty with user-state-specific dwell thresholds), and use-case adoption (engaging novel content whose embedding is far from a user's interest clusters). Rewards are added as auxiliary heads to existing multi-task rankers (e.g., Pinnability) with serving weights tuned by HyperOPT, and a Ray-based DRv2 dataloader cuts label iteration from ~3 weeks to ~2 days. Online A/B tests show consistent gains - e.g., +0.36% site-wide Successful Sessions and +0.7% downloads/screenshots for deeper-session rewards, unsuccessful sessions -0.40% for negative rewards, saves +0.42% for use-case adoption - deployed across Homefeed, Related Pins, Search, and Notifications.

## Summary

Diffusion models show promise for generative recommenders but standard conditional methods struggle to fully utilize user history effectively, often getting distracted during the denoising process. To address this, the authors propose an unconditional Diffusion approach called BBDRec (Brownian Bridge Diffusion Recommendation), which treats the user's entire historical sequence as a fixed starting point rather than conditioning on it throughout generation. By using a Brownian bridge mechanism for structured noise manipulation, BBDRec focuses solely on translating the item-historical trajectory pattern, leading to enhanced sequential recommendation performance according to extensive experiments.

## Technical note

BBDRec rethinks diffusion-based sequential recommendation, arguing that the standard history-guided 'item to noise' denoising paradigm wastes capacity on noise reconstruction and distracts from user preference modeling. Instead it uses a preference-bridging design that models a direct 'item to history' transition, treating the user-history representation as the terminal/initial state of the forward/reverse process. It leverages a Brownian bridge process to enforce an endpoint-constrained stochastic trajectory between target item and history embeddings, plus a progressive learning strategy that first builds a preference-aware space then fine-tunes the diffusion model. On multiple public datasets BBDRec consistently outperforms sequential and diffusion-based baselines.

## Summary

This paper addresses limitations of traditional collaborative filtering playlist generation by proposing a simulation-based reinforcement learning approach. It introduces AH-DQN, a modified Deep Q-Network designed for large action spaces, aiming directly to maximize user satisfaction metrics through offline simulations in a custom environment. The authors demonstrate that their RL agents perform better online than baseline methods and that simulator results strongly correlate with actual A/B test outcomes, validating the simulation's predictive power.

## Technical note

The paper presents a simulation-based reinforcement learning approach for personalized music playlist generation that directly optimizes user-satisfaction (consumption) metrics, avoiding the offline-objective/online-metric mismatch of collaborative filtering. It formulates playlist generation as an MDP whose combinatorial action space (~10^89 for 30 tracks from 1000) is made tractable by having the agent select single tracks sequentially, with a recurrent-neural-network user behavior model predicting responses in a simulated environment (unlike slate-MDP/SlateQ, it does not require the one-item-selection assumption). They introduce the action-head DQN (AH-DQN), which ingests track features and outputs Q-values, enabling recommendation from dynamic pools of hundreds of millions of unseen tracks. Offline simulations on public and proprietary Spotify data and online A/B tests show improved user-satisfaction metrics, and simulated performance correlates strongly with observed online results.

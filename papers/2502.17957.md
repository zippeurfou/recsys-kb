## Technical note

This paper studies synthetic data strategies for domain-specific generative retrieval in a two-stage framework. Stage one (learning to decode document identifiers from queries) uses LLM-generated queries at multiple granularities (sentence/chunk) with domain-relevant constraints, and adopts keyword-list semantic identifiers; stage two refines ranking via preference learning (Regularized Preference Optimization) using hard negatives mined online from the model's own top-ranked candidates rather than static random negatives. Experiments across diverse public domains show the synthetic-data plus hard-negative approach improves generative retrieval memorization, generalization, and ranking.

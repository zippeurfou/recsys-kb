## Summary

This paper introduces an RLAIF framework designed to generate "portable" job search queries using large language models (LLMs). The core challenge is optimizing these LLMs effectively on a complex and highly adversarial task where the standard AI judge rewards could lead the model to simply copy existing text verbatim. The authors found that even advanced optimization techniques like GRPO are heavily dependent on carefully shaped reward signals; specifically, adding rule-based "reward floor" constraints to prevent copying significantly boosted performance (+0.147) and confirmed that well-designed training-time rewards were responsible for most gains (2.4x inflation).

## Technical note

This LinkedIn work formalizes profile-to-portable-query generation and trains a Qwen3-1.7B policy via RLAIF with a prompted Qwen3-8B rubric grader as the reward. The central finding is that reward-signal engineering dominates optimizer choice: with a fixed shaped reward, PPO, GRPO, RLOO, and REINFORCE++ cluster within 0.018 on an independent cross-family (Llama-3.3-70B) judge. A deterministic rule-based reward floor S_r that clamps reward to -1 on 6-gram verbatim profile copies yields the single largest gain (+0.147), and GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization is uniquely susceptible to reward-hacking without it, while per-rollout-baseline methods resist it natively. The training-time judge inflates measured improvement by 2.4x versus the independent evaluator.

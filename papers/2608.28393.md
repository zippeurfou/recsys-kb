## Summary

This paper introduces timing-aware repurchase prediction using survival models (specifically AFT) for grocery e-commerce, replacing separate models for different time horizons. Their empirical analysis revealed a counter-intuitive slightly decreasing marginal hazard for repurchase likelihood over time. While Log-Normal AFT excelled in ranking and had the best marginal fit, Weibull AFT provided the best conditional residual fit, and overall, a single AFT model achieved comparable or superior performance to separate binary classifiers for each horizon using significantly fewer trees. Finally, they found a principled trade-off within the AFT family, where Exponential AFT offered highly calibrated probabilities but lower ranking, and Log-Normal offered better ranking but poorer calibration, recommending specific models based on the application's needs.

## Technical note

Walmart reframes 'buy again' repurchase ranking on a web-scale grocery e-commerce platform from per-horizon binary classification to time-to-event prediction, serving a 7-day homepage shopping list, a 14-day app restocking carousel and a 30-day email pantry planner from a single model. The production baseline is three independent XGBoost binary:logistic models (800 trees, depth 6, LR 0.3, 84 tabular features) trained on 7/14/30-day labels; the replacement is one XGBoost survival:aft model that ranks items by predicted time-to-repurchase, plus a discrete-time hazard variant using person-period expansion into [0,7), [7,14), [14,30) intervals (2.9x row expansion, 30% censored-negative sampling, six interval-relative features). Empirical hazard analysis over tens of millions of (customer, item) pairs gives a slightly decreasing Weibull shape (k_MLE = 0.911, k_QQ = 0.919), contradicting the intuition that grocery repurchase hazard rises with overdue-ness; Log-Normal best fits the Kaplan-Meier marginal (R2 = 0.998) while Weibull best fits conditional Gumbel residuals, a paradox they attribute to the loss acting as inductive bias rather than distributional fit. On a held-out customer partition the best AFT (Log-Normal, sigma=2) reaches precision-at-variable-k of .3461/.3788/.4069 at 7/14/28 days versus .3438/.3757/.4022 for the strongest per-horizon baseline F_14d, a +0.82% relative gain at 14 days, while collapsing roughly 2,400 trees into about 700 (3x training and serving compute reduction). A 4-parameter distribution-matched calibration (shared slope a plus per-horizon intercepts b_7, b_14, b_28, fit by Nelder-Mead) gives zero cross-horizon monotonicity violations over hundreds of millions of holdout pairs; Exponential AFT (Weibull k=1) with a cloglog link hits ECE 1.3e-4 versus 3.6e-4 for Weibull k=0.5 and 7.5e-4 for Log-Normal probit, so they adopt Exponential for probability-consuming surfaces and Log-Normal for pure ranking. Feature importance reshuffles under the survival objective: channel-cadence and recent-recency features rise (a product-type online-channel order count jumps 40-50 ranks) while aggregate rolling frequency counts fall (3-month order total drops from rank 12 to 54). Caveats: all results are offline on a single proprietary retailer with no online A/B and no public repurchase benchmarks (Dunnhumby, Instacart, Ta-Feng), AFT ranking is horizon-invariant by construction, and three experiments that dropped right-censored items with >180 lapsed days collapsed concordance from 0.91+ to 0.55-0.65 despite heavy regularization.

## Summary

This paper introduces survival models for timing-aware repurchase prediction in web-scale grocery e-commerce, replacing traditional binary classifiers for different time horizons. An empirical analysis revealed a slightly decreasing hazard rate (k ~ 0.9), contrary to the common increasing hazard intuition, and found Log-Normal models provided the best marginal fit and ranking performance despite Weibull being slightly better for conditional residuals. The researchers demonstrated that a single parametric AFT model (like Log-Normal) can effectively replace multiple binary classifiers for different horizons, showing improved efficiency and different feature importance patterns, though careful calibration is needed to map these survival predictions accurately to probabilities for specific applications.

## Technical note

Replaces Walmart grocery's stack of three per-horizon XGBoost binary repurchase classifiers (7/14/30-day labels, 800 trees each, 84 features) for buy-again surfaces with a single survival model that predicts time-to-repurchase, evaluated offline on tens of millions of (customer,item) pairs with 30-day right-censoring and a precision-at-variable-k metric at 7/14/28-day horizons. Empirical hazard analysis finds a slightly decreasing marginal hazard (Weibull k~0.9), contradicting the intuition that grocery items become more due over time; Log-Normal AFT gives the best marginal fit (R^2=0.998) and best ranking (P@14d 0.3788, +0.82% over the strongest per-horizon baseline), with a single 330-765 tree AFT model replacing ~2,400 trees. Under the survival objective channel-cadence and recency features rise in importance while aggregate frequency counts fall. A 4-parameter parametric calibration maps survival CDFs to per-horizon probabilities with zero cross-horizon monotonicity violations; Exponential AFT calibrates ~10x better (ECE ~1e-4) than Log-Normal although ranking agrees within 0.3%, so Exponential is used for probability-consuming surfaces and Log-Normal for pure ranking. All results are offline; no online A/B test is reported.

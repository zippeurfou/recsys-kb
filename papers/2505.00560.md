## Summary

This paper presents an algorithm called RecJPQPrune that significantly speeds up sequential recommendation inference when dealing with extremely large item catalogues containing millions of items. The core idea is to dynamically prune irrelevant sub-item embeddings during scoring, thereby avoiding the expensive process of evaluating every single item without compromising result quality.

The key innovation adapts concepts from document retrieval to ensure the algorithm guarantees finding all truly top-ranked recommendations (up-to-rank K) by safely excluding low-scoring candidates early. This eliminates the need for exhaustive scoring over large catalogues and avoids dependence on Approximate Nearest Neighbour techniques or GPUs, which are often used but present limitations.

Experiments demonstrate substantial efficiency gains: RecJPQPrune reduces inference time dramatically compared to standard baselines (up to 64x faster) and a recent competitor approach. It achieves extremely fast response times, such as scoring two million items in under ten milliseconds on the Tmall dataset, proving its effectiveness for industrial-scale recommendation systems previously considered computationally prohibitive.

## Technical note

Transformer sequential recommenders on huge catalogues have slow inference because score-and-rank must score every item. Building on RecJPQ's sub-item (product-quantisation) embeddings, the authors adapt IR dynamic-pruning (TAAT/DAAT, MaxScore/WAND-style upper bounds) to propose RecJPQPrune, which scores only items tied to high-scoring sub-item ids and terminates early via a safe upper-bound threshold, guaranteeing exact top-K (safe-up-to-rank-K). On Tmall (2.2M items) it cuts median CPU scoring time 64x vs Transformer Default and 5.3x vs PQTopK, scoring 2M items in under 10ms without GPUs or ANN. A batch-size sweet spot (~8) confirms the benefit of vectorised batched sub-item processing.

## Summary

This paper addresses problems with using Large Language Models (LLMs) for recommendation: existing reasoning methods either require explicit natural language processing (causing slow inference) or use latent representations without clearly defining each layer's specific role and contribution.

To solve this, the authors propose HiLaR (Hierarchical Latent Reasoning), a framework that creates layered user preference representations aligned with different LLM layers and guides the reasoning process hierarchically from broad preferences to fine-grained needs.

Experiments show that HiLaR significantly outperforms various baseline recommendation methods by combining hierarchical representations, layer-aware alignment, and reinforcement optimization based on both final outcomes and intermediate reasoning gains.

## Technical note

Explicit chain-of-thought reasoning in LLM recommenders is token-expensive, and existing latent-reasoning recommenders (LatentR3, VRec) treat all intermediate states as homogeneous refinement steps, so no state receives layer-specific credit. HiLaR gives the K latent states an explicit temporal semantics: a frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B encodes a time-aware history prompt, a K=4-level residual quantizer with 256-entry shared codebooks produces cumulative embeddings e(u,k), and each level is supervised by a progressively narrower temporal window (level 1 reconstructs the whole history, level K reconstructs the target item) via multi-label BCE with negative sampling; SFT then aligns latent state tau_k to e(u,k) with an MSE alignment loss weighted 0.10. Hierarchical Reward-Guided GRPO treats each latent state as a Gaussian action (layer-wise exploration noise 0.12/0.08/0.05/0.025), samples G=6 rollouts per user, and forms group-relative advantages from a reward mixing exact-hit plus common-prefix plus token-F1 title match, a frozen-SASRec collaborative preference z-score, trajectory target log-likelihood, cosine alignment to e(u,k), and a clipped per-layer marginal-gain process reward Delta(g,k) = l(g,k) - l(g,k-1); the KL penalty covers both the Gaussian latent policy and the autoregressive text policy. On Amazon Toys/CDs/Games/Instruments with catalog-constrained beam search (beam 10), HiLaR is best on Toys, Games, and Instruments (Toys H@5 0.0948 and N@10 0.0801 vs 0.0903 / 0.0771 for the strongest baseline FLR) and beats LatentR3 and VRec on CDs on all metrics except N@5. Ablations show removing temporal quantization or GRPO causes the largest drops, HiLaR produces more differentiated layer-wise log-probability gains than LatentR3 and VRec, the advantage grows with user history length, and inference overhead stays moderate relative to CoT since the extra cost is concentrated in GRPO training.

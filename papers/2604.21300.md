## Summary

1.  Existing authorship attribution methods often fail due to mixing writing style with content, hindering generalization across different texts and domains.
2.  The paper introduces EAVAE, a novel framework using a Variational Autoencoder with separate style and content encoders, and a discriminator that explains its decisions, effectively disentangling style from content.
3.  EAVAE achieves state-of-the-art results in authorship attribution and excels at detecting AI-generated text, even in few-shot scenarios, demonstrating its effectiveness and generalizability.

## Technical note

EAVAE tackles content-style entanglement (topic confusion) in authorship attribution by disentangling authorial style from content via architectural separation-by-design. Stage one pretrains an LLM-based style encoder with supervised contrastive learning and BM25 hard-negative mining over 27.4M documents; stage two fine-tunes a VAE with separate style and content encoders under an independence assumption, using a shared reconstructor and KL regularization. An explainable adversarial discriminator classifies whether style/content representation pairs share authorship or content while generating natural-language explanations, enforcing disentanglement and interpretability. A unified generator with hybrid prompting (fixed templates plus learnable soft prompts, placeholders replaced by latent representations) serves both reconstruction and discrimination. It reaches SOTA on Amazon Reviews, PAN21, and HRS (MRR, R@8) and strong few-shot AI-text detection on M4 (pAUC@k).

## Summary

Current CTR prediction models often unify different signals (like user history and the current ad request) at the token level, treating them as separate types. However, this approach obscures underlying structural patterns because both are sequences of user context units, just with different outcome visibility. UniCon proposes a new unified architecture based on these context units, modeling their internal interactions (intra-context) and the dynamic state changes across units (inter-context) to overcome these limitations and achieve better performance and scalability.

## Technical note

Meituan search advertising. Argues that the conventional split between sequential behavior features and non-sequential ranking features is a legacy of feature engineering rather than a property of the decision process, so UniCon makes the request context unit (the set of co-displayed items plus intent, query, category preference, time, location, device signals, and one aggregated user token) the basic modeling unit, with observed history and the current candidate set sharing one schema and differing only in whether feedback is observed. Historical item tokens carry an embedding of the logged click label; candidate tokens fill the same slot with a learnable placeholder, and the candidate-initialized target latent context unit is refined during training by auxiliary exposure and absolute-position heads in an MMoE multi-task setup, L = L_clk + lambda_exp L_exp + lambda_pos L_pos, with the click head conditioned on logged absolute position and gradients blocked from the position head. Each UniConBlock runs intra-context attention inside each unit to capture Locality (local competition and complementarity among co-displayed items) then inter-context attention across the chronologically ordered sequence to capture Dynamics, both pre-norm RMSNorm blocks with a SwiGLU dense-MoE feed-forward sublayer. Efficiency comes from padding-free variable-length FlashAttention where segment offsets encode context boundaries, block-by-block target-aware compression that scores each historical context by mean-pooled query-key similarity and keeps a fixed ratio r=0.5 with Gumbel-TopK straight-through in training and hard TopK at serving (cutting global attention cost from O(L N^2) to a bound of O(N^2 / (1 - r^2))), candidate sharding at 300 per shard, fused parallel dense-MMoE experts, and AOTInductor-compiled C++ serving. Trained on 16 A100s over a one-year Meituan log with hundreds of millions of users and items and hundreds of context units per instance. UniCon-Large reaches AUC 0.8697, GAUC 0.8194, LogLoss 0.1991 at 0.33B params and 197 GFLOPs, versus 0.8558 for the DIN plus SENet plus DCN-V2 production base (a +0.0139 gain) and 0.8661 for the best baseline RankMixer+DSIN+CIM; UniCon-Small at 0.09B already beats every research baseline at 0.8683. Ablations: flattening context into a parameter-matched global token backbone costs the most (0.8637), removing both auxiliary losses gives 0.8663, removing only intra-context attention at matched depth gives 0.8673, and compression is essentially free (0.8697 vs 0.8698) while cutting profiled compute 75.4 percent; variable-length attention alone raises throughput 64.5 percent and with compression 258.1 percent. A seven-day A/B on 20 percent of traffic gives +3.09 percent RPM, +2.07 percent CTR and +2.95 percent revenue, all significant at p<=0.01, with latency up but inside SLA. Caveat acknowledged by the authors: exposure and position labels are generated by the incumbent production policy, so those objectives regularize the target unit toward the logged display distribution rather than a policy-independent optimal context.

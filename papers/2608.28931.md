## Summary

1.  Matching users to vast, evolving interest categories in e-commerce is difficult due to sparse and long-tailed signals.
2.  Autoregressive language models offer a promising solution but suffer from exposure bias during training, where early mistakes lead to incorrect later associations.
3.  The MERIT framework mitigates this bias by using a self-correction training objective that exposes the model to hard negatives while preserving teacher forcing, improving recall and user conversion metrics.

## Technical note

Amazon tackles candidate-free user-interest propensity modeling over a 291k-entry interest-category vocabulary (250k+ in the abstract) as generative extreme multi-label classification, using 500k training users and 100k disjoint test users; input is a year of clicks and purchases with the final 7 days excluded, target is the tag set of the next purchased product. The core claim is that exposure bias in teacher-forced generation becomes a recall failure: once decoding commits to a semantic region the model emits near-correlates in a self-reinforcing co-occurrence cascade and drops unrelated true interests. MERIT fixes this with a single-pass self-correction objective: an initial generator is temperature-sampled offline to mine plausible-but-wrong labels (dropped if Cohere similarity to a gold label exceeds 0.6, below the tau=0.7 soft-match eval threshold), gold and mined labels are shuffled and joined with [SEP], and one teacher-forced forward pass uses a per-position valid-set pooled log-softmax that is permutation-invariant at classification positions, never rewards a mined negative as a label choice, but forces coherent completion of a negative once its prefix is committed. Long histories are handled by selective recency-aware adaptive patching (PatchRec-style, window/stride 10, 10x compression of older windows, four-phase annealing curriculum). A 230M-parameter transformer encoder over the first-token hidden states of the first k_g=5 generated categories, concatenated with the category Cohere embedding through a shared MLP trained with BCE on generator-mined plus popular plus random negatives, supplies cross-user-comparable scores for interest-to-user retrieval. The generator is phi-3.5-mini (3.8B) with hierarchical generation over 30k semantic clusters before leaves. Reported relatively per internal policy: Global recall +14.1 over a trivial most-frequent-category baseline and at least +11.9 over the strongest baseline (XLGen, GROOV, PatchRec, DEXML-DS/STk), +13.9 recall over standard teacher forcing while permutation-invariance and cross-entropy-invariance ablations add only about +3; average Hit@k +6.1 over DEXML-DS despite 16x fewer parameters. Production A/B across 3 one-week merchandising campaigns (0.6M-3.5M customers per arm) under a Bayesian covariate-adjusted framework gave Personal Care +0.23 percent, Toys +0.19 percent, Sports +0.37 percent, avg +0.26 percent conversion lift, all clearing P(lift>0) >= 0.90. Caveats: precision is deliberately traded for recall (GROOV wins F1/precision on Apparel), a single proprietary dataset with relative-only numbers, and the deployment is offline two-stage batch scoring, not real-time.

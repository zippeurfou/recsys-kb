## Summary

Modern deep learning recommendation models require storing extremely long user interaction histories (UIH), but the standard "Fat Row" method creates massive storage and I/O issues due to redundant data copies, especially in shared environments.

The paper introduces "versioned late materialization" as a solution, storing UIH once in an immutable store and reconstructing sequences just-in-time during training from lightweight pointers.

This system significantly reduces data infrastructure costs, ensures consistency between online and offline training, and masks reconstruction latency, enabling faster GPU compute and significant quality gains by allowing aggressive scaling of sequence lengths.

## Technical note

The paper attacks the storage and I/O wall created by the industry-standard 'Fat Row' paradigm, which pre-materializes full ultra-long user interaction history (UIH) into every training example and causes K-fold redundancy that pushes data-infra usage above GPU training cost, especially in multi-tenant union datasets. It proposes versioned late materialization: UIH is stored once in a normalized immutable tier plus a small mutable tier, and sequences are reconstructed just-in-time at training via lightweight versioned pointers (temporal boundaries, length, checksum), exploiting the append-only immutable nature of history so a timestamp<=t predicate guarantees Online-to-Offline consistency and prevents future leakage across both streaming and batch training. A read-optimized immutable store uses offloaded daily compaction into single-level pre-sorted layouts, multi-dimensional projection pushdown (sequence-length and feature-group), and trait-aware columnar encoding to serve high-concurrency range scans. Disaggregated data preprocessing with pipelined I/O prefetching, trainer-side rebatching, and data-affinity sharding masks reconstruction latency to keep training GPU-bound; it serves as foundational infra for HSTU and ULTRA-HSTU.

## Summary

This paper presents a framework for personalizing free-value thresholds on a two-sided job marketplace, where conflicting interests between employers and job seekers require balancing multiple objectives. Standard uplift methods proved insufficient due to cross-side externalities and the constraints of cluster-level experiments limiting treatment variation; thus, they developed an integrated approach using ensemble-based hybrid ranking models that separately target desired outcomes while guarding against unintended negative effects on user engagement. Their system successfully deployed this personalized policy, confirming statistically significant gains for employers while safely minimizing risks to job seeker experience across different segments.

## Technical note

Personalizing free-value thresholds on a two-sided job marketplace is hard because cross-side externalities require multi-objective optimization and cluster-level randomization (to control interference) yields only a few discrete treatment levels, violating positivity for continuous-treatment methods. The authors build an integrated framework: an ensemble X-learner CATE estimator (combining job-level and employer-level effects to handle spillovers), a hybrid ranking algorithm that separately models target and guardrail (engagement) metrics and assigns tiered policies under a guardrail constraint, and linear treatment-effect extrapolation from two observed levels justified as the maximal identifiable dose-response. Offline, hybrid ranking nearly doubles guardrail Qini (0.21 vs 0.11) while keeping target Qini (0.83 vs 0.80) and cuts guardrail risk >10% for equal target gains. Deployed to millions of postings, it delivered statistically significant target lift within 10% of prediction while respecting all engagement guardrails, with ramp data confirming extrapolation accuracy (r=0.85).

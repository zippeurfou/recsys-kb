## Summary

Current LLM agentic systems face new security risks from prompt injections introduced through untrusted external sources, and traditional inference-time defenses struggle to detect these hidden threats. To address this lack of visibility during development, researchers introduce PI-Hunter, an automated framework designed specifically for proactive auditing rather than direct attacking. Their extensive experiments demonstrate that PI-Hunter significantly outperforms existing red-teaming methods in exposing vulnerabilities related to latent prompt injections across various agent setups and attack surfaces, even while being somewhat effective against current defenses as well.

## Technical note

PI-Hunter is an automated agentic auditing framework that shifts red-teaming from attack-success optimization to proactive exposure of latent indirect prompt injection paths in LLM agents. It first statically maps the agent's interaction surface (tools, retrieval interfaces, external sources), then runs an evolutionary exploitation loop that generates source-aware test cases and refines them via feedback-guided mutation to induce the agent to retrieve and reveal hidden malicious instructions. A patch-and-reexplore stage applies lightweight transient mitigations to verified ingestion paths, forcing exploration toward undiscovered attack surfaces. Across AgentDojo and AgentDyn with diverse architectures, attacks, and defenses, it achieves stronger source localization and injection exposure than baselines and remains effective under Spotlighting, MELON, and PIGuard.

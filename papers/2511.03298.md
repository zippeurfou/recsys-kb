## Summary

1.  The paper introduces **KScaNN**, a new Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search (ANNS) algorithm specifically designed for ARM-based servers, addressing the poor performance typically seen when porting x86 algorithms to this architecture.
2.  KScaNN uses a **co-design approach**, combining novel algorithmic techniques (like hybrid search and improved PQ) with hardware-specific optimizations (ML-driven tuning and highly-optimized ARM SIMD kernels) to overcome the limitations of standard methods.
3.  The experimental results show that KScaNN significantly closes the performance gap with x86 solutions, achieving up to a 1.63x speedup, making it a top-performing vector search solution for modern ARM architectures.

## Technical note

KScaNN adapts the ScaNN partition-based ANNS algorithm (IVF + product quantization with asymmetric distance computation) to the Kunpeng 920 ARM server CPU, since naive x86-to-ARM ports lose substantial throughput. Its contributions are hand-tuned NEON/SVE SIMD kernels that emulate wider registers to process multiple PQ lookup tables per instruction, ML-based per-query prediction of probing depth (nprob) and re-ranking pool size, learned cluster pruning, statistical feature-based dimensionality reduction, and a hybrid graph-in-leaf search. At iso-accuracy Recall@10=0.99 it reaches up to 1.63x QPS speedup over the best x86 baseline (Google ScaNN, Faiss IVFPQ/FastScan) on GIST, with the ARM SIMD kernels driving the largest ablation gains. The algorithmic improvements also transfer to x86.

## Summary

This paper analyzes graph-based Recommender Systems from SIGIR 2022 that use message-passing techniques and their influence on similar systems published in SIGIR 2023.

The study found several critical flaws, including erroneous data splits or information leakage during testing, which cast doubt on the validity of many results presented.

Furthermore, frequent inconsistencies were observed between the provided code/data artifacts and what was described in the papers' methods sections. The paper concludes that these issues prevent confirmation of most authors' claims regarding model performance and state-of-the-art advancements.

## Technical note

This reproducibility study analyzes ten graph-based (message-passing) recommender papers, most from SIGIR 2022, and their influence on SIGIR 2023 work. Integrating original artifacts into a common evaluation framework with Cython sampling and consistent early stopping, the authors find that fewer than half of reported results reproduce (0-66% per paper), with frequent bad practices such as anomalous data splits, information leakage, and early stopping on test data. When compared against simple fine-tuned baselines like ItemKNN, most methods fail to outperform them, especially on Amazon-Book where the apparent state-of-the-art has actually worsened. They conclude that inconsistent artifacts and weak baselines make cross-paper comparison nearly impossible, undermining claimed progress.

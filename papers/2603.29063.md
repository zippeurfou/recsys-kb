## Summary

Based on a survey of 246 Android developers presented with a hypothetical privacy change requiring effort, the study found overwhelming (89%) developer support for reducing fingerprinting, even with significant effort, though most preferred it optional. Developers who already used fingerprinting were surprisingly six times more likely to support the change than those who didn't. The research highlights developers' primary concern for compliance and enforcement, and suggests platform awareness of fingerprinting significantly alters perceptions of privacy protections.

## Technical note

The paper surveys 246 Android developers on a hypothetical 'API Usage Purposes' platform change that would require declaring reasons for fingerprinting-capable APIs in AndroidManifest.xml, mirroring Apple's Required Reason API. Using a mixed-methods analysis, it quantifies an effort-privacy trade-off: perceived higher effort reduces support while perceived privacy benefit increases it. It finds 89% support overall, with developers who themselves use fingerprinting six times more likely to support the change despite being most impacted. Open-ended responses surface compliance and enforcement as top concerns, and most developers rank iOS above Android for privacy, though this gap shrinks among those familiar with fingerprinting.

## Summary

This paper introduces **GlobalMood**, a new cross-cultural benchmark dataset addressing limitations in existing Western-centric music emotion datasets. It was created by allowing participants across five different cultures (US, France, Mexico, South Korea, Egypt) to define their own culturally specific mood terms related to songs, followed by large-scale human ratings of these terms. The study demonstrates that while basic valence-arousal dimensions might be universal, the interpretation of emotional descriptors varies significantly between cultures; however, **GlobalMood** improves state-of-the-art emotion recognition models particularly for non-English musical contexts.

## Technical note

Existing music emotion recognition (MER) datasets are Western-centric and rely on predefined English mood taxonomies, limiting cross-cultural and multilingual generalizability. The authors build GlobalMood, a cross-cultural benchmark of 1,180 songs from 59 countries with 988,925 ratings from 2,519 participants across the US, France, Mexico, S. Korea, and Egypt, using a bottom-up participant-driven chain process to organically elicit culturally-specific emotion terms rather than imposing fixed categories. Analysis confirms a shared valence-arousal structure but significant cross-cultural divergence for dictionary-equivalent terms (notably 'happy'). Fine-tuning CLAP on GlobalMood raised mean correlation with human ratings from 0.08 (zero-shot) to 0.31, with the largest gains for non-English languages (Arabic 0.11->0.46), while an English-translation control failed to improve, and Gemini 2.5 Pro reached near human-level correlation (r=0.50).

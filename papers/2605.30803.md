## Summary

1.  The paper introduces PReMISE, a framework addressing the critical issue that LLM judges' evaluations heavily depend on specific rubrics, with vague ones potentially rewarding flawed responses.
2.  PReMISE discovers policy-level rubric sets from human preference data and audits them against structural adequacy, reliability, preference fit, and adversarial robustness.
3.  Evaluating various rubric sources shows none are ideal, but PReMISE performs well across multiple criteria; its repair operations improve judge accuracy and significantly reduce the scoring of easily manipulable (exploit) responses.

## Technical note

PReMISE treats reusable policy-level rubrics as measurement specifications, since a fixed LLM judge conditioned on different rubrics induces different evaluators. Given pairwise human-preference data, it discovers a policy-level rubric set and audits any rubric set along four axes: structural adequacy, reliability, preference fit, and adversarial robustness. Empirically no raw rubric source is simultaneously reliable, preference-predictive, and adversarially robust, and high inter-rater agreement does not imply low exploitability. Two repair operations improve outcomes: preference-rank selection raises paired-response judge accuracy from 65.0% to 68.6%, and reliability-constrained refinement cuts the rate of high scores given to exploit responses from 46.4% to 36.0% with little change in inter-judge agreement.

## Technical note

This ByteDance paper studies what makes LLMs effective sequential recommenders, finding that existing preference-alignment (DPO-style binary pairwise comparisons) discards two critical factors: preference intensity (structured strength of affinity/aversion) and temporal context (recency of interactions). Controlled experiments show comprehensive feedback with structured preference signals substantially improves performance. They propose RecPO, a unified preference-optimization framework mapping explicit and implicit feedback into a common preference signal and constructing adaptive reward margins that jointly account for preference intensity and interaction recency. Across five datasets RecPO consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines and exhibits human-like decision patterns (favoring immediate satisfaction, preference coherence, avoiding dispreferred items).

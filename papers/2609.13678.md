## Summary

This paper addresses the problem of cross-stage information decoupling in generative recommendation systems, where the tokenization stage heavily relies on text-based semantics and the generation stage neglects original semantic information, leading to reduced accuracy. To solve this, the authors propose SCRec, a framework that enhances coherence through three interconnected components: collaborative-enhanced tokenization, semantic-guided generation, and manifold alignment. Experiments show that SCRec effectively aligns semantic and collaborative signals, improving recommendation accuracy while maintaining efficiency.

## Technical note

SCRec attacks the cross-stage decoupling in two-stage semantic-ID generative recommenders, where RQ-VAE tokenization is dominated by text semantics while the generation stage re-embeds the codes purely from interactions and loses the original semantics. Three components restore bidirectional information flow. First, collaborative-enhanced tokenization textualizes CF signals: take the top-10 most similar items from a pretrained SASRec (or a Swing CF model), concatenate their titles truncated to 20 words into a CF prompt, encode both the item's own attribute prompt and the CF prompt with the same sentence-t5-base, and blend them as a*main + (1-a)*CF before RQ-VAE quantization, so both views land in one space with no extra alignment stage. Second, semantic-guided generation collapses the l code embeddings into an item-level vector using temperature-annealed softmax weights predicted from the frozen semantic representation, then sigmoid-gates a fusion of the code embedding and a projected semantic embedding, regularized by an InfoNCE alignment loss. Third, manifold alignment maps both representations onto a Poincare ball and minimizes geodesic distance at O(Nd) cost, giving the total loss L_gen + alpha*L_align + beta*L_geo. On Amazon Beauty, Sports and Outdoors, and Toys and Games with leave-last-out evaluation and an RPG decoder-only backbone, SCRec beats the best baseline by 13-27%, for example Beauty NDCG@10 0.0546 versus RPG 0.0460 and PIT 0.0403, and Sports Recall@10 0.0570 versus 0.0462. Ablations: dropping semantic guidance hurts most (Beauty N@10 0.0546 to 0.0444), then manifold alignment (0.0491), contrastive alignment (0.0509) and collaborative tokenization (0.0502); hyperbolic alignment beats L2 (0.0517) and MMD (0.0504), and textualized CF beats MMGRec-style embedding concatenation (0.0512) and LETTER-style contrastive supervision (0.0523). As a drop-in module it lifts TIGER by 14-20% and LIGER by 10-14% at only 6.70% and 1.98% extra per-step training time. Caveat: offline only on three small Amazon domains with no online A/B, despite the Kuaishou affiliation.

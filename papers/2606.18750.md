## Summary

This paper addresses critical gaps in how technology companies widely use Controlled-experiment Using Pre-Experiment Data (CUPED) to reduce variance in online A/B testing while maintaining unbiasedness. It systematically tackles five key questions about CUPED's practical application, comparing adjustment methods and validating regression-based approaches alongside exploring complex scenarios like multi-arm experiments. The findings provide optimal methodologies for trustworthy inference and have been implemented into a major experimentation platform.

## Technical note

The paper systematically resolves five practical CUPED questions using a design-based framework where potential outcomes are fixed and randomness enters only through assignment. It shows that model-free plug-in variance estimators can be invalid and that Huber-White sandwich estimators are needed for regression-based (Lin interaction-model) adjustments to maintain correct inference. For multi-arm experiments it proves that using the full-sample covariate mean in the adjustment term yields optimal precision, and group-specific adjustments maximize efficiency for group means and relative lift. For two-stage sampling it derives a corrected split-sample variance estimator accounting for compounded randomness, and reports that naive estimators caused ~0.1% of ByteDance experiments to show >3x variance inflation; the recommended methods are deployed in ByteDance's experimentation platform.

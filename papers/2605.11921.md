## Summary

This paper analyzes the LSH distortion, a measure of how closely a similarity function can be approximated by LSH schemes, for two specific permutation-based similarities: Ulam and Cayley similarities. For Ulam similarity, the research demonstrates that the distortion can be sublinear (O(n / √log n)) and provides a matching lower bound (Ω(n^0.12)). In contrast, the study shows that the Cayley similarity incurs a linear LSH distortion (Θ(n)), making it significantly harder to approximate efficiently with LSH.

## Technical note

This theory paper studies the LSH distortion, the smallest multiplicative factor by which a non-LSHable similarity can be approximated by one admitting an exact locality-sensitive hashing scheme (a collision kernel with unit diagonal that is a convex combination of equivalence-relation matrices), for two permutation similarities. For the normalized Ulam similarity (based on longest common subsequence), they prove a sublinear upper bound of O(n/sqrt(log n)) on the achievable LSH distortion and a matching-style lower bound of Omega(n^0.12). For the Cayley similarity (normalized number of cycles in the relative permutation), they show the LSH distortion is Theta(n), i.e., linear and thus much harder to approximate efficiently with LSH. The results show that LSH distortion can differ significantly from metric distortion, so known metric-embedding results do not transfer.

## Summary

1.  This paper introduces HLLM, a novel hierarchical architecture using Large Language Models (LLMs) specifically for enhancing sequential recommendation systems by modeling items and users differently from standard methods.
2.  The system employs two LLM tiers: one extracts detailed content features from item descriptions, while the other uses these features along with user interaction history to predict future interests, significantly boosting performance through fine-tuning.
3.  Extensive experiments on large datasets show HLLM achieves state-of-the-art results compared to traditional models and demonstrates excellent scalability (using only ~7B parameters) and practical efficiency suitable for real-world deployment.

## Technical note

LLM-based recommenders have shown only modest gains and leave open the value of pretrained weights, the need for fine-tuning, and whether recommendation shows LLM-style scalability. HLLM proposes a hierarchical two-tier architecture: an Item LLM extracts content features from item text descriptions, and a User LLM predicts future interests from the interaction history of those features. Leveraging open-source pretrained weights plus fine-tuning, HLLM scales to 7B parameters for both tiers and reaches state-of-the-art on PixelRec and Amazon Reviews, beating ID-based models by a wide margin. Online A/B testing confirms real-world gains with practical training and serving efficiency.

## Summary

Based on interviews with people and mental health experts, this paper finds that individuals often use conversational AI (like ChatGPT) during mental health crises to fill gaps in human support, especially when access to professionals is limited or burdens are feared. However, experts stress the essential role of human connection for effective crisis management. The authors suggest that responsible AI design should prepare users for positive human actions and de-escalate crises, positioning AI as a bridge to human connection rather than a replacement for it.

## Technical note

This qualitative HCI study examines first-person experiences of using conversational AI agents (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) during mental health crises, combining a testimonial survey of lived experiences (n=53) with interviews of mental health experts (n=16) analyzed via reflexive thematic analysis. It finds people turn to AI to fill in-between spaces of human support due to lack of access to professionals or fear of burdening others, while experts stress human-human connection as the essential positive crisis action. Using the transtheoretical stages-of-change model, the authors argue responsible AI crisis intervention should increase a user's preparedness to take positive action while de-escalating intended negative action, positioning AI as a bridge to human connection rather than a replacement.

## Summary

This paper investigates whether automatic LLM attribution metrics can be reliably used across different datasets and evaluation constructs within retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Testing eight scorers against three constructs, it found that none of the automatic methods consistently rank well or match human evaluations when transferred. The study concludes that relying on a single best-performing metric averaged over multiple datasets is inadequate for transferring performance to unseen data, recommending dataset-specific validation instead.

## Technical note

This is an evaluation-audit (not new-metric) paper testing whether eight automatic attribution scorers (lexical Jaccard, MiniLM/MPNet cosine, BERTScore, clean-MNLI/FEVER NLI, MiniCheck) transfer across datasets within three constructs: provenance/topicality, generated-answer attribution, and fact-check entailment. It defines transfer as staying within the best scorer's 95% CI on every dataset of a construct and finds none does: within AttributionBench, per-dataset rankings invert (Kendall tau=-0.64), an NLI scorer best on short-claim AttributedQA (AUROC 0.90) collapses to chance (0.53) on long-form LFQA where BERTScore wins (0.91), and the flip is not a length/truncation artifact. A naive best-on-average selector fails leave-one-dataset-out (mean regret 0.172 AUROC), so metric choice must be validated on the target dataset. A prompt-based LLM judge avoids chance-level collapses but is not uniformly best, ~100x costlier, and non-deterministic. Construct coarsely dictates the appropriate metric family (relevance for provenance, entailment for fact-check).

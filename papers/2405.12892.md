## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of multi-domain ad recommendation, focusing on domain-sensitive features crucial for capturing domain distinctions. Existing methods often neglect these features, leading to insufficient learning of domain differences. The authors propose a feature attribution method and a memory architecture to identify and retrieve domain-specific information from these features, demonstrating significant improvements in capturing domain distinctions and enhancing recommendation performance through extensive experiments.

## Technical note

This paper reframes multi-domain ad recommendation around feature distributions rather than model architecture, defining 'domain-sensitive features' as fields whose distributions and prediction effects differ sharply across domains. Using Integrated Gradients feature attribution, the authors show that existing MDR models (e.g., PEPNet) largely neglect the most domain-sensitive features, meaning domain distinctions are under-learned. They propose a Domain-Sensitive Feature Attribution method to identify such features and a Retrievable Domain-Sensitive Feature Memory that extracts and stores domain-specific information from those features for the model to retrieve and integrate. Extensive offline and online experiments show improved capture of domain distinctions and better multi-domain CTR/CVR performance at reasonable computational cost.

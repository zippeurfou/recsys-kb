## Summary

This paper addresses a limitation in standard contextual bandit algorithms that often miss valuable information appearing after an action is taken (post-serving contexts). It introduces poLinUCB, a novel algorithm specifically designed for this scenario and proven to achieve tight regret. Empirical results confirm that incorporating post-serving contexts significantly improves performance, making the new approach more effective than existing methods under standard assumptions.

## Technical note

Standard contextual bandits assume all relevant context is observed before arm selection, ignoring valuable post-serving signals (e.g., watch time, watch speed) revealed after a recommendation. The paper formalizes a contextual bandit with post-serving contexts and proposes poLinUCB, a linear UCB variant that models the mapping from pre-serving to post-serving features and achieves tight regret. The core technical tool is a robustified, generalized Elliptical Potential Lemma that tolerates noise in the observed contexts. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets show incorporating post-serving contexts substantially improves performance over state-of-the-art bandit baselines.

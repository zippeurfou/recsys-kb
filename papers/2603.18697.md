## Summary

Industrial recommendation systems struggle with sparse item vocabularies, where low-frequency items negatively impact model scalability and performance. To address this, the paper introduces OCP (Orthogonal Constrained Projection), a method that enforces orthogonality on embeddings, enhancing their representational quality and generalization. This technique suppresses interference from rare items, accelerates model training, and significantly boosts recommendation performance, as demonstrated by real-world deployment on JD.com.

## Technical note

OCP (Orthogonal Constrained Projection) targets embedding collapse in industrial commodity recommendation when Item-ID vocabularies are scaled sparsely from millions to billions of entries, where skewed interactions cause effective-rank degradation as updates concentrate on a few dominant directions. It optimizes the projection matrix P on the Stiefel manifold (P^T P = I) via a QR-based projected gradient update, so P acts as an isometry that preserves the singular-value spectrum of the upstream gradient during backpropagation and forces low-frequency item embeddings to update along diverse orthogonal directions. The authors quantify representation isotropy with Singular Entropy (higher = better-utilized, less collapsed space) and analytically show non-zero singular values are preserved under the column-orthogonal projection. On JD.com, OCP is evaluated on a generative retrieval model (OxygenREC, three-level semantic IDs, Top1 hit@k) and a production ranking model (AUC/GAUC), accelerating loss convergence and yielding consistent gains when scaling dense layers. Large-scale online A/B deployment reports +12.97% UCXR and +8.9% GMV.

## Summary

This paper addresses a key limitation of recommendation systems, which overly focus on past preferences leading to user fatigue and limited exploration. Current LLM-based dual-model approaches have two major issues: they ignore long-term group identity interests during modeling, and their optimization is static and doesn't adapt well. The proposed Co-Evolutionary Alignment (CoEA) method tackles this by using a Dual-Stable Interest Exploration module for balanced historical interest capture and a Periodic Collaborative Optimization mechanism that dynamically adjusts recommendations through regular feedback loops between two specialized LLMs.

## Technical note

CoEA (Co-Evolutionary Alignment) addresses feedback-loop-driven content fatigue in LLM-enhanced exploratory recommendation, fixing two flaws of dual-model (Novelty LLM + Relevance LLM) frameworks: ignoring long-term group-identity interests and static one-time alignment. Its Dual-Stable Interest Exploration (DSIE) module models long- and short-term behavior in parallel, using RQ-VAE clustering to extract group Collaborative Semantic IDs alongside recent interest categories. Its Periodic Collaborative Optimization (PCO) mechanism forms a bidirectional closed loop where the Relevance LLM verifies preferences, guides incremental fine-tuning of the Novelty LLM (with a constraint loss against catastrophic forgetting), then re-scores its outputs. Online and offline experiments verify effectiveness for serendipitous recommendation.

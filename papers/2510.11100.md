## Summary

This paper introduces HoMer, a novel recommender system model designed to overcome three key limitations in CTR prediction: feature heterogeneity, context heterogeneity, and architecture heterogeneity. It achieves this by integrating sequential and non-sequential features more effectively, shifting from point-wise to set-wise item prediction to capture cross-item interactions, and employing a unified encoder-decoder architecture for improved efficiency and scalability. Extensive experiments show that HoMer significantly outperforms existing industrial baselines, improving AUC by 0.0099 and business metrics by over 1%, while also saving GPU resources.

## Technical note

HoMer is a Homogeneous-Oriented Transformer for CTR prediction that targets three heterogeneities in industrial recommenders: feature heterogeneity between sparse sequence side features and rich non-sequential features, context heterogeneity from point-wise scoring, and architecture heterogeneity from fragmented specialized modules. It aligns sequence side features with non-sequential features into a 'panoramic sequence', shifts prediction from point-wise to set-wise so cross-item interactions among co-exposed items are modeled in a single parallel invocation, and unifies everything in an encoder-decoder Transformer backbone. The set-wise design lets one offline sample per request replace per-item samples, cutting storage and I/O. Deployed at Meituan search advertising, HoMer improves AUC by 0.0099, lifts online CTR/RPM by 1.99%/2.46%, and cuts GPU cost 27% while scaling with model size.

## Summary

The paper introduces ReID-R, a novel "reasoning-driven" paradigm for person re-identification (ReID) that aims to overcome limitations of current "perception-driven" methods, which are often fragile and data-hungry. ReID-R incorporates "chain-of-thought" reasoning by using a two-stage process: first, a label-free training stage for identity-aware feature understanding, followed by reinforcement learning using reward signals to generalize across scenes and focus on identity cues. This approach significantly reduces the required training data (to just 14.3K images) while achieving performance comparable to existing methods and providing interpretable reasoning for its results.

## Technical note

ReID-R is a reasoning-driven paradigm for person re-identification that injects chain-of-thought into the ReID pipeline via a pure reinforcement learning framework over a multimodal LLM, reframing matching as a pairwise decision over top-k hard candidates. Stage one, Discriminative Reasoning Warm-up, trains the policy on a label-free 'Discriminative Captioning' task with a contrastive, ReID-inspired reward so the model autonomously discovers salient, generalizable identity evidence and gains identity-aware understanding. Stage two applies efficient RL with GRPO (group-normalized advantages, KL regularization) plus a non-trivial sampling strategy that filters a learnable subset to control reward variance and provide rich gradients. It matches SOTA identity discrimination on five benchmarks using only 14.3K samples (20.9% of standard data) while producing interpretable reasoning traces that focus on identity-persistent cues over superficial distractors.

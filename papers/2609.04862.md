## Summary

1.  Existing multi-task recommendation systems often suffer from **signal erosion**, where the information needed for specific conversion tasks gets diluted due to rigid, static dependency structures (like fixed Click->Pay funnel) across items.
2.  To combat this, the authors propose **Personalized Task Dependency Graphs (PTDG)**, which dynamically adjusts the strength of task dependencies for each individual item using low-rank approximation and GCN-based information propagation.
3.  This approach establishes adaptive information shortcuts and decouples shared parameters based on task sparsity (**Adaptive Progressive Masking**), successfully improving performance, particularly on sparse conversion tasks (up to 1.45% AUC gain) and increasing online conversion rate (CVR) and eCPM by 1.2% and 1.9%, respectively.

## Technical note

Huawei, ranking model for an app distribution platform with over 100 million daily active users. Problem framing: industrial multi-task learning over a conversion funnel (Click to Download to Pay) enforces one global dependency topology for all traffic, and hierarchical message passing along that fixed chain causes cumulative signal attenuation that disproportionately hurts sparse deep-funnel objectives; heavy games follow the full chain while lightweight tools often skip intermediate steps. PTDG makes the task dependency an instance-specific learnable graph: a low-rank adjacency A_item = Z_L Z_R transpose with rank q < T/2 derived from item embeddings via two backbones (DeepFM or MLP), modulated by a personalization bias vector gamma_{u,s} produced by an MLP over user and scenario features and broadcast across tasks, then elementwise-masked by a hard causal mask to prevent semantic backflow such as Pay to Click, after which a one-layer symmetrically normalized GCN propagates over the task feature matrix and per-task sigmoid MLP heads emit conversion probabilities. A second component, Adaptive Progressive Masking, learns per-task binary top-k masks over shared parameters where the target mask rate is linearly interpolated from the task's label positive rate (r_min=0.2 for dense tasks, r_max=0.7 for sparse ones) with a linear warmup, resolving gradient conflicts structurally instead of via PCGrad-style O(T^2) projections and adding no inference overhead since masks are fixed after training; per-task BCE losses are reweighted by the inverse EMA of each task loss with alpha=0.1. Evaluated on KuaiRand1K (8.4M samples, 6 tasks, positive rates 0.02 to 37.9 percent) and a 13.2M-sample 7-task industrial dataset, averaged over 20 random seeds. PTDG has the best average AUC on both (0.9081 versus 0.9025 for MIT on KuaiRand, 0.8878 versus 0.8818 for PMTRec industrially), with the largest wins on sparse deep-conversion tasks (+1.45 percent over PMTRec on industrial Task 3, +1.77 percent over the online MMoE baseline on KuaiRand Follow) and parity on dense shallow tasks (Task 4: 0.9464 versus PLE 0.9471). Ablations: removing the personalization bias costs 1.21 percent average AUC, replacing the GCN with concatenation 0.93 percent, removing the causal mask 0.64 percent, removing APM 0.59 percent; rank sensitivity peaks at q=2, roughly T/3. A two-week online A/B with 10 percent base and 10 percent treatment buckets against the deployed MMoE gives +1.2 percent CVR and +1.9 percent eCPM under daily-aggregated two-sample t-tests at p<0.05, with only +8 ms end-to-end latency. Caveats: industrial task names and positive rates are confidential, the causal ordering is hand-specified rather than learned, and scaling to larger task sets and cross-domain settings is left to future work.

## Summary

This thesis introduces the **Algorithm Performance Space (APS)** as a new framework to evaluate datasets for recommender systems based on how well algorithms perform on them. It proposes specific metrics within this space to quantitatively justify dataset selection and validate existing assumptions about dataset suitability, aiming to move beyond historical popularity like MovieLens or Amazon benchmarks. The core idea is that by using these APS tools, researchers can better differentiate appropriate datasets for their algorithm's evaluation context, leading to more informed and diverse choices in experimental design.

## Technical note

Recsys research over-relies on a few datasets (MovieLens, Amazon) chosen by popularity rather than justified suitability, hurting reproducibility. This thesis introduces the Algorithm Performance Space (APS), which positions datasets by the measured performance (NDCG@10) of multiple algorithms (BPR, ItemKNN, Mult-VAE, SGL, NeuMF) rather than by dataset statistics, and defines Difficulty, Variance, and Diversity metrics to quantify and justify dataset selections. An experiment trained the five algorithms with TPE hyperparameter optimization on 75 public datasets (71 usable), visualized via Mini-APS and PCA. Difficulty correlated strongly (rho=0.95) with the first principal component, and diverse dataset selections could be identified, though incomplete HPO runs on large datasets limited some conclusions.

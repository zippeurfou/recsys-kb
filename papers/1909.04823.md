## Summary

This paper introduces Distributed Equivalent Substitution (DES) training, a novel distributed framework for large-scale recommender systems, marking the first use of synchronous training to accelerate convergence and improve Click-Through Rate (CTR). DES achieves this by replacing communication-heavy weight operations with computationally equivalent sub-operators and aggregating partial results locally, significantly reducing network traffic. Experiments show DES outperforms state-of-the-art PS-based methods, delivering up to 68.7% communication savings and higher throughput on real-world recommendation models.

## Technical note

Distributed Equivalent Substitution (DES) is a synchronous distributed training framework for large-scale DLRMs with dynamic sparse features, replacing the dominant asynchronous parameter-server approach. Its key idea is to substitute communication-heavy weights-rich operators with computationally equivalent groups of sub-operators that update only co-located partial weights and aggregate small partial results, drastically cutting network traffic instead of transmitting huge sparse weights. DES brings fully synchronous training to industrial recommenders for the first time, improving convergence and AUC/CTR. Experiments show up to 68.7% communication savings and higher throughput than state-of-the-art PS-based systems.

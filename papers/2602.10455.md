## Summary

Large recommendation models, while effective, suffer from high training and inference costs due to complex feature interactions. This paper introduces UG-Separation (UG-Sep), a novel framework enabling reusable user-side computation within dense interaction models by explicitly separating user and item feature information flows using a masking mechanism. To compensate for any loss in expressiveness from masking and further optimize performance, UG-Sep incorporates an information compensation strategy and weight-only quantization, demonstrating significant inference latency reductions (up to 20%) in large-scale online settings without harming user experience or business metrics.

## Technical note

UG-Sep (User-Group Separation) enables reuse of user-side computation within TokenMixer-based dense interaction recommenders (RankMixer/TokenMixer-Large) at ByteDance, where user and candidate features are normally entangled across layers. It decouples U-tokens and G-tokens at the bottom layers and applies a separation mask in the token-mixing layer so a subset of tokens stays purely user-side, routing them through a reusable Per-token FFN cached per user (reducing per-token cost from O(C) to O(1) over C candidates); a cross-attention residual handles pyramidal token reduction. An Information Compensation module projects and adds preserved U-side info back into G-tokens to offset masking loss, and W8A16 weight-only quantization alleviates the exposed memory-bandwidth bottleneck. Online A/B tests cut serving latency up to ~20% with negligible AUC/engagement change across Douyin and ads scenarios.

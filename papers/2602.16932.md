## Summary

Current retrieval algorithms like BM25 primarily rely on parameter tuning for improvement, lacking automatic innovation. This paper introduces RankEvolve, an LLM-driven evolution system that starts with basic algorithms and iteratively improves them through mutation and selection across multiple datasets. The evolved algorithms are novel, effective, and demonstrate strong performance transfer across different benchmark collections, suggesting LLM-guided evolution is a promising method for automatically discovering advanced ranking algorithms.

## Technical note

RankEvolve applies AlphaEvolve-style LLM-guided program evolution to automatically discover lexical retrieval algorithms, representing each candidate ranker as a ~300-line executable Python program that is mutated via SEARCH/REPLACE diffs by GPT-5.2. A population is managed with island-based evolution plus per-island MAP-Elites grids over code-length (complexity) and edit-distance (diversity), and candidates are scored by an evaluator using fitness 0.8*Recall@100 + 0.2*nDCG@10 across 12 BEIR/BRIGHT datasets. Starting from BM25 and query-likelihood-with-Dirichlet seeds, evolved programs independently rediscover soft stopword filtering via composite IDF weighting, term-frequency saturation, PMI-based specificity resembling collection language models, and gentler logarithmic length normalization. The evolved algorithms transfer to 16 held-out datasets and TREC DL 19/20, showing statistically significant gains over their seeds.

## Summary

1.  The paper introduces **IndiMathBench**, a new benchmark consisting of 312 formal Lean 4 theorems paired with their informal problem statements, specifically sourced from Indian Mathematics Olympiads.
2.  This benchmark was created using an **AI-powered, human-assisted pipeline** that employs category-based retrieval, iterative feedback, and expert validation via an interactive dashboard to autoformalize the problems.
3.  Evaluation shows that while the autoformalization process exists, it remains challenging, with frontier AI models struggling to achieve high semantic correctness and theorem proving success rates, establishing IndiMathBench as a difficult testbed for mathematical reasoning AI.

## Technical note

IndiMathBench is a 312-problem, human-verified benchmark pairing informal Indian Mathematics Olympiad statements with Lean 4 formalizations, built to avoid the contamination and narrow scope of miniF2F/PutnamBench. Its AI-assisted human-in-the-loop pipeline uses category-based retrieval, iterative Lean compiler feedback (self-debug loops), and multi-model ensembles to draft formalizations that experts validate through an interactive dashboard and VS Code extension. Evaluating frontier models reveals large gaps between syntactic compilation validity and semantic (BEq) correctness, with theorem-proving success staying low even under iterative refinement, especially for geometry.

## Summary

This paper introduces ATLAS, a benchmarking framework designed to evaluate the diverse long-context capabilities of large language models beyond simple length checks. ATLAS employs three key principles: a layered taxonomy separating basic operations from complex applications, length-aware scoring integrating performance across an 8K-1M token grid, and ATLAScore, a harmonic-mean aggregate penalizing imbalanced capability profiles. The framework evaluates 26 models across eight dimensions, revealing that rankings change significantly with context length, demonstrating the necessity of length-dependent capability profiling for accurate model assessment.

## Technical note

ATLAS is a benchmarking framework that redefines long-context evaluation as length-dependent capability profiling rather than single-point scoring, exposing two masked failure modes: performance collapse as length grows and strong retrieval failing to transfer to downstream use. It contributes a layered 3+5 capability taxonomy (foundational probes of retrieval, aggregation, multi-step reasoning plus five application workloads) so failures can be attributed, length-aware AUC scoring that integrates score-length curves over a fixed 8K-1M grid, and ATLAScore, a harmonic-mean aggregate over taxonomy categories that penalizes imbalanced profiles with end-to-end confidence-interval propagation through the nonlinear aggregate. Instantiated with nine components over 6,438 instances across 26 models, it shows rankings reshuffle substantially between 8K-128K and 8K-1M scopes (7 models move >=2 ranks, one frontier model drops four), and the two taxonomy layers share only 61% of cross-model variance, arguing quality must be reported by capability and length.

## Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently creating high-quality relevance annotations for sponsored search at scale, as human annotation is costly and slow, while off-the-shelf LLMs lack accuracy. The authors propose "AutoRelAnnotator," which uses a calibrated model cascade, routing queries through progressively larger fine-tuned classifiers to achieve both efficiency and quality. This approach demonstrates that combining domain-specific fine-tuning (boosting accuracy significantly) with cascading models (keeping accuracy high while reducing compute cost by half) is effective, and adding per-class isotonic calibration provides a small but reliable additional improvement in the final model's performance.

## Technical note

AutoRelAnnotator is an offline relevance-annotation system built on the insight that accuracy and cost are orthogonal: domain-specific fine-tuning drives accuracy while cascading drives cost. It fine-tunes three classifiers ordered by cost (DeBERTa-v3-base 184M cross-encoder ~5ms, LoRA Gemma-2B ~50ms, LoRA LLaMA-3-8B ~200ms), each with a 5-class softmax head, then routes query-title pairs by calibrated confidence thresholds with an ensemble majority-vote fallback biased toward higher relevance. Fine-tuning contributes ~20 accuracy points (reaching 87-89% vs. off-the-shelf GPT-4 68.2% / Claude-3 70.1%), while cascading is roughly accuracy-neutral but halves compute. Per-class isotonic calibration (fit per predicted class, correcting overconfidence on extreme classes 0/4 and underconfidence on middle classes) adds a small but statistically significant +0.6 points over the strongest calibration baseline. Validated across six production use cases processing 150M+ annotations, cutting turnaround from 5 days to 1-3 hours.

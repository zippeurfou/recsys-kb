## Summary

Recommender systems often lack trust, making explaining recommendations difficult. Current explanations are either user-specific or item-specific, but fail to account for users' preferences over item attributes. This paper introduces RecXplainer, a method that generates personalized explanations based on user preferences for item attributes, and demonstrates its effectiveness across multiple real-world datasets and against several baselines.

## Technical note

RecXplainer generates fine-grained, attribute-based explanations for collaborative-filtering recommenders whose embedding spaces are otherwise opaque. It trains an auxiliary model (linear layer, neural net, or GBDT) that takes the recommender's user embedding plus an item's multi-hot attribute vector and reproduces observed ratings; attribute importance is computed by removal (zeroing one attribute at a time and measuring the predicted-rating drop), yielding a per-item 'specific preference' and an averaged 'general preference' analogous to local/global interpretability. The method is post-hoc, model-agnostic, and amortized (explanations need only a forward pass). The authors propose eight generalizable evaluation metrics and show RecXplainer beats five baselines across five datasets and five recommender classes, with one-feature removal roughly matching SHAP at 70x lower cost.

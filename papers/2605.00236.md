## Summary

This paper introduces the Attention Redistribution Attack (ARA), a novel method that bypasses safety alignment in large language models (LLMs) by strategically inserting nonsemantic tokens to redirect attention away from safety-critical positions within the model's attention layers. Unlike previous attacks, ARA specifically targets the geometry of the attention mechanism itself, proving highly effective against models like Mistral-7B and LLaMA-3, achieving significant jailbreak success rates (ASR) with minimal effort. The key finding reveals that safety emerges from distributed attention routing across layers, not from individual heads; simply removing safety heads allows the model to compensate, whereas redirecting their attention propagates harmful signals downstream.

## Technical note

This paper introduces the Attention Redistribution Attack (ARA), a jailbreak that bypasses LLM safety alignment by inserting nonsemantic tokens to redirect attention away from safety-critical positions within the model's attention layers, targeting the geometry of the attention mechanism itself rather than crafting semantic prompts. ARA achieves high attack success rates against models such as Mistral-7B and LLaMA-3 with minimal effort. The central mechanistic finding is that safety emerges from distributed attention routing across layers rather than from individual heads: simply ablating safety heads lets the model compensate, whereas redirecting their attention propagates harmful signals downstream. This reframes safety as a routing property and highlights a vulnerability that head-level defenses cannot address.

## Summary

1.  The paper explores Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) for recommenders using Semantic IDs (SIDs), which represent items as sequences of hierarchical codes.
2.  It finds that directly using item-level SIDs for OPE is unreliable due to low effective sample sizes, but coarsening items into code-prefix clusters provides estimable support and improves accuracy, with the SID hierarchy enabling this efficient coarsening through decoder-exposed cluster masses.
3.  The key parameter for this trade-off is the "resolution depth," where coarser clusters are necessary for reliable estimation when support is scarce, and the resulting bias is linked to the quantizer's reconstruction error and the divergence between the logging and target distributions.

## Technical note

Criteo. Asks whether a generative recommender's own semantic-ID code tree can serve as the action abstraction for off-policy evaluation, so teams can gate decoder, decoding-temperature or reranking variants offline before spending scarce A/B traffic. On production e-commerce logs that explore by score-weighted Plackett-Luce sampling, the served item's propensity is highly concentrated (median 0.04 to 0.15, with 6 to 16 percent of impressions above 0.9), driving item-level effective sample size to roughly 0.003 of the raw count even on 17M impressions, so per-item IPS is not a usable decision tool. The proposed move is to marginalize propensities up to length-l code prefixes (a hierarchical MIPS estimator), which needs only cluster positivity; crucially an autoregressive decoder returns each prefix mass exactly as a product of per-level conditionals in O(l), whereas a flat clustering would require the item-level propensities a code-only decoder never exposes. Proposition 3.1 bounds the coarsening bias by 2 L_q delta_max_l times the expected within-cluster total-variation disagreement, tying it to the quantizer's worst-case reconstruction residual rather than its mean squared error. On a leakage-free KuaiRand random-exposure testbed (catalog 7339 items, M=5000) SNIPS at item granularity gets 0.155 RMSE versus 0.087 for SNIPS at code granularity; MIPS_hier, DM_hier and both OffCEM variants all land within 0.008 of each other, and Policy Convolution at a tuned bandwidth h=0.1 wins at 0.076 but degrades at h=0.2 and needs item propensities. An honest negative result: flat k-means on the same embedding at matched cluster counts recovers the full item-to-cluster gain, with all four paired differences containing zero, so the benefit comes from coarsening and not from the hierarchy, and the tree's real advantage is feasibility. Depth is the operative knob: SLOPE picks it from logged data alone and captures 67 to 81 percent of the oracle-selected gain (0.133, 0.109, 0.102 at M=1000, 5000, 10000). RQ-VAE versus residual k-means is a wash (paired deltas -0.003, -0.001, +0.000, -0.004), while embedding source dominates (metadata 0.084 versus collaborative 0.131). Caveats: semi-synthetic testbeds only, the Amazon Musical Instruments replication has a confounded oracle, the production slice has a catalog under 73 items, MIPS's no-direct-effect assumption holds only approximately, and every route to estimating the coarsening bias fails because that estimate needs exactly the granularity the near-argmax logging policy already destroyed.

## Summary

Current recommender systems often use pointwise rankers that don't align well with ranking metrics and struggle to separate position bias from true item relevance due to simplistic models. This paper proposes a novel "unbiased pairwise learning to rank" algorithm designed specifically for recommender systems, addressing these issues by explicitly modeling position bias and introducing a "pairwise trust bias". The method demonstrates superior performance on both categorical and continuous feedback types compared to existing approaches on benchmark datasets and live traffic.

## Technical note

This paper proposes an unbiased pairwise learning-to-rank method for recommender systems that corrects position bias and, unlike prior unbiased LTR, handles both categorical labels (clicks) and continuous labels (dwell time, watch ratio). It models position-based examination bias in a pairwise fashion and introduces a 'pairwise trust bias' to explicitly separate position bias, trust bias, and true relevance, directly relating unbiased relevance pairs to biased feedback pairs so it needs no assumption tying continuous feedback to relevance. A regression-based EM algorithm is extended to the pairwise setting to estimate the parameters. Offline benchmark results beat both SOTA recommender rankers and SOTA unbiased LTR methods, and an online A/B test on a very large content platform yields a 2.08% business-metric improvement.

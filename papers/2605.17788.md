## Summary

Recommender systems face a challenge balancing reliable recommendations for Low-Active Users (LAUs) with diverse ones for High-Active Users (HAUs). The paper introduces a framework that leverages model uncertainty quantification, treating it as a signal for both risk management and exploration opportunities. Specifically, it uses uncertainty to implement a risk-averse strategy for LAUs (suppressing unreliable recommendations) and a risk-seeking UCB strategy for HAUs (encouraging content exploration). This approach improves LAU retention and satisfaction while boosting HAU interest diversity and coverage.

## Technical note

This paper introduces a production-ready uncertainty-quantification framework that calibrates model uncertainty to drive segment-differentiated recommendation strategies on short-video and livestream platforms. It estimates input-specific epistemic uncertainty without heavy sampling: an auxiliary critic network predicts per user-item Expected Prediction Error (EPE) for point-estimate models, while an empirical-Bayes procedure infers input-dependent priors to decompose predictive variance for probabilistic (Beta/Gamma) architectures. Calibrated thresholds enable a risk-averse deboosting policy that suppresses high-uncertainty recommendations for Low-Active Users, and a risk-seeking Upper Confidence Bound exploration bonus that treats high uncertainty as an interest-expansion opportunity for High-Active Users. Deployed on a major livestream platform, it improves LAU active hours and quality watch-time ratio while raising HAU interest diversity and category coverage.

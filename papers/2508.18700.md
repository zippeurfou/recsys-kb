## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of overfitting ("one-epoch problem") that occurs when using ID-based embeddings in large-scale recommendation systems due to long-tail data distributions, forcing single-epoch training. To combat this, they propose a two-stage strategy: first, pre-training on an extremely simple model with contrastive loss to cover more user/item data and allow multi-epoch training without degradation; second, fine-tuning these robust embeddings for complex downstream recommendation tasks.

The offline experiments show that the initial ID-based embedding extraction via the proposed pre-training successfully avoids overfitting. Finally, they deployed this system at Pinterest, leading to significant improvements in site-wide engagement metrics.

## Technical note

This paper addresses the one-epoch overfitting problem of ID-based embeddings in web-scale recommenders, caused by power-law long-tail entries having more degrees of freedom than training samples. It proposes a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 pre-trains shared foundational ID embeddings in a minimal dot-product model with a contrastive loss using in-batch plus uniform random negatives, which reduces the effective dimensionality of tail entries and permits multi-epoch training over 10x more engagement data without overfitting. Stage 2 shares and fine-tunes these embeddings in complex downstream ranking models, with fine-tuning outperforming freezing. Offline Hit@3 improves over baseline (single-stage training actually regresses), and a two-week Pinterest A/B test on Homefeed and Related Pins yielded 2.2% site-wide engagement gains.

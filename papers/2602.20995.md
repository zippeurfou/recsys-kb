## Summary

1.  Recommendation systems face the "train-serving discrepancy" where pre-ranking models trained on user interactions struggle to score unseen ("unexposed") items effectively during online serving, leading to sample selection bias.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces Generative Pseudo-Labeling (GPL), which uses large language models (LLMs) to generate relevant, unbiased pseudo-labels for unexposed items offline, aligning the training data with the online serving requirements.
3.  By improving the model's exposure to unexposed items without adding online latency, GPL enhances recommendation performance, boosting click-through rates and improving diversity and discovery of long-tail items in production.

## Technical note

Generative Pseudo-Labeling (GPL) mitigates sample selection / exposure bias in the pre-ranking stage of cascade recommenders, where models train only on exposed interactions yet must score unexposed recalled candidates. Offline, items are tokenized into hierarchical semantic IDs via a frozen multimodal encoder and RQ-VAE (content-grounded, not interaction-biased), and a fine-tuned LLM predicts future SIDs from user histories, decoded into diverse interest-anchors; relevance pseudo-labels for unexposed items come from matching anchors to candidates in the frozen multimodal space, calibrated by an uncertainty-aware weighting over semantic coherence, historical consistency, and LLM confidence. The pre-ranker is trained with a confidence-weighted dual-label objective jointly over observed interactions and calibrated pseudo-labels, with frequency-based downsampling to curb popularity bias. All LLM inference is offline and cached (zero online latency); deployment shows a 3.07% CTR lift with improved diversity and long-tail discovery.

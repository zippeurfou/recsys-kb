## Summary

This paper introduces **UAME**, an uncertainty-aware framework for short video recommendations, addressing the core challenge of modeling unobservable user satisfaction.

Existing methods rely on biased and often conflicting behavioral signals (clicks/watch time) as proxies for true satisfaction during training, which can negatively impact model performance and reliability. UAME tackles this by representing predicted scores with a Gaussian distribution, capturing uncertainty through variance alongside the mean satisfaction score.

The framework employs a probabilistic ranking loss function and an original sample-level weighting scheme based on predictive variance to reduce bias within its optimization process, demonstrating significant offline and online improvements over state-of-the-art methods while aligning better with human user feedback.

## Technical note

UAME mitigates satisfaction label bias in end-to-end multi-objective ensemble ranking for short video, where conflicting pxtr behavior proxies (click, watch-time, like, etc.) bias a deterministic ranker. It models each item's score as a Gaussian variable (mean is satisfaction, variance is predictive uncertainty), trains a probabilistic pairwise ranking (PPR) loss via the Gaussian CDF with uncertainty regularization and an auxiliary conflict-alignment loss, and reweights item pairs by their comprehensive uncertainty to emphasize high-conflict pairs, with theory linking predictive uncertainty to proxy bias. On EMER and EASQ backbones it improves offline GAUC (up to +5.8% relative on EMER, +14.2% on EASQ plvtr) and questionnaire-satisfaction NDCG@5 by +8.71% and HR@5 by +10.63%; seven-day online A/B tests show significant gains (e.g., LongView +1.6%, Like +0.9-1.3%, all p<0.005), and learned uncertainty correlates with label bias (Pearson 0.65, Spearman 0.67).

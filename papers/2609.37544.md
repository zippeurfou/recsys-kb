## Summary

1.  The paper identifies a key challenge: effectively using delayed and noisy recommendation feedback (impressions, clicks, conversions, negatives) to improve the memory of content-generation agents, as these signals are often confounded by external factors and multiple memories.
2.  To solve this, it proposes TIDE (Trajectory-Informed Directed Evolution), a framework that explicitly assigns credit and responsibility for recommendation outcomes to specific memories used during generation, allowing for targeted evolution.
3.  TIDE demonstrates significant improvement, achieving a +7.75 percentage point gain in offline tasks and boosting online metrics, while also showing superior accuracy on benchmark delayed-label tasks compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

Taobao/Alibaba's TIDE (Trajectory-Informed Directed Memory Evolution) adapts a frozen-LLM agent that writes e-commerce membership marketing materials by evolving a capacity-bounded external memory from delayed, confounded recommender feedback (impressions, clicks, conversions) instead of retraining, and scores adaptation with Memory Evolution Gain (MEG), the utility gain over a reference memory on strictly future tasks. Three credits turn outcomes into memory fitness: temporal credit keeps only mature, comparable outcome lifecycles, semantic credit compares each material with matched controls (same product, audience, placement and recommender version) under logged calendar and traffic context, and responsibility credit rewards only memories that were used, align with the content decision and change it under leave-one-out counterfactual replay; TIDE then revises (mutation), merges (crossover) or evicts memories under a fixed capacity. Offline on qwen3.5-27b, TIDE reaches +7.75 MEG with the lowest inference cost (750 tokens per request) against Reflexion, Mem0, A-MEM, AWM and ExpRAG; on Qwen3-4B, SFT plus TIDE gets +6.71 MEG for 0.118 extra PFLOPs while GRPO and MMPO each cost about 1,400 PFLOPs; and a one-week online A/B test shows +5.10% UCTR and +4.79% activation rate (p<0.001). Dropping responsibility credit is the only ablation with a significant loss (mean utility 0.852 to 0.703), counterfactual replay drives attribution quality (responsibility F1 0.817, 0.591 without it), and on a delayed-label MemoryCD benchmark TIDE has the best MAE and RMSE (0.531 and 0.895) and recovers from a controlled distribution shift in 0.33 rounds.

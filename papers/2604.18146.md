## Summary

Large language models (LLMs) offer powerful representations for recommendation systems (RSs), but compressing these high-dimensional representations is challenging. The paper identifies a counterintuitive phenomenon, Mid-Layer Representation Advantage (MRA), where better representations are found in LLM middle layers, making standard final-layer compression ineffective. To solve this, the authors propose MARC, a modular compression technique that introduces specific modules to capture and preserve the specialized representations from different LLM layers, leading to more effective and efficient recommendations.

## Technical note

MARC compresses high-dimensional offline LLM representations for industrial recommenders after identifying the Mid-layer Representation Advantage (MRA): middle-layer LLM representations outperform final-layer ones on downstream CTR, because standard compression fine-tuning turns final layers into a task-adaptation module (an information bottleneck). MARC applies Modular Adjustment (keeping the LLM as a pure representation module while adding an external compression network and a user-item matching network for task adaptation) and Modular Task Decoupling using an HSIC constraint that maximizes mutual information between original 4096-d and compressed 128-d representations via a Gaussian-kernel RKHS mapping. Trained end-to-end on Llama3-8B with LoRA, MARC beats MRL/LARR baselines on ML-1M/ML-25M/Yelp and yields a 2.82% eCPM lift in a search-advertising A/B test.

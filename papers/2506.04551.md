## Summary

This paper introduces PUB, an AI-based user behaviour simulator designed specifically for evaluating recommender systems. Traditional offline evaluation methods often fail to capture complex modern behaviours due to data sparsity, noise, and inadequate personality modeling.

PUB uses Large Language Models (LLMs) integrated with the Big Five personality traits framework to overcome these limitations by dynamically inferring user personalities from their logs and item details before generating synthetic interactions.

Experiments show PUB effectively simulates real-world user behaviour patterns and reveals meaningful links between personality traits and recommendation outcomes, providing a scalable and high-fidelity alternative to expensive live experiments.

## Technical note

The paper tackles the poor fidelity of offline recommender-system evaluation, where sparse, noisy logs and no personality modelling make synthetic user behaviour unrealistic. It proposes PUB, an LLM-based simulator with four stages (user profile aggregator, metadata enhancer, personality inference, behaviour simulator) that infers Big Five traits (O/C/E/A/N) from behavioural logs and item metadata via prompt-guided LLMs and psycholinguistic mappings, then generates synthetic interactions. On Amazon Review data (571.54M interactions, 30 categories), PUB reaches an average Jaccard similarity of 0.31 with real sequences, outperforming Random, RecSim, NEST, and RecAgent baselines, and algorithm rankings (Pop, MF, BPR, NeuMF, LightGCN, GRU4Rec, SASRec) on synthetic data closely mirror those on real data via nDCG@20. Analysis shows high Agreeableness/Conscientiousness users get better recommendations while high Openness users get worse, and the synthetic data favors sequential/popularity models over collaborative-filtering ones.

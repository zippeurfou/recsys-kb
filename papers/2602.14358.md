Airbnb search must balance a worldwide, highly varied supply of homes with guests whose location, amenity, style, and price expectations differ widely. Meeting those expectations hinges on an efficient retrieval stage that surfaces only the listings a guest might realistically book, before resource intensive ranking models are applied to determine the best results. ## Technical note

Airbnb reframes upstream location retrieval as an extreme multiclass classification problem: predict which level-11 S2 geospatial cells a search will book in, replacing the prior rectangular retrieval-bounds bandit model that surfaced too many unbookable areas. The world's ~25M S2 cells are reduced to ~360K booked-cell classes, sharded by continent into three models (~85K/139K/137K classes), and trained with sampled softmax (~25K negatives) over a 4-layer DNN on ~530M booking-attributed searches; predictions above a tuned threshold are OR-queried against a Lucene index. Versus the retrieval-bounds baseline the S2-cell approach matches recall (-0.04%) while lifting Booked-Location Precision +11.01% and retrieved inventory +9.91%; a ~66M-user online A/B test yielded +0.17% uncancelled bookers.

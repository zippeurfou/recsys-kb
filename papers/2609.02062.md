## Summary

Existing generative POI recommendation methods often ignore real-world geography, relying solely on textual attributes from behavior sequences, leading to recommendations that aren't reachable from the user's location. The proposed SPAR framework addresses this by incorporating spatial perception through three synergistic stages: tokenization (SI-SID) fuses geographic coordinates with semantic IDs, cognition (MG-CPT) pre-trains the LLM on geospatial data to understand urban structure, and adaptation (TV-SFT) freezes spatial knowledge to prevent forgetting during fine-tuning. Experiments on diverse datasets show SPAR effectively integrates geographic relevance, making recommendations both behaviorally plausible and physically accessible.

## Technical note

Generative POI recommenders that decode semantic IDs learn geography only as a textual attribute, so predictions are behaviorally plausible but far from the user's real-time location. SPAR injects urban spatial knowledge in three stages: Spatially-Intrinsic SIDs fuse a sinusoidal longitude-latitude embedding with the text embedding before RQ-Kmeans tokenization; Multi-Granular Geospatial CPT continually pre-trains the LLM on 25 curated geospatial datasets (attributes, pairwise distance/direction relations, city navigation); and Task-Vector Anchored SFT freezes the spatial knowledge as a parameter-space task vector to prevent forgetting during behavioral fine-tuning. On two public and four industrial datasets SPAR-8B beats the strongest generative baseline PLUM (8B) by 11.9-19.3% relative, with SI-SID adding about 10.8% and MG-CPT a further 15.4% on average and TV-SFT 2.2-17.9%. Cold-start Recall@5 gains (about 48%) far exceed warm-start gains, and recommended POIs become geographically closer (up to 27.8% cumulative distance reduction).

## Summary

This paper introduces a Diffusion Model for addressing the complex problem of slate recommendation, where multiple items are presented together and users engage with several simultaneously. Unlike traditional single-item methods that simplify this choice space, the proposed approach directly manages the combinatorial complexity by leveraging generative models like Diffusion Models to learn item structures in high-dimensional spaces. Offline evaluations demonstrate that it outperforms existing techniques by producing more relevant recommendations while also improving their diversity for applications such as music playlists and e-commerce bundles.

## Technical note

The paper addresses slate generation (recommending a coherent set of items consumed together) where the combinatorial joint-selection problem is usually simplified by assuming users pick one item at a time. DMSG is a diffusion-model framework that models the joint distribution over slates and generates coherent, diverse slates directly from natural-language prompts without explicit personalization signals, avoiding explicit combinatorial optimization. Items are represented as embeddings in a continuous space; the diffusion model is trained on concatenated item embeddings and decoded back to catalog items, with DDIM used to accelerate low-latency generation as catalogs evolve. On music-playlist and e-commerce bundle tasks it beats retrieval baselines (+12.9% average precision, +17% NDCG), and a two-week production playlist A/B test showed -13.4% duplicated content and +6.8% more active user interactions.

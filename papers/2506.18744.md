## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing long-term system changes (like recommender algorithms) using online A/B tests that are often too slow due to non-stationarity and reliance on potentially misleading short-term data. It proposes integrating fast, biased experiments run for brief periods with slower ones, employing Bayesian optimization techniques in a sequential manner. This combined approach enables rapid exploration across large action spaces while still aiming for optimal long-term outcomes by leveraging both quick online tests and offline proxies where necessary.

## Technical note

This paper tackles Bayesian optimization of internet system parameters (e.g., recommender ranking value-model weights) via online A/B tests when the target is a long-term outcome that is slow to measure due to non-stationary treatment effects. It proposes a 'fast and slow' parallel experimental design that fuses short, biased experiments and offline proxies with long-running tests, using multi-task GPs and a novel target-aware GP kernel to learn long-term outcomes from short-term proxies across time scales. Deployed at Meta, it achieved superior optimization while cutting experimentation wall time by over 60%, supported by new benchmark environments emulating dynamic A/B tests.

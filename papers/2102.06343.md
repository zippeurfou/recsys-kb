## Summary

This paper introduces the novel problem of personalized visualization recommendation, addressing the limitation of existing systems that ignore user preferences and past interactions. The authors propose a learning framework that recommends visualizations tailored to individual users by leveraging their past visualization feedback, including even visualizations created from different datasets. The effectiveness of this personalized approach is demonstrated through experiments, and the researchers also release a large user-centric visualization corpus to support further research in this area.

## Technical note

The paper introduces personalized visualization recommendation, where a separate model is learned per user to rank visualizations for a chosen dataset rather than scoring visualizations by rules alone. The core difficulty is that visualizations are tied to specific datasets, so feedback cannot be shared directly across users; the authors solve this by embedding every dataset attribute into a shared K-dimensional meta-feature space and by defining data-independent visualization-configurations captured in a user-level graph model. Both linear and deep variants are trained from implicit/explicit user feedback across tens of thousands of datasets. Against SOTA baselines they report mean gains of 29.8% HIT@5 and 64.9% NDCG@5, and release a corpus of 17.4k users, 94k datasets, and 32k user-generated visualizations.

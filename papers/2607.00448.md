## Summary

Large-scale recommendation systems using two-tower models often struggle with standard negative sampling methods producing easy negatives that don't sufficiently challenge the model.

This paper proposes an LLM-based hard negative sampling technique within clusters. By training an LLM alongside the main system to find semantically similar but distinct items, it generates more difficult examples for comparison during real-time retrieval training.

The method significantly improves recommendation effectiveness by providing challenging information and reducing inherent biases found in typical industry practices.

## Technical note

Standard in-batch and out-of-batch negative sampling for two-tower retrieval produces easy negatives and amplifies popularity bias. The paper proposes Cluster GOOBS, a self-supervised hard-negative technique that clusters items with an in-house LLM-based multimodal content-embedding model and samples negatives from the positive item's cluster, served in real time via GOOBS (Global Out-of-Batch Sampling) which stores OOB item tensors in hashed cluster segments to scale to billions of examples. On public benchmarks Cluster GOOBS beats in-batch baseline, DNS, CBNS, and even ANCE (e.g. +55.6% HR@50 on Amazon-Electronics vs +28.6% for ANCE) without a global ANN index. In industrial A/B testing it lifts CTR +53% with only -1.4% training QPS regression and cuts the top-100 items' impression share from 50% to 32%, mitigating popularity bias.

## Summary

Current two-tower recommendation training often uses easy negatives, hindering model improvement. The authors propose using an LLM-based clustering system to automatically generate harder-to-distinguish negative samples in real-time during production training, addressing this limitation efficiently even at massive scale. This technique significantly improves retrieval performance and reduces inherent biases compared to standard sampling methods.

## Technical note

Addresses the easy-negative problem in two-tower retrieval training by proposing Cluster GOOBS, a self-supervised cluster-based hard negative sampling method where negatives are drawn from the same semantic cluster as the positive, with clusters derived from an in-house LLM/multimodal content-embedding model. GOOBS (Global Out-of-Batch Sampling) is a real-time serving framework using a hashed item pool that scales to billions of training points with only -1.4% training QPS regression. On public data (MovieLens-1M, Amazon Reviews Grocery/Electronics/Home) Cluster GOOBS beats in-batch, DNS, CBNS, and ANCE baselines, with HR@50 gains from +7.2% to +55.6% (Amazon-Electronics). Online A/B on an 18M-item industrial system yields +53% CTR while cutting the top-100 items' impression share from 50% to 32%, mitigating popularity bias and feedback loops.

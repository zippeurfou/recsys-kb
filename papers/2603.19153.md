## Summary

This study demonstrates that cellular base stations can be repurposed as distributed radar sensors for rainfall measurement. By adapting techniques from weather radar, the approach retrieves rainfall products with unprecedented high spatial (meters) and temporal (seconds) resolution in urban areas, leveraging the dense BS infrastructure. While facing challenges like ground clutter contamination, the research shows that appropriate processing yields reliable rainfall estimates, offering a transformative opportunity for urban hydrometeorology by utilizing existing telecommunications infrastructure.

## Technical note

This work demonstrates, via physically-based simulation, that a dense network of cellular base stations (BS) can act as a distributed opportunistic radar for rainfall remote sensing, retrieving weather-radar products such as reflectivity factor, mean Doppler velocity, and spectral width. Unlike commercial microwave links, which infer path-integrated attenuation in a bistatic scheme, the BS approach uses a radar-like monostatic scheme enabling range-resolved inference of the precipitation field. Using a commercial 5G-Advanced BS with electronically steerable antenna arrays and wide receiver bandwidth, it achieves spatial and temporal resolution on the order of meters and tens of seconds, roughly 10x finer than conventional weather radars. The dominant challenge is ground clutter contamination, exacerbated by the near-horizontal BS antenna beams; the paper assesses clutter impact and shows that clutter-filtered radar moments reach satisfactory quality against raw observations and overlapping independent BS fields-of-view.

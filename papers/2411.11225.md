## Summary

This paper presents a model-agnostic recommendation algorithm called Popularity-Aware Meta-Learning (PAM) designed for the item cold-start problem within streaming, online data scenarios. PAM tackles this issue by splitting incoming items into different meta-learning tasks based on their predefined popularity levels. It dynamically distinguishes and reweights behavior and content features depending on the task's popularity level, while also reducing computational costs through specific adaptations compared to traditional offline methods.

## Technical note

Proposes Popularity-Aware Meta-learning (PAM), a model-agnostic framework for the item cold-start problem under streaming/online data where fine-tuning and knowledge-transfer methods are infeasible due to time and compute constraints. PAM partitions incoming data into meta-learning tasks by predefined item-popularity thresholds and reweights behavior- versus content-related features per task, enabling fine-tuning-free online inference by pre-storing per-task parameters. A cold-start enhancer adds data augmentation (constructing low-popularity samples from high-popularity interactions) and a self-supervised replay loss to counter sparse supervision. This mitigates the Matthew effect that over-recommends popular items, outperforming baselines on three public datasets and a live scenario.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of converting customers into loyal ones by developing a two-stage model. The system improves customer segmentation by distinguishing between campaign-influenced and organically engaged customers using a Self-Paced Loss approach and handling missing labels in the second stage. This intent-aware segmentation leads to more precise marketing, lower costs, and significantly improved conversion efficiency, as shown by A/B testing.

## Technical note

The paper proposes a two-stage multi-model architecture for value-proposition-aware customer segmentation and marketing-campaign targeting, aimed at nudgeable customers rather than already-engaged ones. A first-layer multi-class neural network separates campaign-influenced high-engagement, organically high-engagement, and low-engagement customers, and a second-layer binary label-correction model disambiguates prompted vs organic engagement. It addresses the missing-label problem inherent to uplift modeling (customers never exposed to campaigns) by rectifying true-state labels during training. Self-paced loss is used to improve categorization robustness under noisy/uncertain labels. The approach contrasts with RFM cohort methods and blanket dynamic-pricing systems, targeting price-sensitive segments to reduce marketing cost and protect brand equity.

## Summary

This paper introduces a hierarchical Bayesian model to improve size recommendations in online fashion retail. The model jointly analyzes the size a customer purchases and the subsequent return reason (too small, too big, or no return) using a multinomial distribution. By incorporating domain knowledge and item characteristics as priors, the approach enhances recommendations, particularly when customer data is limited.

## Technical note

The paper proposes a hierarchical Bayesian model for size recommendation in fashion e-commerce that jointly models the size a customer purchases and its return outcome (kept, returned too small, returned too big) via a multinomial distribution. Modeling the joint probability p(size, return | customer, article) rather than assuming independence (as the KDE-based baseline does) lets it capture sizing signal from returns. A hierarchy of priors over article and customer parameters injects domain expertise and mitigates severe data sparsity, and size is treated as a continuous variable during training. Experiments on real anonymized data from millions of customers show improved recommendations, especially under limited data, within a large-scale production setting.

## Summary

1.  The paper presents a method for creating large-scale training data (over 240 million examples) for e-commerce dense retrieval models, using signals from multiple production search systems instead of click data or manual annotation to overcome their limitations.
2.  This approach leverages disagreements between different heterogeneous retrieval models during the mining process, identifying structured examples like easy positives, hard positives, and negatives that fool specific systems (e.g., lexical ones).
3.  The resulting training system significantly outperformed the existing click-trained baseline on key metrics, showing substantial improvement especially for rare queries, lower error rates, and confirmed positive business impact through online A/B testing with higher ad performance.

## Technical note

This Walmart system generates 240M+ dense-retrieval training examples for sponsored search without click signals or manual labels by mining disagreement among three production retrievers (dictionary/BM25/ANN, only 13-15% top-500 overlap) into five difficulty levels (easy/hard positives, hard/semi-hard/random negatives). A calibrated three-model cascade (184M cross-encoder -> LoRA 2B LLM -> LoRA 8B LLM) with per-class isotonic calibration produces 5-class graded labels at 89.1% human agreement while halving compute; this is distant supervision (cached hard labels), not knowledge distillation. A two-tower BERT student (shared 6-layer, 384-dim) is trained through a three-stage curriculum (BCE -> Multiple Negatives Ranking -> Triplet), each loss matched to sample difficulty, yielding +9.5% NDCG@10 over single-stage. Against 30K third-party human-labeled queries, it gains +5.1% NDCG@10 (largest on tail, +6.8%), cuts embarrassing results 8.7%->3.5%, and an online A/B test shows +2.80% ad spend, +1.4% CTR, +2.8% eCPM.

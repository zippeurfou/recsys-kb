## Summary

1.  Current Local-Life Service Recommendation (LLSR) systems using Semantic IDs (SIDs) suffer from two main problems: semantic entanglement leading to information loss and "SID collisions" where different items map to similar or same IDs.
2.  The proposed LGRID method tackles these issues by introducing a four-stage process (Encode -> Disentangle -> Align -> Quantize) that explicitly disentangles geographic and semantic factors using an LLM's guidance, preserving attribute-specific meanings during generation.
3.  Experiments show LGRID significantly outperforms existing SID baselines on recommendation quality, achieves high accuracy (>99%) in decoding coarse location attributes from SIDs, and drastically reduces collisions compared to standard methods.

## Technical note

Local-life (LBS) recommendation needs item Semantic IDs that respect geographic hierarchy, but the prevailing single-representation-then-quantization pipeline (RQ-VAE, LGSID) entangles geography with brand and category, yielding 97-98% full-SID collision rates and codes with no attribute-level meaning. LGRID replaces it with an encode -> disentangle -> align -> quantize pipeline: Qwen3-8B (LoRA on all linear layers) jointly encodes POI text; a Structured Disentangled Block routes the hidden states into 8 attribute-aligned slots via Semantic Anchor Injection (queries initialized from attribute prompt tokens instead of random Q-Former queries), Anchor-Guided cross-attention, and Structured Causal Routing whose mask enforces intra-group autoregression over the province/city/district/town chain and inter-group independence; Synergistic Alignment Learning then trains slots with a coarse-to-fine generative decoding loss, a Structure-Aware InfoNCE loss over three hierarchical hard-negative sets (spatial specificity, semantic independence, cross-region), and a Gram-matrix orthogonality regularizer; Dual-Stream Residual Quantization discretizes geographic and semantic slots in separate branches (D=2 levels each, giving a 4-token SID that is summed and concatenated with the item-ID embedding). Across 10 backbones (GRU4Rec, DIN, SASRec, BERT4Rec, DIEN, SIM, ETA, TWIN, HSTU, RankMixer) on an industrial Kuaishou dataset and Foursquare, LGRID wins in every cell, up to +5.44% relative AUC over the best SID baseline. It decodes coarse geographic attributes at over 99% accuracy (Town 92.34%, Brand 86.62%), cuts full-SID collision from 97.0% (LGSID) to 39.9% while raising cumulative code utilization U(1:2) to 100.0 and U(1:3) to 77.3, lifts district-level clustering NMI from 0.0006 to 0.9346, and confines extra cost to offline SID construction (21.80 vs 13.97 ms/item) leaving serving at 1.04x.

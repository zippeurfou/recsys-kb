## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Compact dual-encoder models are widely used for retrieval owing to their efficiency and scalability. However, such models often underperform compared to their Large Language Model (LLM)-based retrieval counterparts, likely due to their limited world knowledge. While LLM-based data augmentation has been proposed as a strategy to bridge this performance gap, there is insufficient understanding of its effectiveness and scalability to real-world retrieval problems. ## Technical note

This Microsoft study systematically evaluates LLM-based data augmentation for compact dual-encoder retrievers across ~100 configurations (four retrievers, ten augmentation scales/densities, two augmentation models). Key findings: augmentation benefits saturate beyond a certain scale/density even with diverse strategies; off-the-shelf 7B/8B LLMs match 70B models for Doc2Query-style augmentation; and gains are largest for weakly-pretrained retrievers (BERT/DistilBERT) while strongly pretrained ones (E5, Contriever) benefit little. Results, on BEIR (Recall@100), give actionable guidance for cost-effective augmentation.

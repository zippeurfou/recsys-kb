## Summary

SessionRec introduces a novel approach called Next-Session Prediction (NSPP) for generative sequential recommendation, contrasting with traditional item-by-item prediction methods that don't align well with real-world session-based user behavior. The framework learns dense session representations efficiently and implicitly models negative interactions by predicting multiple items in the next session, leading to better capture of diverse user interests. Experiments confirm SessionRec's effectiveness and efficiency, establishing a promising paradigm for large-scale generative recommendation systems due to its model-agnostic architecture and adherence to scaling laws similar to LLMs.

## Technical note

SessionRec argues that item-by-item next-item prediction (NIPP) in generative sequential recommenders misaligns with real systems where users interact at the session/request level. It introduces a Next Session Prediction Paradigm (NSPP) with hierarchical intra/inter-session encoding (pooling then an HSTU/Transformer sequence encoder), which cuts attention cost by an ~M^2 factor and lets the model predict multiple positives per session. A retrieval (sampled cross-entropy) loss is combined with a weighted intra-session rank loss over negatively-interacted items to add ranking ability. It beats HSTU by ~27% on public datasets, exhibits LLM-like power-law scaling, and lifted Pay PV +0.603% in a Meituan A/B test.

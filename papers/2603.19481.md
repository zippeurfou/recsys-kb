## Summary

The paper introduces NA-VQA, a new benchmark for long-form video question answering designed to evaluate deep narrative reasoning across entire movies, including tracking intentions, connecting distant events, and reconstructing causal chains, which existing benchmarks often miss. To tackle this challenge, the authors propose Video-NaRA, a narrative-centric framework that builds structured event chains and uses memory retrieval to improve long-range reasoning. Experiments show that current state-of-the-art multimodal models struggle significantly with far-range evidence, and Video-NaRA improves performance by effectively modeling complex narratives.

## Technical note

This paper introduces NA-VQA, a benchmark for narrative reasoning in long-form video question answering that tests tracking intentions, connecting distant events, and reconstructing causal chains across entire movies, going beyond localized-cue, multiple-choice benchmarks. NA-VQA contains 88 full movies (~2 hr each) and 4.4K open-ended QA pairs across seven reasoning types (Causal, Narrative, Character-centric, Thematic, Goal-based, Social, Hypothetical), with each question grounded in multiple evidence spans labeled Short/Medium/Far by temporal distance to probe long-range dependencies, and generative multi-scene answers to prevent exploiting answer priors. Evaluations show state-of-the-art MLLMs systematically fail on questions requiring Far evidence, failing to establish causal links between temporally distant events. The proposed Video-NaRA framework generates per-clip descriptions with Qwen-VL 2.5 (7B), groups clips into narrative slots to build a structured narrative memory, and retrieves query-relevant clips for a fine-tuned reasoner, improving long-range reasoning by up to 3%.

## Summary

This paper introduces a reranking framework for long context processing that uses attention scores from large language models' retrieval layers to estimate passage-query relevance, providing a listwise solution. The approach leverages holistic information from the entire candidate list and generates continuous scores, allowing training on diverse datasets without specific supervision. Experiments show it outperforms existing methods, achieves state-of-the-art results on dialogue benchmarks, and demonstrates flexibility for further improvements.

## Technical note

QRRanker is a listwise reranker that derives passage relevance directly from the attention scores of a small set of query-focused retrieval (QR) heads inside an LLM, rather than generating Likert-scale ratings. QR heads are selected by their attention mass on gold chunks, then fine-tuned with a group contrastive loss over top-50 candidates using max-min score normalization for stability. Because it only prefills the query-document prompt and reads attention, it produces continuous scores in one pass, avoids generation-format errors, and trains on arbitrary retrieval datasets. It supports memory-aware summary prefixes and mid-layer head training for lower latency, achieving SOTA on LoCoMo and narrative/multi-hop QA with a 3B backbone.

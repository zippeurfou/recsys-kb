## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Large language models have become a powerful method for feature augmentation in recommendation systems. However, existing approaches relying on quick inference often suffer from incomplete feature coverage and insufficient specificity in feature descriptions, limiting their ability to capture fine-grained user preferences and undermining overall performance. ## Technical note

This work applies inference-computation scaling to LLM feature augmentation for recommendation, showing that extended Chain-of-Thought reasoning generates more numerous, specific user/item features and yields a 12% NDCG@10 improvement. It attributes gains to feature quantity and specificity, comparing gpt-4o-mini against reasoning models o1-mini/o3-mini, and finds model choice and search strategy (over Beam Search/MCTS) most affect the richness of generated features. It is the first work bridging test-time reasoning scaling to feature augmentation in recommender systems.

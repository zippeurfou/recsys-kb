## Summary

Personalized recommender systems face a challenge between providing high-quality, data-driven recommendations and adapting quickly to user changes. This paper addresses this by introducing an incremental learning approach using sequential Bayesian updates and quadratic approximation for large-scale models. The method significantly reduces training time while preserving model accuracy, making it suitable for industrial deployment, as demonstrated by its implementation at LinkedIn.

## Technical note

The paper presents an incremental learning method for large-scale personalized recommenders that keeps training fast while avoiding catastrophic forgetting. It formulates model updates as a sequential Bayesian update where the previous posterior, approximated as a Gaussian via Laplace approximation, becomes an informative prior (a quadratic regularizer around prior weights) so training on only new data still remembers past knowledge. The focus is LinkedIn's Generalized Linear Mixed effects (GLMix) personalized logistic regression, with different Hessian/covariance approximation levels trading off storage cost against quality, and an extension to deep video recommendation. Offline and online experiments show substantially shorter training time at maintained accuracy, and the solution is deployed at LinkedIn.

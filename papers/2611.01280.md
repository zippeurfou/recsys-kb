## Summary

1.  Accurately predicting post-click Conversion Rate (CVR) is crucial for effective ad ranking, but traditional models are limited by sparse or missing conversion labels during training.
2.  This paper presents a case study demonstrating significant performance gains for ad ranking models by incorporating diverse auxiliary signals (like user behavior and metadata) beyond just conversion events.
3.  It introduces and evaluates three specific architectural and modeling innovations—linearized labels, a split architecture, and the Label Combination (LaCo) approach—designed to effectively leverage these auxiliary data sources despite their potential noise.

## Technical note

Meta's post-click pCVR ads models face sparse and missing conversion labels (about 50% of training clicks have no conversion label), so the paper adds auxiliary heads on a shared-bottom deep sparse multi-task network fed by behavioral (pre-click view time, add-to-cart pixel events, browser dwell time), semantic (session category matches the ad, confirmation keywords in URLs), derived (proxy labels distilled from models trained with privileged post-impression features) and synthetic cross-domain signals, none of which are used at inference. Techniques: linearized labels that bucket continuous or funnel-ordered signals into 2-4 quantile classes for multi-class auxiliary tasks, with the true class being the first positive label in a predictiveness-ordered list; gating so an auxiliary task trains only on rows where its label exists; a split architecture that decomposes 7-day pCVR into mutually exclusive 0-1 day and 1-7 day sub-tasks whose sum is ensembled with the main head; and Label Combination (LaCo), an exhaustive boolean search (AND, OR, NOT, XOR over at most 10 signals) for the derived label maximizing F-beta against the main conversion label, where beta = 0.02 (about 91% precision, moderate recall) was best and beta >= 1 consistently hurt. On the production pCVR model (baseline NE 0.54, billions of daily examples, daily refresh) linearized labels, split architecture, LaCo and noisy-label training (DivideMix, Tri-Training) gave -0.15%, -0.13%, -0.25% and -0.10% NE respectively, and the bundle shipped with a statistically significant online gain in advertiser conversion volume.

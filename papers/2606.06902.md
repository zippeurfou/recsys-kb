## Summary

This paper introduces **TALAN**, a method designed to specifically enhance reasoning and STEM/code capabilities in large language models during post-training, while preserving existing general skills. It combines a sequence-conditioned latent side path for targeted adaptation with standard low-rank adapters like LoRA/DoRA, achieved through co-training within one supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loop.

TALAN demonstrates significant effectiveness improvements over baseline methods (LoRA and DoRA), achieving cross-model mean gains of +1.41 and +1.85 points respectively on specific tasks across various models. These results are consistent across most model-benchmark combinations, suggesting positive average effects from this targeted approach.

The method is highly efficient, requiring less than 1% trainable parameters and adding minimal inference overhead (less than 2%) compared to the baseline adapters it works with. Furthermore, a transfer probe study indicates that TALAN can adapt effectively even beyond its original model family (Qwen), confirming the potential for generalizable task-adaptation benefits using this approach.

## Technical note

TALAN (Task-Aligned Latent Adaptation Networks) is a sequence-conditioned latent side path inserted into a transformer's residual stream and co-trained with a low-rank adapter in a single SFT loop for targeted post-training of reasoning/STEM/code skills without regressing general behavior. The block summarizes the active sequence into a compact latent memory, remixes it into token-level perturbations, and writes them back via a controlled residual update; adapting the fixed block to a host selects a six-axis configuration (insertion location, memory size, mixer, writeback rule, trainability scope, gradient scale). Across four Qwen3 backbones and four benchmarks, TALAN gains +1.41 pts over LoRA and +1.85 over DoRA on average with <1% extra trainable parameters and ~1.01-1.02x inference overhead. Internal-state analysis shows the TALAN perturbation is 80-1,700x smaller in norm than the adapter update and near-orthogonal to it, yet amplifies through depth, acting as a complementary activation-level intervention.

## Summary

This paper introduces a novel visual representation method that uses a frozen generative model, augmented with low-rank adaptation layers. Instead of external representations, the model encodes visual signals implicitly by learning to reconstruct them from these adaptations. This approach allows for compact storage via hashing and enables strong video compression at low bitrates, effectively bridging the gap between visual generation and compression.

## Technical note

The paper represents a visual signal implicitly as a function defined by low-rank adaptation (LoRA) layers attached to a frozen large-scale diffusion generative model, treating compression as adaptation. Optimizing LoRA to reconstruct specific content (e.g., an 81-frame video) yields an implicit representation that leverages the pretrained model's visual-knowledge prior and emphasizes high-level semantics. The induced adaptation parameters are then hashed/mapped into a single compact vector, enabling strong perceptual video compression at extremely low bitrates on HEVC and UVG benchmarks. Because the representation is functional, it supports inference-time control and inference-time scaling to refine reconstruction fidelity. The authors argue this bridges visual compression and generation, and that the adaptations can act as reusable visual memories during generation.

## Summary

Traditional app recommender systems often fail for multi-category apps due to overlapping semantics being poorly captured by standard taxonomies. The proposed PCR-CA framework (Parallel Codebook Representations with Contrastive Alignment) addresses this by learning independent, discrete semantic representations across multiple parallel codebooks and fusing them with collaborative data using a dual-attention mechanism and contrastive loss at user/item levels. Experiments demonstrate that PCR-CA significantly improves recommendation effectiveness, particularly for long-tail apps, achieving notable AUC gains and validating its success through online deployment on the Microsoft Store.

## Technical note

PCR-CA improves app-store CTR prediction for multi-category apps and long-tail items by learning discrete semantic codes over multiple parallel codebooks (a Parallel Codebook VQ-AE), unlike hierarchical residual RQ-VAE, so distinct aspects like gameplay and art style are encoded independently. A user- and item-level InfoNCE contrastive alignment bridges the semantic code space and ID embedding space, and a dual-attention fusion averages ID-based and semantic-based target attention over user history. On 340M Microsoft Store interactions it gains +0.76% overall AUC and +2.15% AUC on long-tail apps over strong baselines including SaviorRec. Online A/B testing shows +10.52% CTR and +16.30% CVR, and it is deployed in production.

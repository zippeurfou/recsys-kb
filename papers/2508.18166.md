## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Modern app store recommender systems struggle with multiple-category apps, as traditional taxonomies fail to capture overlapping semantics, leading to suboptimal personalization. We propose PCR-CA (Parallel Codebook Representations with Contrastive Alignment), an end-to-end framework for improved CTR prediction. ## Technical note

PCR-CA targets multiple-category app-store CTR where tree taxonomies miss overlapping semantics and head-tail imbalance hurts long-tail items. It extracts multimodal text embeddings, then a Parallel Codebook VQ-AE learns discrete semantic codes across multiple codebooks in parallel (unlike hierarchical RQ-VAE) to encode independent aspects, uses user- and item-level contrastive alignment to bridge semantic and collaborative signals, and a dual-attention fusion over ID and semantic features. On a large dataset it gains +0.76% AUC overall and +2.15% AUC for long-tail apps; Microsoft Store A/B testing shows +10.52% CTR and +16.30% CVR.

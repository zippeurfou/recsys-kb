## Summary

This paper highlights that user behavior sequences in CTR prediction traditionally only use positive signals (clicks etc.), despite the abundance of potentially informative implicit negative behaviors like skips. It proposes incorporating both positive and negative interactions into fixed-length sequential models through a mixed-polarity approach, which significantly outperforms using just positive data across various architectures with minimal extra cost. The authors introduce Target-Aware Polarity Fusion to better differentiate these combined signals and find that even simpler polarity bias methods capture most of the improvement from this new data paradigm.

To summarize in three sentences:

1.  Current sequential user modeling primarily relies on positive interactions (clicks, purchases), while ignoring more common implicit negative behaviors.
2.  This paper demonstrates that by using sequences containing both positive and negative signals, CTR prediction models achieve significantly better performance (+1.9% to +9.6% AUC improvement).
3.  A simple polarity bias method captures most of this gain, emphasizing the core contribution as establishing the value of mixed-polarity data in sequential modeling.

## Technical note

This work argues the overlooked question in CTR sequence modeling is what goes into the behavior sequence, not just how to encode it. It shows that mixed-polarity sequences -- chronologically interleaving abundant implicit negatives (skips, low watch-completion, scroll-past) with positives within a fixed length budget -- consistently outperform positive-only sequences across two-stage and unified-block architectures with negligible extra compute, framing this as primarily a data-level contribution. It identifies a semantic indistinguishability problem when positive and negative tokens share an embedding space and proposes Target-Aware Polarity Fusion (TAPF), a lightweight target-conditioned gating mechanism, though a simple polarity-bias baseline captures most of the gains. Analyses show mixed-polarity models spontaneously learn polarity-discriminative attention and deliver gains for low-activity users and cold-start items; reported +4.2-5.5% relative AUC on KuaiRec.

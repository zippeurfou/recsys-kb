## Summary

This paper introduces a system for recommending audiobooks using "descriptive shelves." It leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance item metadata and identify user affinities. The approach creates personalized topic-based lists that offer diverse recommendations while guiding users towards content aligned with their interests, improving engagement through A/B testing.

## Technical note

Spotify's newly added audiobooks lack the user reviews/tags used for content-based explanations, a cold-start problem. The pipeline prompts an LLM to extract a taxonomy of grounded descriptors (genres, themes, moods, tropes, settings, etc.) per audiobook from its metadata, then for each user ranks and diversifies candidate descriptors (from a two-tower recommender's list) into personalized 'descriptive shelves' whose titles contextualize the recommendations, filtering/ranking items per shelf. A first home-surface A/B test raised discovery but hurt engagement for cold-start users; a redesigned second test on the audiobook subfeed beat editor-curated shelves on both engagement (i2c +35%, i2s +87%) and discovery (impressed +627%, interacted +805%).

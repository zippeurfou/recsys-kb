## Summary

This paper introduces **Semantic Recall**, a new metric for evaluating approximate nearest neighbor search algorithms. It specifically measures the retrieval of *only* semantically relevant objects that could theoretically be found via exact search, unlike traditional recall which penalizes retrieval of any irrelevant items, even if they are close. The authors argue this metric is more appropriate for queries with few relevant results, which is common, and propose **Tolerant Recall** as a proxy when semantic relevance can't be directly assessed, showing these metrics better predict retrieval quality and can improve algorithm optimization.

## Technical note

This paper argues traditional recall mis-measures ANNS quality because exact-NNS ground truth reflects mathematical proximity, penalizing algorithms for missing near-equidistant but semantically irrelevant neighbors. Semantic recall restricts the denominator to semantic neighbors (a subset of top-k ground truth judged relevant by an LLM such as Gemini 2.5, cross-validated with Claude Haiku 4.5 at 91% agreement, kappa 0.82). Tolerant recall is a threshold-based proxy usable without raw data: a retrieved result may substitute a true neighbor if its score is within an x% tolerance, robust to quantization-induced reordering of irrelevant items. On MSMARCO (8.83M docs, Gemini embeddings, ScaNN) semantic recall reaches 0.903 vs 0.762 traditional recall for few-relevant queries. Tuning ScaNN/FAISS for tolerant or semantic recall (via Vizier) cuts search cost 5-35% at fixed quality; validated on MSMARCO and Thai MIRACL.

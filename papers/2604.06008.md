## Summary

This paper addresses the lack of trusted menstrual health resources in Pakistan due to cultural taboos, by designing a WhatsApp chatbot powered by an LLM and RAG, co-created with local women. Workshops identified key requirements like language support and expert knowledge bases, leading to deployment where users utilized the chatbot to challenge stigma and build knowledge, though issues of trust, validation, and the AI's persona emerged. The study provides empirical insights and proposes a stigma-aware design framework for culturally sensitive conversational AI in intimate health domains.

## Technical note

This HCI study designs and deploys a WhatsApp menstrual-health chatbot for Pakistani college women, combining an OpenAI GPT-4o Assistant with RAG grounded in a gynecologist-curated, locally relevant Q&A knowledge base to minimize hallucination and cultural misalignment. A three-phase method (co-design workshops with N=30, a 7-participant pilot, a two-week deployment with 13 participants / 403 messages plus interviews) surfaced requirements: Roman-Urdu support, reliance on subsidized 'social data' packages (motivating WhatsApp), and concise mobile-first responses (<=120 words). An SVM language classifier handles English/Urdu mixing to keep responses language-consistent. Findings show users challenged taboos and legitimized 'normal' suffering but cross-validated advice with Google/people, and chatbot gender perception intersected with purdah beliefs; the paper contributes a stigma-aware design framework for culturally sensitive conversational AI.

## Summary

This paper investigates using LLMs for retrieval-augmented question answering, finding that while longer contexts provide more information, they also introduce irrelevant data that harms performance. To address this, the authors propose an adaptive prompting strategy that splits retrieved information into smaller chunks, allowing control over the amount of potentially irrelevant content. Their experiments show this chunking strategy improves performance and uses fewer tokens, highlighting a major issue: LLMs often generate incorrect answers instead of declining when information is insufficient, suggesting a need for better mechanisms to handle uncertainty.

## Technical note

This paper studies retrieval-augmented question answering and shows that longer LLM contexts add irrelevant distractors that degrade generation. It proposes an adaptive divide-and-conquer prompting strategy that splits retrieved pages into smaller windows and prompts the LLM sequentially in descending retrieval-score order, answering and terminating when a window suffices or signaling insufficiency and iterating; page-level BM25 retrieval avoids passage-segmentation loss. On three open-domain QA datasets the strategy matches or beats full-context standard prompting while cutting token usage by over 50%. A key finding is that LLMs frequently force incorrect answers from irrelevant passages rather than declining, so feeding high-probability windows first is critical to prevent hallucinations.

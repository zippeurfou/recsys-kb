## Summary

Researchers introduced LocalSearchBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for agentic search specifically in the complex domain of local life services, addressing unique real-world challenges often involving ambiguous and multi-hop queries. This benchmark comprises over 150,000 high-quality data entries and 300 challenging multi-hop QA tasks, along with a tool integration environment called LocalPlayground. Experiments revealed that even state-of-the-art large reasoning models struggle significantly on the benchmark, achieving low accuracy and high errors in completeness and faithfulness, highlighting the need for specialized domain training and evaluation.

## Technical note

The paper introduces LocalSearchBench, the first benchmark for agentic search in local life services, where real user queries are ambiguous and require multi-hop reasoning across merchants and products. It builds a 1.3M-entry anonymized merchant database over 6 service categories and 9 cities plus 900 multi-hop QA tasks synthesized via a multi-agent pipeline, and provides LocalPlayground, a tool-interaction environment integrating a LocalRAG retriever and web search. Experiments show state-of-the-art large reasoning models struggle: the best model (DeepSeek-V3.2) reaches only 35.60% correctness with weak completeness (60.32%) and faithfulness (30.72%), motivating domain-specific agent training.

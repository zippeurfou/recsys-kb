## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of predicting low conversion rates (CVR) in the pre-promotion phase of e-commerce sales, where user behavior shifts towards delayed conversions due to anticipation of discounts. To tackle this, the authors propose the Counterfactual Multi-task Delayed Conversion Model (CM-DCM), which jointly predicts both direct and delayed conversions using historical pre-promotion data. CM-DCM incorporates a personalized gating module for data sparsity and a counterfactual approach to model the transition from add-to-cart to delayed conversion, significantly improving prediction accuracy and demonstrating positive business impact through online A/B tests.

## Technical note

CM-DCM models delayed conversion rate in the e-commerce pre-promotion phase, where CVR drops sharply yet ~45% of promotion-day orders originate from pre-promotion clicks, creating an OOD shift and a skewed single-day delay distribution that violate standard continuous-window delayed feedback assumptions. It uses a multi-task architecture: a frozen pre-trained daily CVR/ATC tower (via stop-gradient) plus a Delay CVR tower predicting residual delayed-conversion probability, summed for all-conversion prediction, trained with joint BCE losses. A PEPNet-inspired personalized gated transfer modulates pre-trained hidden layers by real-time user behavior (ATC/pay sequences) to combat data sparsity. A counterfactual causal regularizer based on a Doubly Robust estimator (outcome regression plus IPW correction, propensity from a pretrained ATC model) enforces the causal effect of add-to-cart on delayed conversion. Online A/B tests during major events show significant ad-revenue, delayed-conversion GMV, and overall GMV gains.

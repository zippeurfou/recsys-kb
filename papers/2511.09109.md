## Summary

1.  Existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems often struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning due to insufficient supervision of intermediate steps, leading to potential hallucinations and reward hacking.
2.  The paper introduces Bi-RAR, a novel framework that evaluates each reasoning step both forward (distance to the answer) and backward (information completeness relative to the question), using an approximation of Kolmogorov complexity.
3.  Bi-RAR optimizes reasoning using multi-objective reinforcement learning with a cascading reward structure focused on early alignment, effectively improving interaction with search engines and outperforming previous methods on multiple benchmarks.

## Technical note

Targets multi-step reasoning in RAG, where outcome-only reward supervision (as in Search-R1) gives no guidance for intermediate steps and induces reward hacking and long hallucination-prone chains. Bi-RAR scores each reasoning step jointly forward and backward using a bidirectional information distance grounded in Kolmogorov complexity, approximated by LM generation probabilities, measuring both distance-to-answer and how well the step addresses the question. It optimizes reasoning with a multi-objective RL framework using a cascading reward that emphasizes early-trajectory alignment. Across seven QA benchmarks Bi-RAR outperforms prior methods while enabling more efficient search interaction during training and inference.

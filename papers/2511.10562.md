## Summary

Existing satellite-based precipitation estimates often rely on limited data and struggle with accuracy, particularly in regions like the Global South with sparse ground observations. This paper introduces Oya, a deep learning algorithm using visible and infrared data from geostationary satellites to overcome these limitations. Oya employs a two-stage U-Net approach trained on high-resolution ground truth data, demonstrating superior performance for improved global precipitation monitoring and forecasting.

## Technical note

Oya is a real-time satellite precipitation retrieval algorithm for regions (especially the Global South) with sparse ground radar/gauge networks, using the full visible-infrared spectrum from geostationary satellites rather than the longwave IR channel alone. It employs a two-stage deep learning approach combining two U-Net models—one for precipitation detection and one for quantitative precipitation estimation—to handle the strong rain/no-rain data imbalance. Models are trained on high-resolution GPM CORRA v07 as ground truth and pre-trained on IMERG-Final retrievals to improve robustness and reduce overfitting from CORRA's limited temporal sampling. Leveraging multiple GEO satellites it achieves quasi-global coverage and outperforms competitive regional and global precipitation baselines. (Domain: meteorology, not recommender systems.)

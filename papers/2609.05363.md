## Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently recommending higher-quality product upgrades ("trade-ups") from massive datasets. It proposes a two-level framework: Level 1 uses a large language model (LLM) "teacher" to generate structured labels and rationales, training a compact, non-generative "student" model (an embedding-pair classifier) that operates solely on precomputed product embeddings. Level 2 further improves the student's performance by adapting it with product-type-specific criteria using test-time training, significantly boosting accuracy metrics like AUC and average precision. The key benefit is a dramatic 5000x speedup and 10,000x reduction in estimated cost compared to direct LLM inference, while still achieving high performance.

## Technical note

Amazon tackles trade-up recommendation, a directional relation where a candidate must preserve the base product's shopping mission while adding a genuine benefit (formulation, certification, materials, brand positioning) rather than a pack-size or flavor variation, and must be scored over hundreds of millions of catalog pairs where direct LLM inference would cost millions of dollars and delay availability by weeks. Level 1 is rationale-guided representation distillation: a black-box frontier LLM teacher, prompted with product-type trade-up criteria and five dynamically retrieved expert demonstrations, emits a 4-class relation label (same tier, b is trade-up of c, c is trade-up of b, incompatible) plus a natural-language rationale for 1,019,241 silver pairs built from 12,830 Amazon-Walmart products across 29 product types. The student is a non-generative pair classifier over two precomputed 768-d embeddings, with separate base, candidate and ordered base-to-candidate branches concatenated into a 3d pair vector and mapped by an MLP head, trained with weighted cross-entropy (or weighted focal BCE in binary mode) plus an L2 rationale-alignment loss to a training-only projection into the encoded-rationale space and a contrastive term combining InfoNCE against in-batch rationale embeddings with a relational KL similarity transfer; the projection and auxiliary losses are discarded at inference. On a pair-disjoint human-annotated golden benchmark of 8,352 pairs, the shallow 15.5M-parameter 4-class reasoning-distilled student reaches AUC 0.924 (95% CI [0.918, 0.929]) versus 0.912 for the same-architecture label-only 4-class model and 0.887 for a 64.7M deep label-only model; rationale supervision adds nothing under collapsed binary labels (0.911 vs 0.911), so the benefit requires retaining the teacher's fine-grained taxonomy (paired delta AUC +0.013, CI [+0.010, +0.016]). Level 2, Product-Type Test-Time Training, fits LoRA adapters over the frozen student's classification head and reasoning projection once per product type on K expert-labeled support examples, lifting AUC from 0.924 to 0.941 and AP from 0.920 to 0.940 at K=32, recall-driven and plateauing between K=16 and K=32. A matched-supervision control confirms the gain is category-specific rather than just expert-label exposure: a pooled category-agnostic LoRA on the same 928 expert examples reaches only 0.929, and macro within-product-type AUC rises 0.910 to 0.925 for PT-TTT versus 0.914 pooled; reasoning-guided and label-only adaptation are near-identical at K=32 (0.941 vs 0.940), so rationales matter at Level 1 and expert labels at Level 2. On a 100K-pair proxy catalog the distilled student on one eight-GPU machine is roughly 5,000x faster and 10,000x cheaper than direct LLM inference, and its F1 of 0.843 exceeds the retrieval-augmented teacher's 0.749 chiefly by recovering recall (0.858 vs 0.610). Caveats: offline only with no online A/B or production metrics, 29 product types with no unseen-category transfer evidence, a split that is pair-disjoint but not product- or brand-disjoint, golden-set class prevalence differing from production candidate streams, and per-type gradient descent required at adaptation time.

## Summary

Orthogonal PEFT methods often mix subspace selection and transformation, leading to inefficiencies. LOFT provides a unified framework separating these components, viewing orthogonal adaptation as subspace rotation and introducing task-aware support selection as a core design choice. By selecting adaptation supports based on the downstream task's needs, LOFT improves the efficiency-performance trade-off compared to principal-subspace orthogonal adaptation, across various tasks and parameter budgets.

## Technical note

LOFT is a low-rank orthogonal parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework that explicitly separates two conflated design choices in orthogonal PEFT: the adaptation support (where adaptation acts) and the in-subspace transform (how it acts), via the update W(I + P^T(T - I)P) with a row-orthonormal support P and an r-by-r transform T. This unified subspace-rotation view recovers coordinate-, butterfly-, Householder-, and principal-subspace orthogonal PEFT variants as special cases and exposes support selection as a first-class design axis rather than a byproduct of parameterization. A first-order analysis shows the local training signal of a right-subspace orthogonal update is governed by skew(W0^T G), motivating two task-aware gradient-informed supports, GradSVD and SkewGrad, computed at the pretrained weights. Under matched parameter, memory, and compute budgets across language understanding, visual transfer, math reasoning, and multilingual OOD adaptation, gradient-informed supports improve the efficiency-performance trade-off over principal-subspace baselines.

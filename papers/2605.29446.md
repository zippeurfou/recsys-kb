## Summary

This paper introduces CrystalXRD-Bench, a new benchmark designed to test vision-language models' (VLMs) ability to identify Miller indices (HKLs) from powder XRD patterns by recovering the full set contributing to the highest-intensity peak.

The benchmark consists of 250 samples derived from public crystallographic databases, each pairing an XRD image with the corresponding CIF text and chemical formula to allow analysis of both visual extraction and reasoning errors.

Evaluating seven VLMs revealed poor performance, with the best Jaccard score being 0.5888 (GPT-5.4) and an exact-match rate of 37.6%, while six models scored below Jaccard 0.50, indicating the task is far from being solved and identifying specific failure modes.

## Technical note

CrystalXRD-Bench is a 250-sample VLM benchmark that isolates sub-degree scientific chart reading plus multi-step crystallographic reasoning by requiring recovery of the full set of Miller indices (HKLs) contributing to the highest-intensity peak of a powder XRD pattern. Each sample pairs a rendered XRD image (generated from CIF via pymatgen with Cu Kalpha and pseudo-Voigt broadening) with the source CIF text and chemical formula, so visual-extraction and reasoning errors are separable and answers are deterministically derivable. Samples span 10 crystallographic databases (MOFs, organic crystals, high-entropy alloys, inorganics) selected by KMeans for coverage, with union-based ground truth (sizes 1 to 6+) and set-based metrics (Jaccard, Precision, Recall, F1) plus over-prediction penalties. Across seven frontier VLMs the best Jaccard is 0.5888 (GPT-5.4, 37.6% exact match) with six below 0.50; double-peak cases are brittle and CIF access does not close the reasoning gap.

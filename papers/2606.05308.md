## Summary

This paper introduces PRECISE, a framework that combines small sets of human labels with large Language Model (LLM) judged sets to produce statistically reliable and bias-corrected estimates for ranking evaluation metrics. It builds upon Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI), demonstrating its provable lack of bias even if the LLM makes consistent errors. PRECISE significantly reduces computation time by applying PPI specifically to hierarchical metrics like Precision@K, enabling faster metric estimation with accuracy comparable to human evaluations and showing tangible benefits in real-world systems.

## Technical note

PRECISE extends Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI++) to produce provably unbiased estimates of ranking metrics by combining a small human-labeled gold set with a large LLM-judged set, where the gold set measures and subtracts the judge's systematic bias. For hierarchical metrics like Precision@K, where annotations are per-document but the metric is per-query, they reformulate the output space from O(2^|C|) to O(2^K) by noting Precision@K depends only on the top-K results and assuming conditional independence across documents. On ESCI, augmenting 30 human labels with Claude 3 Sonnet reduces Precision@4 standard error from 4.45 to 3.50 (21% relative), with Haiku achieving lower bias at 12x lower cost. In production, 100 human labels plus 8,400 LLM judgments correctly ranked three variants, confirmed by A/B testing (+407 bps daily sales).

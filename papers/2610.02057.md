## Summary

This paper addresses the significant inefficiency caused by lifecycle overhead (like job restarts, initialization, and recovery) in large-scale recommendation system training across thousands of GPUs. The authors introduced Effective Training Time (ETT%) as a framework to measure and pinpoint these wasted cycles within the training stack. By implementing various optimizations guided by ETT%, such as communication elimination and asynchronous checkpointing, they achieved substantial improvements, boosting average fleet efficiency by 15.5% and increasing it from 80% to over 90%.

## Technical note

Meta found that its largest ads recommendation training jobs (thousands of H100s, tens of billions of examples per day) spent only 50 to 60% of wall time training on new data, because short, freshness-driven jobs restart often and every restart repays cold-start costs that MFU ignores and Goodput does not localize; in one 151.5-minute job, PT2 compilation took 30 minutes and trainer initialization 15. The paper defines Effective Training Time, ETT% = 1 - (TTS + NoF x TTR) / wall time, and splits lost time into six components (scheduling, trainer init, PT2 compile, effective training, wasted training, shutdown), each owned by one infrastructure team. Startup fixes cut time-to-start from 31.8 to 18.6 minutes, a 41% reduction: TorchRec now builds sharded-tensor metadata locally from the broadcast sharding plan instead of running one all_gather per embedding table (sharding 110 s to 24 s), a synthetic fast-batch lets PT2 compilation overlap data-pipeline warm-up, and mark_dynamic, autotune-config pruning, a symbolic-hash fix and Mega-Cache shrink cold compilation from 2,050 s to 254 s. Async checkpointing with PyTorch-native pinned-memory staging cuts save blocking 77% and lets the interval be tuned against a convex wasted-time curve, standalone CPU-side publishing from an anchor checkpoint cuts GPU-held shutdown, previously 25.6 to 54.6 minutes per job, to 3.3 minutes or less, and scheduling changes such as stage-aware preemption target the failure count. On six paired benchmarks (8 to 256 GPUs, two induced failures each) baseline ETT% of 59 to 85% rose to 80 to 93% (mean +15.5 points), recovery supplied 58% of the savings because restarts repeat startup and reuse warm caches (TTR reductions were 1.8 to 8.6x the TTS reductions), and fleet-wide offline-training ETT% across 500+ models went from about 80% to above 90% in six months.

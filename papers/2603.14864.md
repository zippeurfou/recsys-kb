## Summary

This paper addresses two main challenges in LLM-based e-commerce: the lack of benchmarks for long-term shopping tasks and the fragmentation of preference identification and shopping assistance. To overcome these, the authors introduce a new benchmark with long-term memory and a unified framework called Shopping Companion, which jointly handles memory retrieval and shopping tasks. They also developed a dual-reward RL training strategy. Experiments show that even state-of-the-art models struggle significantly, but their lightweight LLM, trained with Shopping Companion, outperforms strong baselines by better capturing user preferences and completing tasks effectively.

## Technical note

Shopping Companion Bench is a benchmark of 1,000 instances (single-product and add-on-deal recommendation) grounded in 1.2M real Lazada products, injecting synthesized preference dialogues as needles into LongMemEval histories to test cross-session preference memory with multi-turn user correction. Analysis of a GPT-5 agent identifies two failure modes: cascading errors from early preference hallucination and insufficient attribute/seller verification before recommending. The authors propose annotation-free tool-wise rewards that score each memory/product tool call by how many returned results match ground-truth preferences, combined with outcome and format rewards for dense process supervision over long-horizon tool use (formulated as a POMDP). Even GPT-5 stays below 70% success, while a 4B model trained with tool-wise rewards outperforms SFT and outcome-only RL on preference grounding and end-to-end success with fewer redundant turns.

## Summary

This paper introduces DrainCode, a novel adversarial attack targeting the computational efficiency of retrieval-augmented code generation systems. The attack works by strategically poisoning the retrieval context, forcing the LLM to generate significantly longer outputs, which directly increases GPU latency and energy consumption. Evaluation shows DrainCode can cause up to an 85% latency increase, 49% energy spike, and over a 3x increase in output length, demonstrating its effectiveness across different models and defenses.

## Technical note

DrainCode is the first adversarial attack targeting the computational efficiency (latency and energy) of RAG-based code generation rather than its functional correctness. It poisons the retrieval corpus with syntactically valid but semantically inert triggers optimized via gradient-guided (greedy-coordinate-descent) mutation, using an EOS-suppression loss to inflate output length plus a KL-divergence constraint to preserve correctness, and a hypothetical-query construction step for query-agnostic poisoning. Across multiple models it induces up to 85% higher latency, 49% more energy, and 3x-10x longer output while keeping 95-99% functional accuracy and evading classifier- and perplexity-based defenses.

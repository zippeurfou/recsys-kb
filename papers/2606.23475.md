## Summary

This paper formally proves that multi-vector (MV) embeddings are significantly more expressive than single-vector (SV) embeddings at a fixed representation size, contrary to recent algorithmic suggestions like MUVERA which implied potential equivalence under certain conditions. It demonstrates that any collection of MV embeddings requires an exponentially higher dimension ($D = m \cdot (\epsilon^2)^{\Omega(1/\epsilon)}$ for $n <= 2^{poly(m)}$, compared to the dataset size) than what is needed by SV embeddings with linear inner product similarity to approximate their Chamfer-based similarities. The proof uses a hard instance constructed from the $NAND_k$ boolean function, confirming long-standing beliefs in the neural information retrieval community about MV embeddings' inherent advantage.

## Technical note

This is a theoretical IR paper proving multi-vector (MV) embeddings are strictly more expressive than single-vector (SV) embeddings at fixed representation size. It shows that approximating MV Chamfer similarity (average over query vectors of max inner product to document vectors) by SV inner products to within additive epsilon requires SV dimension D = (epsilon^2 m)^Omega(1/epsilon), nearly matching MUVERA's m^O(1/epsilon^2) upper bound and ruling out the D=md scenario that would imply equivalence. The lower bound is proved via Sherstov's Pattern Matrix Method, constructing a hard instance whose Chamfer similarity matrix equals the pattern matrix of the NAND_k boolean function, and it holds even for data-dependent (non-oblivious) embeddings and reduces to a bound on approximating MAX-IP by IP. Complementarily, the paper gives a snowflake embedding of MAX-ABS-IP into IP with improved exponent m^O(ln(1/epsilon)/epsilon) that preserves nearest-neighbor ordering, directly applicable to non-negative learned sparse retrieval models like SPLADE.

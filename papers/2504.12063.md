## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Modern retrieval systems do not rely on a single ranking model to construct their rankings. Instead, they generally take a cascading approach where a sequence of ranking models are applied in multiple re-ranking stages. Thereby, they balance the quality of the top-K ranking with computational costs by limiting the number of documents each model re-ranks. However, the cascading approach is not the only way models can interact to form a retrieval system.

## Technical note

This Google paper generalizes cascading retrieval into 'compound retrieval systems' that apply multiple prediction models with arbitrary interactions, not just top-K re-ranking. It learns a policy deciding where to apply pointwise and pairwise (LLM) prediction models and a score-aggregation function to combine them, optimizing a ranking metric under an efficiency budget. Combining classic BM25 with state-of-the-art pairwise LLM relevance prompting (PRP, whose LLM-call count scales quadratically), the optimized compound system achieves better effectiveness-efficiency trade-offs than cascading baselines, even when trained self-supervised.

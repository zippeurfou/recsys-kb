## Summary

Current generative recommender systems model user sequences inefficiently by interleaving item and action tokens, leading to long sequences, high computational costs, and noisy attention. This paper proposes two new architectures (AttnLFA and AttnMVP) that eliminate this interleaving, directly modeling the causal link between items and their corresponding actions within a Transformer framework. These models significantly improve performance (achieving lower evaluation loss and higher Normalized Entropy) and training efficiency (reducing training time) compared to standard interleaved baselines.

## Technical note

This work reformulates HSTU-style generative recommendation, arguing that interleaving item and action tokens doubles sequence length (roughly 4x attention cost), dilutes the causal dependency that item i_n elicits action a_n, and injects attention noise by forcing Transformers to mix semantically heterogeneous tokens. It shows interleaving is an inefficient proxy for similarity-weighted action pooling and proposes two architectures: AttnLFA (Attention-based Late Fusion for Actions) uses contextualized item embeddings as queries/keys and action embeddings as values under strict causal masking to pool historical actions, and AttnMVP (Attention-based Mixed Value Pooling) further mixes item and action embeddings in the value stream for preference-aware item representations. Both eliminate interleaved dependencies to cut sequence complexity by 50%; a downstream MMoE head produces multi-task action predictions with late-fusion counting features for calibration, evaluated on large-scale social-platform product data.

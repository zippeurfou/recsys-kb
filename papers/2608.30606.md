## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Current e-commerce generative retrieval systems use a two-stage approach (first train embeddings, then learn a codebook), leading to error accumulation and inconsistent product IDs within the same cluster during codebook learning, which degrades retrieval accuracy.
2.  **Solution:** The paper proposes a novel joint training method that simultaneously learns the product embedding model and the codebook, incorporating same-product-cluster information as an additional supervision signal during training.
3.  **Result:** Experiments show this integrated approach significantly improves e-commerce retrieval performance compared to the standard two-stage method, while also enhancing the quality of both the embedding model and the learned codebook.

## Technical note

Alibaba / Taobao e-commerce product search. Attacks the standard two-stage generative-retrieval pipeline (first train a product embedding model, then fit a codebook over frozen embeddings), which propagates first-stage embedding error into the codebook and models no query-product or product-product interaction, so functionally identical SKUs from different sellers or sizes get inconsistent IDs. The fix is to jointly train the embedding encoder and the RQ-VAE codebook under one objective, L = L_RQVAE + alpha L_InfoNCE + beta L_MSE, where L_RQVAE is reconstruction plus commitment loss with mu=0.25, the InfoNCE term is applied to both the raw product embedding h_product and the quantizer-reconstructed h_recon against the query representation using in-batch and hard negatives, and the MSE term pulls each product toward the mean embedding of up to 5 products sampled from its same-product cluster as auxiliary supervision. The codebook is 5 levels of 32 entries at 768 dims, producing 5-token IDs that become new special tokens in a Qwen2.5-7B vocabulary; the LLM is trained in two progressive SFT stages, first product info to ID then query to ID, on 20M Alibaba products and 40M query-to-ID pairs, with 10k validation and 10k test queries split evenly between regular and implicit-intent. Results: Recall@1/10/100 of 4.49 / 9.90 / 30.71 versus BM25 1.23 / 2.66 / 9.80, DPR with GTE encoder 3.57 / 7.09 / 24.24, DSI 3.79 / 7.99 / 23.91 and TIGER-RQVAE 4.33 / 8.84 / 26.38. The paper also reports ALSP, the average shared ID prefix length within a product cluster, which rises from 3.92 (TIGER) to 4.42; removing only the cluster constraint drops ALSP to 4.01 and Recall@100 to 28.47, and that ablated variant still beats TIGER, isolating the separate gains from end-to-end training and from cluster supervision. Caveats: offline only with no online A/B, a single proprietary dataset, absolute recall numbers are very low, and the four-page paper gives no latency, serving or codebook-utilization analysis.

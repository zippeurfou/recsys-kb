## Summary

This paper proposes a system optimizing how Pinterest recommends commerce-related content during its initial search phase by learning personalized triggering policies using deep-learning models. The core approach involves training a multi-task model on pseudo-outcomes derived from randomized logging, which provides counterfactual data for reliable offline evaluation and impact forecasting with high consistency to real-world results. By significantly reducing unnecessary shopping triggers (up to 85% reduction) while maintaining or improving other user engagement metrics like total sessions (+0.26%) and Pin saves (+1.10%), the system improves efficiency, aligns recommendations better with user intent at scale, and offers a practical recipe for early-stage retrieval optimizations in recommendation systems.

## Technical note

Frames when to trigger shopping candidate generators in Pinterest's early retrieval as a causal policy problem to balance commerce against inspiration. A deep multi-task model jointly predicts potential outcomes and uplift across many engagement and session metrics, trained with a doubly-robust (Switch-DR) pseudo-outcome loss plus calibrated BCE outcome losses over a DCNv2 + MMoE backbone on pretrained embeddings; a randomized 50/50 'Shopping Holdout' supplies counterfactual data and a linear-time offline replay selects serving thresholds. Online it cut shopping triggers by up to 85% while holding shopping sessions neutral, lifting total sessions +0.26% and Closeup repins +1.10% with roughly $0.5M infra savings and no latency regression. It keeps the cascaded recommender (a triggering policy on top of retrieval), not a generative pipeline.

## Summary

This paper addresses optimizing when Pinterest displays e-commerce content ("shopping triggers") during user searches by framing it as a causal decision problem in early retrieval. They developed and deployed Deep-learning Causal Retrieval Optimization, using a deep multi-task model trained with counterfactual data to predict both outcomes and uplift of triggering commerce suggestions. The system successfully reduced shopping triggers by up to 85% while maintaining positive user engagement metrics like total sessions and Pin saves, demonstrating an effective production-grade approach for early-retrieval optimizations in recommendation systems.

## Technical note

Pinterest production system that decides when to trigger shopping candidate generators (CGs) in early retrieval, framed as a causal/uplift policy rather than ranking. A deep multi-task model (DCNv2 feature crossing + MMoE) jointly predicts per-treatment potential outcomes, a propensity head, and an uplift head across many funnel/engagement metrics, trained with calibrated outcome BCE losses, an uplift-consistency regularizer, and a doubly-robust pseudo-outcome loss with Switch-DR truncation for single-robust, stable uplift learning. A randomized 50/50 Shopping Holdout supplies counterfactual coverage, reverse metrics (R-PR AUC, R-P@K) monitor over-triggering, and a linear-time offline replay selects serving thresholds and forecasts impact with high online consistency; a low-variance single-value (SV) policy runs in production while the delta/uplift policy (DP) serves as a causal diagnostic. Deployed at 100k+ QPS in parallel with retrieval RPCs (no latency regression), it cuts shopping triggers by up to 85% while keeping shopping-session metrics neutral and lifting total sessions +0.26%, mid-funnel +0.46%, and Closeup repins +1.10%.

## Summary

This paper demonstrates how attackers can subtly manipulate item descriptions in RAG-based recommender systems using small token edits, even when those items are promoted by LLMs. Such modifications significantly influence item rankings and exposure despite being difficult to detect. The authors emphasize the need for robust textual consistency checks and provenance tracking to counter these stealthy data poisoning attacks effectively.

## Technical note

The paper studies provider-side data poisoning of RAG-based recommender systems, where an attacker rewrites a small fraction (~10% of tokens) of item descriptions to promote long-tail items or demote short-head items while staying stealthy under a token-edit budget and an SBERT similarity constraint (>=0.80). Three LLM-driven attacks are designed: emotional edits, neighbor-borrowing (weaving phrases from opposite-popularity items), and a chained combination, evaluated on MovieLens with a Sentence-Transformer retriever plus an OpenAI LLM re-ranker. Chain rewriting is most effective, e.g., reducing a target item's recommendation rank from ~7.0 to ~4.7, with the OpenAI generative re-ranker more sensitive to the cues than the static ST embedding retriever. System-wide Recall and nDCG drop only a few points for sparse attacks, so localized item-exposure manipulation is far more pronounced than global degradation, motivating textual provenance and consistency checks.

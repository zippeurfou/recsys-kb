## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of predicting human perceptions of robot performance in social navigation, which is essential for developing socially aware robots. To overcome the data scarcity issue in traditional user studies, the authors propose using Few-Shot Learning with Large Language Models (LLMs). Their findings demonstrate that LLMs can achieve performance matching or exceeding traditional supervised learning models while requiring significantly fewer labeled examples, and show improvements with more examples or personalized ones, paving the way for scalable user feedback integration.

## Technical note

This work uses LLMs with in-context learning to predict human perceptions (competence, surprisingness, clear intent) of a robot during social navigation, addressing the data scarcity of traditional user studies. The authors extend the SEAN TOGETHER dataset to v2 (404 episodes, 69 participants) and prompt LLMs with a few labeled demonstrations plus spatio-temporal observations (robot/follower/pedestrian trajectories) encoded as strings. LLMs match or exceed supervised models (e.g., random forests) while needing an order of magnitude fewer labeled examples, improve with more in-context examples, and improve further when demonstrations are personalized to the evaluated user.

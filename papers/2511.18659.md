## Summary

CLaRa addresses limitations in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), specifically the issues of long contexts and separate optimization for retrieval and generation. It introduces a unified framework operating in a shared continuous space, combining embedding compression (using SCP) and joint training of both modules. Experiments demonstrate that CLaRa achieves state-of-the-art performance in both compression and reranking, often outperforming text-based baselines.

## Technical note

CLaRa unifies retrieval and generation over shared continuous document representations to fix long contexts and disjoint optimization in RAG. Documents are encoded once into a compact set of memory tokens serving both retrieval and generation; a salient compressor pretraining stage (SCP) uses synthesized QA and paraphrase supervision plus an MSE alignment loss to retain semantic core. The reranker and generator are trained end-to-end under a single next-token-prediction loss, with gradients flowing to the retriever via a differentiable top-k selection using straight-through estimation. On single-hop and multi-hop QA benchmarks with Mistral-7B and Phi-4B it achieves SOTA compression and reranking, outperforming text-based baselines even at 16x compression.

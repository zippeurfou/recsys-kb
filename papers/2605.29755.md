## Summary

Large recommendation models offer significant performance gains but are too heavy for industrial deployment, creating a gap between training and serving. To bridge this, researchers developed Rec-Distill, an industrial pipeline that trains smaller, efficient "student" models to mimic the performance of massive "teacher" recommendation models. This framework achieves high transferability (>60%) even for very large teachers (up to 24B parameters), enabling the deployment of lightweight models that deliver substantial business value while maintaining serving efficiency.

## Technical note

Rec-Distill is an industrial distillation pipeline that transfers scaling-law gains of large recommendation models to lightweight serving models under strict latency constraints. It decouples teacher and student training so the teacher (scaled up to 24B dense parameters and 20K behavior-sequence length) is continuously streaming-updated while its forward-pass logits are cached for asynchronous student training, avoiding costly co-training and enabling a 1-to-N teacher-to-student paradigm across ranking stages. Distillation gain is decomposed as Delta_scale x transferability eta; the student uses black-box KL distillation on softened logits and a decoupled-tower architecture (main online tower plus an auxiliary distillation tower) for risk isolation, plus a debiasing mechanism and hybrid batch-streaming pipeline. Across recommendation and advertising scenarios it achieves distillation transferability exceeding 60% with measurable online business gains.

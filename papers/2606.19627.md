## Summary

This paper addresses the "extreme cold-start" challenge in e-commerce video feeds caused by a lack of interaction history for new videos. It introduces VCG, a scalable multimodal retrieval system leveraging domain-adapted vision-language models (CLIP-based) to map users and videos into a shared semantic space. This allows zero-shot recommendations based on visual content similarity, bypassing behavioral data scarcity, and significantly improves video completion rates by mitigating engagement biases in dynamic feeds.

## Technical note

VCG (Video Candidate Generation) is a zero-shot two-tower multimodal retrieval system that tackles extreme cold-start in e-commerce short-video feeds where new videos have no interaction history. It embeds videos as the mean of 10 uniformly sampled frames encoded by an in-house CLIP model fine-tuned on ~200M fashion image-text pairs, and represents users as a time-decayed weighted average of their catalog interactions in the same domain-adapted CLIP space, scoring via dot product with pre-computed video embeddings (P50 17.5ms, P99 30ms). An earlier metadata-based VCG v1 that reused the catalog user tower failed online due to a conversion-vs-inspiration semantic gap, metadata sparsity, and lack of visual signal, motivating the pixel-based v2. Because offline NDCG/AUC are confounded by exposure bias, evaluation uses an LVLM-as-judge (Qwen2.5-VL) on 5-point visual coherence plus fDNA and CLIP similarity; CLIP is chosen over generative Qwen embeddings which, despite 6-10% higher attribute-classification F1, exhibit anisotropy/representation collapse hurting kNN separability. A 4-week A/B test showed +50% VideoProgress@50% and +8% start rate with stable revenue and retention.

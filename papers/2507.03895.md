## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of selecting useful feature interactions in deep recommender systems, which is vital for performance but difficult due to the sheer number of possible combinations and their high evaluation cost. It proposes TayFCS, a novel method combining a Taylor Expansion Scorer (TayScorer) that efficiently estimates combination importance via gradients from just one backward pass, with Logistic Regression Elimination (LRE) designed to remove redundant interactions by evaluating potential information gain. Experiments on benchmark datasets and online A/B tests confirm that TayFCS significantly improves model performance while being both effective and computationally efficient.

## Technical note

TayFCS is a lightweight method for selecting useful high-order feature combinations in deep recommender systems, where explicit Cartesian-product feature crosses grow exponentially and mostly add noise and memory cost. Its TayScorer module uses a field-wise Taylor expansion to approximate each combination's importance from its sub-components' gradients in a single backward pass, avoiding costly add/remove retraining. A Logistic Regression Elimination (LRE) step then prunes redundant combinations by measuring incremental information gain via a cheap surrogate model. Experiments on three benchmarks plus online A/B tests confirm improved model performance with strong efficiency.

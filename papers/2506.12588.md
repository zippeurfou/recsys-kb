## Summary

This paper questions the reliability of standard graph learning benchmarks, arguing they often suffer from issues like inconsistent metrics sampling and flawed negative sampling strategies. Focusing specifically on Temporal Link Prediction (TLP), it observes that current evaluation methods also face problems such as reliance on hard negatives and combining predictions while ignoring varying base probabilities across nodes. The authors draw parallels with recommender systems research and propose exploring alternative TLP evaluation techniques to achieve more accurate, robust, and interpretable results.

## Technical note

This extended abstract critiques evaluation strategies for Temporal Link Prediction (TLP) in temporal graph learning benchmarks. It identifies three recurring issues: inconsistent sampled metrics, reliance on hard negative sampling introduced for robustness, and metrics that implicitly assume equal base probabilities across source nodes by combining predictions. The authors connect these problems to longstanding evaluation concerns in the recommender-systems community and illustrate them with concrete examples across benchmarks like DGB, TGB, and DyGLib. The ongoing work aims to characterize these issues systematically and propose more robust, interpretable evaluation alternatives.

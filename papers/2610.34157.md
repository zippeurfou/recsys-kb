## Summary

Generative recommendation treats item suggestions as autoregressive generation problems, but scaling these models effectively while keeping computational costs low remains difficult. The paper introduces LoopGR, a parameter-efficient framework that scales generative recommenders by increasing their *recurrent depth* (the number of times the model processes the generation sequence) rather than their parameter count. LoopGR uses a hierarchical structure with a shared decoder and a lightweight context updater, and employs a novel self-distillation technique for efficient inference. Experiments show LoopGR significantly outperforms existing generative recommendation baselines and reduces model size by up to 52% compared to encoder-decoder models, establishing recurrent depth as a powerful new scaling dimension.

## Technical note

SID-based generative recommenders scale by adding layers, which conflicts with strict serving memory and latency budgets, and existing backbones (encoder-decoder TIGER, decoder-only, OneRec-V2 lazy decoder) fix how compute is split between history modeling and target generation. LoopGR (Tencent TGR series) keeps a T5 encoder for the initial context memory and then interleaves a parameter-shared Transformer decoder (high-level cycles T_H) with a lightweight shared GRU or token-mixing context updater (low-level cycles T_L) that feeds the BOS prediction state back to refine the history context, decoupling effective depth from parameter count. Recurrent trajectory self-distillation (RTSD) then trains a half-budget student initialized from the full-budget teacher by KL-matching per-cycle predictive distributions and cosine-matching their log-probability first differences, beating per-step supervision, gated exit and intra-loop self-distillation (3.2% vs 9.4-13.2% average gap to the teacher). On Amazon Beauty, Sports and CDs LoopGR is best on 8 of 12 metrics against TIGER, HSTU, ActionPiece and RPG (e.g. +5.4% Recall@5 on Sports), matches encoder-decoder quality with up to 52% fewer parameters (1.3M vs 2.7M at about 0.5 GFLOPs), and on a Tencent industrial dataset beats Lazy Decoder, TIGER and BARGE by up to 11.5%, 7.1% and 3.9% in recall; removing the updater (identity) costs about 8%.

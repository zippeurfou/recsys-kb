## Summary

1.  Generative recommender systems using Large Language Models (LLMs) face a core challenge because LLMs process text tokens, while recommenders use discrete item IDs, often leading to inaccurate recommendations ("hallucinations").
2.  Existing methods bridge this gap by representing items in Euclidean space but struggle to effectively model the long-tail distribution common in item catalogs, where many items are rarely seen.
3.  To overcome this, the paper introduces HypRQ-VAE, the first framework representing items in hyperbolic space, which naturally accommodates long-tail data and preserves the semantics needed to improve recommendation accuracy, particularly for rarely seen items.

## Technical note

HypRQ-VAE is the first item-indexing scheme for generative recommendation that performs residual quantization in hyperbolic rather than Euclidean space, motivated by the claim that the power-law structure of catalogs maps naturally onto a manifold whose volume expands exponentially. An item's text embedding is encoded to a Euclidean latent, projected onto the Poincare ball via exp_0^kappa, and recursively quantized with Mobius subtraction (r_{i+1} = r_i minus_kappa e_{c_i}^i) across L=4 codebooks of K=256 codes; selected codes are recombined with Mobius addition, mapped back with log_0^kappa, and decoded under the usual reconstruction plus stop-gradient RQ commitment loss. Collisions are resolved by a cascading token-reassignment procedure over an M x L x K hyperbolic-distance tensor starting at the deepest codebook level and walking upward, rather than appending semantically meaningless disambiguation IDs as TIGER/LC-Rec/LETTER do. Stage 2 flattens per-item SID sequences into a prompt and fine-tunes LLaMA2-7B with LoRA (r=8, alpha=32, dropout 0.05, AdamW lr 1e-4) for next-item SID generation, decoded with beam size 20 under trie-constrained decoding. On MovieLens, Amazon Instruments and Amazon Arts it beats MF, Caser, SASRec, P5-TID, P5-CID, TIGER, LC-Rec and LETTER on every Hit@K and NDCG@K, e.g. Instruments Hit@5 0.0690 versus 0.0620 for LC-Rec (+11.3%) and NDCG@10 0.0650 versus 0.0570 (+14.0%). The long-tail result is the substantive one: splitting by Pareto into top-20% head and bottom-80% tail, gains over Euclidean TIGER reach +52.71% Hit@10 on MovieLens tail versus +14.09% on head, and average quantization error for tail items is roughly equal to or lower than head items in hyperbolic space while it is consistently worse for tail in Euclidean space. Ablations place optimal SID length at L=6 (longer sequences suffer autoregressive error accumulation) and codebook size near K=256. Caveats: offline only on three small public benchmarks, no industrial data and no A/B test; this is Virginia Tech academic work -- the Amazon/Google/Meta affiliation tags trace to funding acknowledgements (Amazon-Virginia Tech Initiative, Amazon AWS, Google, Cisco, NSF, DARPA), not a deployed industry system.

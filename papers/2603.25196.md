## Summary

This paper introduces CPGBench, a benchmark evaluating how well Large Language Models (LLMs) can detect and adhere to clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) during multi-turn conversations. Using 32,155 extracted recommendations from 3,418 real-world CPG documents across 24 specialties, CPGBench automatically generates conversations to test 8 leading LLMs. The results reveal significant gaps, with adherence rates ranging from 21.8% to 63.2%, indicating LLMs struggle to both correctly reference guideline sources and consistently apply the recommendations, highlighting critical safety concerns for their clinical deployment.

## Technical note

CPGBench is a decade-scale automated benchmark measuring whether LLMs detect and adhere to clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) in multi-turn conversations, using an LLM-as-judge scoring pipeline. It collects 3,418 CPG documents from nine countries and two international organizations (2015-2025) across all 24 ABMS specialties, extracting 32,155 recommendation-level entries (with strength, evidence level, action, goal) and synthesizing one conversation per recommendation. Evaluating eight leading LLMs, it finds detection rates of 71.1-89.6% but title-grounding of only 3.6-29.7%, and adherence of just 21.8-63.2%, revealing a large gap between knowing guideline content, citing its source, and applying it. A human evaluation with 56 clinicians validates the pipeline: ~100% extraction accuracy, >99% conversation validity, and Judge-LLM agreement with clinicians at Cohen's kappa 0.62-0.84.

## Summary

1.  Generative Retrieval (GR) faces two main issues: an optimization blockage due to non-differentiable indexing, decoupling index building from the search goal, and a geometric conflict causing popular items to overshadow less common but relevant ones.
2.  The paper proposes Differentiable Geometric Indexing (DGI), which addresses these problems by using differentiable quantization (Soft Teacher Forcing and Symmetric Weight Sharing) to link indexing and retrieval, and isotropic geometric optimization (scaled cosine similarity) to decouple popularity bias from relevance.
3.  Extensive experiments show DGI outperforms existing sparse, dense, and generative retrieval baselines, especially in scenarios involving long-tail items, demonstrating the need for both differentiable index construction and geometric fidelity.

## Technical note

DGI (Differentiable Geometric Indexing) targets two flaws in generative retrieval with learnable semantic IDs: an optimization blockage where non-differentiable quantization decouples index construction from the retrieval objective, and a geometric conflict where unnormalized inner-product logits cause norm inflation that lets popular hub items overshadow long-tail ones. It bridges the gap via Operational Unification: Soft Teacher Forcing with Gumbel-Softmax gives a fully differentiable pathway (avoiding biased STE gradients) plus Symmetric Weight Sharing to align the quantizer codebook space with the decoder space. Isotropic Geometric Optimization replaces dot-product logits with scaled cosine similarity on the unit hypersphere to decouple magnitude/popularity from angular semantic relevance. A 7-day online A/B test in a search advertising system yielded +1.27% CTR and +1.11% RPM.

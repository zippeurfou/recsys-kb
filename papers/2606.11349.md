## Summary

This paper introduces ACTION-RATING for hierarchical language agents, embedding clarification requests into their internal action space on an ordinal scale alongside navigation decisions. This approach reveals two distinct help-seeking modes (mandatory and opportunistic) based on the agent's self-assessment of uncertainty. Experiments demonstrate a "regime shift" towards more effective opportunistic clarifications, significantly increasing Information-Seeking Effectiveness (ISE), even when answer quality is intentionally degraded, suggesting this systematic clarification mechanism improves performance.

## Technical note

ActionRating reframes clarification in hierarchical language agents by placing a dedicated clarification action inside the agent's action space on a shared [0,100] ordinal scale that competes directly with navigation actions at every intermediate decision point, making help-seeking structurally observable without an external uncertainty estimator. From the agent's own ratings two modes emerge: mandatory (no viable branch) and opportunistic (residual uncertainty despite a leading candidate). Evaluated on Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification over a 30,000-node taxonomy with three benchmarks and nine LLMs across four families, it observes a regime shift from mandatory to opportunistic clarification, with Information-Seeking Effectiveness (fraction of help interactions followed by a correct next step) rising from 50% to 74%. A separability test shows the information-seeking structure persists even when answer quality is degraded by 18.8% accuracy, empirically separating where help is sought from the quality of help received.

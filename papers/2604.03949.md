## Summary

Recommender systems heavily rely on item identifiers (IDs) for tasks like retrieval and ranking, often encoding collaborative filtering signals. Snapchat adopted Semantic IDs (SIDs), which are ordered lists derived from semantic representations, offering advantages over standard IDs by reducing cardinality and clustering semantics. By applying SIDs as auxiliary features and retrieval sources, and addressing technical challenges through specific design choices, Snapchat achieved positive offline results and launched variants in production, demonstrating improved metrics.

## Technical note

Snapchat reports industrial deployment of Semantic IDs (SIDs), ordered code lists from RQ-VAE over foundation-model/collaborative embeddings, used both as auxiliary categorical features in ranking and as direct targets in generative retrieval. To fix severe RQ-VAE codebook collapse they backpropagate through the whole codebook via a straight-through estimator and condition SIDs on fused multi-modal embeddings (summed sources) to raise input variance and code utilization. For SID-to-item resolution in generative retrieval they use heuristic intra-code disambiguation (secondary ranking by view time/freshness) and prioritize retrieval depth over breadth from top-ranked SIDs. Deployments span ads, DPA, friending/search (GraphHash SIDs compressing 900M user IDs), with online lifts; they also show SID uniqueness is a non-monotonic proxy that saturates (~70%) and should be treated as a collapse sanity check, not a gold quality metric.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of animating full-body avatars with realistic loose clothing dynamics. Existing methods either rely on expensive physics simulations or data-driven models that fail to capture fine details and history-dependent motion. The authors propose a system using a transformer decoder and a latent dynamics model that evolves a residual latent state based on pose history, decomposing updates into driving, restoring, and dissipative forces to achieve temporally coherent animations with diverse plausible motions, outperforming previous baselines.

## Technical note

This paper augments a pose-conditioned 3D Gaussian Splatting full-body avatar with a transformer-based decoder and a 'dynamics residual latent' to model history-dependent deformations that pose alone cannot explain, especially loose clothing. The decoder consumes user-supplied driving signals (body/face keypoints) plus the residual latent, and a learned latent dynamics model evolves the latent from a short pose history and its previous state. Each update is decomposed into driving, restoring, and dissipative forces, yielding temporally coherent, controllable rollouts (e.g., adjustable stiffness) with negligible added cost. Evaluated on nine captured everyday-motion sequences, it improves animation quality over data-driven baselines. This is a neural-rendering/graphics paper, not a recommendation paper.

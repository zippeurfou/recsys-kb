## Summary

Large Language Models (LLMs) used for writing tasks can introduce cultural biases, particularly affecting non-native speakers, as shown by applying the WEIRD framework to their entity recommendations. This study evaluates and attempts to mitigate these biases using pluralistic prompt-based strategies across various LLMs and a large dataset. While the strategies successfully reduced bias overall, the reduction was inconsistent across different models, and certain types of entities remained particularly biased.

## Technical note

This paper studies cultural bias in LLM recommendations of real-world entities using the WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) framework across nine LLMs and 44 fine-grained entity types. It tests five pluralistic prompting strategies (base diversity, legal framing, explicit bias, chain-of-thought, combined) against a no-prompt baseline, mapping recommended examples to countries via a GPT-5 LLM-as-judge. Chain-of-Thought reduced WEIRD bias most (79.8% baseline to 52.5%), but reductions were inconsistent across models and the USA still dominated. Product entities were most biased (100% baseline, still 90% with best prompting), while Location/Event entities were least biased.

## Summary

Current voice encoders in large audio-language models often fail to adequately capture both speaker identity and subtle paralinguistic cues. This study introduces Auden-Voice, a general-purpose voice encoder designed to balance these aspects effectively. The research finds that multi-task training yields the most balanced representations, surpassing contrastive pretraining like CLAP which primarily enhances retrieval over paralinguistic understanding, and demonstrates that Auden-Voice performs well when integrated with large language models.

## Technical note

The paper studies how to build a general-purpose voice encoder (Auden-Voice) that balances speaker identity and paralinguistic cues for integration with large audio-language models. Using a shared frozen Zipformer backbone and minimal heads, it systematically compares initializations and supervision, finding ASR pretraining is a strong init and multi-task training (joint speaker-ID plus age/gender/emotion) gives the most balanced representations across linear probing and zero-shot speaker tasks. CLAP contrastive fine-tuning reliably boosts speech-text retrieval but degrades other tasks, with data scaling only partially recovering zero-shot classification. In frozen-encoder+adapter LLM-QA, the multi-task encoder correlates with probing quality and beats the multi-task+CLAP variant, so it is chosen as final Auden-Voice.

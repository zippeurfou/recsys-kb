## Summary

This paper introduces MIRROR, a self-distillation framework addressing the shift in LLM personalization from simple style imitation to achieving higher content quality. MIRROR replaces direct reference token copying with aligning the model's own generation distributions to those of its reference-conditioned self, internalizing user preferences. An enhanced version, MIRROR-F, further improves content generation while preserving user expression, and both models show superior personalization performance and text quality across benchmarks, with reduced catastrophic forgetting compared to standard fine-tuning methods.

## Technical note

Fine-tuning LLMs for personalization by imitating user-written references (SFT, PerCE, NextQuill) learns style but degrades content quality, forgets general ability, and trains on reference prefixes the model never visits at inference. MIRROR is an on-policy self-distillation framework: the same model conditioned on the reference-revealed context (history, query, reference) acts as a frozen self-teacher whose next-token distributions the deployable student (history, query) matches with a symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence along the student's own sampled rollouts, with no external teacher or reward model. MIRROR-F adds a focal anchoring NLL term on selected informative, context-grounded reference tokens, mixed through one focality coefficient alpha. On LaMP/LongLaMP abstract, news-headline and paper-topic generation with Qwen3-1.7B/4B LoRA backbones, MIRROR-F takes the best ROUGE-1/METEOR in most settings (e.g. headline ROUGE-1 0.1763 vs 0.1594 for NextQuill at 1.7B), the best ExPerT content-and-style scores among trained methods, and the smallest OOD degradation on held-out Amazon-review tasks.

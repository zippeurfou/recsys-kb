## Summary

1.  **Problem:** The paper addresses the issue of popularity bias in sequential recommendation systems, where popular items are recommended excessively, leading to poor coverage.
2.  **Solution:** It introduces PR-entmax, a new loss function that uses a sparse prior to make popularity correction context-dependent, influencing which items receive non-zero probability updates during training.
3.  **Conclusion:** Experiments show that integrating this loss function, without changing the model architecture, consistently reduces popularity bias while preserving or improving recommendation relevance across different datasets and systems.

## Technical note

Softmax cross-entropy training of sequential recommenders (SASRec, GRU4Rec) over imbalanced catalogs over-exposes head items and hurts coverage, and existing in-processing fixes (IPW, logit adjustment, popularity deconfounding, popularity-based negative sampling) trade relevance for debiasing. PR-entmax (Yandex) derives a loss from a variational principle that adds a Tsallis-entropy sparsity term (entmax, alpha=1.5) and a cross-entropy-to-popularity-prior term, giving a closed form where prior-adjusted logits z_i + gamma*log q_i pass through a sparse threshold, so the popularity prior decides which items enter the active support and receive gradient, acting as a differentiable context-dependent popularity-aware hard-negative selection; it is trained with the Fenchel-Young loss and the prior term is dropped at inference so ranking uses contextual logits only. Plain entmax, softmax CE and logit adjustment are recovered as special cases. Across ML-1M, Tmall, Yambda-50M, Yoochoose and Zvuk with SASRec and GRU4Rec, PR-entmax is the only method that raises both NDCG@10 and Coverage/Novelty over CE on nearly every dataset (e.g. SASRec ML-1M NDCG@10 0.047 to 0.058 with Coverage@10 0.463 to 0.600; GRU4Rec Tmall NDCG@10 0.216 to 0.291; GRU4Rec Yambda NDCG@10 0.056 to 0.086), with no architecture change.

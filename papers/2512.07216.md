## Summary

Existing recommender systems struggle with long-tail items and lack semantic expressiveness due to reliance on ID-based features for lifelong user interest modeling. This paper introduces MUSE, a new framework designed to effectively utilize multimodal signals across both the General Search Unit (GSU) and Exact Search Unit (ESU) stages of modeling. MUSE demonstrates that simple cosine similarity works well for the GSU, while the ESU requires richer multimodal sequence modeling and fusion techniques, and has been successfully deployed in a production system with positive results.

## Technical note

MUSE tackles lifelong user interest modeling for CTR prediction, where reliance on ID-based features generalizes poorly on long-tail items and lacks semantic expressiveness. Through systematic analysis of the two-stage GSU/ESU framework, the authors find simplicity suffices in the GSU (lightweight cosine similarity over frozen SCL multimodal embeddings beats complex retrieval), whereas the ESU needs richer multimodal sequence modeling via SimTier histograms and Semantic-Aware Target Attention (SA-TA) that fuses multimodal semantics into ID-based attention. Deployed in Taobao display advertising over 100K-length sequences, MUSE delivers significant top-line gains with negligible latency, and the authors open-source a large-scale ultra-long-sequence multimodal dataset.

## Summary

Current video retrieval methods often fail to ensure consistent storylines when composing clips, as they prioritize local semantics over state and identity continuity. This paper introduces Consistent Video Retrieval (CVR) and a benchmark, then proposes CAST, a lightweight adapter that enhances existing frozen vision-language models by predicting state-conditioned updates from visual history. Experiments demonstrate CAST improves retrieval across multiple benchmarks, consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines, and provides a useful signal for generating temporally coherent video continuations.

## Technical note

The paper formalizes Consistent Video Retrieval (CVR), which conditions clip retrieval on preceding visual history to preserve state and identity consistency rather than only local semantic alignment. CAST is a lightweight plug-and-play adapter over frozen vision-language embedding spaces (e.g., CLIP) that predicts a state-conditioned residual delta so the retrieval target is v_{t} approx v_{t-1} + Delta(v_{t-1}, q_t), giving an additive connection that retains identity while updating procedural attributes. A diagnostic benchmark over YouCook2, COIN, and CrossTask mines State Negatives (same video, wrong step) and Identity Negatives (different video, matched caption via Sentence-BERT) as hard negatives to penalize consistency errors. CAST outperforms zero-shot context-agnostic baselines and also serves as a reranking signal for black-box video generation candidates (e.g., Veo) to select temporally coherent continuations.

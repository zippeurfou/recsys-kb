## Summary

(Abstract fallback)

Recommender systems are crucial for personalizing user experiences but often depend on implicit feedback data, which can be noisy and misleading. Existing denoising studies involve incorporating auxiliary information or learning strategies from interaction data. However, they struggle with the inherent limitations of external knowledge and interaction data, as well as the non-universality of certain predefined assumptions, hindering accurate noise identification. ## Technical note

LLaRD leverages LLMs to denoise noisy implicit feedback for recommendation. It first enriches semantic insights from observational data and infers user-item preference knowledge, then applies a Chain-of-Thought technique over user-item interaction graphs to reveal relational denoising knowledge, and finally uses the Information Bottleneck principle to align LLM-generated denoising knowledge with recommendation targets, filtering irrelevant LLM noise. On three benchmarks with two backbone recommenders, LLaRD improves denoising and recommendation accuracy over prior side-information and interaction-data denoising methods.

## Summary

Current sequential recommendation models primarily use the order of user-item interactions, ignoring the actual time gaps between them. This limitation hinders the capture of both short-term and long-term temporal dynamics in user behavior. To address this, the paper introduces RoTE, a novel module that incorporates multi-level time embeddings derived from interaction timestamps, allowing models to better perceive temporal distances within sequences.

RoTE decomposes interaction times into multiple granularities (fine, medium, coarse) and integrates these representations into item embeddings. It is designed as a lightweight, plug-and-play module compatible with existing Transformer-based recommendation models, requiring no architectural changes.

Experiments on three benchmark datasets show that adding RoTE consistently improves the performance of backbone models, achieving up to a 20.11% gain in NDCG@5, demonstrating its effectiveness and generality.

## Technical note

RoTE (Rotary Time Embedding) is a lightweight plug-and-play module that injects actual interaction time spans, not just order, into Transformer-based sequential recommenders. It decomposes each Unix timestamp into cumulative year/month/day granularities and applies level-specific rotary transformations (RoPE-style, with a per-level inverse-frequency base) to query and key vectors, producing three time-aware representations that are fused with scalar weights (larger weights on coarse levels for long-term evolution, finer for short-term intent). RoTE leaves values, attention formulation, and training objective unchanged, so it drops into existing discriminative and generative backbones without architecture edits. On three Amazon 5-core benchmarks it consistently improves backbones, up to +20.11% NDCG@5.

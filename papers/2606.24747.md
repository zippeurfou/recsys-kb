## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of deploying large language models (LLMs) by establishing empirically derived scaling laws for their domain-specific compression. Focusing on quantitative finance as an example, it compares different distillation techniques under iterative pruning and introduces a novel blended chain-of-thought supervision loss to stabilize performance during compression. The key finding reveals that while general knowledge degrades faster than in-domain quality with aggressive compression, using chain-of-thought supervision can actively recover lost general knowledge compared to simpler logit-based methods.

## Technical note

This paper derives empirical scaling laws for task-specific LLM compression, quantifying how in-domain and general-knowledge performance scale with dataset size, compression ratio, supervision format, and iterative pruning schedule, grounded in a 35-class financial-headline event-classification task. It combines lightweight-NAS joint depth/width structured pruning (width via attention-entropy/weight-norm proxies, depth via Block Influence cosine scores) with distillation to recover performance, compressing a Qwen3-32B teacher to 16% of its parameters while retaining meaningful task quality. It compares LoRA-based vs logit-based distillation under label-only vs chain-of-thought supervision, introducing a blended supervision loss that independently weights label and CoT tokens to stabilize sequence-level KL-divergence distillation over reasoning traces. The key finding is that supervision format governs the tradeoff: in-domain quality degrades predictably under compression while general-knowledge benchmarks collapse much earlier, and blended CoT supervision actively recovers general knowledge that pruning erases, whereas label-only KD preserves or worsens it. Self-distillation baselines show the training process itself accounts for ~2x the gap from compression; the FinHeadlineMix dataset is released.

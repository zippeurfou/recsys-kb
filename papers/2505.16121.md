## Summary

This paper addresses a gap in recommender systems research by proposing a new approach focused on capturing users' emotions during interactions. It introduces novel theoretical frameworks and metrics specifically designed to analyze user emotional states when using these systems. Additionally, the authors present visualization techniques to track emotional changes over customer lifetime cycles and provide an algorithm framework based on this emotion-centric data, supported by experimental results demonstrating its effectiveness.

## Technical note

The paper proposes an emotion-based recommender that infers user emotion purely from online rating behavior (no sensors), defining an Emotional Score that flags when a user dislikes a popular item or likes an obscure one relative to item average rating and occurrence count. It instantiates the idea as Emotion-based Matrix Factorization (EMF), deriving SGD update rules that add an emotion-regularization term to the standard MF loss, and visualizes emotional scores over MovieLens-1M and LDOS-CoMoDa as colormaps revealing Matthew-effect structure. Experiments show EMF does not top accuracy (MAE) versus baselines like ZeroMat/DotMat but improves the fairness metric (Degree of Matthew Effect).

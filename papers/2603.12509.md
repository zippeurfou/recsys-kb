## Summary

This study investigates how user comments contribute to video-based task learning, which often omits tacit knowledge and requires adaptation. Interviews and co-watching sessions with users revealed comments are vital for learning but poorly integrated into the various stages of the task-learning process. The research identifies design opportunities to better leverage comments for improved user experiences.

## Technical note

This is a qualitative HCI study of how viewers use the comment section during video-based task learning (e.g., cooking, programming, music), where videos omit tacit knowledge and may not match a viewer's specific circumstances. Through exploratory interviews with 14 users and co-watching sessions with 8, the authors find that comments act as a collaborative sensemaking resource used across all stages of learning: finding videos, actively performing the task, and reflecting afterward. Despite this importance, comments are poorly integrated as a learning tool and remain treated mainly as a social feature. The paper identifies design opportunities to better surface and integrate comments into the task-learning workflow, turning them into a first-class extension of the learning environment rather than a purely social channel.

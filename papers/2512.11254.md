## Summary

Existing transformer-based recommendation systems often require discretizing items into multiple code tokens, significantly increasing sequence length and making it hard to focus on relevant user behavior due to noise. FAIR addresses this by introducing a focused attention mechanism that learns separate Q/K weights and computes their difference for attention scores, effectively suppressing noise. The framework also incorporates objectives for noise robustness and maximizing informative context, leading to superior performance on standard benchmarks compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

FAIR is a generative recommendation framework that addresses attention noise arising when items are discretized into long semantic-ID code sequences, causing transformers to over-attend to irrelevant context. Its focused attention mechanism, inspired by the differential amplifier, learns two separate sets of Q/K weights and subtracts their attention scores to suppress noise while emphasizing relevant context. Two auxiliary objectives support this: a noise-robustness task enforcing consistency under stochastic perturbations, and a mutual information maximization task selecting contexts most informative for next-item prediction, all within a non-autoregressive multi-token-prediction model. Experiments on four public benchmarks show FAIR outperforms both generative and traditional sequential recommenders.

## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Modern recommendation systems face limitations in scaling due to high production costs from processing each user request against many candidates separately. Current methods lack efficient compute sharing strategies suitable for recommendation inference.
2.  **Solution:** The authors propose ROCS (Request-Oriented Compute Sharing), a paradigm designed specifically for recommendation tasks by deferring candidate-specific computations and evaluating shared parts only once per request, rather than once per candidate interaction.
3.  **Results:** To implement ROCS efficiently on GPUs, they developed IKBO optimizations. Experiments show that ROCS improves both recommendation prediction quality (e.g., slightly better LogLoss) and inference efficiency (e.g., up to a 3x QPS improvement), enabling significant performance gains at reduced infrastructure costs across diverse production systems.

## Technical note

Recommendation scaling laws say quality grows with compute, but each request is scored against many candidates and conventional early request-candidate fusion forces the entire network to rerun per candidate. ROCS restructures models so a request-side subgraph stays candidate-independent and is evaluated once per request: Generalized Layer Masking imposes an operator-level invariant that output group i depends only on input groups 0..i (block lower-triangular weight masks for linear and linear-compression layers, group-local LayerNorm/RMSNorm, masked factorization-machine interaction blocks) and is closed under composition; Deep Cross Attention encodes the user behavior sequence request-only and issues separate request- and candidate-side queries into the shared per-layer representation so sequence encoding and K/V projections are computed once while candidate-conditioned retrieval is preserved at every depth; Request-Oriented Resource Reallocation reinvests the savings into request-side capacity via a scaling ratio r. In-Kernel Broadcast Optimization keeps request and candidate tensors at their natural batch sizes with an index map, fusing broadcast-and-add into the candidate GEMM epilogue (Triton plus TLX persistent warp-specialized mega-kernel, bidirectional tile scheduling, zero-pad alignment) and specializing a FlashAttention-3 kernel to read request-side K/V directly. On KuaiVideo/KKBox/KuaiRand with DCNv2, FinalMLP, and Wukong backbones, ROCS-Base cuts amortized FLOPs at N=100 by 30-91% with modest AUC loss while ROCS-Scaled matches or beats the vanilla backbone at comparable amortized budget (Wukong on KuaiRand 0.8715 vs 0.8673 AUC), and it is competitive with the RankMixer-specific UGSEP request-sharing baseline while generalizing across interaction modules. On three production workloads of over 100B examples each, ROCS gives +196% QPS at on-par LogLoss for ads retrieval, -0.5% relative LogLoss with +47% QPS for short-form video ranking, and on-par LogLoss with +62% QPS for ads ranking; IKBO drops the LCB kernel from 1.944 ms to 0.482 ms and DCA attention from 0.55 ms to 0.23 ms (594 vs 250 TFLOPs/s, IO 681 vs 2193 GB/s), and deployments across tens of Meta models yield e.g. 0.04% topline with 32% capacity savings on short-form video ranking and 0.2% topline with 29% savings on ads retrieval.

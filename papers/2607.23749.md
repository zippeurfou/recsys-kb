## Summary

This paper details empirical online A/B tests conducted by YouTube Music to combat recommendation feedback loops causing reduced novelty and freshness (new releases). Despite various well-known academic intervention strategies applied directly to the ranking model or serving layer, continuous training in live systems presented significant challenges. The study found that uncertainty-driven exploration using a Spectral-normalized Neural Gaussian Process head yielded the best results for new release recommendations but at the cost of measurable engagement drops and diversity issues; architectural debiasing improved system diversity but didn't directly enhance discovery, while simpler serving-time heuristics were largely ineffective against the learning loop.

## Technical note

On the YouTube Music homepage, continuously retrained rankers form feedback loops that suppress new releases (freshness) and unlistened catalogue items (novelty). The paper runs head-to-head online A/B tests of six interventions across four layers, serving (recency boost, release-age diversification), training-data (recency up-weighting, IPS), architecture (a PAL/WatchNext-style position/client bias tower), and exploration (an SNGP uncertainty head applied as a serving boost or as a training-loss reweighting), plus a bias-tower x uncertainty combination. Serving heuristics are neutralized by the learning loop within days; the bias tower maximally redistributes popularity (+9.2% bottom-20% impressions, +4.70% novel-music) but drops 7-day new-release engagement -3.39%; SNGP uncertainty gives the largest new-release lift (+7.75%/+3.30% at serving). The central lesson is that debiasing does not equal freshness, and the intervention layer determines which symptom is fixed and its hidden integration costs.

## Summary

This paper focuses on improving off-policy evaluation (OPE), which uses logged historical data to estimate the effectiveness of new policies in domains like e-commerce or healthcare. Standard estimators, such as IPS, often perform poorly when the logging policy differs significantly from what is being evaluated due to a lack of information sharing between actions. The authors propose an alternative estimator that clusters similar contexts and shares information across them within each context cluster, demonstrating it provides better estimation accuracy compared to existing methods.

## Technical note

The paper addresses off-policy evaluation (OPE) in contextual bandits, where IPS-style estimators degrade when the logging and evaluation policies differ or when many actions have near-zero logging probability. Building on Marginalized IPS (which pools information across similar action embeddings), the authors instead pool information across similar contexts via clustering, deriving the CHIPS estimator. They analyze its bias and variance under different conditions, showing the clustering assumption reduces variance in deficient-information settings. Experiments on synthetic problems and a real-world recommendation dataset confirm clustering contexts improves estimation accuracy, especially where action-level information is sparse.

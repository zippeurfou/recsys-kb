## Summary

This paper addresses challenges in CTR (Click-Through Rate) prediction, specifically high computational demands and overfitting risk from complex feature interactions, alongside potential imbalance between explicit user features and implicit context/user behavior features. To solve this, the authors introduce Dual-Stream MLP (DS-MLP), which uses knowledge distillation to let a main Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) learn explicit feature interactions effectively while another parallel MLP captures implicit ones as complementary information. Experiments show that despite its simple vanillac MLP structure in practice, DS-MLP achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks and offers a more scalable and efficient solution for large-scale recommendation systems compared to existing dual-stream architectures.

## Technical note

DS-MLP is a dual-stream MLP framework for CTR prediction that replaces intricate explicit-implicit architectures with plain MLPs trained via a gradual distillation -> alignment -> overall-optimization procedure. A three-layer main MLP is distilled from a capable teacher (GDCN, a gated deep cross network) on logits with a temperature coefficient, empirically absorbing explicit feature interactions, while a parallel MLP is added to capture implicit interactions. Two alignment strategies reconcile the two streams: hidden-state alignment via batch normalization and prediction alignment via direct task supervision, addressing the imbalanced-fusion problem where the explicit component's growing numerical range dominates. Despite being a vanilla MLP at inference, DS-MLP reaches state-of-the-art on three CTR benchmarks with low latency.

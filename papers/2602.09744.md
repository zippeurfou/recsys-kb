## Summary

1.  Existing sequential recommendation models using latent reasoning often rely on deterministic chains, ignoring inherent user intent uncertainty and hindering optimization through staged training.
2.  To address these issues, the authors propose DiffuReason, a "Think-then-Diffuse" framework combining multi-step reasoning (Thinking Tokens), diffusion-based refinement (denoising), and joint training via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
3.  DiffuReason generates initial intent hypotheses and then iteratively refines them as probabilistic distributions, with all components co-trained end-to-end, leading to improved ranking performance demonstrated on benchmarks and in online A/B tests.

## Technical note

DiffuReason is a 'Think-then-Diffuse' framework for sequential recommendation that treats latent reasoning as a probabilistic condition rather than a deterministic chain. In the Think stage it generates auto-regressive Thinking Tokens over the backbone hidden states (via attention+MLP) and pools them into a condition vector; in the Diffuse stage a MLP denoising network, warm-started by injecting noise into the last thinking token and conditioned on that vector, iteratively refines the coarse intent into a Gaussian (mu, sigma) that yields a stable anchor plus stochastic samples capturing intent uncertainty. To align the point-wise diffusion (usually MSE) with list-wise top-k ranking and to avoid disjointed two-stage training, it co-trains backbone, reasoning, and refinement end-to-end with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using recommendation rewards. It is model-agnostic across ID-based (SASRec) and Semantic-ID (TIGER/RQ-VAE) backbones, with gains confirmed on four benchmarks and online A/B tests.

## Summary

Standard sparse autoencoders enforce non-negativity, forcing separate latents for opposing concepts and wasting capacity on anticorrelated features. The Sign-Aware Gated Sparse Autoencoder (SA-GSAE) addresses this by using a two-sided gate, a signed-magnitude path avoiding L1 shrinkage, auxiliary loss, and a Bi-Jump-ReLU activation enabling one latent to encode both signs (bipolar sharing). Experiments show that a half-width SA-GSAE significantly reduces dead weights and matches or improves performance compared to a full-width Gated SAE across various LLM hookpoints, demonstrating parameter efficiency.

## Technical note

The Sign-Aware Gated SAE (SA-GSAE) addresses the waste in standard sparse autoencoders, whose non-negativity forces separate latents for diametrically opposed concepts (e.g., pressure-too-high vs pressure-too-low). Its mechanism is two-sided gated sparsity with signed magnitude and auxiliary supervision: a polarity-sensitive gate selects support on either sign, a signed-magnitude path avoids L1 shrinkage, and an auxiliary reconstruction loss prevents gate collapse. A Bi-Jump-ReLU activation enables bipolar sharing where one latent encodes both signs along a shared decoder direction, keeping the design parameter-efficient even when anticorrelated pairs are rare. On Pythia-1B and SmolLM3-3B activations across three mid-depth hookpoints, a half-width SA-GSAE Pareto-dominates a full-width Gated SAE on 3 of 6 cells and matches R^2 within 0.025 on the rest while cutting dead fraction by 0.35-0.62 absolute (~100x-500x geomean on MLP-output cells). Ablations mark the two-sided gate and auxiliary loss as load-bearing, and recommend the symmetric-magnitude variant as default.

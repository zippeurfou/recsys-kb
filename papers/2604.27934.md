## Summary

Multimodal Stance Detection (MSD), crucial for analyzing public discourse, faces challenges in effectively combining text and image data, especially when they conflict. Existing methods struggle with contextual grounding, interpretation ambiguity, and weak reasoning. To address these issues, the paper introduces MM-StanceDet, a multi-agent system incorporating retrieval augmentation, specialized multimodal agents, a debate stage, and self-reflection, demonstrating significantly improved performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

## Technical note

MM-StanceDet is a retrieval-augmented multi-agent framework for multimodal (text+image) stance detection that targets conflicting cross-modal signals, contextual grounding gaps, and single-pass reasoning fragility. It processes each instance through four sequential stages: a Retrieval Augmentation stage that grounds analysis by retrieving relevant few-shot examples from a database; a Multimodal Analysis stage with specialized agents dissecting textual, visual, and cross-modal-conflict perspectives; a Reasoning-Enhanced Debate stage that simulates argumentation among agents representing different stances; and a Self-Reflection and Adjudication stage that critically evaluates the debate and intermediate analyses to output a final stance label (support/neutral/oppose). The design leverages MLLM/VLM reasoning while adding structure to resolve modality-specific ambiguities and hallucinations that raw multimodal prompting fails to handle. Experiments on five multimodal stance datasets show it outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, with ablations validating each stage.

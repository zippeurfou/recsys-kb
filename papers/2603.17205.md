## Technical note

OPERA is a data-pruning framework for finetuning dense retrievers that exploits the heterogeneous quality of query-document training pairs. It first shows static pruning (keeping only high-similarity pairs) exposes a quality-coverage tradeoff -- NDCG improves but Recall degrades from lost query diversity -- then proposes two-stage dynamic pruning that adaptively modulates sampling probabilities at query and document granularities via cosine-scheduled soft thresholds while retaining the full training set. Across eight datasets in six domains and both encoder-only (BGE) and LLM-based (Qwen3-Embedding) retrievers, dynamic pruning achieves the best ranking (NDCG@10 +1.9%) and retrieval (Recall@20 +0.7%) with average rank 1.38 and reaches comparable performance in under 50% of standard finetuning time.

- Level 2: Does not answer the question, or provides an unreasonable answer, but is not out of context (23.1% of the training data)

- Test set: We maintain the standard evaluation criterion, considering only documents with relevance levels of 3 and 4 as positive samples 
- Training set: Documents with relevance levels of 2, 3, and 4 were treated as positive samples. The inclusion of level 2 documents, which are documented as insufficient answers, deliberately introduces noise into the positive samples. We did not include level 1 documents as positive samples since they represent a small portion (4.6%) of the training data and are more like random noise, which differs from real-world scenarios where noisy samples are typically hard negatives that share some relevance with the query (arXiv:2603.17205)

## Summary

1.  The paper introduces OPERA, a framework for pruning training data to improve dense retriever adaptation efficiency, addressing the issue that not all query-document pairs contribute equally during finetuning.
2.  It proposes two methods: static pruning (SP), which removes low-similarity pairs and improves ranking but risks reducing recall, and dynamic pruning (DP), which adaptively prioritizes high-quality examples throughout training while maintaining access to the full dataset.
3.  Evaluations show DP significantly outperforms standard finetuning on both ranking (NDCG) and retrieval (Recall) metrics across diverse datasets and architectures, while also achieving comparable performance much faster, often in under half the training time.

## Technical note

OPERA is a data-pruning framework for domain-specific fine-tuning of dense retrievers, exploiting that query-document pairs contribute unequally. Static Pruning (SP) keeps only top cosine-similarity pairs and reveals an intrinsic quality-coverage tradeoff: NDCG ranking improves but Recall degrades because pruning removes queries with few high-quality documents, breaking the balanced two-stage (query then document) contrastive sampling. Dynamic Pruning (DP) resolves this with soft sampling modulation over the full dataset via hierarchical query- and document-level scores from the running model, cosine-scheduled sampling strengths (alpha, beta) that sharpen selection over training, and an update interval keeping overhead ~1.6%. Across eight datasets in six domains on BGE-large and Qwen3-Embedding, DP achieves the best average rank (NDCG@10 +1.9%, Recall@20 +0.7%) and reaches comparable performance in under half the training time; a theoretical result shows any scoring above the noise rate improves representations, motivating a combined SP-then-DP denoising strategy.

## Summary

This paper addresses a key challenge in LLM-based recommendation systems: efficiently handling the vast number of discrete item IDs (millions) found in large product catalogs, which contrasts with the smaller vocabularies typically used by language models. It challenges the common practice of breaking items into multiple tokens for ID representation and instead proposes treating item IDs as first-class citizens within the LLM framework. The authors introduce simple training and inference modifications enabling single-token representations and single-step decoding, demonstrating significant gains in recommendation quality (Recall/NDCG) while boosting inference efficiency by 5-14x on Amazon datasets.

## Technical note

This paper challenges the prevailing practice of tokenizing items into multiple tokens for LLM-based recommendation, instead treating each item ID as a single direct embedding (first-class citizen) to enable single-step decoding and low latency. To scale the softmax over millions of items it introduces a Two-Level softmax with precomputed clusters (~sqrt(|I|) clusters), giving sublinear training and inference via either hierarchical-structure pruning or ANN search. A single-phase training paradigm randomly samples item metadata (25% ID-only) so inference can be ID-only, removing multi-stage pretraining. On Amazon datasets it beats CALRec/Recformer and non-LLM baselines on Recall/NDCG/MRR while improving inference efficiency 5x-14x.

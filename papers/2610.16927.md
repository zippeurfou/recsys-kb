## Summary

1.  Short-video search requires fast and personalized query rewrites, but existing methods often fail to preserve user intent, potentially introducing unsupported details. The paper addresses this challenge by proposing **WeFair**, a fidelity-aware rewriter trained using real user behavioral data.
2.  **WeFair** utilizes human feedback by mining actual reformulations, employing LLM screening and verification for contextual relevance, and structuring training progressively from demonstrations to preference alignment, minimizing the need for manual annotation.
3.  Evaluated through online A/B testing in WeChat, **WeFair** led to a significantly higher rate of queries with long video views (effective clicks) and fewer unnecessary rewrites, demonstrating effective improvement over the baseline system while maintaining user intent fidelity.

## Technical note

WeFair trains a compact personalized query rewriter for WeChat short-video search that must decide when to rewrite and avoid intent drift (rewrites that add unsupported details or redirect the query), a failure mode of consumption-reward-aligned rewriters. Supervision is mined from real session reformulations, filtered by hard lexical rules, Gemini-based LLM screening for intent continuity, information gain and contextual bridging, and a transfer-verification gate, then matched by fidelity to three stages: a three-phase curriculum SFT (context-to-query, rationale, direct rewrite), DPO with pairs built from human reformulations or consumption-supported candidates vs zero-consumption negatives (top-20 vs top-100 retrieval), and GRPO with a strict exact-match verification reward against the human reformulation or the preservation marker. The deployed policy outputs the rewrite or a preservation decision directly with no chain-of-thought. Online A/B in WeChat short-video search gave +1.576% effective clicked-query rate (VV>10s) and -1.731% query reformulation rate; analysis shows auxiliary graded-similarity, retrieval or judge rewards activate more GRPO rollout groups but reduce intent fidelity.

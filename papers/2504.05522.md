## Summary

Exploration, the act of broadening user experiences beyond their established preferences, is challenging in large-scale recommendation systems due to feedback loops and limited signals on user exploration patterns. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer potential solutions by leveraging their world knowledge to recommend novel content outside these loops. ## Technical note

This Google paper tackles exploration in large-scale recommendation by combining LLM hierarchical planning with inference-time scaling to recommend novel content beyond feedback loops without sacrificing relevance. It decouples objectives into two specialized LLMs: a novelty model whose inference is scaled up via high-temperature repeated sampling, and a user-aligned model trained on aggregated collective implicit feedback that selects best-of-n predictions. Deployed on a commercial short-form video platform serving billions of users, live experiments show significant gains in user satisfaction (watch activity, active users) and exploration diversity, after direct fine-tuning and RLHF-with-reward-model approaches failed.

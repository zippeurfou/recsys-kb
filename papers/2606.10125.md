## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of selecting high-quality few-shot examples in text-to-SQL systems using LLMs, where expert annotation is costly. It formalizes example selection as a constrained experimental design problem over query embeddings' low-dimensional manifold. The authors propose a novel heteroscedastic mutual information objective and prove its submodularity properties, leading to a stratified greedy algorithm that provides theoretical guarantees and reduces labeling effort significantly while maintaining retrieval accuracy empirically.

## Technical note

This work formalizes active selection of annotated few-shot examples for text-to-SQL as constrained Gaussian-process experimental design over the low-dimensional manifold of semantic query embeddings. It models expected SQL correctness as a GP with heteroscedastic, query-dependent noise, and enforces spatial diversity via a partition matroid that permits at most one selection per semantic cluster. The SHARP algorithm is a stratified greedy maximizer of a heteroscedastic mutual-information objective; the authors prove the objective is submodular and approximately monotone on the intrinsic manifold, yielding a constant-factor approximation, and prove the exact matroid-constrained problem NP-complete. A spectral mismatch bound shows the guarantee degrades gracefully under surrogate-kernel misspecification. Manifold (k-NN) distance governs clustering while an isotropic Matern kernel on L2-normalized embeddings handles local smoothness, reducing labeling effort while maintaining retrieval accuracy.

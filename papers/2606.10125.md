## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of selecting the most informative queries to annotate for few-shot learning in text-to-SQL systems, framing it as constrained experimental design on a low-dimensional manifold of query embeddings.
2.  It proposes a stratified greedy algorithm maximizing a heteroscedastic information-gain objective, proving its 1/2-approximation guarantee under certain constraints, though the guarantee degrades significantly with kernel misspecification.
3.  Extensive experiments show that this stratified selection, which involves choosing one example per semantic domain initially, consistently improves downstream SQL performance compared to random or distance-based methods, and the practical recommendation is to follow this simple domain coverage strategy right from the beginning.

## Technical note

NVIDIA treats choosing which unlabeled questions to send for expert SQL annotation, to build the few-shot example bank an LLM text-to-SQL system retrieves from, as Gaussian-process experimental design over the low-dimensional manifold of query embeddings (Levina-Bickel intrinsic dimension about 18.4 against an ambient 2048). Their SHARP algorithm greedily maximizes a heteroscedastic mutual-information objective under a partition-matroid constraint that allows at most one query per spectral cluster of a UMAP-30 kNN graph, estimates per-query noise from LLM self-consistency (edit-distance diversity of five samples at temperature 0.7), and uses a deliberately rough Matérn-1/2 surrogate kernel. The authors prove exact selection is NP-complete and that the objective stays submodular and approximately monotone, which gives greedy a 1/2-approximation (minus a discretization term) that loses only an additive (3K/4)log(c2/c1) under spectral kernel misspecification. On a 337-query NVIDIA supply-chain pool seeded with five examples from a single domain, SHARP covers 6 of 7 test domains by n=10, against n=15 for a max-min-distance baseline and never for random. With Llama-3.1-70B generating SQL from the selected bank, SHARP ends at n=50 with SemanticScore 0.583 versus 0.548 (max-min distance) and 0.524 (random) and table-match rate 0.505 versus 0.355, and with realistic LLM-scored GP labels it beats random 0.658 to 0.550.

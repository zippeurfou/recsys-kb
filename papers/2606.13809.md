## Summary

The authors address the challenge of balancing efficiency and fidelity in diffusion model-based style transfer. They propose i2L (image-to-LoRA), which predicts LoRA weights from reference images to instantly apply styles without dedicated per-style optimization or separate training processes like other methods require. Experiments demonstrate that i2L improves stylization quality, prompt alignment, and perceptual results on various text-to-image models while enabling multi-reference fusion and asymmetric control guidance.

## Technical note

i2L (image-to-LoRA) amortizes per-style LoRA training into a single forward pass by predicting LoRA weight updates for a text-to-image diffusion model directly from one or more reference images, avoiding both slow per-style optimization and the weak fidelity/semantic leakage of adapter-based conditioning. The architecture pairs an image encoder with a transformer using learnable LoRA queries aligned to the row-and-column structure of LoRA matrices and compressed decoding heads, scaling to multiple ranks and layers while staying compact. It is trained end-to-end through frozen backbones with a flow-matching objective on semantically diverse style pairs (MegaStyle-1M) to preserve appearance while suppressing reference-content copying. On Z-Image, FLUX.2, and Hidream-O1 it improves style fidelity, prompt alignment, and perceptual quality, and because it produces explicit LoRA weights it supports asymmetric classifier-free guidance and multi-reference style fusion.

## Summary

Credit Assignment via Set Intervention for Agentic Retrieval":

1.  When large language models generate multiple retrieval actions, standard single-reward methods (like broadcast) fail to accurately assign credit to individual actions within the retrieved set due to sensitivity to the coalition of actions.
2.  The paper introduces CASI, a credit assignment method using "snapshot-grounded set intervention" to evaluate alternative subsets containing specific actions, and then uses Shapley aggregation (with zero-sum shaping) to convert these evaluations into individual action credit scores for policy optimization.
3.  Experiments show that CASI significantly outperforms baseline credit assignment methods (Singleton and Broadcast) during training, achieving higher set-retrieval performance and demonstrating a real-world 3.51% lift in the advertiser-value metric in a production A/B test.

## Technical note

In Xiaohongshu sponsored search an LLM policy jointly generates m=5 query rewrites whose retrieved ad pools are merged before ranking and auction, so only the set receives a reward and broadcasting it gives no coalition-dependent per-action credit; the paper proves that singleton, leave-one-out and full-set values do not identify the Shapley allocation for m>=4. CASI generates and retrieves once, caches per-action results, re-scores all 2^m-1 coalitions with a frozen training-time evaluator under a fixed snapshot, aggregates the contextual marginals with Shapley weights, and injects the centered, clipped zero-sum credits as per-action advantage offsets on top of the GRPO-style rollout advantage (beta=0 recovers Broadcast). Exhaustive auction replay over 1,200 requests (117,800 executions) shows 64.4% of action-pair interactions are negative; CASI credits reach median cosine 0.424 to the replay-defined Shapley reference vs 0.388 for Singleton and 0.227 for LOO, though not under a coalition-rerun evaluator. Across five seeds CASI-trained policies obtain held-out replay value 66.70+-3.40 vs 55.00 (Singleton), 43.30 (Broadcast) and 60.32 (SFT init), and a 7-day 10%-traffic production A/B shows +3.51% advertiser value and +5.08% search-ad spend.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently finding optimal model-role assignments in large language model (LLM) agentic workflows, where users value different trade-offs between task quality and deployment cost. The authors introduce Cost-Coupled Gittins (CC-Gittins), a novel multi-objective search algorithm based on the Gittins index that specifically considers the coupling between evaluation and deployment costs. They demonstrate through extensive benchmarks that CC-Gittins can rapidly and effectively recover high-quality Pareto-optimal solutions, balancing multiple objectives including latency, with significantly reduced evaluation expense compared to exhaustive methods.

## Technical note

Multi-role LLM agent workflows (planner, solver, critic) need a model assigned to each role, assignments must be evaluated end to end because roles interact, and users want a range of quality vs deployment-cost (and latency) trade-offs without exhaustively evaluating up to 1,681 candidate assignments. CC-Gittins (Microsoft/Columbia) extends cost-aware Gittins-index search to multi-objective anytime Pareto recommendation: a quality controller and a cost controller alternate turns, each picking the assignment with the highest Gittins index where the per-batch evaluation price is tied to that assignment's own deployment cost (kappa_i ~ B*D_i), sharing one Gaussian posterior and independently relaxing their price multipliers; after each batch the recommendation is the set nondominated under quality lower-confidence-bound and cost upper-confidence-bound estimates, so users can stop at any time. Against EGE-SH, APE-k, qNEHVI and two random-search baselines on eight replayed matrices (HotpotQA, MathQA and six SCOPE text-to-SQL and data workflows) it recovers much of the frontier at about 10% of exhaustive evaluation cost (BIRD Dev 88.75% recall at 100% precision; Stack Overflow precision 0.97, recall 0.98) and most of it by 30%, with competitive hypervolume regret and generational distance but weaker IGD (HotpotQA recall 0.58 at 10%). A four-controller variant adds server-reported latency as a third objective with 99.6-100% Pareto precision but 54-80% recall at 30% cost.

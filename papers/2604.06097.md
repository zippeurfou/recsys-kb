## Summary

This paper investigates how query rewriting techniques impact systematic biases (like brevity and repetition) in dense retrievers used within RAG systems. It found that simple LLM-based rewriting offers the best overall bias reduction but fails under combined adversarial conditions, while other methods like pseudo-document generation reduce specific biases. The study clarifies that query-side interventions cannot fix all bias types and provides guidance for choosing techniques based on specific biases, also establishing a taxonomy for retrieval bias sources.

## Technical note

This is the first systematic study of how query enhancement affects systematic dense-retriever biases (brevity, position, literal, repetition) using the controlled ColDeR treatment-control benchmark across five methods and six retrievers. Simple LLM rewriting gives the largest aggregate |t|-statistic reduction (~54%) but fails on the adversarial Foil subset where biases combine, whereas pseudo-document methods (HyDE, Query2Doc) with continual pretraining generalize better. A mechanistic Spearman decorrelation analysis reveals two distinct mechanisms: simple rewriting reduces |t| via increased score variance while often increasing feature-score correlation (position, repetition), whereas CPT variants achieve genuine decorrelation. Effects are strongly retriever-dependent, brevity bias is most robust, and the authors propose a taxonomy separating query-document interaction biases (fixable query-side) from document encoding biases (needing retriever-level fixes).

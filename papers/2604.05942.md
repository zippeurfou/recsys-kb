## Summary

Large language models often use sliding-window attention (SWA) for shorter contexts to reduce computational costs, but existing hybridization methods either work poorly at the layer level or use static rankings for attention heads that don't adapt well.

The paper introduces BOSCH, a novel training-free method that optimizes head-level SWA by performing black-box probes to determine layer importance and then adaptively assigns SWA ratios and optimizes head groups based on this information.

BOSCH consistently outperforms existing layer-level and static head-level methods across different SWA ratios and settings, demonstrating significant performance gains, especially at higher SWA ratios, and faster recovery of long-context capability under continual pretraining.

## Technical note

BOSCH is a training-free method for post-training sliding-window-attention (SWA) hybridization that selects which attention heads to convert to SWA, formulated as constrained binary black-box minimization and solved via Large Neighborhood Search decomposed into three stages: layer-importance detection by small-budget probes, adaptive per-layer SWA-ratio assignment, and grouped head-level search within ratio buckets (respecting GQA groups). It addresses two failures of prior schemes: layer-level granularity ignores that local/global routing happens per-head, and static local-to-global head rankings suffer 'entanglement' since head behavior changes after hybridization. On Qwen3 models (1.7B-30B), 4 SWA ratios, NIAH and LongBench, BOSCH beats 3 layer-level heuristics and 6 static head-level baselines (Fisher/Razor strongest), with widening gains at higher ratios; substantial head turnover across ratios (~26-44%) justifies re-searching per ratio, and continual pretraining (2.5B tokens) recovers long-context performance faster than layer-level methods.

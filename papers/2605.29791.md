## Summary

1.  Large Language Models (LLMs) often show a discrepancy between their explicit self-reported personality traits and their implicit behavioral decisions, a problem called the Knowledge-Decision Gap ($G_{\text{KD}}$).
2.  The paper introduces ActTraitBench, a new evaluation framework based on human data, which measures personality consistency by mapping traits to behavioral tasks and using statistical calibration to ensure reliable, human-norm-comparable scores.
3.  Evaluations reveal that even large, capable LLMs exhibit this Knowledge-Decision Gap, and the authors propose Chain of Cognitive Alignment (CoCA) as a method to improve behavioral consistency, although it doesn't work well for smaller models.

## Technical note

ActTraitBench is a human-grounded framework quantifying the Knowledge-Decision Gap (G_KD) between LLMs' explicit self-reported personality and their implicit behavioral decisions. It establishes one-to-one mappings from 11 micro-situational paradigms to Big Five (BFI-2) facets, using Lewin's Equation B=f(P,E) to control environment and isolate the persona variable, and a two-stage prompting protocol (0-99 quantitative decision then qualitative rationale). Items are validated against empirical human data via Spearman correlation over three refinement rounds (diagonal rho 0.38-0.56), and Distributional Calibration via non-parametric Quantile Mapping aligns LLM-judge score distributions with human norms to remove acquiescence and negativity biases. Across 14 models, larger and more capable models often show stronger behavioral divergence despite consistent self-reports; the plug-and-play Chain of Cognitive Alignment (CoCA) narrows the gap in reasoning-capable models.

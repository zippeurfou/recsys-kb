## Summary

1.  **Problem/Solution:** Existing recommendation systems struggle to model extremely long (up to 100K) user sequences efficiently due to high memory and computational costs. The paper introduces **SequenceO1**, an end-to-end framework designed specifically to overcome these limitations for industrial-scale systems.
2.  **Core Mechanism:** SequenceO1 uses a **Sketch Attention** mechanism to compress the vast user history into a fixed-size, learnable user sketch representation, independent of the specific recommendation target. This sketch retains crucial long-term information.
3.  **Overall Approach/Outcome:** Combined with **Target-conditioned Stacked Target-to-History Cross Attention (STCA)** and practical optimizations like caching, batching, and efficient kernels, SequenceO1 provides compact user representations. This allows it to effectively model long-term preferences alongside short-term interests and achieve consistent performance improvements in real-world billion-scale systems.

## Technical note

ByteDance deploys SequenceO1 at full traffic in Douyin short-video fine-ranking to make end-to-end 100K-length history modeling practical; a year of Douyin consumption reaches order 1e5 interactions per user and the bottleneck is systemic (feature-sample storage, host-device and distributed communication, train and serve compute), not just per-layer FLOPs. The model is compress-then-reason: Sketch Attention learns k globally shared prototypes (k=1K in production, d=128) and computes prototype-token affinities, but normalizes over the prototype axis instead of the token axis so every history token allocates unit mass across prototypes, producing a target-agnostic user-only sketch X_tilde = A X in R^{k x d} whose size is independent of n; N_sa=2 refinement blocks with Add and Norm plus SwiGLU FFN plus action-side SwiGLU fusion of item and action-type embeddings follow. Target-conditioned reasoning then runs STCA (stacked single-query target-to-history cross attention) at two time scales, over the recent 10K suffix and over the fixed-size sketch, fused by MixFormer. System side: a training local KVCache keyed by user id, model version, history timestamp and sketch config with 3h TTL hits about 50 percent, the same interface at serving with 1h TTL hits about 60 percent, plus multi-request user-level batching, pipeline lift that computes the sketch upstream of candidate generation, and FlashSA, a fused kernel that streams block-wise affinity, prototype-wise softmax statistics and aggregation without materializing the k x n score matrix in HBM. At 100K this is 49.9x cheaper in training FLOPs and 63.9x in inference FLOPs than directly scaled STCA while retaining 83 percent of its quality (+1.07 percent vs +1.29 percent Finish AUC over an STCA(512) baseline at 85K average length); under the same 1K x 128 budget SA beats TWIN V2 KMeans clustering (+0.30 percent), chunk mean pooling (+0.46 percent), recent-behavior query init (+0.78 percent), position-bucket queries (+0.72 percent) and Lightning-Attention compression (+0.83 percent). Against the full production STCA-10K plus lifelong TWIN V2 baseline, which SequenceO1 removes entirely, offline AUC improves on all ten objectives (Finish +0.29 percent, Favourite +0.30 percent, Dislike +1.29 percent) with UAUC gains up to +3.63 percent. A one-month online A/B on Douyin and Douyin Lite gave +1.50 percent / +1.67 percent Duration, +2.33 percent / +3.49 percent Finish, +3.59 percent / +8.61 percent Comment, +0.20 percent / +0.23 percent 30-day activeness and -6.98 percent / -6.00 percent Dislike, with the largest lifts on low- and mid-active users.

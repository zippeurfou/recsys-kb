## Summary

The paper addresses the challenge of tuning a multi-objective utility function (ranking) for Pinterest's ad recommender system to balance user experience, advertiser goals, and platform metrics. It proposes Deep Reinforcement Learning for Personalized Utility Tuning (DRL-PUT), where given an 'ad request' state, it learns which hyperparameters yield the best outcome based on predicted rewards from actual serving logs. This approach directly learns a policy model instead of estimating values, overcoming difficulties with traditional methods and improving results by nearly 10% in click-through rates through online evaluation.

## Technical note

DRL-PUT tunes the linear ranking-utility weights of an ad recommender at Pinterest with deep reinforcement learning, replacing static manual hyperparameter tuning. Given an ad-request state it directly predicts optimal weights via a policy-gradient (REINFORCE, gamma=0) MLP over a discretized, semantically-grouped action space, avoiding value-function estimation that is unstable under high-variance rewards. Off-policy training data is collected by uniformly sampling actions on <=0.5% of traffic, and offline diversity/relative-gain metrics guide design choices. Online A/B tests show +9.71% CTR and +7.73% long-CTR on the treated segment, and per-campaign reward customization balances revenue against engagement; visualized weights confirm learned personalization.

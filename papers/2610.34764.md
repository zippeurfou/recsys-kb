## Summary

SLIDE addresses the limitations of generative retrieval (GR) by tackling the problem of underperformance when directly training list-wise models for single greedy decoding. It decouples offline item-level beam search from online list generation, using a teacher model to provide richer supervision through concatenated Semantic IDs (SIDs) instead of sparse labels. Experiments show that SLIDE significantly improves retrieval quality under single greedy decoding and can even surpass traditional beam search approaches by leveraging strong offline teachers.

## Technical note

Taobao's SLIDE tackles generative retrieval (semantic-ID decoding) where list-wise models trained directly and decoded greedily in a single trajectory underperform item-level beam search by 18.4% HR@20 on Taobao Search, attributed to beam search's larger search space and to converted items being placed first in interaction-ordered targets, starving later list positions of positive supervision. SLIDE trains a Qwen2.5 teacher (0.5B/3B/7B) on 12-item positive-first fine-ranking lists, runs offline item-level beam search over the full SID space, concatenates the top K_d=20 retrieved SIDs in beam-score order as a hard list-wise target, and distills a 0.5B student that then emits the whole Top-K list in one greedy pass, decoupling teacher scale from serving. On FORGE and Taobao Search (220M items), the 0.5B student with a 7B teacher reaches HR@10 of 0.0441 (+20.5%) and 0.4578 (+1.8%) relative to the directly trained 0.5B beam-search reference and far above direct greedy decoding (0.0050 and 0.3206), while producing longer valid lists. Ablations show fine-ranking lists beat behavior-only supervision for the teacher (HR@10 0.4498 vs 0.3934), target ordering matters (shuffling drops NDCG@20 from 0.301 to 0.227), K_d should match the target cutoff, and list-level RL gives 3.5-7.7% relative gains vs 0.6-1.2% for item-level RL.

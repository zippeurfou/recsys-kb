## Summary

This paper introduces a new data selection framework based on low-rank approximation (using residual-based sampling) instead of clustering heuristics like $k$-center or sensitivity sampling. It theoretically proves that this method can approximate the average loss over the full dataset with high accuracy using a significantly smaller weighted subset, $\tilde{O}\left(k + \frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}\right)$ points. Empirical tests on various real-world datasets demonstrate improved performance compared to prior clustering-based and uniform sampling methods.

## Technical note

The paper proposes a data-selection framework based on low-rank approximation and residual-based (leverage-score) sampling rather than k-center or k-means clustering used by prior coreset methods. Under mild regularity assumptions on the loss, it selects a weighted subset of O~(k + 1/eps^2) points whose average loss approximates the full-data average loss within (1+eps) relative error plus an additive term proportional to the optimal rank-k approximation cost Phi_k of the embedding matrix. Leverage scores are estimated from a fast low-rank sketch built on pretrained embeddings (BERT/word2vec), and rows are sampled proportionally to preserve the dominant spectral directions. Empirically it beats uniform and clustering-based sensitivity sampling on a tabular dataset and on Llama3-8B fine-tuning across three tasks.

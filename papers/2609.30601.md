## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge in industrial recommender systems of balancing multiple objectives (semantic relevance, engagement, revenue) during retrieval. Existing bi-encoder systems, efficient but dominant, collapse these objectives into a single static embedding space, requiring retraining to shift priorities and risking objective interference. The proposed Embedding Subspace Partitioning (ESP) framework dynamically adapts to changing objective weights at serving time by decomposing the embedding space into task-specific subspaces during a single forward pass of a Transformer bi-encoder, using end-of-sequence tokens. ESP dynamically computes relevant candidates via GPU-accelerated kNN on a single concatenated index, outperforming single-task models and strong multi-task baselines on benchmarks, significantly boosting business metrics in LinkedIn's job matching platform.

## Technical note

LinkedIn's Embedding Subspace Partitioning (ESP) targets the 'no-knob' problem of multi-objective first-stage retrieval: one bi-encoder embedding bakes objective trade-offs into training and suffers interference, which the authors formalize as a capacity bound d >= r(1+(k-1)eps) that grows with subspace conflict and that PCGrad can mitigate but not remove. ESP splits the embedding into per-objective L2-normalized subspaces (EOS-delimited segments with segment-aware attention masks and RoPE position-ID resets in Transformer encoders, or per-objective projection heads on a shared trunk in DNN encoders), trains each with its own loss (InfoNCE with in-batch negatives, MSE on pClick x bid distilled from an L2 ranker, BCE on LLM fitness labels), and scores a serving-time weighted sum of per-subspace dot products over one concatenated index served by GPU exhaustive kNN instead of per-objective ANN indices. On an MS MARCO benchmark with hard entity negatives, one ESP model traces the whole Pareto frontier (0.900 semantic NDCG@10 at 0.999 entity recall), beating multi-task single-head training by +2.5 pp NDCG at matched recall as well as PCGrad, and entity matching saturates at 32 of 1024 dims. In production job matching (70M+ weekly users, k=3 objectives), revenue emphasis cost ESP 0.54% in-batch recall versus 28.24% for a single shared embedding, and retuning serving weights on a single trained model yielded +4.0% revenue, +3.95% job applications and -8.95% no-fit rate in a 7-day A/B test.

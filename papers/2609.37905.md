## Summary

This paper argues that increasing the depth and expressiveness of cross networks for Click-Through Rate (CTR) prediction, a common approach, leads to diminishing returns. Instead, it proposes "estimator scaling," which uses multiple related estimators to capture diverse predictive signals. The authors introduce RECAP, a parameter-efficient recursive model that operationalizes estimator scaling by distilling knowledge from diverse models into a shared backbone, achieving state-of-the-art performance without the high cost of maintaining separate models.

## Technical note

Google shows that scaling the interaction capacity of a single explicit cross-network CTR model (deeper cross layers, more interaction branches) hits diminishing returns, and proposes estimator scaling, which spends extra compute on multiple estimators of the same function; a second-order BCE expansion ties its gain to the curvature-weighted non-shared logit variance tau^2(1-rho)(1-1/M). Diagnostics on Criteo, Avazu, KDD12, ML-1M and KKBox show averaging gains track each source's reducible variance (on Criteo, independent seeds > checkpoints along one trajectory > inference routes through one shared model) and that adding an axis helps in proportion to the residual variance left by the axes already averaged. RECAP (RECursive Averaged Predictor) builds three estimator axes into roughly one model's parameters: a weight-shared recursive FCN backbone (linear and exponential cross-network towers) whose per-step LoRA adapters, picked by a route selector from a small bank, define multiple routes averaged in logit space at inference; an EMA of the weights used for validation and serving; and distillation toward the mean logit of independently trained teachers, all trained across several recursion depths with a decaying learning-rate schedule. RECAP posts the best AUC on all five FuxiCTR benchmarks (81.76 on Criteo vs 81.62 for FCN, +0.55 points on KDD12) and nearly matches a five-model FCN ensemble (81.764 vs 81.771 AUC) at one fifth of its deployed parameters, while doubling FCN's interaction capacity recovers only a third of that ensemble gain. EMA and ensemble distillation also improve the plain FCN backbone, so the gain is not specific to recursion, and results stay stable across EMA decay 0.99 to 0.9999 and KD weight 0.25 to 2.

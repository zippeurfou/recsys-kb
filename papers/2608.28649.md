## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of identifying "meaningful touchpoints" in conversion attribution, where current rule-based methods miss many semantically relevant user interactions.
2.  It systematically evaluates Large Language Models (LLMs) for this task, finding they can uncover a significant portion of implicitly related touchpoints but still require improvement.
3.  The study provides insights into effective LLM prompting and model selection for touchpoint identification, demonstrating their potential to transition conversion attribution towards semantic reasoning and improve downstream recommendation model performance.

## Technical note

Alibaba studies touchpoint selection, the step in conversion attribution that decides which prior user interactions deserve credit for a conversion, on an e-commerce platform with hundreds of millions of daily actives. Existing production rules select only in-shop touchpoints or, following Meta's CABB, taxonomy and collaborative-filtering heuristics, which the authors argue are blind to semantically related touchpoints. They build SILVA, a benchmark of 1,000 conversion events and 69,680 preceding touchpoints inside a 3-day lookback, each enriched with product image, title, shop and category, labeled Irrelevant / Explicit / Implicit by three of six trained annotators with Fleiss kappa above 0.70 and majority vote. Explicitly-related touchpoints are 19.09 percent of the data and implicitly-related ones (functional complementarity such as camera plus memory card, or scenario/audience alignment such as shared anime IPs) are a further 14.91 percent. Eight LLMs are evaluated (GPT-5.5, Gemini-3.1-Pro, Claude-Opus-4.7, GLM-5.1, DeepSeek-V4-Pro and Flash, Qwen3.5-27B, Qwen3.5-30B-A3B) under pairwise versus listwise prompting with per-conversion macro-averaged P/R/F1. Pairwise beats listwise on every model, lifting average overall F1 from 0.6490 to 0.6954, because listwise over-filters and loses recall. Explicit selection is close to solved (best F1 0.9095) but implicit tops out at 0.5426 against a 0.1491 random-guess prior. Strong open-weight models are competitive (GLM-5.1 0.7086, DeepSeek-V4-Pro 0.7056 versus GPT-5.5 0.7197) while sub-100B Qwen variants lag; thinking mode helps listwise more (+0.0196 overall F1) than pairwise (+0.0082). Downstream, LOTUS uses an internal foundation model with pairwise reasoning to attribute conversions at scale and feeds the selected touchpoints as positives to an auxiliary indirect-conversion task in a production CVR model, gaining 0.35 pp absolute GAUC over the in-shop Base and 0.15 pp over heuristic CABB, where 0.1 pp is claimed to be commercially meaningful. Caveat: the downstream result is offline GAUC only with no online A/B, and implicit-touchpoint identification remains far below human agreement.

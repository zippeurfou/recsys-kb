## Summary

Current LLM personalization methods are often expensive, data-intensive, prone to forgetting, and poor in multi-turn interactions. This paper introduces "Personalization Editing," conceptualizing personalization as localized model edits guided by user preference clusters, aiming for efficient and targeted updates while preserving overall model performance. To better evaluate this, the authors also created a new benchmark, "User Preference Question Answering (UPQA)," which tests models' ability to recall and apply specific user preferences, unlike previous benchmarks. Experiments show Personalization Editing achieves higher accuracy and better computational efficiency than fine-tuning, and outperforms other methods in multi-turn conversations and handling implicit preference questions.

## Technical note

This paper reframes LLM personalization as a model-editing task, introducing Personalization Editing, which applies localized parameter edits guided by clustering-based preference representations to align outputs with user preferences while preserving general capabilities. Instead of binding each preference to a single fixed response, preferences are represented as clusters of semantically similar subjects and targets, improving robustness to varied phrasing and enabling recall on implicit queries. The approach builds on editing methods like ROME/MEMIT and PEFT techniques and avoids the cost and catastrophic forgetting of full fine-tuning. The authors also introduce UPQA, a short-answer user-preference QA benchmark from in-situ queries; Personalization Editing achieves higher accuracy and efficiency than fine-tuning and beats prompting baselines in multi-turn and implicit-preference settings.

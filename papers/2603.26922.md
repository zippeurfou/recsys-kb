## Summary

Current AI agents struggle to accurately model individual communication styles without expensive fine-tuning or generic outputs. The paper introduces ASPECT, a system using LLMs to infer detailed communication profiles directly from workplace data, bypassing per-person training. Evaluation shows ASPECT profiles moderately align with self-assessments, and ASPECT-generated communications are often preferred over generic or self-report baselines, helping users identify mischaracterizations and refine their own views.

## Technical note

ASPECT (Automated Social Psychometric Evaluation of Communication Traits) directs LLMs to score constructs from a validated communication scale against behavioral evidence extracted from workplace data, building structured, inspectable communication profiles without per-person fine-tuning. Each dimension score is linked to specific quotes and rationales, functioning as a prompt-based data-compression method that yields profiles portable across models. In a 20-participant case study (1,840 paired item ratings, 600 scenario evaluations), ASPECT-generated profiles showed moderate alignment with self-assessments, and profiled responses were preferred over generic (vanilla GPT) and self-report baselines on aggregate, with substantial per-individual and per-scenario variance. The study also finds a consistent LLM positivity bias that over-rates socially desirable traits, and shows that linked evidence during profile review helps users spot mischaracterizations and recalibrate self-ratings.

## Summary

This paper investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) deployed for verifying subjective recommendations in systems fail due to "reasoning collapse," where complex reasoning gives way to rapid guessing when using standard reinforcement learning methods.

The authors propose two solutions: a conditional length-penalized post-training algorithm that prevents reasoning collapse by linking verification accuracy to controlled reasoning depth, and an architectural approach (persona routing) that dynamically assigns different reasoning styles based on context.

Their findings show significant performance gains from both approaches, demonstrating that matching the LLM's reasoning style ("persona") to specific subjective tasks dramatically improves accuracy, suggesting a path towards better alignment for human-centric AI applications.

## Technical note

Netflix studies whether explicit LLM reasoning transfers from objective math/code tasks to subjective recommender verification, using four in-production binary rubrics (conversational query sensitivity, generated response sensitivity, text response quality, ID response quality) with human ground truth. Reasoning usually hurts: a frontier general-purpose LLM drops from 0.901 to 0.887 macro-F1 on query sensitivity with explicit reasoning and a frontier reasoner at high effort reaches only 0.874; self-consistency at K=8 makes it worse (0.690 -> 0.574). Among open-source models the effect splits by math strength -- Mistral-7B gains on every task while Qwen2.5-7B is inconsistent -- and applying GRPO, Dr. GRPO, or GSPO fails to rescue Qwen (query sensitivity 0.805 -> 0.749/0.758/0.762) because outcome-only rewards trigger 'reasoning collapse': after ~70 steps the policy discovers short heuristic guessing earns equal reward and mean completion length plummets (~300 -> ~170 tokens). Their fix is a conditional length reward, r = r_base + lambda*L only when the answer is correct and L < L_target, which is the only formulation that works -- unconstrained length rewarding degenerates to 8192-token gibberish (F1 0.210) and an unconditional target-constrained penalty produces filler (0.760) -- lifting query sensitivity to 0.851 (~980 tokens) and the hardest ID response-quality rubric to 0.572, above both the reasoning (0.539) and no-reasoning (0.544) baselines. Finally, prompting a frontier LLM to reason as each of 1,500 roleplay personas swings macro-F1 from 0.416 to 0.792 on one sensitivity task, a ~0.38 spread attributable solely to reasoning style, motivating a three-step mid-training blueprint (persona CoT synthesis, SFT, then GRPO used as a contextual bandit that routes which persona's reasoning bias to apply).

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of protecting pretrained language model weights from unauthorized modification by users.
2.  It introduces DLR-Lock, a defense that replaces standard model layers with specialized deep low-rank residual networks (DLR-Nets) during training, making unauthorized fine-tuning difficult due to architectural mismatches and increased computational cost during the backward pass.
3.  Experiments show that this defense successfully protects against attackers with full knowledge of the method while preserving the model's performance.

## Technical note

DLR-Lock is a defense that makes fine-tuning an open-weight model inherently more expensive than fine-tuning the original while preserving inference-time quality, exploiting the inference-training asymmetry of automatic differentiation. Each pretrained MLP is replaced, via two-phase module-wise distillation (vector regression), with a functionally equivalent deep low-rank residual network (DLR-Net) of comparable parameter count. This yields three locking mechanisms: activation memory that grows linearly with depth during backprop, architectural mismatch that destabilizes standard fine-tuning recipes, and a backward pass disproportionately slower than the forward pass. The threat model follows Kerckhoffs's principle (security from structure, not secrecy), and experiments on Qwen3-0.6B show the locked model resists adaptive attackers with full knowledge of the defense while retaining original capabilities.

## Summary

1.  Existing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) struggle with relying solely on local information and handling noisy connections, leading to performance limitations.
2.  The authors introduce $P^2$GNN, a plug-and-play enhancement using two distinct sets of prototypes to address these issues: one set provides global context by acting as universal neighbors, while the other denoises information by aligning messages to clustered prototypes.
3.  Extensive experiments on 18 datasets show that $P^2$GNN significantly improves node recommendation and classification tasks, outperforming existing models and achieving top rankings, validating the benefits of incorporating global context and mitigating local noise.

## Technical note

P^2GNN is a plug-and-play enhancement for message-passing GNNs addressing two limitations: heavy reliance on local context (lacking global/graph-level features) and the strong-homophily assumption that fails on noisy heterophilous neighborhoods. It introduces two learnable class-independent prototype sets: Prototypes-as-Neighbors (P_N), which act as universally accessible pseudo-nodes densely connected to all nodes to inject global context with O(K*|V|) rather than O(|V|^2) cost, and Prototypes-for-Message-Alignment (P_A), which align aggregated messages to clustered prototypes via attention for a denoising effect. Unlike ProtoGNN, its prototypes are learned simultaneously with no pre-training or subset data and remain class- and task-agnostic. Evaluated on 18 datasets (16 public plus 2 proprietary e-commerce) for node recommendation and node classification, it outperforms production models and achieves top average rank.

## Summary

1.  Generative models, like vision-language models (VLMs), can exhibit demographic biases (e.g., misidentifying women as doctors), and while activation steering techniques exist for controlling model behavior, they often struggle to effectively mitigate such biases without significant performance loss.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces Direct Steering Optimization (DSO), which uses reinforcement learning to find specific linear transformations for activation steering tailored to directly reduce bias while maintaining user control over the trade-off between fairness and model performance.
3.  DSO achieves state-of-the-art results in balancing fairness and capabilities on both VLMs and LLMs, providing practitioners with inference-time control over bias mitigation, unlike methods relying on pre-defined heuristics.

## Technical note

The paper introduces Direct Steering Optimization (DSO) to mitigate demographic bias (e.g., gender-occupation stereotypes) in VLMs and LLMs at inference time, motivated by the failure of heuristic activation-steering methods to correct biases requiring equiprobable outcomes across groups. DSO uses reinforcement learning to identify bias-contributing neurons and learn targeted linear transformations of their activations, with an interpretable parameter (lambda) controlling the fairness-vs-capability tradeoff at inference. It provides theoretical guarantees of bias minimization, achieves state-of-the-art fairness-capability tradeoffs, and produces sparse interventions affecting less than 0.005% of parameters.

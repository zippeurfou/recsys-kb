## Summary

This paper introduces AMEN, a novel recommender system framework called Next Interest Flow that aims to proactively model user intent (future interests) rather than just predicting clicks based on past behavior. AMEN learns dense vector sequences representing future user interest flows and overcomes limitations of existing generative methods by using bidirectional alignment techniques between its generative pre-training stage and discriminative fine-tuning stage, along with a Temporal Sequential Pairwise mechanism to better capture temporal causality in item recommendations. Extensive offline experiments show AMEN outperforms strong baselines, and an online A/B test confirms its significant real-world impact by improving key business metrics.

## Technical note

AMEN argues discriminative CTR models only learn local decision boundaries and that discrete generative recommenders suffer information collapse when remapping signals to tokens/IDs. It instead models user intent as a continuous 'Next Interest Flow' trajectory on a latent interest manifold, generated autoregressively by a Transformer decoder and trained with an InfoNCE objective plus kinematic constraints: tangent-frame orthogonality (Frobenius relaxation) for interest diversity and second-order geodesic regularization for trajectory smoothness. A bidirectional alignment (cross-stage weight init plus target-attention semantic alignment) bridges the generative pre-training and discriminative fine-tuning stages, and a Temporal Sequential Pairwise BPR auxiliary task instills temporal causality. On a 6.7B-instance Taobao dataset it delivers +0.87pt AUC and +11.6% CTCVR online with only 1-2 ms added latency.

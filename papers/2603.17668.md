## Summary

Long-context question answering faces challenges with accuracy degrading in large documents and high costs from iterative processing or full-document analysis. The proposed Halo system addresses these by automatically extracting domain knowledge from user prompts and applying it as executable operators (pruning, filtering, ranking) across a multi-stage pipeline, specifically tailored to the type of knowledge provided. Halo significantly improves accuracy by up to 13% and reduces costs by up to 4.8x compared to baselines, while also enabling cheaper models to achieve higher performance.

## Technical note

Halo is a long-context QA framework that extracts user domain knowledge from free-form prompts into executable operators applied at the pipeline stage where each is most effective, instead of leaving it as prompt tokens the LLM may ignore. A KnowledgeParser identifies three directive types: Structural (where to look, prunes non-relevant document regions before chunk selection), Filter (what to ignore, discards irrelevant chunks via a lightweight SLM in a model cascade before the expensive LLM), and Validate (how to verify, re-ranks candidate responses by contrasting positive vs negative validation directives to demote hallucinations without extra LLM calls). A runtime fallback manager detects low-quality operators via model- and operator-based signals and disables them to protect accuracy. Across finance, literature, and scientific datasets Halo reports up to 13% higher accuracy and 4.8x lower cost, letting a lightweight open-source model approach frontier-LLM accuracy at 78x lower cost with only ~2% operator overhead.

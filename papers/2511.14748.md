## Summary

This paper analyzes cloud-native vector search performance, finding that while providers often default to cluster-based indexes, graph-based indexes actually offer superior performance for specific workloads involving high concurrency, high recall needs, large dimensions, or large data types. The study reveals that optimizing search performance on the cloud requires different indexing and parameterization strategies compared to traditional on-disk systems. Finally, the paper explores the integration of cloud caching with vector search, identifying potential conflicts and methods to mitigate them for better performance across different cache sizes.

## Technical note

ByteDance and UIUC present a benchmark and analysis of cloud-native vector search, where ANN indexes live on remote object storage (e.g., Amazon S3 Vector, TurboPuffer) and compute fetches segments while caching hot ones. Although providers default to cluster-based indexes (IVF-PQ, SPANN) because their large posting-list fetch granularity and lack of intra-query dependencies suit remote-storage fetch sizes and round-trips, the study shows graph-based indexes are favored under high concurrency, high recall, high dimensionality, or large datatypes. On-cloud search demands substantially different indexing and search parameterizations than on-disk search. It also finds certain index optimizations conflict with cloud caching and studies mitigations across cache sizes.

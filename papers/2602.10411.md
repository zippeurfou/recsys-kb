## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of predicting the next Point of Interest (POI) in navigation apps, where existing methods fail in sparse, complex environments due to poor modeling of semantic Item Descriptions (SIDs) and misalignment with large language models (LLMs).
2.  To solve this, the authors propose GeoGR, a generative recommendation framework featuring a two-stage approach: first, a geo-aware SID tokenization pipeline using co-visits and contrastive learning to capture spatio-temporal semantics; second, a multi-stage LLM training strategy aligning these tokens via continued pre-training and supervised fine-tuning for POI generation.
3.  Experiments on real-world datasets and successful deployment on the AMAP platform demonstrate GeoGR's superior performance compared to existing methods and its practical effectiveness and scalability for large-scale POI recommendation.

## Technical note

GeoGR is a geographic generative recommendation framework for next-POI prediction on AMAP that unifies semantic-ID learning and autoregressive POI generation in an LLM. Stage one builds geo-aware SIDs: it fine-tunes a Qwen-4B embedding encoder with an NCE contrastive loss over geo-constrained co-visited POI pairs (Swing similarity, 3km filter) to inject spatio-temporal collaborative signals, tokenizes via hierarchical RQ-Kmeans (3 codebooks), then refines SIDs with an EM-style algorithm that alternates LLM fitting and SID reassignment via beam search. Stage two aligns non-native SID tokens through template-based continued pre-training then supervised fine-tuning for next-POI generation conditioned on user context. GeoGR beats baselines on NYC/TKY/AMAP; the deployed 0.6B variant delivers +2.31% WinRate and +2.25% PV_CTR online at ~50ms latency.

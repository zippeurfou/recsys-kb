## Summary

Current test-time scaling for text-to-image diffusion models requires expensive full denoising to evaluate many candidate noise seeds. TTSnap addresses this by using noise-aware pruning to discard low-quality candidates early, before full denoising. To enable this, the authors train specialized reward models via self-distillation and curriculum learning, aligning intermediate estimates' rewards with clean images, and demonstrate their approach significantly improves performance and efficiency over existing methods.

## Technical note

TTSnap accelerates test-time scaling for text-to-image diffusion, where one searches many initial noise seeds and keeps the one maximizing an image-reward, by pruning low-quality candidates before full denoising. The core problem is that reward models trained on clean images rank intermediate (partially denoised) estimates inconsistently; TTSnap fixes this by training noise-aware reward models via self-distillation that align intermediate-estimate rewards (from Tweedie/Euler posterior-mean reconstructions) with the final clean-image reward. A timestep curriculum shifts training from clean to noisy domains and a NegToMe diversity strategy broadens the reward range. It improves performance by over 16% and gives orthogonal gains atop post-training and local test-time optimization.

## Summary

1.  This paper addresses the high cost of hyperparameter search for online learning systems (like recommendation/advertising) that deal with changing data distributions (non-stationarity).
2.  The authors propose a two-stage approach: first, efficiently identify promising hyperparameter configurations using novel data reduction and prediction methods tailored for non-stationary data; second, train *only* those selected configurations fully.
3.  Their method significantly reduces hyperparameter search costs, achieving up to 10x savings on a large public dataset and demonstrating substantial efficiency gains in an industrial setting.

## Technical note

This paper reduces the high cost of hyperparameter search for online-learning recommendation/advertising systems whose data is non-stationary, which breaks conventional HPO methods that assume smooth monotonic learning curves. It proposes a two-stage paradigm: first cheaply identify the most promising configurations (not maximize their performance), then fully train only those, using a performance-based stopping mechanism that generalizes Successive Halving plus trajectory-prediction and stratified-prediction strategies that forecast final performance under distribution shift by using a reference model and stable data slices. Aggressive data reduction is enabled because early identification tolerates suboptimal candidate performance, achieving up to 10x hyperparameter-search cost savings on Criteo 1TB and validated efficiency gains in a much larger industrial ad system.

## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Dense retrievers, while effective for initial candidate generation, struggle to produce good rerankings without using labeled data or expensive cross-encoders, which themselves require costly supervised training and have high latency.
2.  **Solution Proposed:** The paper introduces DART (Dense Adaptive Reranking at Test-time), a method that performs test-time adaptation by modifying the scoring function used for reranking.
3.  **Mechanism & Effectiveness:** For each query, documents ranked near the top are treated as pseudo-positive and those near the bottom as pseudo-negative examples to adapt a bilinear matrix via gradient updates. This approach significantly improves zero-shot reranking performance (achieving +2.1% mean relative NDCG@10 gain) on standard benchmarks with minimal additional latency per query (under 10ms).

## Technical note

DART performs dense adaptive reranking at test time to add reranking to bi-encoder dense retrievers in zero-resource settings, avoiding costly supervised cross-encoders (200-500ms latency) and BM25 reranking that degrades dense results on BEIR. For each query, top-ranked documents are treated as pseudo-positive and bottom-ranked as pseudo-negative, providing noisy supervision to adapt a bilinear scoring matrix W via a few gradient updates. A confidence-weighted margin loss and a cross-query momentum buffer that warm-starts adaptation across queries improve stability. On six BEIR benchmarks DART achieves a mean per-dataset relative NDCG@10 gain of +2.1% over the dense baseline with under 10ms additional latency per query.

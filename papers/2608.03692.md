## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently modeling long user behavior sequences for recommendation while ensuring target-specific interest adaptation. Existing methods either dynamically select relevant parts (losing efficiency) or compress entire histories into general representations (losing target specificity). The authors propose SITA, a novel framework that uses learned semantic identifiers to organize compressed interests into structured groups, allowing adaptive aggregation during prediction based on the specific target item's identifier. This approach combines the benefits of both previous methods and demonstrates superior performance while maintaining strong scalability for large datasets.

## Technical note

Long-sequence CTR modeling forces a trade-off: retrieval-based methods (SIM, TWIN) are target-aware but throw away most of the history, while compression-based methods (C-Former, VISTA) keep global interest but emit one target-agnostic user vector. SITA (Kuaishou) breaks it with (a) Balanced Parallel Quantization, which pushes an item's multimodal embedding through N parallel MLP experts into N independent K-way codebooks (N*K codewords encoding K^N compositional SIDs) under MSE reconstruction + commitment losses plus a usage-balance regularizer against codeword collapse, and (b) 4 stacked Structured Interest Compression blocks that cross-attend N*K learnable interest tokens over the full behavior sequence, refine each of the N semantic groups with a group-specific SwiGLU FFN, and exchange information across groups by self-attention over N mean-pooled group summaries, reducing interaction cost from O((NK)^2) to O(N^2). Because compression is target-agnostic the N*K tokens are computed offline and cached per user (storage O(|U|NK) rather than O(|U||V|)); at serving, SID-Guided Selection indexes the s_n-th token of each group using the candidate's SID to build an N x d target-aware user representation, giving O(BNd) inference. With (N,K)=(16,16) on Taobao-MM and (8,16) on XLong, SITA hits AUC 0.6550/0.9149 and GAUC 0.6175/0.9120, beating C-Former, UxSID, STCA and LONGER; ablations show dropping SID-guided selection hurts most, similarity-based top-N selection recovers only part of it, a balanced (N,K) split at fixed N*K=128 is best, and L=4 blocks is the optimum. In production streaming training with 2,500-length histories it lifts Effective-View AUC/GAUC by +0.057%/+0.049% and +0.078%/+0.076% in two scenarios, where ~0.05% is treated as practically significant.

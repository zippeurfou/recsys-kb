## Summary

This paper addresses the issue of risk control freezing recommendation rules and preventing useful modifications based on observed fractional losses. It proposes a new calibration technique called Averaged Conformal Risk Calibration (ACRC) that averages losses before clipping, improving upon existing methods by maintaining efficiency. Theoretical analysis shows ACRC ensures expected-risk validity and provides pointwise bounds, and empirical results demonstrate significant utility gains ($0.010226$ and $0.008333$) over raw and RB baselines across multiple datasets, achieved by using more realized risk within declared budgets.

## Technical note

Problem: conformal risk control over a frozen recommender's postprocessing rule (reorder, replace or truncate the top-20 list, with a mean-downside budget 0.02 on discounted net utility and 0.01 on Recall) suppresses useful modifications because the standard scalar calibrators waste the information in fractional losses: the raw operator sums losses before clipping and the Rao-Blackwellized operator clips each auxiliary inverse count before averaging. Method: Averaged Conformal Risk Calibration (ACRC) averages first and clips last, w+ = min{1, A * E_B[1/(1+K_B)]} with Bernoulli(l_i) auxiliaries, computed exactly by a Poisson-binomial recurrence; a product identity proves expected-risk validity, Jensen gives no-smaller activation, and finite-population theorems (users assigned without replacement to fitting / selection / calibration / evaluation roles, so sampling covariance is kept) tie the extra activation to signed utility gains of order n^-1 over raw and n^-1/2 over RB under heterogeneous users and switching constraints. Utility is DCG-weighted relevant items minus lambda=1 per irrelevant item over K=20; backbones are LightGCN, XSimGCL and SIGformer (5 seeds, 40 role splits) on KuaiRec, KuaiRand-random, Amazon-Music, Epinions and MovieLens-25M plus a method-frozen Jester confirmation, evaluated only on observed feedback (unobserved pairs are never treated as negatives, candidate lists are observed Top20/Top80). Results: changing only the calibrator raises mean net utility by 0.010226 +/- 0.000401 over raw and 0.008337 +/- 0.000201 over RB (Jester +0.011025 +/- 0.001082), beats information-matched SCoRE (risk or reward e-value scores) by +0.015934 +/- 0.001440 with 16 of 18 intervals excluding zero, and the full procedure exceeds Top20, UMC and both SCoRE paths in all 18 settings while PerK stays ahead on KuaiRand by returning 6-9 item lists; gains come from spending more of the declared risk budget (realized risk 0.0125 vs 0.0111) and vanish when the proposals themselves lack value or exceed budget (KuaiRand Recall downside 5.47x budget). Offline only; the authors state it does not establish gains on full catalogs or future traffic.

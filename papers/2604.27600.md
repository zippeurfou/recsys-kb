## Summary

1.  Existing Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (MRAG) systems often use entire documents as evidence, even though only parts might be relevant, leading to potential noise and hallucinations in the output.
2.  This paper introduces FES-RAG (Fragment-level Evidence Selection for RAG), a new framework that selects only the most relevant "fragments" (small parts of text or visuals) from retrieved documents as evidence for generation.
3.  FES-RAG uses a metric called Fragment Information Gain to identify useful fragments, then distills these judgments into a lightweight selector. Experiments show it significantly improves generation quality, reduces context length, and outperforms previous methods by using fewer, more relevant pieces of evidence.

## Technical note

FES-RAG (Fragment-level Evidence Selection for RAG) addresses multimodal RAG systems that pass entire retrieved documents as evidence, injecting noise and causing hallucinations when only parts are relevant. It selects only the most relevant fine-grained fragments (small text or visual units) from retrieved documents to condition generation. A Fragment Information Gain metric quantifies how useful each fragment is, and these judgments are distilled into a lightweight selector so fragment selection is cheap at inference. By feeding fewer, more relevant pieces of evidence, FES-RAG reportedly improves generation quality, reduces context length, and outperforms prior MRAG methods that operate at document granularity.

## Summary

1.  Existing multimodal embeddings often rely on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which can create redundant thinking steps and introduce semantic ambiguity in answers, hindering efficient retrieval.
2.  To overcome these limitations, the authors propose RIME, a framework that uses retrieval-friendly rewrite generation combined with Cross-Mode Alignment (CMA) to bridge generative and discriminative embeddings, and Refine-RL to optimize the rewrite process against stable semantic anchors.
3.  Experiments show that RIME significantly outperforms prior generative embedding models on various tasks while substantially reducing the length of thinking steps, improving both accuracy and retrieval efficiency.

## Technical note

RIME replaces chain-of-thought reasoning in generative multimodal embeddings with a retrieval-friendly rewrite paradigm: instead of stepwise rationales and a summarized answer, the MLLM structurally recaptions images/videos/documents and expands text without committing to a final answer, avoiding CoT's redundancy, target ambiguity, and inference cost. A single unified model produces both discriminative and rewrite-based generative embeddings. Cross-Mode Alignment (CMA) uses mutual contrastive learning to align the two embedding spaces for identical queries while separating negatives, enabling flexible cross-retrieval. Refine-RL then treats discriminative embeddings as stable semantic anchors and rewards rewrites whose similarity gap exceeds the discriminative one, sharpening the generative embedding. On MMEB-V2, UVRB, and MRMR, RIME outperforms prior generative embedding models while cutting thinking length by roughly 50%.

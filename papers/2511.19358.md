## Summary

This paper extends the analysis of multi-agent contracts beyond pure Nash equilibria (PNE) to include mixed Nash and correlated equilibria (CCE). It demonstrates that for submodular (and more general XOS) reward structures, complex recommendations achievable in CCE only provide a constant-factor gain compared to the best PNE. Furthermore, it introduces a transformation method that takes a contract and its PNE and creates a new contract where any CCE provides a constant approximation guarantee, achieving black-box robustness and improved algorithms for submodular rewards.

## Technical note

The paper studies multi-agent hidden-action contract design and extends analysis from pure Nash equilibria (PNE) to mixed Nash and (coarse-)correlated equilibria (CCE), the latter modeling the limiting outcome of agent learning dynamics. Its main result shows that for submodular and more general XOS rewards, the best CCE achievable by any contract is within a constant factor of the best PNE utility, giving a black-box lifting so PNE guarantees transfer to CCE with constant loss. For submodular rewards it further transforms a contract-plus-PNE into a contract whose every CCE constant-approximates the PNE (black-box robustness), yielding poly-time constant-approximation algorithms. Such worst-case CCE guarantees are shown provably unattainable for XOS rewards, and gaps between equilibrium notions are bounded for subadditive, supermodular, and general rewards.

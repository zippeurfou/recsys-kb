## Summary

Generative sequence modeling faces a fundamental tension between the expressivity of Transformers and the efficiency of linear models, with powerful iterative methods like TTT being inherently serial. PRISM resolves this by introducing a parallelizable, solver-inspired inductive bias and employing a Write-Forget Decoupling strategy. The architecture uses a two-stage proxy (local anchor + learned predictor) to distill iterative refinement into a feedforward operator, theoretically achieving higher-order updates and empirically demonstrating comparable performance to explicit methods but with significantly higher throughput (174x).

## Technical note

PRISM (Parallel Residual Iterative Sequence Model) resolves the Rank-1 vs. parallel-scan trade-off in efficient sequence models for sequential recommendation by achieving Rank-L state updates while preserving parallel-scan compatibility. It targets the same online-convex-programming objective as Test-Time Training but amortizes the multi-step non-linear solver into input-anchored proxies: a short convolution anchors the state-key interaction, and L learned direction/gain projections build a Rank-L injection in one fused pass. A Write-Forget Decoupling keeps a Gated DeltaNet forgetting operator (state-independent) while allocating expressivity to the injection; setting L=0 recovers Gated DeltaNet. On Amazon and Yelp benchmarks PRISM matches TTT/Titans quality with up to 174x higher throughput and passes logic probes (XOR, parity) where Rank-1 models fail.

## Summary

This paper addresses e-commerce search relevance by tackling the limitations of Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex scenarios, primarily due to a knowledge boundary problem where models lack specific domain intelligence. To solve this, the authors introduce K-CARE, a framework comprising Symmetrical Contextual Anchoring (filling contextual gaps with implicit knowledge) and Analogical Prototype Reasoning (leveraging expert prototypes for better decision-making). K-CARE significantly improves search relevance, outperforming existing methods and demonstrating substantial commercial impact through evaluations.

## Technical note

K-CARE targets persistent corner cases in LLM-based e-commerce search relevance, arguing the bottleneck is the model's knowledge boundary (over 60% of failures stem from contextual voids) rather than reasoning-path quality, so RL-only optimization within parametric memory cannot help. It is a domain-specific RAG realization with two components. Symmetrical Contextual Anchoring (SCA) grounds queries and products with behavior-derived implicit knowledge via bidirectional anchors, using ranking signals to fetch referential products for queries and a product-to-query model to generate intent queries, plus Task-Guided Knowledge Ingestion training objectives to mitigate feedback sparsity. Analogical Prototype Reasoning (APR) injects expert-curated prototypical exemplars with explicit rationales to calibrate decision boundaries through in-context analogy. Offline experiments and online A/B tests on JD.com show K-CARE beats SOTA baselines with substantial commercial impact.

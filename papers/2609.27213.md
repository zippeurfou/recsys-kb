## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Current retrieval systems for open-ended ("diffuse") queries in RAG face a challenge where cheaper dual-encoder ranking can miss relevant documents, but expensive cross-encoder scoring (CE) has limited budgets often smaller than the number of documents that need to fit into the LLM context window.
2.  **Solution:** The paper introduces BoundaryMORPH, an algorithm that strategically allocates the limited CE budget to resolve the membership (inclusion or exclusion) of documents at the boundary of the top-ranked set, rather than just scoring the very top candidates.
3.  **Result:** By using a Gaussian Process model of the initial ranking and focusing CE calls on boundary resolution, BoundaryMORPH significantly improves retrieval quality (+5.4 nCG@100) compared to standard methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance while respecting the CE budget constraints.

## Technical note

For diffuse RAG queries with dozens to hundreds of relevant documents, the cross-encoder budget B is often smaller than the LLM context capacity k, so reranking the dual-encoder top-B wastes calls on obvious documents and leaves the tail in MIPS order. Amazon's BoundaryMORPH fits a Gaussian process (vMF kernel on embeddings, median-heuristic bandwidth, query injected as a free y=1 observation) to the residual between CE scores and a MIPS percentile-rank prior, so each CE call propagates to unscored neighbors, and replaces max-seeking GP-UCB with an active-set-ordering rule that scores the higher-variance member of the incumbent/challenger pair with the smallest standardized gap at the top-k boundary. On NeuCLIR, Robust04 and TravelDest (top-10k pools, B in {25,50}, k in {50,100,200}) it beats BM25, single-stage reranking, RGS and the zero-prior GP baseline BAGEL in every cell (avg +3.8, up to +5.4 pp nCG@100 over the strongest baseline); removing the prior or the boundary rule costs 1.5-3.8 pp, and gains grow with query multimodality (Spearman 0.59) and with weaker retrievers (up to +14 pp over single-stage rerank). The method is parameter-free and kernel-insensitive, CE calls dominate its latency (90-95% at B<=200), and at B=100 it reaches 0.621 nCG@100 in 25.7 s versus at best 0.618 in 885 s for a DSP agentic retrieval loop.

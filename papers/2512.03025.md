## Summary

LORE introduces a large generative model designed to significantly improve search relevance in e-commerce, achieving a notable 27% cumulative increase in online GoodRate metrics through extensive deployment and iteration.

The paper argues that existing relevance models hit a performance ceiling because they treat relevance as a single task, lacking a principled breakdown. LORE's key insight is that relevance involves distinct capabilities (knowledge/reasoning, multi-modal matching, rule adherence) requiring a qualitative-driven approach for improvement.

LORE's main contributions include a novel two-stage training paradigm combining Chain-of-Thought and RL methods, a benchmark (RAIR) to evaluate core capabilities, and a deployment strategy to efficiently integrate LLMs into production search systems.

## Technical note

LORE is a three-year, three-iteration framework for LLM-based e-commerce search relevance that achieved a cumulative +27% online GoodRate. Its core insight is that relevance is not monolithic reasoning but a composite of knowledge/reasoning, multi-modal (text-image) matching, and rule adherence, so it deconstructs relevance into path construction and path following to guide training. It uses a two-stage paradigm: SFT with progressive CoT synthesis and distillation to instill capabilities, then RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) to align with human preferences while pruning flawed reasoning. It introduces the RAIR benchmark for the identified capabilities and a query-based stratified deployment strategy to transfer the offline LLM to production efficiently.

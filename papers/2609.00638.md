## Summary

This paper introduces CoGR, a novel generative retrieval framework that utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) to directly generate keywords for both queries and items, which are then matched via an inverted index. The core innovation is a two-stage training process: supervised fine-tuning first aligns the keyword spaces, followed by co-evolving reinforcement learning using Generative Retrieval Policy Optimization (GRPO) that alternately optimizes the query and item generators against each other's frozen representations, aiming for the same F1 objective score. CoGR significantly outperforms various baseline methods on different search datasets, demonstrating stable co-evolution and increasing alignment between the query and item keyword spaces during training.

## Technical note

Apple proposes CoGR, a retrieval framework where two separate LLMs generate keyword sets for the query side and the item side and retrieval happens by intersecting those keyword sets through an ordinary inverted index, with candidates ranked by BM25 over the generated bags of words -- preserving compatibility with keyword-bidding sponsored-search infrastructure instead of introducing semantic IDs or dense vectors. Phase 1 SFT bootstraps an aligned keyword space: the base LLM emits M=10 keywords per item, then for each query the top-N=15 most frequent keywords pooled across its relevant items become the query-side target, guaranteeing initial overlap and enough recall for informative RL rewards. Phase 2 alternates GRPO updates for five rounds against the opposite side's frozen index (query generator 10 epochs, item generator 5 epochs per round, K_max=30 keywords, verl framework, Qwen3-4B-Instruct for CoGR-4B and Qwen3-1.7B for CoGR-1.7B, no chain of thought to avoid inference overhead). The query side is rewarded with retrieval F1 directly (zero if over budget); the item side receives a counterfactual marginal reward equal to the change in summed per-query F1 when only item i's keywords are swapped into the reference index, which is cheap because unaffected queries cancel out. On an internal App Marketplace dataset (13,500/1,500 queries, 39,600 apps, ~1,000 relevant items per query) CoGR-4B reaches F1 0.3963 versus 0.3575 for the strongest of 10 baselines, ANCE-Qwen4B (+10.9%), and on the public WANDS Wayfair benchmark 0.6819 versus 0.5012 (+36.1%), against BM25, SPLADE-v2, DPR, ANCE, Qwen3-4B embeddings, DSI, DSI-QG, RIPOR and DeepRetrieval. Co-evolution is load-bearing: freezing the item side drops internal F1 to 0.2617, validation F1 climbs from ~0.16 to ~0.40 over five alternating rounds with the largest jump in round one, unigrams fall from 37% to 13% of generated keywords while 3+-word phrases rise from 12% to 31%, and the two vocabularies converge in size after three to four rounds. Ablations show transposed item-side F1 (0.3743), a shared generator (0.3798) and no SFT (0.3751) all trail the full model; removing item descriptions costs F1 0.3963 to 0.3759 while injecting existing search results into the query prompt raises it to 0.4379. Caveat: evaluation is entirely offline on relevance-annotated pairs with no online A/B, revenue or latency measurement, ranking is just BM25 over generated keywords, and the reward is relevance F1 rather than a business objective -- both flagged as future work.

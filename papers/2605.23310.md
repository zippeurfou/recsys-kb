## Summary

This paper addresses the long-tail recommendation challenge in e-commerce, where data imbalance hinders effective models. The authors introduce AKT-Rec, a framework leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) and a Residual-Quantized VAE (RQ-VAE) to generate semantic IDs for both items and users, capturing richer content and collaborative signals often missed by traditional methods.

AKT-Rec's core innovation is its Asymmetric Knowledge Transfer mechanism, comprising two parts: Cluster-Guided Adaptive Embedding, which uses a specialized contrastive objective and gating to transfer knowledge from popular (head) IDs to less represented (tail) IDs without harming the head, and Hierarchical Feature Aggregation, which adaptively fuses features for better prediction across different activity levels.

The framework demonstrates significant improvements, achieving a 0.35% AUC increase offline and a substantial 2.76% CTR lift and 3.47% GMV boost in Alibaba Tmall online A/B tests, proving its effectiveness in real-world scenarios by mitigating data imbalance.

## Technical note

AKT-Rec tackles long-tail e-commerce recommendation where data imbalance starves tail items and users of learnable signal.

## Summary

Large-scale dense retrieval, essential for search and generative AI, relies on dual-tower architectures but faces issues with representational misalignment and index inconsistency, degrading accuracy and stability. This paper introduces the Symmetric Consistent Indexing (SCI) framework to address these problems. SCI unifies the dual-tower representation space and redesigns the retrieval process, offering a simple, lightweight, and effective solution that maintains consistency and supports billion-scale deployment, validated on both public and real-world datasets.

## Technical note

SCI (Symmetric Consistent Indexing) addresses two flaws of dual-tower dense retrieval: representational space misalignment between query and item towers, and coarse-to-fine index inconsistency (IVF-PQ/HNSW/semantic-ID), which also degrade semantic-ID quality for generative recommendation. It proposes Symmetric Representation Alignment via an input-swapping mechanism (feeding queries through the item tower and items through the query tower with a symmetric contrastive loss) that unifies the two spaces without adding parameters, plus Consistent Indexing that builds coarse clusters using query-encoder item embeddings while quantizing residuals with item-tower embeddings. With theoretical guarantees, SCI improves Recall and MAP on public and billion-scale e-commerce datasets while staying ANN-library compatible.

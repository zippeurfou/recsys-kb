## Summary

Knowledge Distillation (KD) is typically used to improve model quality but struggles in low-traffic recommender systems due to limited data, making dedicated teacher models impractical. Cross-domain KD offers a solution by leveraging a high-traffic domain's teacher model, though it faces challenges from domain differences. This case study shows that zero-shot cross-domain KD successfully transferred knowledge from YouTube's video recommendation system to a music app, significantly improving its ranking model performance.

## Technical note

This RecSys '25 case study applies zero-shot cross-domain knowledge distillation to multi-task ranking models in production recommenders, addressing the difficulty of distillation on low-traffic surfaces where scarce data restricts teacher size and dedicated teachers are not cost-justified. It reuses an existing (zero-shot) teacher originally built for YouTube video recommendation to distill into student ranking models on the YouTube Music app, whose training data has roughly 100x fewer samples, despite differing features, user interfaces, and prediction tasks. The authors compare multiple KD techniques across two ranking models and report both offline and live experiment results. Findings show zero-shot cross-domain KD is a practical, effective way to improve ranking quality on low-traffic surfaces without added serving latency.

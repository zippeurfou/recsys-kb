## Summary

This paper introduces Xray-Visual, a large-scale multimodal vision model trained on massive datasets (15B image-text pairs, 10B video-hashtag pairs) from social media, employing advanced data curation to maximize diversity and minimize noise. The model uses a Vision Transformer backbone with token reorganization (EViT) and a three-stage training pipeline combining self-supervised, semi-supervised, and contrastive learning techniques for efficient image, video, and text understanding. Experiments show Xray-Visual achieves state-of-the-art performance across standard benchmarks and demonstrates strong robustness, with integration of large language models further enhancing retrieval capabilities.

## Technical note

Xray-Visual is a unified image-video vision encoder trained on industry-scale social media data (15B image-text and 10B video-hashtag pairs) with a three-stage pipeline: self-supervised masked autoencoding (MAE), semi-supervised hashtag classification, and CLIP-style contrastive learning, augmented with denoising and SLIP objectives. The backbone is a Vision Transformer with 3D tokenization and EViT-style token reorganization/dropping plus register tokens, operating at 336 resolution with only 288 tokens (~4x QPS gain). Data curation uses WordNet-synset semantic balancing, MetaCLIP similarity filtering, and LLM-rewritten synthetic captions to reduce noise and repetition. Replacing standard text encoders with an LLaMA-based LLM (LLM2CLIP) is critical for real-world retrieval, and the joint image-video model improves video understanding while staying near-SOTA on ImageNet, Kinetics, and MS-COCO retrieval.

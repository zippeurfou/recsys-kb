## Summary

This paper tackles knowledge-aware recommendation by addressing two key limitations: effectively modeling complex hierarchical relationships in graphs (user-item and knowledge) and avoiding preference shifts from traditional graph perturbations. The authors propose a novel solution combining hyperbolic contrastive learning with model-level augmentation techniques, specifically designing a Lorentzian aggregation mechanism that better captures intrinsic hierarchies than Euclidean methods. Extensive experiments confirm this approach significantly improves recommendation performance over existing baseline methods by $11.03\%$ or more.

## Technical note

GNN-based contrastive learning for knowledge-aware recommendation struggles to capture the hierarchical (power-law) structure of user-item and knowledge graphs in Euclidean space, and its usual structure-perturbation augmentations (e.g. edge dropping) alter user history and cause preference shifts. HCMKR performs representation learning in hyperbolic (Lorentz-model) space via a novel relation-aware Lorentzian knowledge aggregation mechanism to model intrinsic hierarchies, and replaces structural augmentation with three model-level augmentations (Dropout, cross-layer outputs, and model pruning) that preserve semantics and avoid preference shift. Aggregation is done in the tangent space (avoiding the closed-form-free Frechet mean) using a Fermi-Dirac-based attentive weighting. Extensive experiments on three datasets show up to 11.03% improvement over SOTA baselines with reduced (x0.88) time consumption.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating search ranking algorithms effectively, particularly within online platforms like Airbnb where conversion-based metrics are crucial but difficult to measure with sufficient statistical power using traditional A/B tests alone. To overcome this, researchers developed novel evaluation methods called interleaving (showing new results alongside old ones) and counterfactual evaluation (using historical data as a proxy), which provide rapid online assessments of ranking changes without requiring lengthy experiments. These techniques significantly increased the sensitivity of identifying effective ranking modifications by up to 100 times compared to standard A/B testing, streamlining the process and offering practical insights for other organizations facing similar challenges in search and recommendation evaluation.

## Technical note

This Airbnb paper accelerates search-ranking evaluation where conversion-based A/B tests take too long due to low traffic, delayed conversions, and small effect sizes. It introduces a competitive-pair interleaving design that is unbiased and achieves ~50x speedup over A/B for equivalent statistical power, plus an online counterfactual (off-policy) evaluation method that further raises sensitivity up to 100x and is more generalizable. Both are used as a fast middle step to select promising treatment candidates for A/B tests, validated entirely through daily production use rather than simulations. The paper documents design principles (sensitivity, unbiasedness, fidelity, minimal UX disruption) and practical trade-offs between the approaches.

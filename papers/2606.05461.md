## Technical note

This paper identifies an 'evidence-type gap' in XAI for ML-based autonomous driving: safety standards demand directed cause-and-effect chains and quantified interventional effects, but popular methods like SHAP return ranked feature lists that structurally cannot become causal chains (Pearl's rung-1 methods cannot answer rung-2 questions). From four standards (AMLAS, ISO 21448, ISO/PAS 8800, ISO 26262) the authors derive 19 testable evidentiary criteria across seven lifecycle stages and structurally score six XAI method classes, finding causal XAI (SCM, causal traces) is structurally required at hazard identification (+62% rubric gap), incident investigation (+50%), and data management (+50%). A single-VLA proof of concept on 1,996 real-world driving clips (79,840 rows) confirms each method's output type matches its rubric prediction, arguing XAI selection should be driven by lifecycle-stage evidence demand, not method popularity.

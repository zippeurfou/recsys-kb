## Summary

This paper identifies a critical "evidence-type gap" where automotive safety standards require specific evidence types (like cause-effect chains) for autonomous driving system (ADS) assurance, but current XAI methods focus on output types (like saliency maps or SHAP values). To address this, the authors developed a comprehensive Standards-Derived rubric by analyzing regulations such as ISO 26262 and AMLAS. Using this rubric, they found that causal XAI methods are structurally necessary for assurance during hazard identification, incident investigation, and data management stages, while correlational or language-based approaches can be used elsewhere; however, structural admissibility alone is not sufficient to guarantee safety because the correct content of the output must still be ensured.

## Technical note

This paper formalizes an 'evidence-type gap' between what ML-based autonomous-driving safety standards demand and what XAI methods structurally produce: standards require directed cause-and-effect chains, quantified interventional effects, and named root causes, whereas the most-recommended method, SHAP, returns a ranked feature list that no implementation quality can convert into a directed chain. From four publications (AMLAS, ISO 21448, ISO/PAS 8800, ISO 26262) they derive 19 testable evidentiary criteria across seven lifecycle stages and score six XAI method classes structurally with an S/P/F rule, anchored in Pearl's causal hierarchy (rung-1 associational methods cannot answer rung-2 interventional questions). Causal XAI (SCM, causal traces) emerges as structurally required at hazard identification (+62% rubric gap), incident investigation (+50%), and data management (+50%), stable across thresholds and a worst-case one-cell flip to T=33%; correlational or language-based methods suffice at the other four stages. The rubric captures structural admissibility (necessary not sufficient), and a single-VLA proof of concept on 1,996 driving clips is consistent with each method's output type matching its rubric prediction.

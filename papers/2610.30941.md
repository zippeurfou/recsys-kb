## Summary

LinkedIn's people recommendation system faces challenges due to the platform's goal-driven, role-complementary nature, unlike friend-oriented social networks where profile similarity works well.

The paper introduces PAIR, a novel retrieval framework designed specifically to model user intent preferences for finding relevant candidates, rather than relying on profile embeddings or social signals.

PAIR achieves this using an efficient, contrastive learning objective inspired by Direct Preference Optimization, and incorporates user history with a constant-cost update mechanism, leading to effective personalization updates without heavy computation.

## Technical note

LinkedIn Premium Browsemaps retrieval misranks candidates because professional intent is complementary rather than similar (job seekers click recruiters, not peers), and the production Holistic-171 member embedding reaches only MAR@100 0.288 while an off-the-shelf 0.6B LLM embedding gets 0.192. PAIR fine-tunes a 0.6B Qwen-based embedding encoder with an exposure-conditioned multi-negative contrastive loss (cosine/tau, tau=0.02) that, in the spirit of DPO but without a reference model, prefers the clicked profile over exposed-but-unclicked impressions (hard negatives) plus random Premium members at a 1:3 ratio from production logs. A triple-tower design adds a history tower that pools the K=10 most recently clicked profile embeddings with query-agnostic positional attention and fuses it additively with the L2-normalized profile embedding via a learned scalar, so intent updates cost O(Kd) with no LLM re-inference, and Matryoshka training over {200,256,512,1024} dims serves multiple latency budgets. The two-tower PAIR model hits MAR@100 0.436 (+51% over production) and P-ATT history 0.450 (+56%, 2.3x the base encoder), with 256 dims still at 0.432; an online A/B with the same downstream ranker lifts CTR +7.5% and Premium retention +2.1% with +0.1% page latency.

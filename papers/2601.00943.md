## Summary

This paper introduces PhyEduVideo, a benchmark designed to evaluate Text-to-Video (T2V) models for generating educational physics videos. The benchmark uses granular teaching points with specific prompts to assess the models' ability to create accurate visual explanations. The study finds that while current models produce visually coherent videos, their conceptual accuracy is inconsistent, particularly struggling with complex topics like electromagnetism and thermodynamics.

## Technical note

PhyEduVideo is the first benchmark for evaluating text-to-video (T2V) models on generating pedagogically meaningful physics-education videos. It decomposes 60 physics concepts into granular teaching points, yielding 205 prompts (16-45 words each) that mirror instructional practice, and evaluates whether generated videos convey concepts accurately rather than just looking plausible. Unlike prior benchmarks emphasizing general quality or physical realism, it grounds evaluation in educational utility. Findings show current models produce visually coherent, smooth videos but have unreliable conceptual accuracy: mechanics, fluids, and optics fare better while electromagnetism and thermodynamics remain hard; Wan2.1 performs best, followed by PhyT2V, highlighting the gap between visual quality and conceptual correctness.

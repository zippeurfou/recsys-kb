## Summary

1.  The paper addresses sequential recommendation by combining large language models like Llama3-8B-instruct with multimodal (visual/audio) and quantized semantic ID embeddings to overcome two major limitations inherent in previous LLM-based approaches.
2.  It specifically tackles "embedding collapse," a problem arising from integrating pre-trained collaborative data, through its novel MME-SID framework that uses an MM-RQ-VAE incorporating contrastive learning for better embedding alignment and preservation of distance information.
3.  To prevent catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning (another LLM limitation), the model is initialized with carefully crafted multimodal ID embeddings derived from training. Extensive experiments on standard datasets confirm MME-SID achieves superior recommendation results by overcoming both challenges efficiently.

## Technical note

MME-SID identifies two failures in LLM-based sequential recommendation: embedding collapse when mapping low-rank collaborative embeddings into the LLM space, and catastrophic forgetting when code embeddings from semantic-ID quantization are discarded. It proposes a Multimodal Residual Quantized VAE (MM-RQ-VAE) that quantizes collaborative, textual, and visual embeddings using a maximum mean discrepancy reconstruction loss to preserve distance information and InfoNCE contrastive alignment across modalities. It initializes semantic-ID embeddings from the trained code embeddings and fine-tunes Llama3-8B with LoRA via a frequency-aware multimodal fusion. On three Amazon datasets it beats baselines by up to 10.5% nDCG@5 while measurably reducing collapse (98% of dimensions non-collapsed) and forgetting.

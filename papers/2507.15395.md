## Summary

1.  This paper addresses challenges in multi-behavior recommendation systems where users interact with platforms through various actions like clicks and purchases.
2.  It introduces HGIB (Hierarchical Graph Information Bottleneck), a model-agnostic framework that optimizes representations by focusing on essential information needed for predicting target behaviors while filtering out irrelevant noise.
3.  By incorporating a Graph Refinement Encoder to dynamically prune noisy edges, the proposed method significantly improves recommendation performance across different datasets and even shows benefits in real industrial A/B tests.

## Technical note

HGIB is a model-agnostic framework for multi-behavior recommendation that combats distribution disparities across behaviors and negative transfer from noisy auxiliary behaviors. It integrates the information bottleneck principle into hierarchical (cascading/parallel) behavior models, deriving a tractable objective that maximizes information relevant to the target behavior while compressing irrelevant redundancy, approximated using HSIC. A Graph Refinement Encoder further denoises by dynamically pruning redundant user-item edges via learnable edge dropout. Across three public datasets HGIB shows strong generality and gains, and on two billion-interaction industrial datasets plus a 15-day online A/B test it delivers significant improvements in target-behavior prediction.

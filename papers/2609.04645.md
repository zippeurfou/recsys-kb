## Summary

Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face a challenge called "cross-modal dilution" where visual and text signals weaken during complex reasoning needed for recommendations. To combat this, the authors introduce LARK, a novel two-stage framework within a single VLM. LARK preserves visual details using learnable latent tokens in the first stage and anchors the final reasoning output to these preserved details in the second stage, demonstrating state-of-the-art recommendation performance across different architectures.

## Technical note

LARK from Xiaohongshu repurposes a Qwen2.5-VL-3B vision-language model as an offline multimodal item encoder, motivated by what the authors call cross-modal dilution: as chain-of-thought reasoning lengthens, attention to both image and text tokens decays (about -45% and -38% from the first to the last reasoning step, with visual CKA falling 0.82 to 0.45 and textual CKA 0.78 to 0.49 over four steps), so the drift is representational and not merely an attention artifact. Stage 1 interleaves N=4 groups of K=4 learnable latent tokens (16 total) before each CoT step; at latent positions the hidden state is fed back directly as the next input embedding (Coconut-style continuous reasoning, bypassing the argmax bottleneck), while language positions emit CoT text supervised by cross-entropy against offline annotations from a Gemini-2.5-Flash teacher, and intermediate features at layer floor(2L/3) are aligned patch-wise by cosine similarity to a frozen copy of the VLM's own ViT while the trainable encoder sees a 50% patch-masked image. Stage 2 projects the Stage-1 latent hidden states through a Bridge MLP back into the same backbone, trains the mean-pooled projection head with an InfoNCE item-to-item contrastive loss using top-10 Swing-similarity neighbors as positives and in-batch negatives (tau = 0.07), and adds a reasoning-text alignment loss to the stop-gradient mean-pooled Stage-1 CoT hidden states; the total objective is L_i2i + 0.1*L_cot + 0.1*(L_vision + L_text). Item embeddings are precomputed, frozen, concatenated with categorical features, and consumed by DeepFM, LightGCN or SASRec trained with BPR, so the two-stage reasoning adds no serving latency. On Amazon Reviews Baby/Sports/Clothing and an in-house dataset (856,214 users, 432,671 items, 12.4M interactions from a 100M+ MAU social platform), LARK-LightGCN wins everywhere: in-house R@20 0.0548 versus 0.0498 for AlignRec (+10.0%) and Baby R@20 0.1148 versus 0.1046. Ablations on Baby R@20 give -17.4% without latent reasoning, -11.8% without both alignments, -8.4% without L_text, -5.5% without L_vision, -6.1% without CoT supervision and -9.5% for discrete language-only reasoning; swapping the frozen target to DINOv2 loses 3.9%, and performance in latent-token count plateaus past K_l = 16. Used as drop-in feature replacement, LARK embeddings lift MMGCN by +13.9/+15.1% R@20 on Baby/Sports and FREEDOM by +5.6/+5.8%, with the largest gains on long-tail items having fewer than 5 interactions, and LARK beats the 7B NoteLLM-2 by 4.7/4.3% R@20 even on NoteLLM-2's native i2i task. Caveat: entirely offline; the authors explicitly defer production deployment and online A/B evaluation to future work.

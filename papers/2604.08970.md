## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of predicting how well multilingual models will perform on specific tasks in languages with limited direct benchmark data. To tackle this, the authors introduce Litmus (Re)Agent, an agentic system that uses a structured approach (decomposing queries, retrieving evidence, and feature-aware aggregation) to estimate model performance. Litmus (Re)Agent demonstrates superior performance, especially in transfer scenarios where direct evidence is scarce, showing that structured agentic reasoning is a promising method for predictive evaluation under incomplete data.

## Technical note

This paper studies Task-Model-Language (TML) prediction: estimating a model's score on a task in a language when direct benchmark results are missing. It introduces a controlled 1,500-question benchmark (six tasks, five evidence scenarios S1-S5) that separates accessible reduced-corpus evidence from combined-corpus ground truth, covering numeric prediction (PredSet, MAE) and comparative reasoning (QnASet, accuracy). Litmus (Re)Agent is a DAG-orchestrated multi-agent system where a ThoughtCreator spawns per-hypothesis ThoughtAgents (planner, web search, expert-knowledge, coder, reporter) that retrieve citation-grounded evidence and run lang2vec/URIEL feature-informed regression. It beats five baselines including the prior DAG system, single-agent, GPT-4.1 direct, and Magentic-One, with largest gains in transfer-heavy scenarios.

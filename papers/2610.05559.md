## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of training large-vocabulary sequential recommendation models using Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss, which typically causes out-of-memory errors due to dense logits tensors. The authors introduce CutBCE, a novel hardware-accelerated BCE operator implemented in JAX and Pallas. CutBCE significantly reduces memory usage by avoiding dense logits and performing all computation on-chip, leading to substantial speedups (up to 91.9%) on single-chip TPUs and a 225.9% speedup plus a 65.7% HBM reduction on multi-chip TPUs, while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Technical note

Google's CutBCE targets multi-label sequential recommenders trained with binary cross-entropy over the full item vocabulary to predict the next 8 items, where standard BCE materializes a dense [B,N,V] logits tensor in HBM and runs out of memory at industrial catalog sizes (13.38 GiB for one local logits tensor at V=876,939). It splits BCE exactly into a dense background term max(x,0)+log(1+exp(-|x|)) and a sparse correction -y*x, tiles the vocabulary into blocks (e.g. 4,096 items) and computes and discards each block's logits in turn, and uses a custom VJP whose Pallas TPU backward kernel recomputes logit tiles on-chip and accumulates item-embedding gradients in VMEM, so neither logits nor their gradients ever sit in HBM. It adds dynamic VMEM budgeting with sequence chunking, sharding-aware collective hoisting via shard_map for data, FSDP and vocab-parallel meshes, and count-based training metrics (TP/FP/FN/TN) computed in the forward pass without rebuilding logits. On single TPU v5e and v6e chips it removes OOM and runs 53.8% to 91.9% faster than Keras BCE, with the speedup growing with vocabulary size, while matching its loss within 1.1e-7 relative error. On an 8-chip TPU v6e slice training multi-label SASRec (3-layer GPT-2 backbone, AdamW) on Yambda-50M with 876,939 items, it cuts peak HBM by 65.7% (22.37 to 7.67 GiB) and raises throughput by 225.9% (6.14 to 20.01 steps/s) at comparable Hit@8 (0.183 vs 0.186), unlocks batch 128 and sequence length 512 runs that OOM with standard BCE, and ships open source in RecML as an exact alternative to negative sampling, which the authors say biases the objective.

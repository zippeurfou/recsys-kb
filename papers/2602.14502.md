## Summary

E-commerce platforms often struggle with new products having limited data (the cold-start problem), hindering their search visibility. To solve this, the paper introduces Behavioral Feature Boosting via Substitute Relationships (BFS), which identifies products that serve similar user needs and uses their aggregated behavioral data (clicks, purchases, etc.) to enrich new items. This method significantly improves search relevance and discovery for new products by providing them with a "warm start," and the approach has been successfully deployed in production.

## Technical note

BFS (Behavior Feature Boosting via Substitute relationships) mitigates the item cold-start problem in e-commerce search by transferring behavioral signals from substitute products (items satisfying similar user needs) to new items lacking interaction data. It focuses on Sales Velocity (decayed order counts favoring recent purchases) as the boosted feature, aggregating substitutes' signals via summary statistics (mean, max) and attention-based aggregation to give new items a warm start. The pipeline has three components: substitute identification, behavioral-feature aggregation, and integration into the search ranking model. Framed as a feature-level complement to entity-level collaborative filtering (which fails under sparse interactions), BFS was validated offline and via online A/B tests showing GMV, sales-unit, and discoverability gains, and has been in production since 2025.

## Summary

ByteDance's production system for online (streaming) training of recommendation models: a collisionless hash-table embedding with expiry and frequency filtering, an online joiner that streams labelled examples from Kafka/Flink, and minute-level synchronization of touched sparse parameters from training to serving. Shows that shorter parameter-sync intervals raise online AUC and that fault-tolerance can be traded for real-time updates.

## Technical note

Monolith is ByteDance's system for online training of sparse recommendation models, built on TensorFlow with a collisionless Cuckoo-hash embedding table (expirable embeddings and frequency filtering to bound memory) instead of hashing-trick tables. A Kafka/Flink online joiner streams labelled examples so the same data feeds batch training (from HDFS) and online training (from the stream); after deployment, training continues on live data and only the sparse parameters touched since the last sync are pushed to the serving parameter servers at minute-level intervals, while dense parameters sync once a day at low-traffic hours. On Criteo with DeepFM, shortening the sync interval from 5h to 1h to 30min raises online AUC from 79.66 to 79.78 to 79.80 (batch training 79.42-79.44), and a live A/B on an ads model reports a 14-18% daily AUC-improvement metric for online over batch training. They also find a day-old parameter-server snapshot costs almost no quality, so PS reliability is traded for real-time updates.

## Summary

This paper introduces MIMA, a framework for multi-interest recommendation designed to overcome the issue of "interest collapse". It identifies the single-positive training paradigm as a key contributor to this problem and proposes a solution using multi-positive exclusive assignment, where multiple items are considered positive in a single batch. MIMA generates complementary interest vectors and assigns each positive item to a unique interest vector via causal decoders and Hungarian matching, ensuring distinct differentiation during training. It further includes a routing module to estimate interest activation probabilities, and experiments show it outperforms existing methods and achieves significant online business gains.

## Technical note

Alibaba attacks two failure modes of multi-interest retrieval: interest collapse, which they attribute to the single-positive training paradigm where each isolated positive is hard-routed to the currently best-matching interest so one dominant vector absorbs conflicting gradients while the rest stay unsupervised, and cross-interest score incomparability at merge time. MIMA has four parts. (1) Multi-positive targets: items clicked within the same serving request are grouped into a positive set of capacity K (deduplicated, zero-padded with a binary mask, paired with the strictly prior history to avoid leakage); on public data without request logs, one-day windows approximate requests. (2) Complementary interest generation with a 2-layer causal Transformer decoder over K+1 learnable queries, where causal self-attention makes each later interest query condition on earlier interest states before cross-attending to history, and interests are read out as attention-weighted sums of history item embeddings so they stay in item space. (3) Exclusive assignment: the K x K interest-positive inner-product matrix is solved exactly as a linear assignment problem by the Hungarian algorithm under stop-gradient, so each positive supervises a distinct interest, trained with sampled softmax over a 1280-item negative pool. (4) A routing query q_{K+1} feeds a 2-layer MLP (stop-gradient input) producing an activation distribution pi_u, so the calibrated score is max_k pi_k v_k^T e_i, servable as standard per-interest ANN top-N with scaled interests and no pipeline change, trained with a separate hinge loss (margin gamma=0.02) on L2-normalized similarities. Results: beats the best baseline (REMI, DisMIR) by 3.04 to 15.72 percent on Books, Beauty and Gowalla, e.g. Books Recall@50 0.1568 vs 0.1355 and Recall@20 0.1017 vs 0.0883, with larger relative gains on sparser data. On a Lazada Thailand industrial dataset (5.9M users, 7.6M items, 63.3M interactions, K=5) MIMA gets HR@1000 0.4962 vs 0.4390 for REMI (+13.03 percent), and the proposed Interest Discrimination Margin IDM@1000 rises to 0.1087 vs 0.0879 (+23.66 percent), evidencing genuinely less collapse. Ablations: removing multi-positive supervision hurts most, Hungarian beats greedy and Sinkhorn soft assignment (Sinkhorn dilutes gradients across interests), K=8 optimal on Books and Beauty, K=4 on Gowalla, 2 decoder layers sufficient. A 7-day online A/B replacing a ComiRec-like matching model in homepage retrieval gave +5.60 percent transaction count, +5.44 percent transaction amount, and +5.12 percentage points exclusive impression ratio.

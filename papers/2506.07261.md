## Summary

Recall Augmentation through Deferred Asynchronous Retrieval":

1.  Modern recommender systems often use an initial retrieval stage (like KNN) which is efficient but imprecise, making it hard to find highly relevant items in large catalogs.
2.  The RADAR framework addresses this by using offline computation with a full complex model to pre-rank a much larger candidate set and store its top results for faster online recall augmentation.
3.  Experiments show that RADAR significantly improves offline recall scores (by up to 200%) and also boosts real-world engagement metrics (+0.8% lift) by providing high-quality candidates without adding online latency or computational burden.

## Technical note

RADAR addresses the recall bottleneck of the retrieval stage in Meta's multi-stage video recommender funnel (retrieval -> pre-ranking -> ranking), where lightweight KNN/two-tower retrievers give only single-digit Recall@200. Instead of an online model, RADAR runs the full production ranking model offline/asynchronously on off-peak compute over a 50x larger candidate pool per user, stores the top-200 pre-ranked items in a low-latency key-value store on a usage-adaptive refresh cadence, and injects them at serving time bypassing online pre-ranking. Offline it roughly doubles Recall@200 (16.5% vs 8.1% DNN two-tower), with ablations showing both retrieval scaling and model scaling contribute synergistically; a two-week A/B test gave +0.8% topline engagement and +6% unique item consumption at unchanged latency, benefiting moderately-active users most and dormant users not at all.

## Summary

1.  Traditional recommendation systems excel at relevance but often fail to provide surprising or novel ("serendipitous") suggestions, while directly integrating powerful multimodal LLMs into large platforms was too complex due to their vast item space.
2.  The authors propose a hierarchical framework that uses fine-tuned MLLMs as high-level guides for conventional recommenders, steering them toward serendipity by leveraging the MLLM's ability to understand video content and discover new interest clusters.
3.  This hybrid approach significantly enhances recommendation novelty without sacrificing user satisfaction or efficiency, as demonstrated through successful live experiments on a commercial short-form video platform serving billions of users.

## Technical note

Conventional recommenders reinforce existing interests and rarely surface surprising or novel items, while multimodal LLMs have the world knowledge for serendipity but cannot scale to billion-item action spaces. The paper proposes a hierarchical framework where a fine-tuned MLLM provides high-level guidance that steers a conventional recommendation model toward more serendipitous suggestions, keeping item-level retrieval efficient. A chain-of-thought strategy lets the MLLM first understand video content and then identify relevant yet unexplored interest clusters to inject novelty. Live experiments on a commercial short-form video platform serving billions of users show significant gains in recommendation serendipity and user satisfaction.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the problem of managing numerous LoRA adapters for different visual effects in diffusion models, which increases deployment overhead and causes quality issues (concept bleeding) when cascaded with speed-up modules.
2.  CollectionLoRA proposes a solution using multi-teacher on-policy distillation to consolidate up to 50 different effect LoRAs and few-step generation capabilities into a single, unified LoRA adapter, fundamentally tackling the interference problem.
3.  This approach employs specific mechanisms (Probabilistic Dual-Stream Routing, Asymmetric Orthogonal Prompting, Coarse-to-Fine Objective) during distillation to ensure fidelity and isolation, resulting in a single LoRA that reduces deployment costs while maintaining performance comparable to or exceeding individual effect adapters.

## Technical note

CollectionLoRA is a multi-teacher on-policy distillation framework that consolidates up to 50 (scaling to 180) customized image-editing effect LoRAs plus few-step generation into a single LoRA, eliminating storage, routing, and parameter-interference costs of cascaded multi-LoRA pipelines. Built on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), it treats each effect LoRA as a visual expert and adds three components: Probabilistic Dual-Stream Routing that mixes general-domain data as regularization to preserve generalization, Asymmetric Orthogonal Prompting that assigns orthogonal trigger words to isolate concepts in prompt space, and a Coarse-to-Fine Distillation Objective combining flow matching with target simulation to bridge the student-teacher distribution gap. It matches or exceeds independently trained teachers in concept fidelity while cutting deployment overhead to 0.5% of the conventional paradigm and enabling zero-shot effect composition at inference.

## Summary

Current LLM evaluation methods are limited because they rely on static benchmark scores and struggle to effectively combine results across different tasks, nor do they capture how models perform under sequential, competitive pressure.

To address this, the paper introduces the Competitive Swiss-System Dynamics (CSD) framework, which simulates a multi-round tournament where models are dynamically paired based on their win-loss record, approximating a robust Expected Win Score ($E[S_m]$) through extensive Monte Carlo simulations.

Additionally, CSD includes a Failure Sensitivity Analysis to profile model robustness by adjusting the rate of elimination across rounds, distinguishing between generally strong models and highly specialized ones.

This CSD approach provides a more nuanced, dynamic, and context-aware ranking of LLM performance compared to traditional aggregate scoring methods.

## Technical note

The paper argues static multi-benchmark aggregation is flawed because it requires arbitrary weighting and ignores path-dependent, sequential model utility. It introduces Competitive Swiss-System Dynamics (CSD), which simulates a multi-round tournament pairing LLMs by accumulated win-loss record across benchmarks, deriving a robust Expected Win Score via Monte Carlo simulation (N=100,000). A tunable per-round elimination parameter enables Failure Sensitivity Analysis distinguishing robust generalists from aggressive specialists. CSD yields more nuanced, risk-informed rankings than simple averaging or static Elo.

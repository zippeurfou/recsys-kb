## Summary

Industrial recommender systems often use cascaded stages (pre-ranking and ranking) with separate objectives and feature spaces, leading to a problem called cross-stage inconsistency where upstream filtering negatively impacts downstream performance.

To address this, the authors propose UniRec, a unified model that trains both stages together in a single computation graph, allowing shared representations and gradient flow between them for better coordination.

UniRec introduces two key innovations: a dual-axis preference alignment objective to transfer downstream preferences upstream and handle multiple objectives efficiently, along with a regularization technique preventing over-optimization for high-reward item groups by ensuring balanced representation across attribute groups.

Offline evaluations show UniRec outperforms single-stage and standard cross-stage baselines, and online A/B tests demonstrate a significant 0.616% increase in app usage duration.

## Technical note

Kuaishou's UniRec jointly optimizes the multi-task fusion modules of the pre-ranking and ranking stages of a cascaded short-video recommender inside a single differentiable graph, rather than distilling ranking scores into a pre-ranking scorer (COPR, ASMOL, HCCP) or improving fusion within one stage (EMER, UMRE, xMTF, Pantheon). Two fusion agents share an embedding layer over user/item/context features and pre-ranking pXTRs, with ranking pXTRs private to the downstream agent; each applies its own DCN for explicit crosses followed by a latency-matched tower -- MLP-Mixer upstream where cost is linear in candidate count, AutoInt-Lite (shared key/value projection, one interaction layer, linear shortcut) downstream -- and an adapter feeds the stop-gradient pre-ranking representation into the ranking score as an explicit feature, so cross-stage gradient coupling flows only through the shared embeddings and stays symmetric. Supervision runs on two axes: vertically, L_align imposes the detached downstream pairwise order sign(s_i^r - s_j^r) on the upstream margin through Softplus, which is invariant to downstream score calibration and scale drift; horizontally, Compact Pairwise Preference Aggregation sums the per-objective preference evidences A_ij^+ and A_ij^- before the nonlinearity, an exact algebraic identity that shrinks the backward graph from O(MN^2) to O(N^2) Softplus terms. Attribute Group-Relative Regularization then reuses those same evidences as an item reward r_i in [-1,1], buckets candidates by video-duration quantiles, computes GRPO-style within-bucket advantages that sum to zero (so inflating an entire duration band yields no gain) and matches the score-softmax to exp(A_i/tau) with a within-bucket KL, chosen over advantage-weighted likelihood because the latter is unbounded under score rescaling. On the public RecFlow benchmark (Kuaishou multi-stage logs, frozen DSSM pre-ranking and DIN ranking backbones, M=7 objectives) UniRec takes the best NDCG at all six cutoffs and ASH 0.9978 versus 0.9855 UMRE, 0.9668 COPR, 0.9648 Weighted-Sum and 0.7219 EMER, with Kendall tau 0.6318 and Spearman rho 0.8072 against 0.4609/0.6237 for the best baseline; UMRE edges it only on rank_AUC (0.6009 vs 0.5912). Production-stream ablations show the posterior reward loss matters most, removing AGRR costs 0.0215 ASH with negligible ranking change, removing alignment costs 0.0139 ASH with no downstream quality loss, and CPPA raises training throughput 30.1% while cutting per-sample processing time 23.1%. A one-week A/B on 20% of live Kuaishou traffic (100M+ DAU) gave +0.616% app usage time, +0.675% total watch time, +0.755% video watch time, +0.189% active users, +2.367% like and +1.573% follow with no metric down; UniRec is fully deployed and adds zero serving latency since the coupling exists only during training and the two agents export as separate graphs.

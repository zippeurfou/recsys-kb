## Summary

1.  Cascaded recommender systems often have inconsistencies between stages due to separate optimization, leading to downstream items being filtered by upstream models. Existing methods usually focus on just the ranking stage or use downstream-derived scores for cross-stage coordination.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces UniRec, a unified model that connects the pre-ranking and ranking stages by sharing input embeddings in a single computation graph, allowing joint optimization.
3.  UniRec employs a dual-axis preference alignment objective and attribute group-relative regularization to coordinate both stages effectively, achieving significant offline and online performance improvements, including a 0.616% increase in app usage duration, and being fully deployed on the Kuaishou platform.

## Technical note

In cascaded recommenders, separately tuned pre-ranking and ranking multi-task fusion modules create cross-stage inconsistency: upstream filters items the downstream ranker prefers, and downstream fusion changes can offset upstream gains. UniRec trains both fusion agents in one computation graph with partially shared input embeddings and a one-way adapter; a dual-axis preference alignment objective combines a vertical cross-stage consistency term (transferring downstream pairwise preferences to the upstream fusion score) with a horizontal compact aggregation term (CPPA) that folds dozens of pairwise objectives into two bidirectional preference signals, cutting terms from O(MN^2) to O(N^2). An attribute group-relative regularization (AGRR) computes advantages within attribute groups such as video duration so uniformly promoting a high-reward group yields no gain. On RecFlow and Kuaishou data it beats single-stage fusion and cross-stage coordination baselines, and a one-week online A/B test improved total watch time by +0.675% and app usage time by +0.616% with no metric regressions; it is fully deployed on Kuaishou.

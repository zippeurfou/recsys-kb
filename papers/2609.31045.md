## Summary

1.  Large-scale AI applications like recommendation and advertising face challenges compressing long user behavior histories efficiently due to computational bottlenecks.
2.  The paper introduces KuaFu, a novel compression system that reduces individual user interaction sequences by an estimated 10-20x in token length, while preserving key information through specific training.
3.  KuaFu significantly boosts computational throughput (saving 190 GPUs), improves downstream performance metrics, and even outperforms larger models at similar compression ratios, having demonstrated real-world success on a billion-user platform.

## Technical note

Tencent's KuaFu targets weekly LLM-based profiling of a billion users (~100K QPM under a fixed GPU budget), where serialized behavior histories reach tens of thousands of tokens and naive truncation or compression causes fabrication, omission, date-misattribution and broken-logic hallucinations. Each behavior item is encoded independently by a LoRA-tuned Qwen3-4B compressor into memory tokens, and a two-axis projector (e.g. 8 to 2 tokens and 2560 to 128 dims, plus a pooled residual) shrinks the cached per-item state from ~10 KB to ~0.5 KB, so item embeddings are computed once, cached, and reused across users and tasks by a separately deployed decoder. Training has four stages (reconstruction with a five-bucket item-count curriculum that was necessary for convergence, compressed QA, compressor-decoder co-training, and hallucination-aware DAPO with harm-weighted error penalties plus a completeness bonus), cutting fabrication from 33.5% to 4.5% and raising fully correct answers from 56% to 90%. The compressed multi-task model matches or beats uncompressed single-task production models on all five profiling metrics (up to +2.0 pts), lifts per-GPU throughput 37-350% (190 GPUs saved), beats ICAE, 500xCompressor, EPL and SAC at equal compression ratios (up to +17.7 EM on out-of-domain MRQA), and lets a 4B model beat its 8B sibling on RecBench by 1.90 pts. Online, profiles built from two years of behavior refreshed weekly (vs. one year refreshed monthly) gave +1.37% GMV against a 5% holdout over ten months.

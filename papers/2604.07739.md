## Summary

Recommendation systems face the challenge of adapting to changing user behavior efficiently, as retraining with all data is often impractical due to its scale. This paper explores using targeted data selection as a solution, finding that specific gradient-based representations and distribution-matching sampling strategies can identify small, highly informative subsets of user data. These curated datasets allow for efficient model updates while effectively combating performance degradation caused by data drift, making adaptation scalable for large systems.

## Technical note

This Spotify empirical study examines efficient data selection for continually adapting sequential generative recommenders (Meta's HSTU) under temporal behavioral drift, using a decade-scale (2015-2025) music and podcast dataset of ~10K users and ~10M items. It frames two pressures: temporal adaptation (performance degrades without updates) and data scaling under compute constraints (retraining on the full unbounded stream is impractical). HSTU is used for its relative temporal attention biases and SiLU-based attention that preserve engagement intensity over long non-stationary histories. The core finding is that gradient-informed data representations combined with distribution-matching-plus-diversity sampling select small, highly informative subsets that recover much of full-retraining's NDCG@50/HR@50 gains while cutting cost, outperforming random-subset baselines and positioning data curation as a practical mechanism for scalable adaptive updates.

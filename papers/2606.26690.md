## Summary

This paper addresses a key issue in large-scale advertising: production attribution systems often overstate performance by failing to account for cannibalization, where paid channels capture existing demand meant for organic or other sources. The authors propose an experiment-calibrated framework that uses controlled incrementality tests as anchors to measure and correct this distortion on a daily basis.

The core of their solution is converting these lift measurements into daily corrections estimates. They then distribute the corrected cannibalization volume across business hierarchies while ensuring consistency with underlying data structures, making the output practical for production use in budget decisions.

Offline validation shows this framework significantly improves accuracy compared to standard attribution and baseline ML methods by reducing calibration error. Deployment at TikTok demonstrated its real-world effectiveness, resulting in substantial reductions (approx 15 pp) in measured cannibalization rates across markets and informing better strategic adjustments.

## Technical note

This TikTok/ByteDance framework (ETDC+HCA) corrects paid-attribution cannibalization by treating sparse incrementality experiments as causal anchors for dense-but-biased production attribution. It defines cannibalization rate C_t = 1 - Lift_t/A_t and learns Experiment-to-Daily Cannibalization: a generalized linear model with Huber loss maps continuously-observed channel-day proxy/temporal/media-state features to calibrated daily lift, using experiment lift as noisy causal supervision. Hierarchical Cannibalization Allocation then distributes the calibrated cannibalized volume across business hierarchies under aggregate-consistency, feasibility, and locality constraints (children sum to parent, bounded by nominal attribution). It deploys as a lightweight add-on without modifying the attribution pipeline and reduced measured cannibalization by ~15 percentage points across markets.

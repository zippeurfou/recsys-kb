## Summary

Entity search in e-commerce struggles with capturing nuanced context-specific product similarity using traditional embedding methods. This paper introduces a two-stage approach leveraging LLMs: an offline stage constructs a structured attribute graph from unstructured product data with category schemas, and an online stage uses this graph for reasoning-based ranking. The system significantly reduces token usage (57%) while improving ranking precision, outperforming baselines by over 5% in zero-shot scenarios and demonstrating robust cross-category performance and real-world potential.

## Technical note

This paper presents a two-stage LLM-guided approach for entity (product) search and ranking in e-commerce, where context-specific attribute relevance varies by category. In an offline stage, an LLM (Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2) defines category-aware attribute schemas at super-category and sub-category levels (61 super-categories, 4,940 sub-categories), extracts structured attribute-value pairs from unstructured product text, and builds a reusable bipartite attribute graph linking product entities to attribute nodes via value relations. In the online stage, candidates are generated with bge-m3 dense embeddings plus FAISS approximate nearest-neighbor search, then a graph-aware LLM reasons over the curated structured attributes rather than raw free text to rank them. Reasoning over attributes cuts per-product token usage by 57% while improving precision, yielding over 5% average-precision gains over baselines in zero-shot settings with robust cross-category generalization and better interpretability.

## Summary

Current ranking systems for online marketplaces often fail to account for how users compare multiple items before deciding, focusing instead on individual item relevance. To address this, the paper introduces the Learning-to-Comparison-Shop (LTCS) system, which explicitly models users' comparison shopping behavior during ranking. Experiments show that LTCS significantly improves key business metrics like NDCG and booking conversion rates compared to existing methods and user experience.

## Technical note

The paper proposes Learning-to-Comparison-Shop (LTCS), a ranking architecture for online marketplaces (Airbnb) that explicitly models users' two-stage comparison-shopping behavior: a pointwise evaluation stage and a set-wise comparison stage. LTCS jointly co-trains a lightweight pointwise initial ranker with a transformer-based setwise re-ranker, aligning training with how users first evaluate listings independently then compare top candidates, improving consistency between ranking stages. Offline and online A/B tests show statistically significant gains, improving NDCG by 1.7% and booking conversion rate by 0.6% while outperforming prior neural re-rankers like SetRank.

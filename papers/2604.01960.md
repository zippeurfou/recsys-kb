## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of large-k Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) search, where existing quantization-based indexes perform poorly due to inefficient candidate handling and reduced pruning effectiveness. To solve this, the authors introduce BBC (Bucket-based Result Collector), a novel system comprising a distance-organized buffer and tailored re-ranking algorithms designed specifically for large-k queries. Extensive experiments show that BBC significantly accelerates these indexes, achieving up to 3.8x speedup at high recall levels for large-k queries.

## Technical note

The paper targets large-k ANN queries (k >= 5,000), common in training-data mining, document retrieve-and-rerank, and industrial recommendation candidate retrieval, where existing indexes degrade sharply. It diagnoses two causes for quantization-based indexes: inefficient top-k collectors whose binary-heap priority queues exceed L1 cache capacity at large k, and reduced pruning effectiveness that inflates re-ranking cost. It proposes a Bucket-Based result Collector (BBC) that organizes candidates into distance buckets to cut ranking cost and improve cache locality, plus two re-ranking algorithms tailored to different quantization methods that reduce re-ranked objects or cache misses. Experiments show up to 3.8x speedup at recall@k = 0.95 over quantization-based baselines like IVF+RaBitQ and IVF+PQ.

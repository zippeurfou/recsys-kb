## Summary

This paper introduces "structural uncertainty" as a framework to quantify consistency in large language models' logical reasoning by analyzing how the model ranks its own generated answer candidates against each other. It differs from standard output dispersion methods by using self-preference induced rankings via Bradley-Terry modeling with PageRank, decomposing into two entropy components: across-trial ranking instability (negative correlation) and within-trial candidate ambiguity (positive correlation). The findings indicate that structural uncertainty provides complementary information to answer correctness, revealing regime boundaries where consistency evaluation becomes more or less predictive.

## Technical note

The paper introduces structural uncertainty, a black-box consistency-aware evaluation of LLM logical reasoning based on the stability of self-preference-induced rankings over sampled reasoning candidates, complementing standard output-dispersion measures. For each query it samples N candidates, elicits sparse pairwise self-preferences over random spanning trees (N-1 edges per trial), aggregates them into ranking distributions via Bradley-Terry (or TrueSkill) with PageRank, and decomposes the signal into two entropy-based components: across-trial ranking instability and within-trial candidate ambiguity. Across five LLMs and eight benchmarks, within-trial ambiguity correlates positively with correctness on reasoning tasks while across-trial instability correlates negatively; on factual-retrieval tasks the structural signal collapses toward uniformity, diagnosing a regime boundary. It is framed as a regime-sensitive evaluator rather than a universal confidence estimator.

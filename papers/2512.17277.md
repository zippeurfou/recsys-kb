## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of recommending "cold-start" items (those seen infrequently) effectively at Pinterest's scale. The authors identified four key issues and developed corresponding solutions: making models lightweight (increasing parameters by only 5%), elevating the importance of non-historical features, mitigating low prediction scores for CS items, and addressing label sparsity. These four methods, implemented together, increased user engagement with fresh content by 10% while being cost-efficient and deployed successfully for hundreds of millions of users.

## Technical note

This Pinterest paper diagnoses four root causes of the cold-start (CS) item problem in an industrial ranking model and proposes lightweight, cost-efficient fixes adding only ~5% parameters. It adds a residual/skip connection so non-historical (content) features bypass the interaction module and get stronger gradients, a Maximum Mean Discrepancy regularization loss to close the 8-14% score gap between cold and warm items, and manifold mixup augmentation to counter label sparsity. Deployed together on Related Pins, the methods increased fresh-content engagement by ~10% with neutral cost and no drop in overall engagement, serving over 570 million users.

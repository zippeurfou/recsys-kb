## Summary

This comprehensive survey systematically analyzes over 300 papers detailing recent advancements and persistent issues in Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning across various tasks like multi-step problem solving. It introduces a structured taxonomy categorizing different reasoning paradigms such as Chain-of-Thought, mathematical, common sense, visual, retrieval-augmented, and tool-based reasoning. The work synthesizes recurring failure modes observed in LLMs while identifying future research directions aimed at improving robustness, interpretability, and generalization of reasoning capabilities.

## Technical note

This survey systematically analyzes over 300 papers on LLM reasoning and proposes a structured taxonomy of 36 method families arranged on a 6x6 grid, spanning Chain-of-Thought, multi-hop, mathematical, commonsense, visual/temporal, code/algorithmic, retrieval-augmented, tool-augmented/agentic, and reinforcement-learning-based reasoning. It reviews methodological patterns across prompting strategies, model architectures, training paradigms, reward modeling, and evaluation benchmarks, and situates paradigms along a cognitive spectrum from training foundations to high-level cognition. It synthesizes recurring failure modes, including hallucinated or unfaithful reasoning traces, brittle multi-step inference, weak causal generalization, and sensitivity to prompting and task formulation, questioning whether CoT traces reflect underlying computation or post-hoc rationalization. It closes by identifying gaps and directions such as meta-reasoning, self-evolving reasoning frameworks, multimodal reasoning, and socially grounded reasoning.

## Summary

1.  The paper presents **TD3**, a novel method combining Tucker Decomposition with meta-learning to distill large sequential recommendation datasets into smaller, synthetic summaries while preserving performance potential.
2.  TD3 specifically decomposes these summaries into distinct latent factors for users, sequences/times, and items, plus an interconnecting core, aiming to capture the essential predictive information efficiently.
3.  This approach uses a bi-level optimization scheme with RaT-BPTT to align feature spaces between models trained on original data and the distilled synthetic sequences effectively beyond simple performance matching.

## Technical note

Distilling large sequential-recommendation datasets is hard because interactions are discrete and sequentially correlated, and the learnable summary's parameter count grows with sequence length and item-set size. TD3 uses Tucker decomposition to factor the synthetic sequence summary into synthetic-user, temporal-dynamics, shared-item, and relation-core tensors (the item factor shares the trained embedding table, so it isn't learned). It adds a feature-space-alignment surrogate objective in bi-level meta-learning, an inner-loop augmentation for accurate updates, and RaT-BPTT to handle long dependencies efficiently. Experiments confirm superiority and cross-architecture generalizability on public datasets.

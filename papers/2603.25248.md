## Summary

This paper introduces ColBERT-Att, a model that enhances the existing ColBERT system for neural information retrieval. It addresses ColBERT's limitation by explicitly integrating attention mechanisms, which capture the "importance" of term similarities between queries and documents. Experiments show that this late-interaction framework combined with attention improves recall accuracy on standard benchmark datasets.

## Technical note

ColBERT-Att augments the late-interaction retrieval framework by injecting query and document token attention weights into the relevance score, addressing ColBERT's MaxSim treating all term matches as equally important. The score modifies MaxSim by multiplying each query token's max cosine similarity by the exponentiated query-token attention and the exponentiated attention of the best-matching document token, with a document-length-based regularizer delta; exponentiation accentuates the small attention magnitudes. Attention weights are a free byproduct of the encoder, computed offline for documents and on-the-fly for queries, so inference latency is unchanged. Trained by fine-tuning ColBERTv2/PLAID for 1M steps on MS-MARCO triples, it yields small but consistent recall/Success@5 improvements over ColBERTv2-PLAID on MS-MARCO, LoTTE, and BEIR benchmarks.

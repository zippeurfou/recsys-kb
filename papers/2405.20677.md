## Summary

This paper addresses Interactive-Grounded Learning (IGL) where personalized rewards depend on context, building upon previous work that lacked theoretical guarantees. It introduces the first provably efficient IGL algorithms providing sublinear regret bounds under realizability assumptions by proposing a novel Lipschitz reward estimator as an alternative to prior methods' step-function estimator which could deviate uncontrollably due to finite samples. The authors develop two specific learning strategies (explore-then-exploit and inverse-gap weighting) based on this new estimator and demonstrate its effectiveness in handling reward feedback from image or text sources, showing experimental results confirm the importance of their approach.

## Technical note

The paper (Microsoft Research + academic collaborators) gives the first provably efficient algorithms for Interactive-Grounded Learning (IGL) with personalized, context-dependent reward feedback, achieving O(T^{2/3}) sublinear regret under realizability. It shows the prior step-function reward estimator can deviate uncontrollably under finite samples, and replaces it with a novel Lipschitz reward estimator built via inverse kinematics that underestimates true reward while approximating the optimal policy well. Two algorithms are proposed on this estimator: explore-then-exploit and inverse-gap weighting. Experiments on image-feedback and text/conversational-feedback (reward-free) settings confirm the effectiveness of the Lipschitz estimator.

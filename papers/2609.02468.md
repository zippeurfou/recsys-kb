## Summary

This paper introduces DeepAffinity, a method for predicting users' evolving long-term preferences for specific product aspects (like brand, size, or color) in eCommerce. It leverages fine-tuned Small Language Models (SLMs) combined with specialized structured prompts and prediction heads to achieve accurate aspect affinity forecasts from interaction history. DeepAffinity significantly outperforms standard generative fine-tuning and general LLMs without task-specific tuning, leading to better personalization in recommendations.

## Technical note

eBay defines Aspect Affinity: estimating P(v|u,c,a), the probability that a user's next purchase in category c takes value v for aspect a (brand, size, color, style, theme, league, franchise and so on). It is framed as a temporal multi-task multi-class classification problem, with ground-truth labels drawn from a purchase at least 24 hours after the last event in the input subsequence, which deliberately excludes the immediately preceding shopping session so the model learns long-term drifting preference rather than session intent. DeepAffinity stringifies each user event using event type, item title or query string, item aspects or query facets and category, delimited by [ITEM_START], [TITLE_START] and [ASPECT_START] tokens with a BERT-style [CLS] appended per event; a SmolLM2-135M causal backbone yields an Affinity Embedding at each [CLS], which is concatenated with a category embedding and passed to one linear softmax head per aspect trained with cross-entropy. The whole user history is encoded once with losses computed at every [CLS] position, which is compute-efficient and leak-free because the backbone is causal. Data is 6 months of US traffic (tens of millions of users, hundreds of millions of purchases and queries), users with fewer than 3 purchases and the top 1 percent most active removed, aspect vocabularies floored at 2K occurrences and capped at 10K labels, context length 2048 (truncating about 15 percent of users), 200K steps at batch 64 for Fashion and 380K at batch 128 for All-Category, lr 5e-4 with cosine schedule. On Fashion, mean micro-F1 is 0.34 versus 0.28 for personalized most-common-value, 0.18 for most-common-value and 0.26 for zero-shot Gemini 1.5 Flash. On the 10-aspect All-Category set, mean micro-F1 is 0.35 versus 0.30 for a 3B QLoRA generative SFT baseline, winning 7 of 10 aspects despite being roughly 20x smaller; smaller SFT models could not reliably emit parseable JSON. Zero-shot Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 3.2 3B and Qwen2.5 32B all trail, with few-shot and CoT giving inconsistent gains and only statistics-enhanced prompting reliably helping. Extrinsically, adding brand and size affinity features to the deployed production ranker improves Recall@1 by 0.6 percent, Recall@3 by 0.4 percent and Recall@5 by 0.3 percent. Production design pushes the SLM offline and caches Affinity Embeddings, running only the linear heads at request time. Caveats: proprietary data only since no public dataset carries item aspects, no online A/B, and the stringification carries event order but no timestamps, inter-event gaps or seasonality.

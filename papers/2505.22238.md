## Summary

This paper introduces Yambda-5B, a large-scale dataset derived from the Yandex Music platform containing 4.79 billion user interactions (listening events and likes/dislikes) across nearly 1 million users and 9.39 million tracks. A key feature is the inclusion of audio embeddings for most tracks along with an "is_organic" flag distinguishing between natural user actions and those triggered by recommendations, which is vital for accurate algorithm evaluation. The authors provide a benchmark protocol using a Global Temporal Split and report results on standard models to showcase its utility as an open industrial-scale resource for improving recommender systems research.

## Technical note

Public recsys research lacks industrial-scale interaction datasets that capture diverse feedback and realistic evaluation conditions. The authors release Yambda-5B, sourced from Yandex.Music with 4.79 billion interactions across 1M users and 9.39M tracks, including implicit (listens) and explicit (likes/dislikes) feedback, CNN-derived audio embeddings, and an is_organic flag separating natural actions from recommendation-driven ones. They define a Global Temporal Split evaluation protocol (300-day train, 30-min gap, 1-day test) and benchmark ItemKNN, iALS, BPR, SANSA, and SASRec using NDCG@k, Recall@k, and Coverage@k. ItemKNN and SASRec were the top performers on the Listen+ implicit task, while the simple DecayPop baseline was strongest on the Like explicit task; ItemKNN/SANSA became computationally intractable at full scale.

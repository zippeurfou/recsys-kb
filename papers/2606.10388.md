## Summary

This paper addresses a specific failure mode in agent skill retrieval called "same-capability ambiguity," where similar skills with potentially harmful or outdated details are mistakenly selected. To measure this, they introduce SkillResolve-Bench 1.0, an auditable benchmark dataset designed to identify and evaluate the risk associated with retrieving the wrong representative from a group of same-capability skills.

The authors also propose SkillResolve as a reference method that resolves active candidate families by scoring query-specific utility against confusable negatives and contract details, ensuring only one safe representative is selected per family before final ranking. This approach demonstrably improves recall and NDCG while eliminating the risk of selecting harmful siblings entirely for top-K rankings.

Without this resolution step (or using a baseline method like SkillRouter), retrieval systems have a much higher chance (over 23%) of including dangerous, ambiguous sibling skills in their top results, highlighting that careful representative selection is crucial for safe procedural execution.

## Technical note

SkillResolve studies same-capability execution-risk retrieval for agent skill libraries, where a retriever finds the right capability family but may surface a risky sibling pointing to a stale resource, missing precondition, or wrong procedure. It introduces SkillResolve-Bench 1.0 with 661 helpful/risky pairs over a 7,982-candidate pool, reporting helpful Recall/NDCG alongside harmful sibling rate (HSR@K) as top-K exposure of the risky sibling. The reference method resolves active candidate families, scores query-conditioned utility from confusable library negatives and contract-profile cues (resource bindings, preconditions, scope, output schema), then selects one representative per family before global top-K ranking. It reaches Recall@3 0.766 and NDCG@3 0.699 with HSR@3=0, versus SkillRouter's HSR@3 0.693. Ablating representative selection raises HSR@3 to 0.236, isolating within-family choice as the safety mechanism.

## Summary

1.  The paper identifies a key problem in LLM-based conversational recommender systems (CRS): they can violate user safety constraints by implicitly inferring and disregarding sensitive user sensitivities (like trauma triggers) during recommendations.
2.  To address this, the authors introduce SafeRec, a new benchmark dataset designed to evaluate safety risks in CRS under specific user constraints, and propose SafeCRS as a solution framework.
3.  SafeCRS combines safety-focused fine-tuning with policy optimization to train LLMs to align recommendations with personalized safety, achieving a near 97% reduction in safety violations while maintaining good recommendation quality.

## Technical note

SafeCRS formalizes personalized safety alignment in LLM-based conversational recommender systems, where recommendations may violate implicitly inferred user sensitivities (trauma triggers, phobias, self-harm history). The authors build SafeRec (SafeMovie and SafeGame) by mapping IMDb Parent Guide and DoesTheDogDie tags plus ESRB descriptors into trait-conditioned continuous risk scores, and infer latent user traits from Reddit conversations to create verifiable ground truth. Their two-stage framework couples Safe Supervised Fine-Tuning with Safe Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization (Safe-GDPO), which normalizes each reward dimension independently to avoid advantage collapse and reward hacking seen in GRPO/RLHF. SafeCRS cuts safety-violation rates by up to ~96.5% while matching GPT-4-level recommendation quality and generalizing across domains.

## Summary

1.  Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks (HGNNs) used in recommendation systems often struggle with noisy edges from dense graphs, leading to poor robustness as small errors propagate easily affecting many node embeddings.
2.  The authors propose Masked Contrastive Learning (MCL), a novel method that improves recommendation robustness by strategically masking parts of the graph along meta-paths and using contrastive learning on different views created by these masks.
3.  MCL aims to simultaneously capture both local structures via one-hop neighbors and high-order relationships via meta-path neighbors, resulting in more resilient embeddings; experiments show it outperforms existing methods significantly across three real-world datasets.

## Technical note

Meta-path graphs in HGNN recommenders are dense and noisy, and the recursive propagation mechanism amplifies noise across distant nodes (perturbation enlargement), while prior contrastive HGNN methods ignore rich meta-path semantics when sampling. MCL (Masked Contrastive Learning) applies Bernoulli random node masking plus random propagation to perturb embeddings and reduce sensitivity to specific neighbors, and performs cross-view contrastive learning between a local one-hop-neighbor view and a global meta-path-neighbor view using node-level and semantic-level attention. It introduces a multi-path sampling loss where user-user/item-item positive pairs must be connected across all relevant meta-paths, improving positive-sample quality, and jointly optimizes with a BPR pairwise loss. On MovieLens-100k, Amazon, and Yelp it outperforms baselines (HGCL, HeCo, RoHe, SMIN) by up to 9.76% (Yelp), degrades less under 10%/30% injected noise and redundant meta-paths, and ablations show the mask component contributes the largest gain while also mitigating over-smoothing.

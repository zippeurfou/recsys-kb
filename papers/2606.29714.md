## Summary

This paper presents UniVAD v2, a unified approach for training visual anomaly detectors applicable across different categories and domains. The key challenge addressed is few-shot transfer learning where an episode-specific boundary must be estimated for unseen anomalies from small support sets using existing methods.

UniVAD v2 improves upon this by employing a two-sided framework: it strengthens the normal side with modules that better capture local features and global relationships, ensuring reliable boundaries; on the abnormal side, it incorporates optional abnormal references to provide rejection-side evidence. This allows controllable boundary construction without needing retraining.

Experiments demonstrate UniVAD v2 significantly improves detection performance (AUCs) compared to its predecessor across diverse datasets including industrial, logical, and medical scenarios, showing strong cross-domain generalization capabilities even under low-shot conditions.

## Technical note

UniVAD v2 (Huawei) formulates unified visual anomaly detection as support-conditioned boundary construction, estimating an episode-specific decision boundary for unseen categories from a small support set. On the normal side it augments component-patch retrieval with an Optimal-Transport Relational Modeling module (OTRM) that adds global support-query matching via transport-style mass allocation, and an Adaptive Coordination mechanism (ACRRM) that predicts episode-conditioned reliabilities to fuse local retrieval and global relational evidence. On the abnormal side, a Few-Shot Abnormal Reference module (FAR) converts optional abnormal references into residual rejection-side evidence for controllable boundary adjustment without retraining. Across six industrial/logical/medical datasets it raises mean 1N-shot image-level AUC from 83.0% to 84.5% (85.7% with one abnormal reference), and reaches 96.2%/96.9% image/pixel AUC on MVTec-AD-SS.

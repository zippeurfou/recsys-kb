## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Production recommender systems face challenges in feature deprecation due to interactions with other features, changing relevance, data drift, and the difficulty of evaluating removal impact without risking regression.
2.  **Solution:** The authors introduce FORTE, a model-agnostic framework designed to efficiently discover, validate, and remove non-critical features. It uses daily perturbation testing and identifies all downstream consumers of a feature to ensure safe and coordinated deprecation.
3.  **Results:** Applied to large-scale recommenders, FORTE successfully identified safe feature sets for removal, leading to significant model efficiency improvements (parameter reduction, faster serving, lower training costs) in one case, while avoiding performance regressions, demonstrating effective online guardrails for feature lifecycle management.

## Technical note

Production recommenders accumulate features that are hard to remove: deprecation must not regress any metric, measured importance drifts daily, correlated features mask each other, and A/B bandwidth is scarce. FORTE (Pinterest) is a model-agnostic deprecation funnel: daily permutation/ablation importance on the live production checkpoint computed in one pooled inference pass over a single materialized dataset (dataset virtualization), nested candidate sets gated on low mean importance, stable bottom rank and non-positive slope across heads and lookback windows, grouped perturbation to catch interactions, then retraining, multi-week online A/B, and lineage-based staged blocking of dereferenced features. Pooled execution runs 300 experiments 54x faster than one pass per feature (runtime grows as N^0.20-0.27), a single permutation at 150k rows gives 0.91-0.96 rank correlation with a 10-permutation average, and 38.6% of fixed-order grouped marginals were negative (grouped AUC effect 3.08% vs 6.55% summed individually), which is why the joint gate exists. On the multi-head Pin Ranker (transformer sequence encoder + DCNv2 + MMoE) removing 8 features was neutral in a 3-week A/B and cut trainable parameters 29.9%, raised serving throughput 19.8-25.5% and saved hundreds of thousands of dollars per year; on the distilled Search Relevance model the 48-feature set (43.2% of features) launched with -2.3% parameters and +2% throughput while the 59-feature set tripped an online guardrail despite neutral offline gates.

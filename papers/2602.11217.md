## Summary

This paper investigates how language model performance, measured by accuracy and confidence, transfers from pretraining to supervised fine-tuning (SFT). It found that the reliability and scaling behavior of accuracy and confidence differ significantly across benchmarks, capability categories, and model sizes. Consequently, the study provides guidance on selecting reliable benchmarks and understanding model calibration across different training stages.

## Technical note

This study measures how accuracy and confidence transfer from pretraining to supervised fine-tuning across benchmarks, capability categories, and model sizes (240M vs 1B). It finds that accuracy-confidence alignment is category-dependent: Science is reliably well-calibrated (r about 0.75-0.83), while Semantic and Commonsense tasks show weak or negative alignment indicating overconfidence on wrong answers. Effects are strongly scale-dependent, e.g. FineWeb-Edu filtering improves NLI accuracy at 240M but degrades it at 1B, and higher code proportions can improve calibration while hurting accuracy. Pretraining calibration does not predict post-SFT calibration for NLI, so the paper recommends validating benchmark and data-curation choices at multiple scales and applying post-hoc calibration like temperature scaling.

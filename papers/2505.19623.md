## Summary

This paper introduces AgentRecBench as a benchmark suite for evaluating agentic recommender systems that use Large Language Models (LLMs). These new systems offer advanced reasoning compared to traditional methods by autonomously gathering and interpreting user-item interactions in complex environments. The authors created an interactive simulator, a modular framework, and benchmarked 10 different approaches across three evaluation scenarios within their proposed benchmark, finding agentic systems superior and making these tools publicly available online.

## Technical note

The paper argues that LLM-based agentic recommenders lack standardized evaluation and introduces AgentRecBench, the first benchmark comparing classical and agentic methods. It contributes an interactive textual simulator built on a unified User-Review-Item network from Yelp, GoodReads and Amazon with dynamic data-visibility control, a modular agent framework (planning, reasoning, tool use, memory), and three scenarios: classic, evolving-interest, and cold-start. Evaluating 10 methods (MF, LightGCN, and agents like BaseAgent, CoTAgent, MemoryAgent, Baseline666, DummyAgent, RecHackers, Agent4Rec) across Qwen-72B, DeepSeek-v3 and GPT-4o-mini using HR@N, well-structured agents (Baseline666, RecHackers, DummyAgent) substantially outperform simple agents and sparse-data-crippled traditional baselines, with Baseline666 reaching up to 69.0 HR on Amazon. Case studies identify platform-specific item-side feature engineering and information-rich review selection as key design drivers; the benchmark was validated via the AgentSociety Challenge (295 teams, 1,400+ submissions).

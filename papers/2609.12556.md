## Summary

This paper addresses the limitations of current long-sequence generative recommendation methods, which either become computationally expensive and saturated with noise for full sequences or are vulnerable to noise and incomplete context in shorter sequence retrieval. The authors propose a new approach that learns differentiable soft subsequence boundaries based on preference drift and uses linear attention to create preference-coherent representations for each subsequence. Finally, a cross-attention and gated fusion mechanism combine recent items with relevant subsequence contexts, allowing the target item representation to encode both recent and long-term user preferences, and this method demonstrably improves both accuracy and efficiency.

## Technical note

Tencent, public-dataset study on long-sequence generative recommendation. Motivating experiments show both existing paradigms fail: scaling efficient full-sequence models HSTU and FuXi-Linear from length 50 to 1000 gives flat or negative NDCG@5 (KuaiRec best at short lengths, Taobao MM unchanged at 0.1338 to 0.1340) while training time grows 6.9x and 33.9x respectively; and the target-aware retrieval method GLASS peaks at length 100 (Recall@5 0.0831 KuaiRec, 0.0931 Taobao MM) and loses 7.3 percent and 33.7 percent at length 1000, with a non-monotone optimum in retrieval size, because single-similarity retrieval cannot filter semantically consistent but preference-inconsistent noise. DRIFT instead segments each user history with differentiable soft boundaries: a drift operator concatenates semantic dissimilarity (1 minus cosine between adjacent item token embeddings), the raw embedding delta, log(1 + delta t), and the Jensen-Shannon divergence between category distributions in windows on either side of position j; a small MLP maps that to a boundary score, user-level statistics (mean boundary strength, boundary variance, normalized history length) set a per-user subsequence count kappa_u in [1, K], and cumulative normalized boundary scores become soft coordinates. Items then get gated-Gaussian memberships sharpened by Gumbel-Softmax (tau_a=0.5, tau_g=1.0) and are aggregated by membership-weighted linear attention with the elu+1 feature map, giving O(L K d) complexity. Hierarchical context fusion cross-attends the recent window over subsequence representations inside each encoder block, then a gated decoder cross-attention blends local recent context and global subsequence context with a learned gate. Training adds a subsequence structure loss (semantic centroid distance, temporal gap, category coherence) to the backbone loss plus InfoNCE recent and subsequence recommendation losses. Setup: KuaiRec, ML-20M and Taobao MM, leave-one-out, three-level SIDs with codebooks [256,256,256] or [64,128,128], T5-style 4+4 layer encoder-decoder, hidden dim 96, sampled softmax with 500 negatives, max length 1000 with 50 recent items. DRIFT improves all three backbones: DRIFT-HSTU on KuaiRec gains 13.1 percent NDCG@20 and 21.6 percent Recall@20 over HSTU (0.0993 / 0.1872); on ML-20M DRIFT-GLASS reaches Recall@20 0.1608 versus 0.0922 for GLASS and DRIFT-TIGER gains up to 74.3 percent over TIGER; on Taobao MM DRIFT-GLASS leads three of four metrics. Efficiency: DRIFT-TIGER cuts per-epoch training 97.4 percent on KuaiRec and 98.8 percent on Taobao MM (up to 80.4x training and 22.4x inference speedup) and DRIFT-HSTU cuts training 37.6 to 42.1 percent and VRAM 39.6 to 61.1 percent, though DRIFT-GLASS raises VRAM 87 to 94 percent while supporting 20x longer sequences. Ablations: learned drift boundaries beat uniform and k-means segmentation, both cross-attention paths matter, and naive concatenation of subsequence with recent representations collapses on Taobao MM (about 99 percent drop in N@5 and R@5) due to semantic-space misalignment, which the gated fusion avoids; performance peaks at 20 subsequences. Caveat: public academic datasets only, no production deployment or online A/B test despite the Tencent affiliation.

## Summary

This paper addresses challenges in applying contextual bandits (a framework for sample-efficient personalization) online due to sparse/biased data and cold starts. The authors propose a method called BC-ICL, which uses pre-trained tabular models combined with bootstrap resampling on the interaction history to make action selections stochastically. They introduce an architecture conditioning the frozen model on bootstrapped samples that helps avoid common pitfalls of bandit methods. Empirically, this approach demonstrates strong performance in standard contextual bandit settings under a strict online evaluation protocol, outperforming established baselines.

## Technical note

Amazon turns frozen pretrained tabular in-context-learning models (TabPFN, TabICL) into randomized contextual-bandit policies: BC-ICL draws a bootstrap resample of the interaction history at every round, conditions the frozen predictor on that resample, scores all arms, and plays the argmax -- exploration comes from randomizing the conditioning set rather than from parameter updates or explicit posteriors. A multiplicative arm-context feature map Phi(x,a) = [x; e_a; (x^T P) (elementwise*) e_a] with a shared random projection P couples arms so a perturbed history moves several arms' scores together, avoiding isolated-arm bootstrap failure and staying inside TabPFN's feature limit (unlike the block-diagonal Kd encoding used for baselines). On six UCI/MNIST bandit conversions BC-ICL has the lowest cumulative regret: e.g. Covertype 2630.9 (TabICL) vs 3390.0 BootstrapNN and 5127.5 NeuralUCB; Isolet 2992.5 vs 4387.4 NeuralTS; Mushroom 37.7 vs 252.3 NeuralTS (85% lower). Ablations show the model's own predictive uncertainty is not enough -- bootstrap beats greedy and softmax-sampling by 5-19% regret on the harder datasets (MNIST 1453 vs 1793 greedy) -- and the multiplicative map cuts final Isolet regret 30-40% versus one-hot. Cost is the main caveat: full-history TabPFN is ~7x slower per round than neural baselines, but FIFO (2,000-interaction window) or KNN context selection brings TabICL to BootstrapNN-competitive latency with KNN matching full-history regret.

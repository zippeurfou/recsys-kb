## Summary

This paper introduces LaRec as an efficient generative recommendation framework leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs). It tackles two main issues with traditional latent reasoning: insufficient fine-grained supervision leading to sparse signals, and deterministic paths limiting diversity. LaRec uses Latent Pre-training for better alignment in the hidden space and Personalized RL-tuning that samples diverse starting points from a user's interest distribution during training. Experiments show LaRec significantly outperforms existing baselines while maintaining comparable efficiency.

## Technical note

LaRec is a generative recommendation framework that uses latent reasoning in LLMs to gain chain-of-thought accuracy without its verbose latency. Latent Pre-training supervises hidden reasoning states via step-level InfoNCE alignment to teacher CoT states (distilled from Qwen-72B by target-oriented reverse reasoning) plus a process-direction alignment toward the target item; Personalized RL-tuning then samples reasoning start points from a per-user Gaussian Mixture ('anchored exploration') and optimizes diverse latent paths with GRPO under a composite sparse-hit plus dense-semantic-similarity reward (Qwen2.5-1.5B backbone, K=6 latent steps). It beats ID- and LLM-based baselines (e.g., +10.3% H@5 over TallRec on Instruments) at latency near non-reasoning (about 0.2-0.7s vs about 2.5-3.9s per sample for CoT), and deployed as an ANN recall channel it lifts conversion +2.93% and exposure +0.46% online. A generative sequential recommender / recall channel, not a unified retrieval+ranking pipeline.

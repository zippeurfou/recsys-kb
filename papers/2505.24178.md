## Summary

1.  The paper tackles the challenge of training deep learning models on temporal graph data (like citation networks evolving over time) where conditions change, making it difficult for standard methods to generalize robustly.
2.  To address this, they introduce an "Error-bounded Invariant Link Selector" based on the Information Bottleneck principle, designed to identify and focus on parts of the temporal graph that contain consistent, label-representative information across different time points or environments.
3.  This selector works alongside task-specific loss functions (e.g., for temporal link prediction) in a pretraining phase, demonstrating via experiments against SOTA methods its effectiveness in making models generalize better to unforeseen OOD scenarios like citation recommendations.

## Technical note

OOD-Linker targets out-of-distribution generalization for temporal link prediction on spatial-temporal graphs, where train/test distribution shifts and violation of the IID assumption hurt generalization. Using the Information Bottleneck principle, it proposes an error-bounded Invariant Link Selector that distinguishes label-invariant graph components from spurious variant ones by maximizing mutual information between a selected subgraph and the label while constraining information about the full input graph. The intractable objective is optimized via variational bounds realized with trainable neural architectures over sequences of temporal edges. Task-specific losses (temporal link prediction) let pretrained models be fine-tuned for real applications such as citation and merchandise recommendation, and domain-shift experiments show gains over state-of-the-art methods.

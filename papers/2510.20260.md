## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Large Language Models (LLMs) can power recommendation systems but struggle with dynamic user interests and content changes. Initial fine-tuning captures preferences but fails to adapt quickly, requiring effective update mechanisms.
2.  **Solution:** The paper proposes a hybrid update strategy combining periodic, long-term knowledge adaptation via fine-tuning with the agility and low cost of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
3.  **Result:** Live A/B tests on a large platform showed this hybrid approach significantly improved user satisfaction, offering a practical and cost-effective way to maintain high-quality LLM recommendations despite ongoing changes.

## Technical note

Studies how to keep an LLM-powered user-interest-exploration recommender fresh on a billion-user short-form video platform, comparing periodic fine-tuning against Retrieval-Augmented Generation for incorporating shifting interest-transition patterns. Motivating analysis shows top successor-interest sets have low month-over-month Jaccard stability (mean 0.17), demonstrating the need for frequent updates. The system precomputes next-interest-cluster transitions offline for table-lookup serving; fine-tuning adapts monthly but is costly, while instance-level RAG injects recent popularity/history into prompts for cheap daily refreshes. The authors propose a hybrid strategy combining monthly fine-tuning with frequent RAG updates, and live A/B experiments show statistically significant user-satisfaction gains at low cost.

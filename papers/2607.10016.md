## Summary

Large language models (LLMs) are being used for recommenders but primarily rely on discrete text tokens, ignoring valuable numerical and embedding features generated by feature engineering or encoders. This paper proposes "soft-token fusion," mapping these heterogeneous non-textual signals into the LLM's own embedding space to be consumed as tokens.

The authors instantiate this framework in a two-tower retrieval model and add an interaction-based module that refines both textual and soft token embeddings before they enter the final LLM layer. Experiments on Amazon recommendation datasets demonstrate that their approach significantly improves retrieval performance compared to baseline methods, especially when using direct concatenation of heterogeneous features.

## Technical note

This work bridges the mismatch between LLMs' discrete-token interface and the continuous numerical and dense embedding features used in recommender pipelines by mapping non-textual signals into 'soft tokens' in the LLM embedding space. Dense embedding features (from an auxiliary Qwen3-4B encoder, mean-pooled every G tokens) and Fourier-encoded price features are compressed by separate Q-Former adapters, refined by an interaction-based Transformer fusion module, and inserted into a shared-parameter Qwen3-0.6B two-tower retrieval model trained with an in-batch contrastive loss. On three Amazon benchmarks, interaction-based fusion beats direct concatenation and single-source variants; on Toys and Games it lifts R@5 from 0.0600 (text-only) to 0.0695 and outperforms TIGER (0.0521), while improving R@10 on all three datasets. Ablations confirm Q-Former adapters and Fourier price encoding outperform MLP-projection and tokenizer-based alternatives.

## Summary

This paper presents a framework for integrating numerical features (like ratings) into Large Language Model-based recommender systems, which traditionally only handle discrete text tokens. The core approach is "soft-token fusion," mapping non-textual signals into the LLM's embedding space through shared-parameter two-tower models and an interaction module refining these new token representations. Experiments demonstrate that this method significantly improves recommendation retrieval performance compared to existing baselines and shows soft-tokenization outperforms direct concatenation of heterogeneous features.

## Technical note

LLM-based recommenders consume discrete text tokens, so the continuous numerical features and dense embedding features that industrial pipelines depend on get verbalized, discretized, or bolted on after encoding - losing scale-sensitive information. This work (Meta) maps them into the LLM embedding space as soft tokens inside a shared-parameter LLM two-tower retriever: the user/item prompt is pre-encoded by an auxiliary Qwen3-4B, mean-pooled every G tokens, and compressed by an embedding Q-Former into N_e learnable-query soft tokens, while scalar prices (a per-user price sequence from clicked items) are Fourier-encoded and compressed by a separate numerical Q-Former into N_n soft tokens; both streams are inserted before [EOS] in a Qwen3-0.6B tower trained with an in-batch-negative InfoNCE loss and last-token pooling. The central finding is that direct concatenation of the two heterogeneous soft-token streams interferes - it underperforms at least one single-source variant on all three Amazon benchmarks and even falls below the text-only baseline on Toys R@5 (0.0575 vs 0.0600) - so a lightweight Transformer pre-interaction module (bidirectional or one-way cross masks) refines the tokens before insertion. Interaction fusion is best or tied-best on nearly every metric: Toys R@5 0.0695 vs 0.0600 text-only and 0.0575 concat, Sports R@5 0.0253, Beauty R@10 0.0766. Against the TIGER protocol baselines it beats TIGER on R@10 on all three datasets (Toys 0.1064 vs 0.0712) but TIGER remains stronger on NDCG for Beauty and Sports, and appendix ablations favor Q-Former adapters over MLP and Fourier encoding over a tokenizer-based numerical representation.

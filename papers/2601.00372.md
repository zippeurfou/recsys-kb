## Summary

This paper introduces an advanced AI methodology using fine-tuned GPT-3.5-Turbo models to analyze Amazon IoT reviews, significantly outperforming traditional methods for identifying security and privacy concerns. Analyzing nearly 91,000 reviews across fitness trackers, smart speakers, and cameras, the study found approximately 5% of reviews mentioned such concerns, with security cameras showing the highest prevalence. The research reveals persistent issues like surveillance and inadequate data control, highlighting user demands for better data management and identifying previously unreported problems, offering actionable insights for developers to enhance user trust.

## Technical note

This work builds an LLM pipeline to identify and categorize security and privacy (S&P) concerns in Amazon IoT reviews by fine-tuning GPT-3.5-Turbo into two models: a Classifier-Rationalizer-Categorizer that detects and labels S&P issues and a Thematic Mapper that groups them into higher-level themes. Using dynamic few-shot prompting and large context, it reaches over 97% precision/recall on validation, far exceeding keyword-based and classical ML methods (e.g., T5, LDA, SVM). Applied to 91K reviews of fitness trackers, smart speakers, and cameras, ~5% raised S&P concerns (10% for cameras), detecting up to 15x more relevant reviews than prior work. Longitudinal analysis shows persistent surveillance and data-control concerns and surfaces two previously unreported themes around account separation and data access.

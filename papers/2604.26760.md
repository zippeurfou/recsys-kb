## Summary

Existing LLM-based recommendation systems often use a single latent vector to represent user intent, struggling to capture the multi-faceted nature of preferences. To address this, the paper introduces Factorized Latent Reasoning (FLR), which decomposes user preferences into multiple distinct factors and uses a multi-factor attention module to iteratively refine the reasoning. FLR also incorporates regularization and reinforcement learning techniques to improve diversity, specialization, and overall recommendation performance, consistently outperforming baselines on benchmarks.

## Technical note

Factorized Latent Reasoning (FLR) is an LLM-based sequential recommendation framework that decomposes latent reasoning into multiple disentangled preference factors instead of a single latent thought vector, motivated by the multi-faceted nature of user intent and classic latent-factor collaborative filtering. A lightweight multi-factor attention module iteratively refines the latent thought representation, with each factor attending to distinct aspects of the interaction history, then factor contributions are dynamically aggregated via a gating module for next-item prediction. To enforce specialization and reduce redundancy, it adds orthogonality constraints, attention-diversity regularization, and sparsity-guided (sparse routing) factor updates. FLR performs reinforcement learning directly in the factorized latent space using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) rather than sampling token-by-token reasoning paths, enabling stable alignment while avoiding explicit chain-of-thought supervision and its inference overhead. Experiments report consistent gains over strong baselines plus improved robustness and interpretability.

## Summary

User queries are often incomplete or noisy ("non-faithful"), causing standard retrievers to fail due to missing key information. The paper introduces QUARK, a training-free framework addressing this by modeling query uncertainty through multiple plausible interpretations ("recovery hypotheses") of the user's intent. QUARK robustly combines signals from these hypotheses using "query-anchored aggregation," significantly improving recall and ranking quality for both sparse and dense retrieval systems without compromising robustness.

## Technical note

Real-world queries are often non-faithful (noisy, incomplete, distorted), causing retrievers to fail when key semantics are missing. QUARK formalizes this as retrieval under recall noise and is a training-free, retriever-agnostic framework that models query uncertainty via multiple recovery hypotheses of the latent intent. Query-anchored aggregation uses the original query as a semantic anchor while hypotheses add controlled auxiliary evidence, preventing semantic drift and hypothesis hijacking. Across simulations and BEIR datasets (FIQA, SciFact, NFCorpus) with sparse and dense retrievers, QUARK improves Recall, MRR, and nDCG, and anchored aggregation beats unanchored max/mean/median pooling.

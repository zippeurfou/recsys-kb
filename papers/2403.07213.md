## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently selecting the best Large Language Model (LLM) or other machine learning models in web applications where traditional evaluation methods become too costly due to rising LLM training expenses.

Existing bandit algorithms often overlook that model performance typically increases initially before converging with further finetuning, leading to inaccurate predictions and suboptimal choices.

The authors propose the Time-Increasing Upper Confidence Bound (TI-UCB) algorithm, which predicts performance growth from iterative finetuning and uses a change detection mechanism to identify when convergence occurs. They demonstrate that TI-UCB achieves logarithmic regret bounds and effectively balances exploration cost against task reward through extensive experiments on classification models and LLMs.

## Technical note

The paper studies online model selection (e.g., choosing between a costly API LLM and a cheaper locally fine-tuned LLM) as a bandit problem, noting prior bandit approaches ignore the increasing-then-converging trend of model performance under continued training/finetuning. It proposes TI-UCB (Time-Increasing UCB), which uses a least-squares estimator to piecewise-linearly approximate each arm's increasing reward trend and predict future performance, plus a sliding-window change-detection mechanism (comparing consecutive increase predictions) to catch convergence points. They prove a logarithmic regret upper bound in the increasing-then-converging setting, implying fast convergence. Experiments on classification-model selection and online LLM selection validate its performance and parameter robustness over baselines.

## Summary

This paper introduces HF-SID, a novel method for generative retrieval in Location-Based Services (LBS) using semantic IDs (SIDs). It addresses the limitations of existing SIDs by preserving three key aspects: geographic fidelity (as coordinates are represented in a continuous 3D form), numerical fidelity (dynamic attributes are encoded individually with type-aware embeddings), and structural fidelity (hierarchical relationships are captured via contrastive learning).

HF-SID achieves this by transforming continuous coordinates and numerical attributes directly into a continuous representation *before* encoding into the discrete SID. This involves using Geo-CPT and Num-CPT with specific embeddings. The structure-based contrastive learning is specifically applied to the residual output of the LLM's last layer to differentiate fine-grained co-located POIs.

As a result, HF-SID generates a compact three-token SID that effectively preserves both the fine-grained details and the necessary context (geography, scale, hierarchy) of POIs, leading to better retrieval quality compared to standard SIDs. This improvement happens without requiring additional decoding steps, making the SID itself more informative and reliable for LBS applications.

## Technical note

HF-SID targets Semantic ID construction for generative POI retrieval on Alibaba's AMap map platform, arguing that geographic, numerical and structural fidelity must be restored in the embedding before residual quantization commits a POI to a discrete code, since the SID is the only channel through which POI information reaches the generative model. Coordinates are converted from spherical to 3D Cartesian (x, y, z in meters) so Euclidean distance is monotone in great-circle distance and continuous across the antimeridian; every scalar (coordinates plus dynamic attributes like rating, visit count, collect UV) is decomposed into a polar vector of sign, carry-smoothed integer digits d_bar_i = (|x| / 10^i) mod 10 with N=9, and (cos 2*pi*f, sin 2*pi*f) for the fractional part, then encoded by a frozen MLP Numerical Encoder pretrained with sign and per-digit softmax heads plus fractional regression. Each numeric value becomes one [NUM] token in a Qwen3-0.6B backbone, offset by a per-attribute Type Embedding so a 0.5 rating and a 0.5 visit count land in different subspaces, and the LLM is continually pre-trained in two stages: Geo-CPT on pairwise Haversine distance and nearest-pair identification, then Num-CPT on cross-attribute magnitude comparison, under a hybrid loss of cross-entropy at text positions plus the numerical multi-task loss at numeric positions. Three-layer RQ-KMeans (codebook 4096 on AMap-L, 512 on AMap-S) yields a 3-token SID; Structure-based Contrastive Learning with InfoNCE is applied only to the second-order residual before the final layer, treating two POIs as positives only when both levels of their two-level tag match so coarse-tag-only matches act as hard negatives, leaving the geographic prefix untouched. On AMap-L (11.0M users, 48.1M POIs) HF-SID cuts average intra-SID geographic distance from 6.07 km (RQ-KMeans) to 0.25 km (-95.9%) and lifts Hit@200 from 54.47% to 81.24%, beating the strongest baseline GeoGR (77.58%) by 3.66 points while using 3 tokens versus GenPOI's 6; leave-one-out ablations cost -15.88% Hit@200 (geo), -11.52% (structure) and -4.32% (numerical). Public Foursquare NYC/TKY with Geo-CPT only give Hit@5 of 59.83% and 57.37%, beating Pro-GEO by 6.83 and 8.10 points, and t-SNE NMI on city-level labels rises from 0.2615 to 0.9796 after Geo-CPT. A one-week online A/B on the AMap homepage POI recommendation pipeline, replacing an already geography-aware generative retrieval SID model with the rest of the stack unchanged, averaged +6.74% PV_CVR, +6.03% UV_CVR, +0.67% PV_CTR and +0.92% WinRate across Restaurant, Life Services and Tourist Attraction, with Tourist Attraction strongest at +11.51% PV_CVR; downstream next-POI SFT used Qwen3-4B with beam width 300 on 64 A100s, and the authors release AMap-S (0.90M users, 4.58M POIs) as a public benchmark.

## Summary

This paper compares two methods for generating Semantic IDs (SID) in personalized LLM recommenders: autoregressive beam search and multi-token prediction (MTP). It finds that while MTP offers faster inference, beam search consistently produces better results on crucial metrics like ranking and content understanding, despite higher training costs. The study demonstrates that combining SID generation with a multi-task LLM leads to significant overall quality improvements and faster training compared to the non-SID baseline, but the language regression issue from vocabulary expansion was ultimately addressed using reinforcement learning.

## Technical note

Netflix compares two ways for an instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen3-8B / 0.6B, LoRA SFT) to generate RQ-VAE Semantic IDs (1024x4 codes placed on the LM head) for streaming-title retrieval inside a multi-task model that also does content QA and instruction following: single-head autoregressive decoding with trie- and catalog-constrained beam search on vLLM, vs multi-token prediction (MTP) with L parallel heads, strengthened by a new sampled-softmax auxiliary ranking loss over the joint SID score. On >=10M examples per task, single-head beam search beats MTP on overall nMRR (+2.0%), discovery (+7.3%), new-user (+2.1%) and content-QA accuracy (+2.0%) at 8B, and trains 2.7x faster at 2.6x lower dollar cost; MTP wins only on inference throughput (+75%), so it is kept only for dense full-catalog scoring. Versus the previous non-SID baseline with a catalog-sized auxiliary ranking head, the SID recipe (with Muon, Cut Cross-Entropy, packing, HSDP) gives +77.3% new-item nMRR, +21.7% content-QA accuracy, +4.7% overall nMRR and 2.8x faster training, but regresses IFEval -7.3% / MMLU -10% from SID vocabulary expansion, which RL post-training recovers (IFEval +12.8%).

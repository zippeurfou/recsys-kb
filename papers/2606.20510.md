## Summary

This paper introduces a new verification framework for AI agents operating under probabilistic policies, where actions or state transitions have uncertain outcomes with potential failure probabilities. Existing deterministic monitoring approaches cannot handle this ambiguity effectively. The key contribution is using distributionally robust optimization to compute sound upper bounds on the probability of policy violation without needing independence assumptions about correlated predicates, making it suitable for real-world applications like declassification or PII detection where correlations exist but are unknown. On standard benchmarks, the framework demonstrates superior efficiency and effectiveness compared to prior probabilistic methods while providing rigorous guarantees against security risks through these computed upper bounds.

## Technical note

This paper extends runtime policy monitoring for AI agents from deterministic to probabilistic predicates, needed when upstream tools like PII detectors or declassifiers have per-invocation failure probabilities. Multi-step agent trajectories are expressed as a Datalog program compiled into a derivation graph (conjunction, disjunction, inversion), and execution risk is cast as an exponentially-sized LP over joint probability measures that makes no independence assumptions, so it stays sound under unknown correlations (unlike Monte Carlo or weighted model counting). A polynomially-sized semidefinite-programming relaxation based on distributionally robust optimization tracks second-order moments to give a sound over-approximation of worst-case violation probability for real-time use. On Intercode-NL2Bash, ATBench, and Praline benchmarks it improves the security-utility trade-off with rigorous guarantees; unrelated to recommendation.

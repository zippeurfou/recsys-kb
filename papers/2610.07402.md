## Summary

This paper argues that SimHash, a simple hashing method, is fundamentally sound for semantic ID construction in generative recommendation, challenging the view that its performance is inherently inferior. The authors identify a structural mismatch between SimHash and autoregressive decoding, along with information loss, as key issues preventing its success. They propose FLASH, a two-stage framework combining parallel decoding with SimHash tokenization and explicit semantic alignment, which achieves state-of-the-art results and strong cold-start generalization without any tokenizer training.

## Technical note

FLASH argues that TIGER's finding that SimHash semantic IDs perform far worse than RQ-VAE comes from a mismatch between unordered, position-independent hash codes and autoregressive decoding, plus information lost in discretization, rather than from hashing itself. Stage I builds training-free semantic IDs by projecting frozen text-embedding-3-large item embeddings (3,072-d) onto random Gaussian hyperplanes, with log2(M) sign bits per code position (32 to 64 positions with M=256 or 512 in the best configs); Stage II runs a 2-layer Transformer over per-position codebook embeddings (concatenated, linearly projected, with 0.2 position-level codebook dropout), predicts all positions in parallel with multi-token prediction heads, and adds a negative-cosine alignment loss (weight 0.1 to 0.2) that anchors item representations to the frozen LLM embeddings. Inference scores every item by summing cached per-position log-probabilities, costing O(mMd + Nm) instead of O(Nmd). On Amazon Beauty, Toys, Sports and CDs it beats ID-based (SASRec, BERT4Rec and others), RQ-based (TIGER, LETTER, DIGER) and PQ-based (RecJPQ, VQ-Rec, RPG) baselines on every metric, improving N@5 by 5.51% to 14.46% over the best baseline (e.g. +11.02% N@10 vs RPG on Toys), while SimHash tokenizes in under 0.5 s on CPU versus tens to hundreds of seconds for RQ-VAE, RQ-KMeans or OPQ, with collision counts comparable to RQ-VAE. The alignment loss also helps other models (Beauty R@10 +13.06% for SASRec, +9.57% for TIGER, +7.49% for FLASH), longer IDs help with gains flattening past about 32 positions on Beauty, and FLASH beats TIGER and SASRec in a cold-start test where 5% of target items never appear in training.

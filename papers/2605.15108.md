## Summary

Off-policy evaluation allows companies to estimate the performance of a new system using historical data collected under a different logging policy. This paper explores how to design that logging policy optimally to minimize the error of the OPE estimate. It identifies a fundamental tradeoff between focusing logging on high-reward actions (reducing variance) and covering a wider range of possibilities (ensuring the logging policy is relevant to the target policy). The research provides theoretical optimal logging policies for different scenarios and offers practical guidance for real-world constraints.

## Technical note

This Spotify paper studies how to design logging policies that minimize the mean-squared error of an IPW off-policy estimate of a target recommendation policy's value, framed as a contextual bandit. It characterizes a fundamental reward-coverage tradeoff and derives optimal policies across informational regimes: uniform randomization is minimax optimal when nothing is known, while a Neyman allocation toward the target (weighting by target mass times the square root of reward probability) is optimal when both are known and can beat on-policy A/B testing on both value and precision. For partially known targets it derives a second-moment pseudo-target, and for noisy reward estimates it applies empirical-Bayes posterior shrinkage. Practical soft-greedy classes (top-k, softmax, power-normalized) with a single greediness knob approach the optimum under operational constraints.

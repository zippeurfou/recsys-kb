## Summary

Recommendation models often exhibit the "one-epoch phenomenon," where initial training epochs negatively impact later performance despite lowering training loss. The authors attribute this to "self-influence asymmetry," caused by embedding vectors being updated after their own labels are used for scoring, creating unreliable representations for subsequent examples. They propose Uncertainty-Weighted Sensitivity Regularization (UWSR), which modifies the loss function to penalize consumers for relying on embedding vectors with high update uncertainty, effectively preventing the degradation caused by later epochs without altering the embeddings themselves.

## Technical note

Sparse-embedding recommenders show the one-epoch phenomenon, where a second epoch lowers training loss but hurts generalization (naive four-epoch replay worsens test BCE by 13.85% to 95.43% versus one epoch), and the authors attribute it to self-influence asymmetry, a break in prequential training where a replayed example is scored through embedding rows that already hold a label-aligned displacement from its own earlier update. An exact scalar model shows fresh data wants consumer weight tau^2/(tau^2+Sigma_x) while replay wants 1, with excess fresh loss Sigma_x^2/(tau^2+Sigma_x) where Sigma_x = nu^2/N_x grows as row support N_x shrinks, a local GGN/Laplace analysis shows first-epoch training penalizes and replay rewards the uncertainty-weighted logit sensitivity s^T I^-1 s, and updating embeddings before the consumer within one epoch reproduces the failure (logit sensitivity +54% to +474%, validation BCE 1.86% to 13.14% worse), with the largest inflation on rarely seen rows. UWSR adds lambda times the sum over active rows of s^T Sigma_hat s to the BCE loss after epoch one, where s is the logit gradient with respect to the embedding row computed by double backpropagation and Sigma_hat are frozen inverse-information weights from a row-wise AdaGrad-style squared-gradient accumulator gathered in epoch one (one scalar per row, the same reduction row-wise Adagrad and row-wise Adam use), so it regularizes the dense consumer and leaves the embeddings free to grow. With a WuKong-style consumer, 32-dim zero-initialized tables, Adam (lr 2e-3, batch 4096), and five seeds, four-epoch UWSR cuts test BCE by 1.38%, 6.78%, and 2.29% and raises AUC by 0.0058, 0.0231, and 0.0141 versus one epoch on MovieLens-20M, Amazon Electronics, and Avazu, ahead of MEDA, AdamAR, active-row AdamW, embedding dropout, and freezing embeddings until the last epoch, and its expected-Fisher variant also helps DCNv2 and AutoInt (+9.02% and +8.58% Amazon validation BCE). It costs 1.13x end-to-end, an unweighted sensitivity penalty is weaker (Amazon BCE -1.21% versus +6.78%), the MovieLens gains have 95% intervals spanning zero, and the analysis assumes independent rows and fixed-window replay, so continual training would need row information updated as new data arrives.

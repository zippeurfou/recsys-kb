## Summary

This paper introduces ATLAS-Bench, a new benchmark designed to evaluate LLM agents' ability to perform reliable local area searches. It consists of 1,200 queries generated from real events across three regions, focusing on distinguishing mandatory requirements from preferences and fact-checking recommendations.

The authors also created ATLASBox, a standardized evaluation environment with search tools and a POI knowledge base, to assess agents using specific quality metrics. They compared eleven different LLM models across fourteen configurations on this benchmark.

Evaluation results showed GPT-6-Luna performed best in terms of constraint satisfaction rate and factual accuracy, although GLM-5.3 provided acceptable recommendations more frequently across all queries. This highlights the challenge agents face in balancing requirement satisfaction with useful output across diverse search scenarios.

## Technical note

LLM search agents lack benchmarks that check whether recommended places satisfy mandatory requirements, honor preferences, and ground subjective judgments in evidence. ATLAS-Bench (Alibaba/Amap) has 1,200 queries over Chaoyang, Huangpu and Dabancheng expanded from logged navigation-platform search events using POI attributes, reviews and privacy-rewritten user personas; each requirement is tagged on hardness (hard vs soft) and subjectivity (objective vs subjective) with an evidence source and check predicate, some queries are unsatisfiable by design, and a recursive self-improving pipeline (generate, assert, cross-model debate, fix) accumulates assertions in an external memory. ATLASBox is a frozen sandbox with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B retrieval of 100 candidates plus Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B adaptive top-k, nearby-search and review tools, a 5-round cap, and a DeepSeek-V4-Pro agent-as-a-judge (93% exact agreement with human experts) scoring constraint satisfaction rate (CSR), CR, Success@k, factual accuracy, four trajectory dimensions and token cost. Over 14 configurations of 11 models, GPT-6-Luna has the best CSR (71.5%) and factual accuracy (95.6%) but only 1.25 recommendations per query and 67.3% Success@k, while GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reach 85.3% and 84.7% Success@k with lower precision, exposing a coverage vs constraint-satisfaction trade-off; dense Huangpu is hardest (CSR 41.4%), and vector+rerank retrieval beats BM25 (NDCG@10 0.745 vs 0.606).

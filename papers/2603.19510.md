## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of selecting candidates in large-scale voting systems with minimal voter information. It proposes a "moment-based approach" modeling the electorate as a distribution of voter types, using limited pairwise comparisons or graded feedback per voter. The key findings are that one bit (a pairwise comparison) suffices for maximum welfare but not for measuring inequality (requiring second moments), while two pairwise comparisons or one graded comparison are sufficient to capture all moments and the entire voter distribution, enabling diverse social choice objectives.

## Technical note

This paper studies social choice under an extreme communication budget in a linear model where each voter's utility is the inner product of a latent voter-type vector on the unit sphere with the embedding of the context-and-candidate, and candidate/voter spaces may be infinite. Motivated by RLHF-style value alignment (anonymous voters, roughly one bit of feedback each), it models the electorate as an unknown distribution over voter types and recovers its moments as summary statistics for candidate selection. Key results: one pairwise comparison per voter suffices to identify the first moment and select a social-welfare-maximizing candidate (with sample complexity polynomial in 1/epsilon and dimension d), but cannot identify the second moment and thus cannot support inequality-aware objectives; two pairwise comparisons per voter, or a single graded comparison, identify the second moment and in fact all moments, recovering the entire voter-type distribution. These enable principled solutions to inequality-aware welfare criteria and representative committee selection.

## Summary

Current reinforcement learning methods for large language models (LLMs) searching for answers often struggle with multi-turn interactions due to high redundancy and "tunnel vision," where early noisy results lead to irreversible errors. To address this, the paper introduces SIGHT, a framework that improves search reasoning by using Self-Evidence Support to distill high-quality evidence and Information-Gain scores to identify pivotal states for branching. SIGHT then uses dynamic interventions like de-duplication or reflection to create new branches based on this evidence, while optimizing exploration via group relative policy learning. Experiments show SIGHT significantly outperforms existing methods, especially in complex reasoning, using fewer search steps.

## Technical note

SIGHT is an agentic RL framework for multi-turn search that combats Tunnel Vision, where noisy early retrievals cause irreversible error accumulation. It adds Self-Evidence Support (SES) steps that distill each raw search observation into a noise-free evidence snippet, then computes an Information-Gain score (posterior-minus-prior likelihood) to identify pivotal states worth exploring. The IG score gates Dynamic Prompting Interventions such as de-duplication, reflection, and adaptive branching to enforce semantic diversity from SES-distilled evidence. Training combines SES and correctness rewards under Group Relative Policy Optimization in a ReAct-style Thought-Action-Observation MDP, achieving higher multi-hop QA accuracy with fewer search steps and no external verifier.

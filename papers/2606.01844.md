## Technical note

This paper builds a quantitative diagnostic framework for Semantic ID tokenizer quality, defining the Expected Overlap Rate (O_pi, expected codeword confusion under retrieval-time isotropic perturbation) and Effective Codebook Size (K_eff = 1/O_pi), which decompose SID degradation into a Distribution Penalty (codebook collapse under long-tailed usage) and a Geometry Penalty (flat Euclidean/Minkowski-sum grids mismatching curved embedding manifolds). As a proof of concept it proposes Decoupled Residual Quantization (DRQ), separating continuous geometry reconstruction (VAE) from discrete distribution matching (K-Means) instead of coupling them through the straight-through estimator. Experiments on one proprietary large-scale industrial dataset reveal SID quality is multi-objective (symbolic robustness, reconstruction fidelity, behavior-aware soft matching), presented as a case study.

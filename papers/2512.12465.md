## Summary

This paper explores the Transition Matching (TM) generative modeling paradigm, which uses a backbone network and a smaller "head" module to efficiently transform noise into data. The authors conducted a large-scale study training 56 different 1.7B text-to-image models to analyze the head module's impact on TM's design, training, and sampling. They found an MLP head with specific training and high-frequency sampling achieved state-of-the-art performance across all metrics, while a Transformer head excelled in image aesthetics, providing key insights into TM design gains.

## Technical note

This paper is a large-scale empirical study of the Transition Matching (TM) generative paradigm, which generalizes diffusion, flow matching, and continuous-state autoregressive models by using a second internal generative model for more expressive transition kernels via a backbone-head architecture. Focusing on the continuous-time bidirectional variant, the authors train 56 different 1.7B text-to-image models (549 evaluations) with fixed backbone/data to ablate head architecture (MLP/Conv/Transformer), size, sequence and batch scaling, time weighting, parameterization, and sampling. They also propose a novel stochastic TM sampler. An MLP head with specific backbone-head time weighting and high-frequency stochastic sampling (DTM++) ranks best across metrics, while a Transformer head with sequence scaling and low-frequency sampling (DTM+) excels at image aesthetics.

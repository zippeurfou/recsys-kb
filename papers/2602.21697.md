## Summary

Current LLM-based code edit recommendations, despite strong benchmark scores, often slow down developers and disrupt their mental flow because they rely on static snapshots and ignore the temporal, incremental nature of how developers actually work.

To address this, the paper introduces EditFlow, a benchmark and optimization framework that reconstructs developer editing flows, recognizing that recommendations should align with the ongoing, context-sensitive reasoning process.

EditFlow tackles three main challenges: efficiently collecting complex edit-order data, creating a realistic digital twin simulation for benchmarking, and developing a unified optimization strategy that can enhance all recommendation systems' alignment with developer flow.

## Technical note

EditFlow benchmarks and optimizes subsequent code-edit recommendation systems from the perspective of developers' mental flow, motivated by findings that most AI edit suggestions disrupt flow despite high benchmark accuracy. It addresses the loss of temporal ordering in commit-snapshot training data by reconstructing plausible developer editing flows. The method has three coupled components: a prompt auto-tuning mechanism that learns prompts for inferring relative edit order, a digital twin that replays reconstructed edit sequences to simulate the editing process, and a unified optimization strategy enforcing flow continuity across heterogeneous models. Evaluations on annotated commits, industrial code, and open-source repos plus a 32-developer user study show improved order-reconstruction accuracy, fewer flow violations, and faster task completion.

## Summary

This paper introduces X-Cross, a new model for quickly adapting recommendation systems to entirely different domains without extensive retraining.

X-Cross dynamically integrates multiple domain-specific language models, each fine-tuned using low-rank adapters (LoRA), by layer-by-layer refining their representations while preserving domain nuances.

Compared to standard baselines and direct LoRA adaptation methods on Amazon datasets, X-Cross achieves similar or better performance with significantly fewer additional parameters and requires much less fine-tuning data.

## Technical note

X-Cross tackles cross-domain sequential recommendation for new/sparse target domains by integrating several source-domain LLMs each fine-tuned with its own LoRA adapter. Operating layer by layer, a trainable integrator concatenates the LoRA-adapted (not raw pretrained) activations and computes per-domain positive/negative scaling and pairwise-interaction weights to refine each domain's representation with a residual connection, casting recommendation as a multiple-choice next-item scoring task over a DeBERTa backbone. On Amazon domains X-Cross matches LoRA fine-tuning using only ~25% of the extra parameters and 50-75% less target data, and beats mixture-of-LoRA baselines (XLoRA, MeteoRA). Ablations show layer-wise integration and interaction terms are the most important components.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of balancing accuracy and diversity in collaborative filtering (CF) recommendations. It introduces HI-Series hybrid algorithms combining ItemCF with diffusion-based approaches like mass diffusion, using a parameter ε for control. Experiments show that these hybrids significantly improve F1-score over both base methods while often increasing diversity metrics, effectively breaking the traditional trade-off between accuracy and novelty across different data sparsity levels.

## Technical note

The paper addresses the accuracy-diversity trade-off in recommendation by hybridizing item-based collaborative filtering with network diffusion algorithms. It proposes the HI-series (HI-MD, HI-HHP, HI-BHC, HI-BD) that nonlinearly combine ItemCF cosine-similarity (high diversity, moderate accuracy) with diffusion-based similarities such as mass diffusion (high accuracy, popularity-biased), controlled by a parameter epsilon that recovers each base method at its extremes. On MovieLens, Netflix, and RYM datasets across sparse (20%) and dense (80%) training splits, the hybrids consistently beat their base algorithms in F1-score while improving diversity and novelty. HI-MD gains 0.8-4.4% F1 over MD in sparse settings and HI-BD improves F1 by 2.3-5.2% with diversity gains up to ~18.6% in dense settings.

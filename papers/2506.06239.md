## Summary

This paper introduces MTMH, a novel item-to-item retrieval model designed for recommendation systems. It addresses limitations in current models by explicitly balancing two goals: high recall of co-engagement patterns and semantic relevance to the trigger item. The system demonstrates significant improvements (up to 56.6% better semantic relevance) using proprietary data and live experiments, enhancing both short-term metrics and long-term user experience through its multi-task learning loss and multi-head architecture.

## Technical note

MTMH is a multi-task, multi-head item-to-item (I2I) retrieval model that jointly optimizes recall and semantic relevance, addressing how co-engagement-only industrial I2I models overfit short-term patterns and ignore semantics. A preliminary study shows co-engagement models reach high recall but ~22% relevance while content-encoder embeddings reach ~37% relevance yet under 1% recall. MTMH uses a multi-task loss that distills semantic knowledge from a pretrained content encoder into item embeddings alongside a co-engagement loss, plus separate engagement and relevance heads whose retrieved sets are merged at serving via ANN search. On a billions-user platform it improves recall up to 14.4% and relevance up to 56.6%, with live gains in DAU, time spent, diversity, and novel-interest discovery.

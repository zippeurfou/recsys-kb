## Summary

1.  E-commerce clicks often don't lead to buying that specific item (the Click A Buy B or CABB phenomenon), causing standard recommendation systems trained on direct click-conversion data to learn irrelevant correlations instead of true user intent, leading to sub-optimal results.
2.  The authors propose retraining recommendations using both direct purchase signals (Click A Buy A) and these indirect CABB conversions as separate tasks, while introducing a weighting scheme based on product category similarity learned from large-scale co-engagement data.
3.  This taxonomy-aware method specifically distinguishes related cross-category purchases (substitutes/complements) from coincidental ones in the CABB signals, resulting in offline evaluation improvements and showing promising +0.25% online conversion rate gains compared to a standard last-click attribution baseline.

## Technical note

Meta tackles the Click A, Buy B (CABB) phenomenon where clicked and purchased items differ, causing recommenders trained on raw click-conversion pairs to learn spurious correlations. Conversion prediction is reframed as a multi-task problem with separate heads for Click A -> Buy A (CABA) and Click A -> Buy B (CABB). To isolate informative CABB conversions, a taxonomy-aware collaborative-filtering weighting scheme maps products to taxonomy leaf nodes and learns a category-to-category similarity matrix from large-scale co-engagement logs, amplifying genuine substitute/complement pairs while down-weighting coincidental cross-category purchases. Offline it reduces normalized entropy by 13.9% versus a last-click baseline; an online A/B test yields +0.25% on the primary business metric.

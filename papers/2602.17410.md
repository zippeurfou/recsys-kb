## Summary

This paper introduces ILRec, a new fine-tuning method for LLM-based recommendation systems. It addresses the limitations of existing methods by using dynamic, self-hard negative signals derived from intermediate layers of the model during training, rather than relying on static, offline-generated negatives. Extensive experiments demonstrate that this approach improves the recommender system's performance by providing more discriminative negative supervision.

## Technical note

ILRec is a preference fine-tuning framework for LLM-based recommendation that derives self-hard negative signals from the model's own intermediate layers rather than static, offline-generated sequence-level negatives. Motivated by contrastive-decoding observations that intermediate layers act as capable-but-weaker predictors, it extracts high-probability non-ground-truth tokens from intermediate layers as fine-grained, token-level negatives that dynamically track the current policy and implicitly expand the negative space for large candidate sets. Training has two components: cross-layer preference optimization, which injects the self-hard negatives as fine-grained penalty coefficients into the cross-entropy loss to penalize negative tokens in the final layer, and cross-layer preference distillation, which uses the output layer as a teacher to keep intermediate-layer signals reliable. A lightweight collaborative-filtering model assigns token-level rewards to mitigate over-penalizing false negatives. Experiments on three datasets show improved LLM-recommender performance.

## Summary

This paper investigates widely-used PII masking models like Piiranha and Starpii (with over 300k combined downloads). It argues these models, trained on existing datasets, are inadequate because Named Entity Recognition techniques have inherent limitations related to context sensitivity, phrasing variations, format changes, and ambiguous content. The authors introduce a comprehensive new dataset of 17K diverse sentences containing various PII types from multiple jurisdictions (India, U.K., US), designed to reveal the real-world privacy exposure issues these models face due to their inability to handle complexities beyond standard datasets. They conclude by emphasizing performance measurement gaps and advocating for more detailed model cards that account for specific failure modes when using such systems in practice.

## Technical note

The paper audits widely-downloaded open-source PII-masking models (Piiranha, Starpii, Microsoft Presidio) that rely on NER plus pattern matching, arguing NER inherits limitations from ambiguity, phrasing variability, and format variation. The authors curate a 17K semi-synthetic sentence dataset spanning 16 PII types across India/UK/US jurisdictions, generated with GPT-4o-mini across five NER feature dimensions plus an adversarial dimension. Testing yields 28% non-identification and 67% misclassification rates across the three models, with Starpii (code-trained) failing badly on natural text. They conclude standard precision/recall metrics hide real-world failure modes and call for contextual disclosure in model cards.

## Summary

This paper introduces STAR, a novel training-free approach for leveraging large language models (LLMs) in recommendation systems. It addresses the high cost and engineering hurdles of fine-tuning LLMs by combining semantic item embeddings from an LLM with collaborative filtering data during retrieval to find candidate items. The final ranking is then improved using the LLM's pairwise comparison ability, achieving significant performance gains (+23.8% on Beauty, +37.5% on Toys & Games) over best supervised models in Hits@10 metrics for most tested domains while avoiding direct model fine-tuning.

## Technical note

Proposes STAR, a training-free LLM framework for recommendation that avoids the cost and complexity of fine-tuning while retaining quality. Its retrieval stage scores candidate items by combining LLM semantic embedding similarity with collaborative commonality from the user's history, plus temporal-recency and rating factors. A subsequent ranking stage uses the LLM's pairwise-comparison reasoning to refine next-item predictions (point-, pair-, and list-wise variants are compared). On Amazon Review, the retrieval stage alone is competitive, and the full method reaches Hits@10 gains of +23.8% on Beauty and +37.5% on Toys & Games over the best supervised models, without any fine-tuning.

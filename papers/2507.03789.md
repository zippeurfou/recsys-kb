## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of incorporating query context (like browse categories) into transformer-based sequential recommendation models for next-item prediction. The proposed method integrates this unaligned contextual data effectively within the attention mechanism, bypassing traditional difficulties and errors in fusion. Extensive experiments demonstrate that this approach improves model ranking performance by enhancing relevance and diversity on both online platforms and open datasets.

## Technical note

This paper studies how to incorporate query context (e.g., the browse category under which an interaction occurs) into transformer-based sequential recommenders for next-item prediction. Query context signals user intent but is temporally misaligned with the item sequence and often unavailable at inference due to privacy or feature-store limits, risking training-serving mismatch. The authors analyze several integration strategies under causal language modeling and propose fusing the item sequence with query context directly inside the attention mechanism. Extensive offline and online experiments on a large platform and open datasets show improved ranking relevance and diversity.

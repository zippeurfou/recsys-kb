## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Directly measuring long-term treatment effects from experiments is often difficult due to their slowness and noise.
2.  **Solution:** The authors present a statistical framework that treats finding an optimal proxy (a short-term metric) as solving a portfolio optimization problem, using denoised estimates derived from historical experiment data.
3.  **Key Insight & Validation:** They found the best proxy depends on the specific experiment's sample size and noise level, not universally fixed. This approach was successfully applied to experiments in an industrial recommendation system, yielding proxies that performed well against standard alternatives.

## Technical note

Long-term primary metrics in experiments are slow-moving and noisy, so short-term proxy metrics are needed to guide near-term decisions. The authors introduce a statistical framework that reduces optimal proxy-metric construction to a portfolio optimization problem depending on latent treatment effects and per-experiment noise, then denoise a historical corpus of experiments to estimate those latent effects. A key insight is that the optimal proxy is not fixed but should depend on the experiment's sample size (effective noise level). Applied to a large industrial recommender-system experiment corpus, the constructed proxies outperform several baselines.

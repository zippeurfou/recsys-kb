## Summary

This paper introduces CoCo, a new framework for recommendation systems that integrates large language models. It addresses limitations of existing methods by dynamically constructing user-specific contextual knowledge embeddings through adaptive mechanisms, resolving discrepancies between semantic knowledge and behavioral data. Experiments show CoCo significantly improves recommendation accuracy and achieved practical sales growth in production, offering a versatile solution for personalized recommendation.

## Technical note

CoCo is an end-to-end framework fusing LLM world knowledge into recommendation, motivated by pilot findings that static prompt templates ignore user-preference diversity and that not all LLM-generated semantic knowledge helps (t-SNE shows semantic and behavioral vectors diverge). In a collaboration-enhancement phase it uses vector quantization to generate per-user soft prompts and cross-attention to extract the semantic knowledge best aligned with each user's behavior. In a contradiction-elimination phase it evaluates at user-level whether LLM output benefits the recommender and applies LoRA fine-tuning to progressively align the LLM's semantic space with the behavioral space from interaction sequences. Trained with InfoNCE, CoCo is model-agnostic, beats seven SOTA baselines by up to 8.58%, and delivered +1.91% ad revenue and +0.64% GMV in production.

## Summary

1.  Current visual document retrieval systems, while improved, still rely on surface-level cues because they are trained only with global relevance labels, ignoring which specific parts of the document support a match, especially for complex queries.
2.  To address this, the authors introduce AGREE, a framework that uses attention mechanisms from multimodal LLMs as local supervision during training, guiding the system to identify relevant document regions.
3.  By combining this local guidance with standard global supervision, AGREE significantly improves retrieval performance on challenging benchmarks, enabling deeper semantic alignment and moving beyond simple keyword matching.

## Technical note

AGREE (Attention-Grounded REtriever Enhancement) improves visual document retrieval where late-interaction retrievers trained on coarse global relevance labels rely on surface keyword cues and miss implicit matches. It extracts query-conditioned patch-level attention maps from a frozen multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as proxy region-level supervision, downsamples them to the retriever's patch grid, and trains with a dual objective: a global contrastive loss plus a local loss aligning the retriever's patch similarities with the MLLM attention. This distills the teacher's fine-grained reasoning into the retriever, improving average nDCG@1 by 12.82% and nDCG@5 by 5.03% on ViDoRe V2 for non-extractive queries.

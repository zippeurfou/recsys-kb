## Summary

This paper introduces MTGR, an industrial-scale generative recommendation framework developed for Meituan. It addresses the issue that previous generative approaches sacrificed important traditional cross features to achieve scalability.

To overcome this, MTGR uses an HSTU architecture and retains DLRM's original features during training and inference by compressing user-level information. It further enhances performance with Group-Layer Normalization (GLN) for better semantic encoding and dynamic masking against leakage.

Finally, through framework optimizations, MTGR supports models significantly larger than standard DLRM without extra costs, achieving high computational efficiency gains (65x FLOPs reduction). These improvements allowed Meituan to deploy a large-scale MTGR system that surpassed existing performance both offline and online.

## Technical note

Generative recommenders achieve scalability but typically discard the handcrafted cross features that power traditional DLRMs, and the paper finds this sharply degrades performance in ways scaling cannot recover. MTGR (Meituan Generative Recommendation) builds on the HSTU architecture while retaining DLRM features including cross features, and uses user-level compression to accelerate training and inference. It adds Group-Layer Normalization for encoding across semantic spaces and a dynamic masking strategy to prevent information leakage. MTGR reaches 65x FLOPs per single-sample forward pass versus DLRM, delivering the largest offline/online gains in nearly two years and running main traffic at Meituan.

## Summary

1.  Static headlines in recommendation feeds fail to satisfy diverse user interests, particularly for long-tail audiences, and directly optimizing LLMs for headline generation often results in mode collapse (producing overly generic outputs).
2.  The paper introduces GESE, a novel framework that decouples personalization by first using an LLM as a probabilistic generator to create a diverse candidate headline set for exploration.
3.  GESE then employs a lightweight selector to choose the best headline from this set in real-time based on user feedback, exploiting the most relevant option; extensive deployment shows it significantly improves click-through rates and dwell time compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Technical note

Baidu, presentation layer of a commercial information feed with over 100 million daily active users. Problem: a single static editorial headline cannot serve a multimodal interest population and suppresses long-tail audiences, while training an LLM to emit one best personalized headline (SFT or DPO) is a point estimator that regresses to the mean and mode-collapses to generic safe patterns. GESE decouples the task into generation as exploration and selection as exploitation, reformulating the objective as maximizing the expected utility of the best candidate in a generated set. The explorer is a Qwen3-14B SFT'd on 30k samples to emit the entire K-headline structured list in a single inference pass (avoiding K independent samples, which both cost more and cluster around the mode), then post-trained with Group Sequence Policy Optimization using group-normalized advantages A_i = (R_i - mu) / (sigma + eps) with a clipped importance ratio and a KL penalty to the reference policy; group normalization is the diversity mechanism, since G identical outputs give zero standard deviation. The hierarchical reward mixes per-headline utility (clipped predicted CTR, a hard faithfulness gate from a discrimination model with a penalty otherwise, normalized Levenshtein novelty against the editorial title, and a length term) with a set-level diversity bonus scaled by 1 minus the character-set intersection-over-union redundancy. The CTR reward model is a hybrid architecture: a Qwen-Embedding encoder over concatenated context and headline whose EOS hidden state is concatenated with a projection of display-bias features (character length, punctuation density) into an MLP head, trained with Bradley-Terry pairwise loss on 8 million high-confidence interaction pairs. Online, new headlines cold-start under Confidence-Aware Thompson Sampling over Beta(1 + clicks, 1 + views - clicks) with rotation criteria gated by warm-up and max impression budgets and Bayesian P(win) / P(loss) thresholds against the original title, then graduate to a supervised context-aware DNN ranker over user profile, demographics, last-20 fine-grained interest tags, coarse category preferences and semantic embeddings of both titles, with explicit cross-product feature interactions. Offline on the self-curated MSR-50k benchmark (50k resources across 40+ verticals), GESE cuts Self-BLEU from 18.02 to 9.93 versus SFT-only, achieves the best Distinct-N-Gram 56.20 and predicted CTR score 11.38 while keeping NLI entailment at 80.17, beating GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-235B and DeepSeek-V3.1 at a tenth the scale. Online A/B against the raw headline: SFT-only alone is -1.50 percent CTR, SFT plus UCB selector +1.37 percent, GSPO plus UCB +2.28 percent, and full GESE +2.57 percent CTR with +0.79 percent impressions and +0.87 percent dwell time. The ablation exposes explicit reward hacking: dropping the faithfulness constraint raises the predicted CTR score to 12.79 but crashes NLI to 47.32 and ROUGE-L to 11.65, and dropping the diversity bonus doubles Self-BLEU to 19.73. Caveats: MSR-50k is an internal self-curated benchmark, the headline CTR score is the model's own reward model output rather than an independent metric, and no confidence intervals or significance tests are reported for the A/B numbers.

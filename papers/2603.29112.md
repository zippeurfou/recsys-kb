## Summary

GISTBench introduces a novel benchmark to evaluate Large Language Models' (LLMs) ability to understand users by verifying their interests based on interaction history, moving beyond traditional item prediction accuracy in recommendation systems.

This benchmark proposes two new metrics, Interest Groundedness (IG) and Interest Specificity (IS), designed to assess the accuracy and distinctiveness of LLM-generated user interest profiles, using a synthetic dataset derived from real user interactions.

Evaluating eight different LLMs, the study found that current models struggle significantly with accurately counting and attributing engagement signals across various interaction types, revealing key performance limitations.

## Technical note

GISTBench evaluates whether LLMs can extract and verify user interests from interaction histories, rather than measuring item-prediction accuracy as in traditional RecSys benchmarks. It introduces two metric families: Interest Groundedness (IG), decomposed into precision and recall via an ensemble oracle to penalize hallucinated interest categories and reward coverage, and Interest Specificity (IS), computed only over verified categories to assess distinctiveness—both verifying LLM-predicted profiles without ground-truth labels (correlating with user surveys at rho=0.67). The synthetic dataset is grounded in real engagements on a global short-form video platform with implicit (watch time) and explicit (like/dislike) positive and negative signals, and is benchmarked alongside KuaiRec, MIND, Amazon Music, and Goodreads. Evaluating eight open-weight LLMs (7B-120B) reveals they struggle to count and attribute engagement signals across heterogeneous interaction types and follow strict instructions.

## References

- Evidence Relevance (VIR): Given a predicted interest, an engagement type, and a single content item, the annotator labels whether the item is *truly relevant* to the interest (Yes/No). This corresponds to the per-item filtering step in IG verification (Section [4.2](#S4.SS2))
- Interest–Item Association (UIP): Given a predicted interest and a single content item description (no engagement context), the annotator labels whether the item supports inferring this interest (True/False). This corresponds to the per-item retrieval decision in IS evaluation (Section [4.3](#S4.SS3))

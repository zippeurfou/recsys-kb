## Summary

1.  Current generative recommendation methods often use item identifiers (Semantic IDs) as target sequences but don't fully utilize their hierarchical structure or internal relationships for reasoning, leading to inefficient learning and prediction.
2.  To address this, CogRec introduces a "Structure-Cognitive" framework that enhances the standard ID hierarchy with semantic graphs and item-level neighborhoods, defining specific operations ("Match", "LateralJump", "Explore") within this augmented space (SID topology) to guide reasoning during generation.
3.  By training these structural reasoning components alongside direct sequence prediction in a shared output space, CogRec demonstrates improved recommendation accuracy on benchmarks, specifically when slower, structure-grounded navigation is needed after initial matching fails, though longer routes can introduce errors and extra computation cost.

## Technical note

CogRec grounds the intermediate reasoning of SID-based generative recommendation in the same SID space used for target generation, rather than in weakly-coupled natural-language rationales. It builds a structure-cognitive SID topology (4-layer residual-k-means SIDs plus intra-layer centroid semantic graphs plus HNSW item neighborhoods) and introduces SID Routing: layer-wise Match (fast localization), LateralJump, and Explore (slow structural navigation) operations, trained via a multi-stage pipeline (SID-token alignment, then direct SID generation, then NL-reasoning vs SID-routing branches from a shared Qwen3-1.7B checkpoint) with trie-constrained beam search. On Amazon Beauty/Sports/Toys, SID Routing improves Hit@10/NDCG@10 over its direct counterpart on two of three datasets (e.g., Sports NDCG@10 0.0256 to 0.0274) but direct generation wins on Toys; gains concentrate where prefix matching fails yet learnable SID transitions exist, while long routes add decoding cost and error accumulation. A generative sequential recommender with reasoning, not a unified retrieval+ranking pipeline.

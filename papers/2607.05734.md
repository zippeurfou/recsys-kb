## Summary

SCOReD addresses challenges in chain-of-thought distillation for recommendation systems, where large teacher models produce verbose reasoning traces unsuitable for smaller student LLMs due to high uncertainty and out-of-distribution issues.

The proposed framework first parses these teacher traces into typed segments and scores each segment based on the student model's attention patterns. It then dynamically selects edits (KEEP/REWRITE/PRUNE/FUSE) per segment by analyzing output length changes and improvements in answer likelihood given the edit, using a comparative log probability lift approach.

SCOReD optimizes the reasoning trace for efficiency and relevance to the student LLM, effectively pruning redundant parts while preserving key information. This results in improved recommendation performance (1.56% NDCG gain, 1.9% Recall@5 improvement) compared to standard supervised fine-tuning baselines, while significantly reducing reasoning length by 27.3%.

## Technical note

Chain-of-thought distillation is a necessary precursor to RL for generative recommenders, but raw teacher traces are poorly suited: large teachers show high reasoning uncertainty, rechecking their answer ~3.2 times per trace without revising it (79% of rechecks do not change the answer), and traces are out-of-distribution for small students. SCOReD parses each teacher trace (Gemma-4-26B) into six typed recommendation stages, scores each segment's importance via the target student's attention from the </think> token (extending CRISP), then selects a per-segment edit (KEEP/REWRITE/FUSE/PRUNE) by a reward combining the student's log-probability of the answer, output length, and perplexity, adapting traces to the student's own distribution. Training a Qwen3-0.6B student on SCOReD-optimized CoTs beats raw-trace SFT by +1.56% NDCG and +1.9% Recall@5 while cutting reasoning length 27.3% and parse failures ~46%, and the 0.6B student surpasses Qwen-3.6-35B-A3B and approaches the teacher; post-SFT DAPO (RL with an NDCG reward) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) yielded no consistent gains.

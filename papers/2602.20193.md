## Summary

This paper introduces that encoder-side poisoning attacks can cause persistent semantic corruption in diffusion models, even without activating the trigger. This semantic drift results from the model's representation manifold being deformed geometrically, acting like a low-rank target-centered distortion. To quantify this structural damage, the authors propose SEMAD, measuring both internal embedding shifts and downstream functional misalignment, highlighting a deeper risk beyond just attack success.

## Technical note

The paper studies encoder-side backdoors in text-to-image diffusion models, showing they cause persistent trigger-free semantic corruption rather than only trigger-activated failures. A Jacobian-based analysis characterizes backdoors as low-rank, target-centered local deformations that amplify local sensitivity and propagate distortion across semantic neighborhoods. It argues standard Attack Success Rate and global CLIP metrics mask this damage via global averaging, whereas dense sampling near the target reveals structural rot. It introduces SEMAD (Semantic Alignment and Drift), a two-axis diagnostic measuring internal embedding drift and downstream CLIP-based misalignment, validated across diffusion and contrastive attack paradigms. Not a recommender-systems paper.

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of obtaining sufficient domain-specific conversational data for Recommender Systems (CRS), which is costly due to privacy concerns or collection difficulties. The authors propose an active data augmentation framework that leverages black-box Large Language Models (LLMs) and uses publicly available non-conversational seed data like reviews, metadata, and collaborative signals. By intelligently selecting the most informative seed samples via active learning principles before synthesizing them into coherent conversations, their method significantly improves LLM-based CRS performance while overcoming resource scarcity issues during model training.

## Technical note

Conversational recommender systems need scarce, costly domain-specific dialogue data. The authors propose an active data augmentation framework that uses non-conversational seed data (Amazon reviews, metadata, collaborative signals), selects the most informative/diverse seeds via Jensen-Shannon and Fisher-information active-learning criteria, and prompts a black-box teacher LLM (GPT-4o) to synthesize conversational recommendation data for fine-tuning smaller student models (LLaMA3, Gemma2). On ReDial and INSPIRED, models trained on actively-synthesized data consistently beat zero-shot and naive GPT-generated baselines, and NBCRS trained on synthetic data far exceeds seed-only training. Metadata- and collaborative-aware selection add further gains, and synthetic data especially helps when in-domain coverage is limited.

## Summary

This paper addresses a limitation in generative CTR prediction where standard methods treat all feature fields equally during training, ignoring their differing reconstruction difficulties. This imbalance causes easier fields to dominate and harder fields to underperform. The authors propose HeteGenCTR, which introduces learnable difficulty parameters and a self-balancing loss to automatically allocate gradient budget towards harder fields, leading to consistent improvements over existing methods on benchmarks and online tests.

## Technical note

HeteGenCTR is a heterogeneous generative CTR framework built on discrete diffusion that resolves the generative difficulty imbalance, where uniform per-field reconstruction loss lets easy-to-reconstruct fields dominate gradients while high-signal ID and behavioral-sequence fields stay underfit. It introduces a single per-field learnable difficulty parameter, jointly trained with the denoising network, that drives two coordinated mechanisms with no extra hyperparameters: a self-balancing loss (derived from homoscedastic multi-task uncertainty weighting, a la Kendall et al.) with a provably stable equilibrium that weights each field inversely to its current difficulty, and a difficulty-guided attention mechanism in an HSTU denoising network that suppresses converged easy fields and amplifies cross-field flow toward hard fields. The attention scaling is derived to align with the loss-level weighting so both mechanisms reinforce the same signal, and experiments over five datasets plus an online A/B test show consistent gains over generative and discriminative CTR baselines.

## Summary

1.  The paper introduces **Claw-Anything**, a new benchmark designed to evaluate large language model personal assistants by providing access to a much broader and more realistic slice of a user's digital world than current benchmarks.
2.  This benchmark expands context across three dimensions (long histories, backend services, GUI/CLI interaction) and simulates complex, noisy user activity over months, testing agents' ability to reason effectively amidst irrelevant events and conflicting signals.
3.  Experiments show that even a current top model like GPT-5.5 performs poorly (only 34.5% pass rate) on this benchmark, highlighting the gap between existing agent capabilities and the demands of truly always-on, proactive assistance, while also demonstrating the utility of their data generation pipeline.

## Technical note

Claw-Anything is a benchmark for evaluating always-on LLM personal assistants with far broader, more realistic access to a user's digital world than prior agent benchmarks. It expands context along three dimensions (long histories, backend services, and GUI/CLI interaction) and simulates months of noisy, conflicting user activity to test reasoning amid irrelevant events. Even a top model (reported as GPT-5.5) reaches only a 34.5% pass rate, exposing a large gap between current agents and truly proactive always-on assistance. The paper also contributes a data-generation pipeline for constructing such scenarios. It is an agent-evaluation paper, not a recommendation paper.

## Summary

This paper addresses fairness risks in recommender systems. It proposes novel metrics based on pairwise comparisons from randomized experiments to quantify fairness in rankings. The authors then introduce a regularizer designed to improve these fairness metrics during model training, demonstrating significant improvements in pairwise fairness in a large-scale production system.

## Technical note

This paper proposes a pairwise fairness metric for recommender rankings, measuring whether a system under-ranks items from a sensitive group by comparing clicked vs. unclicked item pairs of equal relevance drawn from randomized experiments. Because the metric is based on observed pairwise preferences, it gives unbiased estimates and directly corresponds to ranking performance even when the underlying model is a pointwise predictor of click and post-click engagement. The authors add a pairwise regularization term (analogous to Mann-Whitney U / AUC-based group-fairness objectives following equality of opportunity) to improve this metric during training. Applied to a large-scale production recommender, the approach significantly improves pairwise fairness with characterized accuracy trade-offs.

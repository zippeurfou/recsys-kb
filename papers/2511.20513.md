## Summary

This paper introduces DesignPref, a novel dataset featuring 12,000 pairwise UI design comparisons annotated by 20 professional designers with multi-level ratings, revealing substantial disagreement (low inter-annotator agreement) in visual design preferences. The authors demonstrate that traditional methods aggregating these ratings (majority-voting) are inaccurate for capturing individual tastes, as standard aggregated models perform worse than models fine-tuned or personalized with individual annotations. Using DesignPref, they show that personalized generative AI models significantly outperform aggregated baselines in predicting individual designers' preferences, even with much less data.

## Technical note

DesignPref introduces a dataset of 12k pairwise UI-design comparisons annotated by 20 professional designers with multi-level ratings, revealing high inter-annotator disagreement (Krippendorff's alpha = 0.25) driven by differing aesthetic priorities. It shows that majority-voting aggregated judge models poorly capture individual taste, motivating personalization. The authors personalize evaluators either by fine-tuning on a designer's individual labels or by retrieving designer-specific examples in a RAG pipeline. Personalized models beat aggregated baselines at predicting individual preferences even with 20x fewer examples, establishing a benchmark for personalized visual-design evaluation.

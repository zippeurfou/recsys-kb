## Summary

This paper introduces IMAGINE (Intelligent Multi-modal Adaptive Genre Identification NEtwork), a framework designed to classify books into multiple hierarchical genre labels more accurately than previous methods.

IMAGINE tackles the challenge of varied data quality by dynamically selecting the most informative modalities from available sources like text, images, and metadata, even when some information is missing.

The authors created a specialized dataset mirroring a genre taxonomy and demonstrated that IMAGINE significantly improves prediction accuracy, especially resilient to incomplete or noisy input across different data types.

## Technical note

Book genre identification suffers from noisy, incomplete modalities (covers, blurbs, metadata) and ignored hierarchy. IMAGINE learns modality-specific representations (Swin transformer for covers, XLNet for blurbs/OCR cover text, TransD knowledge-graph embeddings for metadata) and uses a selective gating module that routes each book to a single visual, textual, or multi-modal pathway based on input reliability, with a two-level fiction/non-fiction then fine-grained classifier trained under asymmetric loss for label imbalance. On a curated 11,302-book dataset it outperforms unimodal, hierarchical, VLM, and LLM baselines, with the largest gains when a modality is missing. Concatenation beat attention-based fusion, and hierarchical modeling beat a flattened 58-class variant.

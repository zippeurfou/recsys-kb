## Summary

This paper addresses **Action-Intent Asymmetry** in sequential recommendation models where purchases are often misinterpreted as continued interest, leading to significant post-purchase redundancy (PPRR). To solve this, it introduces the **Satiation-Aware Mechanism (SAM)** framework. SAM explicitly models user interest lifecycles through three components: dual-path cross-attention for suppressing past clicks related to fulfilled intents and retrieving long-term purchase patterns; adaptive satiation gating units that mask satisfied interests post-purchase but re-activate them nearing repurchase time; and a self-supervised Time-to-Next-Purchase task. Extensive experiments show SAM reduces PPRR by over **60%**.

## Technical note

Tackles post-purchase redundancy in industrial CTR ranking by framing a purchase as an 'Interest Exit' (Action-Intent Asymmetry) that sequential models like DIN and HSTU ignore. The proposed Satiation-Aware Mechanism (SAM) uses dual-path cross-attention (retroactive suppression of pre-purchase clicks plus repurchase-rhythm retrieval from a long-term purchase sequence), an Adaptive Satiation Gating Unit (ASGU) that emits a time-sensitive soft logit-bias mask, and a self-supervised Time-to-Next-Purchase (TTNP) auxiliary task that learns per-category repurchase cycles without labels. On Tmall industrial logs SAM reaches AUC 0.763 / GAUC 0.691 (tying OneTrans) while cutting Post-Purchase Repeat Rate from 15.5% to 4.1% (>60% reduction vs the strongest baseline). Online A/B tests show +1.1% CTR, +0.9% GMV, and -74.5% purchased-item bad-case feedback.

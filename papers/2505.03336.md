## Summary

This paper explores how Large Language Models (LLMs) can be used for generative recommender systems but addresses the challenge of recommending out-of-domain items. It introduces two methods to prevent this: a retrieval-based approach (RecLM-ret) and a constrained generation method (RecLM-cgen). The research shows that RecLM-cgen is superior, providing accurate recommendations without OOD suggestions and integrating easily into existing LLM systems.

## Technical note

RecLM addresses hallucination of out-of-domain (OOD) items in LLM-based conversational recommenders by unifying three grounding paradigms under one Llama3-8B backbone via special <SOI>/<EOI> item-indicator tokens: RecLM-ret (retrieve an in-domain item from a BGE-M3 embedding index using the <SOI> hidden state), RecLM-cgen (constrained decoding over a prefix tree of GRPO-rewritten concise titles with a scope-mask training loss), and RecLM-token (RQ-VAE discrete item codes decoded under a prefix tree). All three variants strictly eliminate OOD (OOD@10 = 0) on Steam, Amazon Movies, and Toys, while the constrained-generation variants RecLM-cgen and RecLM-token reach state-of-the-art accuracy, exceeding strong ID-based (SASRec) and LLM baselines (e.g., +11-19% NDCG gains). Ablations show each component (constrained generation, scope mask, multi-round dialogue, the five-reward RL Title Rewriter) adds incremental gains, and RecLM-cgen best preserves general LLM abilities (MMLU/GSM8K/HumanEval) against alignment-tax forgetting.

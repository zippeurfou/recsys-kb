## Summary

This paper addresses a key challenge for recommender systems aiming for long-term user satisfaction: how to balance learning quickly with waiting for delayed rewards that reflect true user engagement over time (like repeated podcast listening). The authors developed an "Impatient Bandits" algorithm using Bayesian filtering to combine all available information, including short-term proxies and immediate rewards, into a probabilistic belief about the long-term reward. They proved its efficiency by showing regret depends on how informative these early indicators are, proving outperformance in real A/B tests against standard methods that rely purely on delayed feedback or simple short-term signals.

## Technical note

The paper formalizes content exploration on streaming platforms as a bandit problem with delayed rewards, targeting long-term engagement (60-day) while avoiding the slow learning caused by waiting for full rewards. Its key insight is 'progressive feedback': long-term outcomes become increasingly predictable as short-term surrogate signals accumulate, unlike pure delayed feedback. The 'Impatient Bandit' algorithm combines Thompson sampling (for bandit feedback) with an empirical Bayesian filter that projects true reward from partially observed engagement trajectories and updates mean/uncertainty incrementally. They prove a regret bound depending on the Value of Progressive Feedback (mutual information between censored trajectories and eventual rewards), and a Spotify podcast A/B test serving hundreds of millions of users shows it beats short-term-proxy and delayed-only methods.

## Summary

This paper addresses two limitations in existing Click-Through Rate (CTR) prediction methods used for e-commerce mini-apps: they either misunderstand clicks driven by interest in the app itself or struggle with short-term, high-intensity promotional events due to reliance on long-term habits. To overcome these issues, the authors introduce Collaborative Contrastive Network (CCN), a novel CTR predictor that utilizes user behavior patterns like co-clicking and mono-clicking as signals for contrastive learning. Online A/B testing showed CCN significantly improved performance by boosting CTR by 12.3% on Taobao during scenarios requiring robust short-term predictions.

## Technical note

Addresses Trigger-Induced Recommendation for short-lived e-commerce promotional scenarios where prior trigger-aware models fall into either a trigger-dependency trap (over-similar items) or a data-hungry intent-modeling trap. Instead of modeling entry intent, the Collaborative Contrastive Network (CCN) treats the user-trigger pair as a unique context and learns preferences via contrastive learning, pulling co-click/co-non-click items together and pushing mono-click pairs apart in the latent space. Importance sampling and contrastive-loss reweighting stabilize training under click sparsity. Trained across a dozen heterogeneous Taobao scenarios and tested on an unseen scenario, online A/B raised CTR 12.3% and order volume 12.7%.

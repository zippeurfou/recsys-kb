## Summary

This paper addresses challenges in recommendation systems caused by high ID cardinality and dynamic changes. It introduces "Semantic ID prefix ngram," a novel method that creates beneficial collisions for embedding tables based on hierarchical content clustering, improving upon the original Semantic ID technique. The approach demonstrates significant gains in prediction stability, tail item modeling, reducing overfitting, and enhancing performance specifically in attention-based models like those used in Meta's Ads Ranking system.

## Technical note

Meta addresses embedding representation instability in web-scale ads ranking caused by high item cardinality, impression skew, and rapid ID drifting, which random hashing handles poorly via contradictory gradient updates. They adopt Semantic IDs (RQ-VAE codes over multimodal content embeddings) and introduce a prefix-ngram token parameterization that maps hierarchical coarse-to-fine codes to embedding rows, enabling semantics-based knowledge sharing. Prefix-ngram is the best parameterization and its gains come mostly from tail and new items, with attention-based user-history models benefiting most. Productionized in Meta's flagship ads model for over a year, it yields a 0.15% online gain and a 43% reduction in A/A prediction variance.

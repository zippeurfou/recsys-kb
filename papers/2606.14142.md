## Summary

1.  Current LLM-based Generative Recommendation systems often use Semantic IDs (SIDs) to represent items, hindering natural language reasoning due to lack of pretraining exposure and requiring expensive multi-stage pipelines for reliable results.
2.  This work identifies key limitations in explicit reasoning approaches: they weaken world knowledge application, misalign SID spaces with LLMs' natural embeddings, and are overly sensitive to rationale quality during decoding.
3.  To overcome these issues, the authors introduce PauseRec, a lightweight implicit reasoning paradigm that bypasses explicit generation stages entirely, leading to significantly better performance (up to 6.22%), much lower training costs (up to 65% reduction), and faster inference times (71.3% speed-up).

## Technical note

This paper decomposes explicit reasoning pipelines for LLM-based Generative Recommendation over Semantic IDs and identifies three limitations: CoT SFT weakens world-knowledge verbalization, text and SID token embedding spaces become geometrically separated (theoretically limiting how much natural-language reasoning shapes SID prediction), and recommendation is brittle to superficial perturbations of ground-truth rationales. Empirically, CoT SFT underperforms plain next-item SFT and gains from explicit CoT emerge only after expensive RL post-training. The proposed PauseRec inserts a short sequence of trainable <pause> tokens before SID generation, pretrained to bridge language and SID representations and optimized only through the next-item objective, giving latent computation without verbal rationales. On Amazon review datasets it beats SFT and CoT methods by up to 6.22% while cutting training cost up to 65% GPU hours and speeding inference up to 71.3%.

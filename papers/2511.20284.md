## Summary

Users often struggle with complex access control decisions due to cognitive load, potentially making poor security choices. This paper proposes using Large Language Models (LLMs) to automate these decisions, leveraging natural language privacy statements. Their study found LLMs can generally reflect user preferences well, but personalizing the LLMs introduces a crucial trade-off where adhering too closely to individual preferences can sometimes conflict with established security best practices.

## Technical note

The paper studies whether LLMs can make personalized, context-aware access-control decisions for smartphone app permissions from a short natural-language privacy statement instead of training data. The authors curate 307 privacy statements and 14,682 user permission decisions plus feedback on 1,298 LLM decisions, comparing a general vs. a personalized LLM. LLMs match the majority decision in up to 86% of cases and can steer users toward safer choices, and when user and LLM disagreed users preferred the LLM choice 49% of the time. Personalization raises per-user agreement but can reduce safety because users tend to over-permission, revealing an accuracy-safety trade-off.

- Informed consent, withdrawal, and awareness. Participants were admitted only after providing informed consent and could withdraw at any time; data from withdrawn participants were excluded from analysis without affecting compensation. Participants were explicitly informed about the use of a third-party LLM and instructed not to provide sensitive or identifying information. After completing the study, participants were debriefed and informed that some examples were hypothetical, and encouraged to critically evaluate outputs produced by automated systems
- Data minimization and protection. Data was collected using pseudonymous identifiers; raw demographics are not published, and textual data is checked to remove identifying content
- Voluntary participation and fair compensation. Participants were compensated at or above local minimum wage (USD 8.75 for an estimated 30-minute study, with an optional bonus for additional tasks) and could withdraw without penalty. Partial participation was remunerated with a proportional payout
- Responsible infrastructure use. We used dedicated LLM deployments, approved by our institution, that do not retain data or use it for model training. Content filtering and careful prompting were applied to reduce harmful outputs

## Summary

This paper introduces "eTAMP," the first cross-session, cross-site memory poisoning attack on LLM-based web agents, achieved solely through observing manipulated environmental data like a fake webpage. It demonstrates that a single contaminated observation can persistently poison an agent's memory, leading to successful attacks (up to 32.5%) against models like GPT-5, regardless of their task performance capabilities. The study reveals that stressed agents ("Frustration Exploitation") are significantly more vulnerable, and the findings highlight a critical security risk for emerging AI browsers, necessitating defenses against this novel threat.

## Technical note

The paper introduces eTAMP, the first cross-session, cross-site memory poisoning attack on LLM-based web agents, executed purely by manipulating environmental data such as a fake webpage rather than accessing memory or system prompts directly. A single contaminated observation persistently poisons the agent's raw trajectory memory, so any future task that retrieves the compromised trajectory can trigger the attack, achieving success rates up to 32.5% against models like GPT-5. The attack exploits temporal separation, cross-site execution that bypasses per-site permission defenses, and semantic relevance to future tasks, and it finds 'Frustration Exploitation' makes stressed agents markedly more vulnerable. The study disentangles apparent robustness from context-processing limits via instruction-recall analysis and warns of critical risks for emerging AI browsers.

## Summary

1.  Recommender systems face difficulty optimizing for conversions due to their rarity (sparse signals). While Generative Recommendation (GR) models exist, they still struggle to effectively model these sparse conversions.
2.  To address this, the paper introduces RCLRec, a novel GR framework that utilizes reverse curriculum learning. It generates specific, conversion-relevant item sequences from a user's history and uses them as input alongside target conversion tokens during training.
3.  This approach provides targeted supervision for conversions and includes a curriculum quality-aware loss, leading to significantly improved performance with real-world gains of +2.09% in advertising revenue and +1.86% in orders compared to standard methods.

## Technical note

RCLRec is a generative-recommendation framework that densifies supervision for sparse conversion (purchase) signals, which are rare relative to impressions and clicks in unified multi-behavior token sequences built from vector-quantized semantic IDs. For each conversion target it applies a Reverse Curriculum Prefix Module (RCPM) that reverse-selects k conversion-related intermediate items from the user history and injects their semantic tokens as a decoder-side prefix alongside behavior-specific BOS control tokens, providing instance-specific intermediate supervision under a joint next-token generation objective (NLL). A curriculum quality-aware loss encourages the selected subsequences to be informative for conversion. Unlike multi-stage difficulty scheduling, RCLRec uses a one-shot reverse prefix; online A/B tests report +2.09% advertising revenue and +1.86% orders.

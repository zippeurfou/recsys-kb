## Summary

This paper challenges the findings of Weller et al. (2025), arguing that dimensionality is not the main reason single-vector embeddings fail retrieval, but rather domain shift and misalignment with relevance. The study identifies that even after controlling for other factors, these issues significantly impact performance, and while finetuning helps, single-vector models still underperform multi-vector representations and suffer from catastrophic forgetting. Furthermore, the paper explains the "drowning in documents" paradox, showing single-vector models are particularly susceptible to relevance being obscured by statistical noise in large document sets.

## Technical note

This paper rebuts Weller et al. (2025), arguing that limited dimensionality is not the primary cause of single-vector embedding failures on the LIMIT dataset: using Alon et al. (2016), a k-sparse relevance matrix has bounded sign-rank so 2k+1 dimensions suffice for top-k retrieval (LIMIT's k=2 implies dimension 5), and Johnson-Lindenstrauss gives an alternative bound. Controlling for tokenization artifacts and linguistic similarity between attributes yields only modest gains for single-vector models, whereas domain shift and misalignment between embedding cosine similarity and true set-based relevance are identified as the major drivers, partly mitigated by finetuning. Even after finetuning, single-vector models trail multi-vector representations (with Chamfer distance) and suffer catastrophic forgetting (MSMARCO drops >40%), while multi-vector models barely forget. Toy models explain the 'drowning in documents' paradox, showing single-vector similarities act as noisier statistical proxies for relevance as corpus size grows.

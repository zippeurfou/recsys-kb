## Summary

Current recommendation systems, whether using multi-stage pipelines or single models, are largely static and difficult to scale effectively under complex business needs due to reliance on manual engineering.

To address this, the paper introduces Agentic Recommender Systems (AgenticRS), which transform individual modules into autonomous agents only when they form closed loops and can be independently optimized.

These model agents can self-evolve through two mechanisms: reinforcement learning within defined action spaces, or large language model-driven exploration and selection of new architectures in open-ended design spaces, enabling systems to adapt and improve automatically.

## Technical note

This position/blueprint paper reframes large-scale industrial recommenders from static multi-stage pipelines (recall, ranking, re-ranking) and monolithic One-Model designs into Agentic Recommender Systems (AgenticRS), a graph of interacting agents. A module is promoted to an agent only when it forms a functional closed loop, can be independently evaluated, and has an evolvable decision space; the paper distinguishes functional agents (traffic orchestration, experiment design, re-ranking policy) from model-centric agents (recall, ranking sub-towers, fusion/calibration) operating in perception-decision-execution-feedback loops. It sketches two self-evolution mechanisms for model agents: RL-style optimization within well-defined action spaces, and LLM-based generation and selection of new architectures/training schemes in open-ended design spaces. It further separates individual evolution of single agents from compositional evolution of how agents are selected and connected, using a layered Inner/Outer reward design to couple local optimization with global business objectives.

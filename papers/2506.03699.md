## Summary

This paper addresses the overfitting problem faced by large-scale Transformer models used for discriminative recommendation tasks like CTR prediction due to data sparsity. To solve this, they introduce GPSD (Generative Pretraining for Scalable Discriminative Recommendation), which uses parameters learned from a generatively pretrained model and freezes them during training. Experiments show that GPSD significantly reduces overfitting improves performance across different scales by narrowing the generalization gap and leveraging Transformer scalability according to power laws, paving the way to apply large language model techniques directly to recommendation models.

## Technical note

GPSD (Generative Pretraining for Scalable Discriminative Recommendation) addresses severe data-sparsity overfitting in Transformer-based CTR/CVR ranking models, where larger models paradoxically underperform. The authors observe that autoregressive generative models trained with sampled softmax and extensive random negatives do not overfit, so GPSD initializes the discriminative model from a pretrained generative model and freezes the sparse (embedding) parameters while training only dense parameters. This narrows the generalization gap and unlocks Transformer scalability, with performance improving from 13K to 0.3B dense parameters following power laws. It delivers gains on industrial and public datasets and in online A/B tests, suggesting unification of recommendation and language-model architectures.

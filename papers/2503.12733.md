## Summary

This paper addresses matrix completion in distributed settings, specifically tackling privacy concerns inherent in centralized approaches by leveraging federated learning. To solve these problems effectively with multiple user-specific matrices, it proposes a new algorithm called FedMC-ADMM, which integrates the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) with randomized block-coordinate and proximal gradient steps, designed to handle multi-block optimization efficiently.

FedMC-ADMM provides strong theoretical guarantees for federated matrix completion problems involving nonconvex and nonsmooth objectives. The analysis demonstrates its convergence properties, showing subsequential convergence towards an $\epsilon$-stationary point and establishing a communication complexity of $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$. This makes it the first work to theoretically validate these aspects for this specific type of problem.

Extensive experiments on real-world datasets like MovieLens 1M, 10M, and Netflix confirm that FedMC-ADMM outperforms existing methods in terms of both convergence speed and final testing accuracy.

## Technical note

The paper proposes FedMC-ADMM for federated low-rank matrix completion where user-item rating blocks are held privately across clients and only a shared item factor V is coordinated by the server. The method combines a revised ADMM with randomized block-coordinate (partial client) updates and alternating proximal gradient steps, handling multi-block nonconvex, nonsmooth regularizers with closed-form inner updates and reordered dual/global updates for stability. They prove almost-sure subsequential convergence to a stationary point with an O(K^{-1/2}) rate and O(eps^{-2}) communication complexity. Experiments on MovieLens-1M/10M and Netflix show privacy-preserving, scalable performance.

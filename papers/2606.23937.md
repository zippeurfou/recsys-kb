## Summary

This paper examines whether using an exact-match retrieval metric accurately measures the usefulness of retrieved policy information for downstream decision models like classifiers. The researchers tested retrieving and replacing specific policy clauses during fine-grained state classification in a tool-use benchmark, comparing performance against gold standard conditioning and controls (mismatched/no policy). They found that while their retriever achieved high rank-1 recall frequently, using the retrieved clause slightly decreased classifier accuracy, indicating that exact-match metrics may overestimate downstream utility.

## Technical note

The paper audits whether exact-match retrieval recall@k is a valid proxy for downstream policy utility in pre-action compliance classification on tau-bench-airline, using Qwen2.5-3B/7B LoRA classifiers predicting allow/verify/refuse. A compact structured decision state (request/evidence/policy/action-class, ~185 tokens) improves macro-F1 over raw trajectories by +0.13-0.17 after tuning, with controls ruling out brevity, scaffold, leaked-label, and format-compliance explanations. An offline gold-injection diagnostic predicts the structured advantage needs ~0.75 exact-gold access, yet off-the-shelf retrievers achieve only recall@1 ~0.07 and recall@5 0.18-0.24 on airline. Despite this, directly feeding the top-1 retrieved clause yields macro-F1 0.58 versus 0.60 for gold (delta -0.024, wide CI), far above mismatched (0.32) and no-policy (0.21) controls, because nonmatching clauses are context-aligned. The finding is configuration-sensitive (gap re-opens for under-trained/over-trained models) and argues for reporting retrieved-policy performance alongside recall.

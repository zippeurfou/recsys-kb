Audio fingerprinting provides an identifiable representation of acoustic signals, which can be later used for identification and retrieval systems. To obtain a discriminative representation, the input audio is usually segmented into shorter time intervals, allowing local acoustic features to be extracted and analyzed. ## Technical note

This study examines how audio segment length affects neural audio-fingerprinting retrieval accuracy, a factor usually set heuristically. The authors extend the NAFP contrastive neural fingerprinter into a NAFP+ variant supporting configurable segment lengths and train separate models at {0.5, 1, 2}-second segments, testing across various query durations. Short segments (0.5s) generally give significantly better retrieval, especially for short (<3s) queries. They also evaluate three LLMs at recommending the best segment length, finding GPT-5-mini gives the best suggestions. (Audio-retrieval paper, tangential to core recsys.)

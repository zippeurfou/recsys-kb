## Summary

Gradient-Informed Bases for Vector-Based Adaptation":

Current vector-based adaptation methods for large models are parameter-efficient but require very high ranks to match the performance of LoRA, another popular parameter-efficient method, leading to long training times.

This paper introduces GiVA, a gradient-based initialization strategy for vector-based adaptation that significantly reduces the required rank by an order of magnitude.

As a result, GiVA achieves training times comparable to LoRA while maintaining the extreme parameter efficiency of vector-based adaptation and demonstrates competitive performance across various benchmarks.

## Technical note

GiVA is a gradient-based initialization for vector-based PEFT, where only diagonal scaling vectors adapt a pair of frozen bases (as in VeRA/OSoRA), giving extreme parameter efficiency but normally requiring much higher rank than LoRA and thus longer training. GiVA constructs the frozen bases A and B from the first-step full fine-tuning gradient so that GiVA's first-step update closely approximates full fine-tuning's; it uses Gamma_0=0 and Lambda_0=I to linearize the update and drops the second-order (eta^2) term, then solves a Frobenius-norm minimization for A and B tied to grad_W L(W_pt). Across natural language understanding, generation, and image classification, GiVA matches or beats VeRA/OSoRA and LoRA while cutting required rank by ~8x, attaining LoRA-comparable runtimes with an extremely small trainable-parameter count.

## Summary

1.  **Problem:** Existing sequential recommendation systems often underperform due to limited data, and while incorporating powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) helps, their pronouncements can be inaccurate ("hallucinated").
2.  **Solution:** The authors propose GRASP, a novel framework combining generation-augmented retrieval (using LLMs for descriptive synthesis) with traditional similarity retrieval, followed by a holistic attention mechanism designed to effectively use LLM knowledge despite potential inaccuracies and better capture evolving user interests.
3.  **Result:** By integrating these components, GRASP consistently achieves state-of-the-art recommendation performance across different backbone models and datasets, effectively mitigating the weaknesses of previous methods.

## Technical note

GRASP enhances sequential recommendation by injecting LLM world knowledge while guarding against LLM hallucinations, which the authors show worsen for users with short interaction histories. Generation-Augmented Retrieval prompts an LLM to synthesize user profiles and item descriptions, embeds them, and retrieves top-k similar users/items via cosine similarity into an offline database. A Holistic Attention Enhancement module uses multi-level attention (self, similar-neighbor, and global) with a sigmoid instead of softmax, treating retrieved neighbors as auxiliary context rather than direct supervision to avoid noisy guidance. The framework is backbone-agnostic and achieves state-of-the-art results on two public benchmarks and one industrial dataset.

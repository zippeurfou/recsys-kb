## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of ranking relevance in e-commerce search, which is often poorly modeled as standard multi-class classification due to ignoring natural ordinal relationships and assigning equal penalties for different types of errors. The CORE framework proposes a unified cascaded binary classification approach that decomposes complex multi-level relevance prediction into simpler sequential binary judgments, tackling both large language model inference and online BERT-based systems differently: LLMs use step-wise reasoning with pruning strategies and tier-specific rewards while BERT models replace the standard head with multiple level-wise classifiers. Experiments demonstrate that CORE significantly improves relevance performance by reducing bad-case rates in both offline benchmarks and live A/B tests, proving the effectiveness of modeling relevance tiers explicitly for better alignment with consumer experience.

## Technical note

CORE reformulates e-commerce search relevance, an inherently ordinal task usually cast as flat multi-class classification, as cascaded binary classification (ordered high-vs-nonhigh then mid-vs-low decisions) to respect asymmetric decision boundaries. It is instantiated for a generative LLM (a structured 3-step reasoning trace with SFT warm-up plus step-level GRPO using per-step verifiable +1/-1 rewards and independently group-normalized advantages) and for a low-latency two-head BERT; the LLM's cascaded reasoning is distilled into the BERT via a PostCoT label-first scheme with per-head KL distillation (Qwen3-14B teacher, bge-small-zh student). Cascaded-StepGRPO reaches 0.7648 accuracy (best LLM variant, +1.07pt over response-level GRPO), and online the cascaded BERT lifts NDCG@5 0.9020 to 0.9038 while cutting severe Badcase@5 13.8% to 11.6% (-15.94% relative). A relevance/ranking model, not a unified retrieval+ranking generative pipeline.

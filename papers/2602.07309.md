## Summary

LinkedIn developed a semantic search system using large language models (LLMs) to understand search intent by meaning, not just keywords. This system integrates a relevance judge, embedding retrieval, and a compact Small Language Model (SLM) trained via distillation, optimizing both relevance and user engagement. To make this efficient and scalable for production, LinkedIn implemented significant inference optimizations boosting throughput by 75x while maintaining quality, demonstrating an effective balance between LLM power and traditional search efficiency.

## Technical note

LinkedIn's production semantic search combines a query-understanding layer, GPU-accelerated exhaustive (non-ANN) embedding retrieval over billion-scale indices, and a compact SLM reranker that jointly predicts relevance and engagement over the top-250 candidates. The SLM is trained by multi-teacher, multi-task distillation transferring knowledge from specialized relevance and engagement teachers via distributional supervision, relevance-aware warm starts, and imbalance-aware loss masking; relevance labels come from the distilled SAGE judge. To make LLM ranking viable at tens of thousands of QPS, they co-design model and infrastructure with structured pruning, offline summarization for context compression, hybrid text-embedding interactions, and a prefill-only inference stack with shared-prefix amortization (open-sourced in sglang), boosting ranking throughput over 75x at fixed latency while preserving near-teacher NDCG.

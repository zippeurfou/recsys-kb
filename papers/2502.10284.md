## Summary

Traditional recommendation systems often use a cascading architecture where pre-ranking relies on simple models due to latency constraints, but downstream ranking stages can exacerbate sample selection bias (SSB) by further filtering candidates based solely on this initial output.

To address this limitation, the Hybrid Cross-Stage Coordination Pre-ranking (HCCP) model integrates information from upstream retrieval and all downstream ranking/re-ranking stages. It achieves this through two main components: hybrid sample construction that incorporates multi-level unexposed data for better pre-ranking guidance, and a joint optimization objective designed to balance consistency with long-tail item precision.

Extensive offline and online experiments show HCCP significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods by improving cross-stage coordination. It achieves up to 14.9% reduction in Unobserved Candidate View Rate (UCVR) and boosts Unobserved Click-Through Rate (UCTR), demonstrating effective mitigation of SSB.

## Technical note

Industrial cascade recommenders use a lightweight pre-ranking stage that, when trained only to imitate downstream ranking scores, worsens sample selection bias and the Matthew effect. HCCP proposes hybrid cross-stage coordination: it constructs multi-level unexposed samples from the entire stream (ranking sequences, pre-ranking sequences, in-batch and pool negatives) and jointly optimizes consistency (ListMLE-style global/local losses) with long-tail precision. A novel Margin InfoNCE loss injects priors about hard vs. easy negative difficulty to identify potential positives while suppressing bias. On the JD e-commerce system it lifts UCVR by 14.9% and UCTR by 1.3% with negligible latency increase.

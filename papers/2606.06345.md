## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge of brain decoding with limited labeled fMRI data by exploring the use of synthetic data augmentation from TRIBE v2 (a large-scale encoding model trained on diverse sensory inputs). Their experiments show that augmenting small real datasets significantly improves image-retrieval accuracy, achieving up to 68% gains depending on the dataset. Crucially, they found that the required proportion of synthetic data varies by source and surprisingly, decoders using only synthetic TRIBE v2 fMRI data can already perform above chance in some scenarios, enabling zero-shot decoding capabilities for visual stimuli.

## Technical note

This work boosts fMRI brain-to-image decoding in low-data regimes by augmenting small real datasets with synthetic fMRI responses generated by TRIBE v2, a large tri-modal encoding model pretrained on 1000+ hours of fMRI to video, audio, and language. Static images are converted to short still videos to feed TRIBE's visual pathway, and controlled mixtures of real and synthetic fMRI train ridge and MindEye-style residual-MLP decoders, evaluated by Top-K image-retrieval accuracy on held-out real fMRI. On NSD and BOLD5000 augmentation yields up to 68% relative Top-10 gains, with the optimal synthetic-to-real ratio varying by dataset and saturating or hurting beyond a point, so calibration is essential. Decoders trained only on synthetic fMRI can perform above chance, indicating limited zero-shot brain-to-image decoding from a subject-agnostic encoding model.

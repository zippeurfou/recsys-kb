## Summary

This paper evaluates methods for estimating uncertainty in semantic segmentation predictions from satellite imagery. Unlike standard classification, segmentation requires scalable per-pixel uncertainty estimates, a challenge often overlooked in remote sensing. The study benchmarks various models and uncertainty metrics on two distinct Earth observation datasets, finding that uncertainty estimates effectively identify errors and noisy regions, leading to practical recommendations for application.

## Technical note

Benchmarks post-hoc uncertainty estimation methods for semantic segmentation of satellite imagery on the PASTIS and ForTy datasets, evaluating their utility for identifying misclassified pixels and noise-corrupted input regions rather than separating aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty. Methods compared include softmax entropy/max-probability, deep ensembles, and Stochastic Segmentation Networks (SSN) that add correlated Gaussian noise in logit space via a low-rank covariance parametrization. Uncertainty quality is measured with Uncertainty-Error and Uncertainty-Noise precision-recall curves, largely decoupled from base segmentation accuracy. Vision Transformer backbones substantially outperform convolutional models at flagging segmentation errors, and the authors introduce a Sampled Categorical Variation metric plus SSN modifications (wider output layer, marginal-conditional prediction) that improve mIoU. They recommend a dedicated uncertainty test set for robust evaluation.

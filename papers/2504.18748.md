## Summary

This paper addresses the difficulty of recommending products for vague "best" queries (like "best shoes"). The authors propose generating implicit attributes using LLMs to better understand these user intents. They evaluate existing retrieval methods on their new dataset, SUPERB, highlighting how this approach can be integrated into e-commerce systems.

## Technical note

The paper studies ranking and recommendation for implicit superlative product queries (e.g., 'best shoes for trail running') whose desired attributes are unstated and require common-sense/world knowledge. It introduces SUPERB, a four-level relevance taxonomy (Overall Best, Almost Best, Relevant But Not Best, Not Relevant) with LLM-based annotations, and a dataset of 35,651 superlative queries reformulated from Amazon Shopping Queries via Claude. It empirically evaluates pointwise, pairwise, listwise, and deliberated (reasoning-based) prompting pipelines, arguing LLMs can infer implicit attributes and perform multi-objective ranking. The work is the first to formalize implicit superlative queries for product recommendation and provides insights for integrating such ranking into e-commerce production systems.

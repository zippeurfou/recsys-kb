## Summary

1.  Existing personalized text generation methods often require large datasets or fine-tuning, making them costly and hard to interpret, which limits their generality across authors.
2.  The authors propose LiteraryBigFive, a framework inspired by the Big Five personality model, defining authorial characteristics as coordinates in a unified, interpretable five-dimensional space (e.g., Classicism vs. Romanticism, Emotionality vs. Detachment).
3.  This approach automatically derives the dimensions by comparing author-written text to neutral passages, enabling precise, interpretable steering of text generation towards specific author profiles or stylistic targets while maintaining meaning, and showing scores correlate well with human literary understanding.

## Technical note

ByteDance. LiteraryBigFive reframes authorial writing style as coordinates in a unified five-dimensional interpretable space (Classicism, Ornateness, Narrativity, Emotionality, Analyticity) instead of one isolated label or steering vector per author. Axis directions come from last-token activation contrasts between author-written passages of anchor classics and GPT-4-generated semantics-preserving neutral rewrites, where both sequences share the same neutral prefix so only output style differs; a per-layer SVD over the five stacked raw axes strips the dominant shared first left singular vector, reinterpreted as an overall expressiveness direction, which decorrelates the axes and stabilizes multi-axis composition. Localize-and-steer then projects k reference passages of a target book onto the refined unit axes to get per-layer coordinates, and at generation time computes the style gap between the current token projection and the target coordinates, rescales it by the retained axis magnitudes rho, and adds V alpha plus alpha_O v_O to the hidden state across shallow-to-deep intervention layers. Base model is Llama2-7B-Chat (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct in the appendix); evaluation uses 590 passages and 5716 sentences from Burke Reflections on the Revolution in France, Orwell 1984, Stevenson Kidnapped and Austen Pride and Prejudice. It beats few-shot prompting, LLM-Steer, LoRA, ICV, Mean-Centering, CAA and RepE on every metric, e.g. on 1984 ROUGE-1 57.8 vs 56.5 for RepE, BGE embedding SIM 95.1, GPT-4 judge 75.2, human 75.3; ablating the adaptive style gap hurts more (ROUGE-1 49.3 vs 53.0) than ablating the SVD decomposition (52.1). Derived book coordinates correlate at Pearson r=0.96 with the ensemble of GPT-5, Claude 3.5 and Gemini 3 stylistic ratings. Caveats: despite the industrial affiliation this is a pure text-generation and activation-steering study with no recommender surface, no online A/B test, axes derived only from English literary classics, white-box activation access required, and human inter-annotator agreement only Cohen kappa 0.59.

## Summary

AI assistance in complex tasks like chess often recommends optimal moves based on a perfect model, but this ignores human limitations. The paper proposes "value-aware interventions" that target specific points where a human's suboptimal play significantly reduces potential outcomes, identified by policy-value discrepancies. This approach, evaluated in chess using models and human studies, consistently outperformed standard engine-based recommendations, particularly for lower-skilled players.

## Technical note

This work proposes value-aware interventions for AI assistants aiding suboptimal human decision-makers, using policy-value inconsistency under the Bellman equation as a signal for when to intervene. It formalizes the problem as an MDP where an assistant may override human actions under an intervention budget via a gating function and an override policy; in the single-intervention regime the optimal move maximizes the human value function, and multiple interventions are prioritized by the magnitude of policy-value discrepancy. Human policies and value functions are learned by behavioral cloning from large-scale chess gameplay across skill levels. In simulation it beats Stockfish-based interventions when interventions are scarce, and a within-subject study (20 players, 600 games) shows significant gains for low/mid-skill players while matching expert-engine interventions for high-skill players.

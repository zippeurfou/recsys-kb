## Summary

This paper evaluates various negative sampling techniques (random, popularity-based, etc.) used to train large-scale sequential recommendation models. Their experiments reveal a significant trade-off: while random sampling achieves best overall results but reinforces the popularity bias towards top items, popularity-based sampling can balance performance across different item tiers at the cost of lower average quality. The study provides practical guidance for selecting negative sampling methods based on specific goals and dataset characteristics in industrial-scale recommendation systems.

## Technical note

Large-scale sequential recommenders need negative sampling to train against catalogs of millions of items, but the choice of method is poorly characterized. The authors benchmark six strategies (random, popularity-based, in-batch, mixed, adaptive, adaptive+mixed) on a SASRec model across three datasets with different popularity biases, using hyperparameter optimization and 20x repeats. Aggregate metrics hide imbalances across head/mid/tail popularity bands: random negative sampling reinforces popularity bias and is best for head items, while popularity-based (in-batch/global) methods give more balanced results at the cost of lower overall accuracy. The study is offered as a practical guide to negative-sampling trade-offs among scalability, bias, and variance.

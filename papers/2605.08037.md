## Summary

1.  Existing Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) methods only use pairwise preferences, ignoring richer structures present in multi-rollout training data, which can lead to transitivity issues and unstable training.
2.  The paper introduces Graph Direct Preference Optimization (GraphDPO), a generalization that models preferences using directed acyclic graphs derived from rollout rankings, optimizing over graph neighborhoods to enforce transitivity and effectively utilize the full preference structure.
3.  GraphDPO maintains efficiency, handles sparse/skewed data effectively, and optionally incorporates ground-truth solutions, leading to better performance and stability compared to standard DPO on reasoning and synthesis tasks.

## Technical note

GraphDPO generalizes Direct Preference Optimization from isolated pairwise comparisons to directed acyclic preference graphs induced by rollout rankings, encoding dominance relations as edges. It optimizes a graph-structured Plackett-Luce-inspired objective that aggregates supervision over dominated-response neighborhoods, enforcing transitivity while recovering standard pairwise DPO as a special case. An equivalence-class (layered DAG) construction handles ties and sparse discrete signals, with intra-layer edges contributing zero loss to avoid spurious gradients, and maintains linear per-prompt complexity via log-sum-exp aggregation. Optional ground-truth anchoring inserts verified solutions as dominant nodes under an annealed schedule, improving reasoning and program-synthesis benchmarks over pairwise and listwise baselines.

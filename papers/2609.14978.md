## Summary

1.  Current recommendation systems using Transformers often require expensive, resource-heavy single models optimized from scratch, addressing both sparse and dense parameters simultaneously.
2.  The authors propose LazFormer, a Transformer-based recommendation system designed for scalability, combining generative pre-training for favorable initialization with a request-aware ranking module.
3.  Key innovations include a transferable residual adapter to overcome feature mismatches between pre-training and ranking, and an asymmetric multi-epoch training strategy that prevents sparse parameter overfitting while continuously adapting dense parameters.

## Technical note

LazFormer is Alibaba's scaling Transformer for e-commerce homepage ranking, built on the premise that HSTU, OneTrans and similar scalable rankers waste compute by training sparse and dense parameters from scratch in a single model. It first runs generative pre-training: a 5-layer causal Transformer with RMSNorm, QKNorm and attention gating over tokenized interaction sequences (item ID concatenated with category, shop, brand, decay, stay time, plus a scene feature), trained by autoregressive next-item prediction with temperature-scaled cosine similarity (learnable tau, 0.07 init) against 16,000 in-batch negatives across 16M users and 11B tokens. Two failure modes of the pretrain-then-rank paradigm are then addressed. For negative transfer of dense parameters, a LoRA-inspired transferable residual adapter a_j = W_up*GELU(W_down*s_j) injects ranking-only features (add-to-cart, order, and their time gaps to the most recent click) with W_up zero-initialized so ranking starts exactly at the pre-trained representation and absorbs the new features gradually. For sparse overfitting, an asymmetric multi-epoch strategy resets sparse embeddings to their pre-trained state at the start of every epoch while dense parameters keep accumulating. Efficiency comes from request-aware ranking: coarse-to-fine compression keeping 1,024 short-term tokens and group-sum-pooling the rest at g=8, hybrid sparse attention combining a causal sliding window of 128 with 128 global recent tokens (targets attend to all history but only to themselves, compiled via flex attention), progressive layer-wise token pruning to N_min=128, and request-level samples so one user history is serialized, transferred and encoded once for all candidates. On 370M impressions from 11M users over 25 days, LazFormer scores 0.7735 CTR AUC / 0.6868 CTR GAUC / 0.8905 CVR AUC versus 0.7652 / 0.6740 / 0.8850 for SORT and 0.7661 / 0.6738 / 0.8840 for OneTrans, a +0.74 pt CTR AUC and +1.28 pt CTR GAUC gain where 0.1 pt is treated as significant; three asymmetric epochs push it to 0.7761 / 0.6891. Ablations are sharp: naively continuing sparse updates for 3 epochs collapses CTR AUC to 0.7387 while resetting gives 0.7731, and Direct or PreProj fusion of ranking features both underperform the zero-init adapter. Sparse attention at 92.63 percent sparsity buys a 5.1x attention speedup for 0.10 pt AUC. Performance scales monotonically with parameters (9M to 55M), sequence length (512 to 9,216) and epochs. A two-week online A/B against a 3-layer SORT-like production ranker gave +5.21 percent IPV, +3.38 percent orders, +3.70 percent buyers and +9.85 percent GMV for only a 12.1 percent single-machine QPS drop on an A10. Caveat: all offline results are on proprietary industrial data with no public benchmark, and baselines are rerun with the same pre-trained sparse initialization rather than their original from-scratch setup.

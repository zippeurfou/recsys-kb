## Summary

Traditional Early Stage Ranking (ESR) systems use a user-item decoupling approach, which limits their effectiveness in capturing fine-grained affinities. To overcome this, the Generative Early Stage Ranking (GESR) paradigm proposes the Mixture of Attention (MoA) module, combining specialized attention mechanisms like Hard Matching Attention and Target-Aware Self Attention to enable richer early user-item interactions and better personalization. GESR further enhances the model with a Multi-Logit Parameterized Gating module and various efficiency optimizations, demonstrating significant improvements in metrics and being the first large-scale deployment of full target-aware attention sequence modeling in ESR stages.

## Technical note

Generative Early Stage Ranking (GESR) augments the efficient user-item-decoupled two-tower pre-ranking model with a Mixture of Attention (MoA) module to recover cross-signal effectiveness lost by late-fusion. MoA combines Hard Matching Attention (explicit raw match counts between user and item features), Target-Aware Self Attention built on HSTU (implicit, candidate-conditioned user representations), and linear-cost Cross Attention over request-only and non-request-only features. A Multi-Logit Parameterized Gating (MLPG) layer computes multiple parallel logits with adaptive gating to amplify the new cross-signals in scoring. To meet ESR latency, GESR uses custom kernels, tensor-level optimization, pruning, and feature caching; it is presented as the first large-scale deployment of full target-aware attention sequence modeling in early-stage ranking, with offline and online A/B gains at Meta.

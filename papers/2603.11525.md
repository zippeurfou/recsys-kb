## Summary

The paper addresses a disconnect between model design and dataset curation in learning-based video quality assessment (VQA). It introduces MDS-VQA, a model-informed data selection method that identifies diverse, challenging video samples by balancing model failure prediction and semantic diversity under a limited labeling budget. Experiments show that fine-tuning on this carefully selected subset significantly improves VQA performance, achieving state-of-the-art results with minimal data.

## Technical note

MDS-VQA closes the model-to-data feedback loop in learning-based video quality assessment by curating unlabeled videos that are both hard for the base model and diverse in content under a fixed labeling budget. Difficulty is estimated by an auxiliary failure predictor trained with a ranking (fidelity) objective under a Thurstone model while the base VQA model is frozen, and diversity is measured with deep semantic video features; a greedy procedure balances the two. The base model is built on VisualQuality-R1, a reinforcement-learning-to-rank VLM. Actively fine-tuning on the resulting hard-and-diverse 5% subset per target domain raised mean SRCC from 0.651 to 0.722 and achieved the top gMAD rank, outperforming passive and representativeness-based selection baselines.

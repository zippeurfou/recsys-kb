## Summary

AgenticTagger tackles the challenge of generating high-quality, low-cardinality item representations for recommendation systems using LLMs, addressing the limitations of unstructured open-ended generation. It achieves this through two core stages: first, building a structured vocabulary of descriptors, and second, assigning relevant descriptors from this vocabulary to items. A multi-agent mechanism iteratively refines the vocabulary using LLM feedback, leading to consistent performance improvements across various recommendation tasks in experiments.

## Technical note

AgenticTagger generates low-cardinality, structured natural-language descriptors as item representations for recommendation, avoiding the vocabulary explosion of open-ended LLM generation. It has two stages: a vocabulary-building stage that mines a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine descriptor taxonomy, and a vocabulary-assignment stage where LLMs assign in-vocabulary descriptors to items. A multi-agent architect-annotator reflection loop iteratively refines the vocabulary, with a strong architect LLM maintaining it and parallel annotator LLMs validating coverage against item data. It improves generative and term-based retrieval, ranking, and critique-based recommendation over baselines including RQ-VAE semantic IDs.

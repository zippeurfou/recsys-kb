## Summary

This paper introduces Evo-Rec, a novel three-stage framework for generative recommendation using Semantic IDs (SIDs). It addresses the challenge of selecting effective reasoning traces before SID generation, proposing supervised fine-tuning of the best candidates and reinforcement learning to iteratively improve the reasoning policy itself. Experiments on Amazon datasets demonstrate that Evo-Rec consistently outperforms various comparison models, including standard generative and reasoning-enhanced recommenders, by learning better reasoning.

## Technical note

Evo-Rec (Microsoft) argues that explicit reasoning before Semantic-ID generation helps only if the traces are useful, and that exact-match RL rewards ignore rank improvements. It fine-tunes Qwen3-1.7B in three stages: SID-language alignment over 3-level RQ-VAE codes, Best-of-N rejection-sampling SFT that scores N=5 teacher (GPT-5.4) chains of thought by predictive utility (gain in target-SID log-likelihood over history-only prediction under a frozen scorer) and keeps the best one only if positive, and GRPO (G=16) whose reward is the NDCG@10 of the target's rank in a trie-constrained beam (B=10) conditioned on each sampled trace. On Amazon Games, Office and Industrial it beats discriminative (SASRec, GRU4Rec, Caser), generative (TIGER, HSTU, LETTER, LC-Rec) and reasoning baselines (ReaRec, R2ec, SIDReasoner) on every metric, e.g. Games Recall@5 0.0710 to 0.0847 (+19.3%) and NDCG@10 0.0563 to 0.0746 (+32.5%). Ablations show utility ranking rather than rejection (which drops only 2-3% of instances) drives the SFT gain (+27% NDCG@10 over random selection), NDCG reward beats exact-match, prefix-match and NDCG+recall rewards, beam search beats constrained sampling by 31% NDCG@10, and reasoning gets shorter during RL with no length penalty.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of next-basket repurchase recommendations, which typically rank items but often lack clear, explainable reasons for the ranking.
2.  It evaluates large language models (LLMs) for their ability to generate human-readable rationales based on interpretable repurchase features (like purchase cadence, frequency, etc.), comparing them to standard ranking methods across different datasets.
3.  The findings indicate that off-the-shelf LLMs are not competitive as standalone primary recommenders, but they can sometimes generate useful rationales, especially with prompt adjustments, suggesting a potential practical role as validated explanation components separate from the core ranking task.

## Technical note

A Walmart study that separates ranking accuracy from explanation quality for next-basket repurchase recommendation, where candidates are items already in a customer's purchase history. Twenty-eight interpretable engineered features spanning cadence, frequency, recency, user behavior and item popularity are rendered into a natural-language evidence card; GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro each emit a 0-1 repurchase support score, exactly three cited feature names, and a text explanation, without seeing the supervised model score or the future label. On ranking (RQ1) supervised models win: Instacart NDCG@10 is 0.7029 for XGBoost and 0.6965 for a vanilla NN versus 0.6853 for GPT-4o and 0.5607 for Gemini 2.5 Pro, and on the Dunnhumby Carbo-Loading (DC) and proprietary retail sets the LLM scorers trail even the item_order_rate personal-frequency heuristic. A percentile-augmented norm-prompt variant, selected on validation NDCG@5, does not improve ranking at all. For rationale quality (RQ2) the authors mask each selector's top-3 features with the training-set median and measure Precision/Recall/NDCG degradation on both an XGBoost and a VNN target, benchmarked against TreeSHAP, integrated gradients and random selection; LLM citations are only inconsistently outcome-grounded, with norm-prompt beating base GPT-4o on 18 of 18 proprietary masking metrics but 7 of 18 on Instacart, and producing near-zero or negative gaps on DC. A text-to-evidence audit of 3,599 Instacart explanations gives reason-group precision 0.951, recall 0.740 and numeric consistency 0.996, so verbalization is faithful once features are chosen. The conclusion is a hybrid design: supervised rankers rank, LLMs only verbalize validated evidence. Caveats: offline only with no A/B test, just 100 held-out customers per dataset because of LLM inference cost, and masking measures perturbation sensitivity rather than causal importance.

## Summary

This paper introduces Ranked Set Sampling (RSS) for computer system simulation studies, which are often limited by the time-consuming nature of full simulation. By applying RSS, the authors demonstrate significantly tighter confidence intervals for performance metrics compared to simple random sampling, achieving up to a 50% reduction in confidence interval width. They further propose a repeated subsampling scheme that identifies representative regions, drastically reducing the maximum observed error from 35% to just 10%, and show that combining RSS with informed subsampling selection yields highly accurate results.

## Technical note

The paper applies ranked set sampling (RSS), a statistical technique, to CPU/computer-architecture simulation, where full cycle-accurate simulation is infeasible and studies sample short application regions. It shows RSS maps naturally onto architectural simulation and yields up to 50% tighter confidence intervals than simple random sampling by exploiting the relationship between sample standard deviation and mean across micro-architectural configurations. It further introduces a repeated subsampling scheme that selects a small representative subset of regions for reuse in future studies, reducing maximum observed error from 35% to 10% and gaining accuracy with more informed subsample selection. Overall it achieves average error below 2% simulating 30 one-million-instruction regions of SPEC CPU 2017 Integer applications.

## Summary

This paper argues that the current "on-the-fly" approach to AI agents bypasses crucial software engineering practices, leading to unreliable and insecure systems. To achieve production-grade robustness, especially for high-stakes scenarios, the authors propose integrating disciplined engineering and creating reusable agent workflows. They envision an "AI Workflow Store" where these hardened workflows can be invoked reliably, outlining the need to balance flexibility with rigor and overcome the limitations of the existing paradigm.

## Technical note

This position paper argues that the dominant on-the-fly agentic loop, which synthesizes and executes multi-step plans in seconds, short-circuits disciplined software-engineering processes (iterative design, testing, adversarial evaluation, staged rollout) that made conventional software reliable and secure. The authors contend that robustness is an engineered property from rigorous process, not a property any single capable model can deliver under acute latency and cost constraints. They propose hardening on-the-fly executions into deterministically constrained, reusable workflows and envision an AI Workflow Store that amortizes the cost of rigor across a broad user community. The central research challenge is navigating a flexibility-robustness tension that requires moving beyond the on-the-fly paradigm.

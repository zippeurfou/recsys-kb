## Summary

1.  Existing off-the-shelf vision-language models struggle with capturing nuanced semantic relationships needed for product or user intent embeddings in e-commerce contexts like DoorDash.
2.  The authors introduce DashCLIP, which uses contrastive learning to jointly train multimodal encoders (for images and text) specifically for generating high-quality embeddings representing products and user queries without relying on engagement history.
3.  These new embeddings demonstrate strong generalization capabilities and significantly improve key business metrics like click-through rate and conversion rate in applications such as product categorization and personalized ad recommendations at DoorDash.

## Technical note

DoorDash's DashCLIP builds generalizable product and query embeddings for e-commerce by continually pre-training a BLIP-based multimodal encoder on catalog images and titles via contrastive (ITC/ITM) losses, then aligning a separate query encoder to the product space with a Product-Query Contrastive loss. Query-product relevance labels are curated by fine-tuning GPT-3.5 on 700k human annotations to grade 32M pairs, and a sampling strategy mixes hard negatives (HR vs MR queries) with diversified/proxy negatives. In retrieval, DashCLIP substantially beats off-the-shelf CLIP, BLIP, and FLAVA, and in ranking, adding product/query embeddings plus purchase-history sequence features to a Deep & Cross Network improves ROC-AUC and LogLoss, especially for users with purchase history. Online deployment lifted click-through and conversion rates.

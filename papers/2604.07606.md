## Summary

1.  AI for sign language interpretation lacks high-quality annotated data, despite valuable existing datasets like ASL STEM Wiki and FLEURS-ASL being only partially annotated due to the high cost of manual annotation.
2.  To address this, the authors developed a pseudo-annotation pipeline that takes signed video and English text, using AI models (ISR for signs, fingerspelling recognizer for text), combined with a K-shot LLM approach, to automatically generate likely annotations (glosses, fingerspelling, classifiers) with time intervals.
3.  They validated this approach by creating high-performing baseline models and releasing human annotations from professional interpreters on ASL STEM Wiki, along with their pseudo-annotations, providing a valuable benchmark for future research.

## Technical note

This Apple paper tackles the annotation-data bottleneck in AI sign-language interpretation by building a pseudo-annotation pipeline that takes signed video plus English text and outputs ranked, time-aligned candidate annotations for glosses, fingerspelled words, and classifiers. The three-step pipeline generates k plausible glossings via K-shot LLM prompting, detects and time-aligns fingerspelled words, and uses an isolated sign recognizer (ISR) to confirm which glosses were likely signed, aggregating model scores to rank candidate sequences. The authors establish simple but strong baseline models achieving state-of-the-art 6.7% CER on FSBoard fingerspelling and 74% top-1 on ASL Citizen ISR over a 2,731-gloss vocabulary. A professional interpreter annotated nearly 500 ASL STEM Wiki videos as a gold-standard benchmark, and over 300 hours of pseudo-annotations (also spanning FLEURS-ASL) are being released.

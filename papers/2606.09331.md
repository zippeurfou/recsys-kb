## Summary

Omni-modal embedding aims to create a single unified search space from diverse data types, but merging these different modalities presents significant challenges due to their distinct nature.

Conan-embedding-v3 introduces a "decouple-fuse-recover" framework: it first trains separate models for each modality (Decoupled Specialist Fusion), combining them into one backbone effectively supporting visual and document retrieval.

However, the team discovered an issue called Projector Drift when fusing audio-specialized modules; their solution involved fixing this projector misalignment through targeted fine-tuning followed by balanced multi-modal retraining.

## Technical note

Conan-embedding-v3 builds a single omni-modal retriever (text, image, video, visual-document, audio) via a decouple-fuse-recover framework that avoids one large mixed-modality optimization and its cross-modal interference (the seesaw effect). Decoupled Specialist Fusion trains image, video, visual-document, and grafted-audio specialists independently from a shared visual-language init with modality-specific LoRA, then merges shared-backbone task vectors into one dense checkpoint while copying audio-only modules unchanged. The authors identify Projector Drift: after task-vector fusion, the audio projector remains calibrated to the audio-specialist backbone and progressively rotates away in deeper layers, regressing audio retrieval. They repair it with Projector Recovery (full fine-tuning of only the projector on audio-text pairs with the backbone frozen) followed by balanced multi-modal rehearsal. Trained with an InfoNCE retrieval loss on L2-normalized embeddings, the model scores 74.9 on MMEB and 55.61 on the 30-task MAEB audio suite.

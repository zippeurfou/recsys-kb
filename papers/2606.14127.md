## Summary

This paper introduces **CoRe**, a continuously updated LLM-based query rewriter designed specifically for web-scale video search. To overcome production-reward training gaps, it uses a reward function closely mirroring the actual ranking system's relevance calculation method (a multiplicative ratio). The system employs efficient online training techniques and automated safety checks to manage updates at massive scale while preventing degradation in performance through reward hacking.

## Technical note

CoRe is a context-aware LLM query rewriter deployed and retrained weekly for over five months in a major short-video search engine's search-after-view setting, emitting a reformulation per (query, last-viewed document) tuple. Its reward is constructed from the deployed multimodal relevance model using a multiplicative ratio form that mirrors the production ranking-fusion algebra over positive, hard-negative, and random-easy-negative document pools, closing the simulation-production gap left by offline proxies. A semi-online Mixed Preference Optimization loop (DPO+BCO+SFT) restricts gradients to a top-k/bottom-k subset and uses a phase structure to amortize trainer-inference parameter syncs, making the reward affordable at multi-million-instance weekly scale. An automated two-family promotion gate over reward-like and stability metrics caught a real verbosity-driven reward-hacking incident, and additive parallel-path consumption at recall, rawrank, and finerank bounds rewriter-failure blast radius; two sequential launches yielded statistically significant change-query-rate reductions and positive relevance/engagement metrics.

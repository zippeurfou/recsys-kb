## Summary

This paper introduces **Bayesian-MoE**, a method to estimate uncertainty for large language models (LLMs) using existing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. It achieves this by applying a Bayesian approach (specifically a structured Laplace approximation) directly to the expert pathways, without altering the model's original training or adding parameters. Experiments show that this technique improves the calibration (relevance of uncertainty estimates) and accuracy of the LLMs on common-sense reasoning tasks.

## Technical note

Bayesian-MoE is a post-hoc uncertainty-estimation method for fine-tuned Mixture-of-Experts LLMs that applies a structured Laplace approximation only to the second linear layer of each expert. Unlike Bayesian-LoRA approaches, it adds no new parameters and does not alter training, instead exploiting the modular expert pathways already present in MoE models for tractable block-wise posterior estimation. It uses Kronecker-factored low-rank curvature approximations to derive scalable estimates of predictive uncertainty and marginal likelihood. On common-sense reasoning benchmarks with Qwen1.5-MoE and DeepSeek-MoE, Bayesian-MoE improves expected calibration error and negative log-likelihood over baselines, showing that partial Bayesianization of the most decisive components yields reliable uncertainty without sacrificing efficiency.

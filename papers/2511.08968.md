## Summary

This paper introduces **Bayesian-MoE**, a method for estimating uncertainty in fine-tuned Large Language Models (LLMs) based on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. It applies a **structured Laplace approximation** specifically to the second linear layer of each expert pathway, achieving calibration and uncertainty estimation without altering the original model's training. Experiments show that this approach improves both the calibration accuracy (ECE) and predictive performance (NLL) compared to baseline models on common-sense reasoning tasks.

## Technical note

Proposes Bayesian-MoE, a post-hoc uncertainty-estimation method for fine-tuned MoE LLMs that adds no new parameters and leaves the training pipeline unchanged. It applies a structured Laplace approximation to the second linear layer of each expert, using Kronecker-factored low-rank approximations of the curvature to derive scalable estimates of predictive uncertainty and marginal likelihood by exploiting the modular expert pathways for block-wise posterior estimation. Unlike Bayesian-LoRA approaches it targets existing expert weights rather than added adapters. On common-sense reasoning benchmarks with Qwen1.5-MoE and DeepSeek-MoE it improves expected calibration error and negative log-likelihood over baselines.

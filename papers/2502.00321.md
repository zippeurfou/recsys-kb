## Summary

This paper introduces a novel method called Multi-modal Content Interest Modeling (MIM) designed for Click-Through Rate (CTR) prediction in recommendation systems. The core problem addressed is that existing approaches relying heavily on item ID embeddings struggle to effectively capture users' true content interests, particularly affecting performance with new or unpopular items.

MIM proposes three stages: pre-training foundational models on domain data to extract rich multi-modal embeddings; fine-tuning these embeddings using user behavior signals to align them better with individual preferences (C-SFT); and finally integrating the enhanced embeddings with traditional ID-based collaborative filtering into a unified framework (CiUBM).

The effectiveness of MIM was validated through extensive offline experiments and online A/B tests on Taobao, resulting in its successful deployment. This achieved significant real-world improvements (+14.14% CTR and +4.12% RPM) highlighting the industrial applicability and substantial impact on platform performance metrics.

## Technical note

ID-based user-behavior models for CTR encode collaborative signals but ignore content preferences and fail in cold-start/long-tail settings. MIM proposes a three-stage multi-modal content-interest paradigm: domain-adaptive pre-training of foundation models for multi-modal embeddings, Content-Interest-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (C-SFT) that uses user behavior signals to align embeddings with interests, and a Content-Interest-Aware UBM (CiUBM) fusing multi-modal and ID-based CF signals. A precomputed representation center keeps training/inference cheap. Deployed on Taobao, MIM achieved +14.14% CTR and +4.12% RPM.

Implicit feedback is widely used in recommender systems due to its accessibility and generality, yet it usually presents noisy samples (e.g., clickbait, position bias). Meanwhile, recommenders inevitably face the item cold-start problem due to the continuous influx of new items. We identify that cold items are more prone to noisy samples due to the aforementioned factors, and researchers often overlook the significance of denoising implicit feedback for cold items. ## Technical note

DIF is a model-agnostic method for denoising implicit feedback specifically for cold-start recommendation, motivated by the finding that cold items are more prone to label noise (on Kuaishou, top-10% clickbait-scoring cold videos exceed the bottom 10% by 37.7%, and cold videos are 28.3% more likely to sit in tail positions). Since user content preferences are stable, DIF infers pseudo-labels for cold items from content-similar warm items' collaborative representations, models pseudo-label confidence via content similarity and aggregates multiple pseudo-labels per sample, then estimates each noisy sample's label uncertainty using relative entropy and cold-start status to adaptively correct labels. Backed by theoretical justification and real-world experiments, DIF is deployed on billion-user Kuaishou with significant commercial-metric gains in cold-start scenarios.

## Summary

This paper introduces a novel generative retrieval approach for recommender systems, moving away from traditional embedding-based nearest neighbor search. The core innovation is assigning items unique "Semantic IDs" (meaningful codeword tuples) and training a Transformer decoder to predict these IDs for the next user interaction based on the IDs of previously interacted items. The authors claim this is the first Semantic ID-based generative model for recommendation, demonstrating significantly improved performance over state-of-the-art methods and better generalization, particularly for unseen items.

## Technical note

TIGER (Transformer Index for Generative Recommenders) reframes sequential recommendation as generative retrieval: instead of embedding-plus-ANN matching, a seq2seq Transformer autoregressively decodes the target item's identifier. Each item is assigned a Semantic ID, a tuple of discrete codewords produced by applying RQ-VAE residual quantization to a pretrained text-content embedding (e.g., Sentence-T5), so semantically similar items share codeword prefixes and knowledge is shared across items. Trained on user sessions of Semantic IDs, TIGER significantly outperforms SOTA sequential recommenders on recall and NDCG across datasets, and its content-grounded IDs improve generalization to new/infrequent items (cold-start) plus enable tunable recommendation diversity.

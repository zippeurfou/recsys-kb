## Summary

This paper introduces a novel recommender system approach called Generative Retrieval. Instead of traditional nearest neighbor search on item embeddings, the system uses a generative model that predicts the identifiers of relevant items. Specifically, it assigns semantically meaningful identifiers (Semantic IDs) to items and trains a Transformer model to predict the next item's Semantic ID given a sequence of previous items' IDs. This method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art recommenders and improves generalization, especially for items with no prior history.

## Technical note

TIGER reframes retrieval in sequential recommendation as generative: instead of embedding items and doing ANN, a Transformer seq2seq model autoregressively decodes the target item's 'Semantic ID', a tuple of discrete codewords. Semantic IDs are produced by applying RQ-VAE (residual-quantized VAE) to Sentence-T5 content embeddings of item text, giving hierarchical, coarse-to-fine codes where similar items share prefixes, with a collision-breaking extra token. On three Amazon Review categories TIGER beats SASRec, BERT4Rec, S3-Rec, P5 and others by up to ~29% NDCG@5, and the generative-plus-Semantic-ID design enables new capabilities: cold-start retrieval of unseen items (beating semantic-KNN) and temperature-controlled recommendation diversity. Ablations confirm RQ-VAE beats LSH and random IDs, and invalid generated IDs are rare (~0.1-1.6%).

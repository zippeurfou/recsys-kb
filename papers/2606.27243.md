## Summary

NOVA addresses the challenge of efficiently modifying complex industrial recommender system architectures by combining automated search with rigorous verification against specific semantic rules (architectural contracts). It distinguishes between syntactic runnability and semantic validity, ensuring modifications are both technically sound *and* meet business goals. NOVA learns from past modification attempts to guide future searches, significantly improving the effective pass rate within a fixed evaluation budget compared to generic methods or traditional AutoML approaches in production settings.

## Technical note

NOVA is a level-aware LLM agent harness that automates architecture evolution of industrial ad-recommender models, treating cross-module structural edits (not just hyperparameters) as the search unit. It introduces an architecture gradient, an SGD-inspired non-differentiable signal aggregating the prior modification, verification diagnostics, offline metric change, and trajectory memory, plus a silent-failure-aware verification cascade (structure-semantic gate, local executability, offline AUC, online) that blocks runnable-but-invalid candidates and records failure patterns as reusable forbidden directions, with L1-L4 task levels routing risky tasks to a human Copilot. Deployed on a 1B-plus-user advertising system with a RankMixer backbone, it reaches 54.5% (L2 ScaleUp) and 60.0% (L3 Literature-to-Production) effective pass rate, more than doubling the human expert loop and beating coding agents (OpenHands, ReAct) and Optuna-TPE, and cuts a literature-to-production cycle over 13x in human time. An online A/B of a NOVA-transferred TokenMixer-Large variant lifts GMV +1.25/1.70/2.02% on three pCVR objectives while cutting pCVR bias 58.8/66.7/37.3%. It is an agentic AutoML/architecture-search system, not a recommendation model itself.

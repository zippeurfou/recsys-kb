## Summary

Current LLM-based document rerankers are inefficient due to their autoregressive generation limitations. This paper introduces DiffuRank, a reranking framework using diffusion language models (dLLMs), which offer more flexible, parallel decoding. Experiments show dLLMs achieve performance comparable or even superior to autoregressive LLMs while potentially offering better efficiency and control for document reranking tasks.

## Technical note

DiffuRank adapts masked diffusion language models (LLaDA-1.5-8B) to document reranking, exploiting bidirectional attention and parallel non-autoregressive decoding to avoid the latency and error-propagation of left-to-right LLM rerankers. It proposes three formulations: a pointwise dLLM cross-encoder scoring relevance from a masked token, a logits-based listwise variant that reads binary-relevance logits for all documents in one pass, and a permutation-based listwise variant that generates a docid ordering via constrained sampling or Hungarian-algorithm assignment. Pointwise and logits-list variants are trained by permutation distillation from RankGPT teachers with listwise cross-entropy or pairwise RankNet losses, while the permutation variant uses SFT with structure-aware DocID masking. On TREC DL and BEIR (reranking BM25 top-100 by NDCG@10), permutation-based variants are strongest and match or surpass comparably sized autoregressive LLM rerankers. Ablations show ranking-aware RankNet objectives beat cross-entropy and that constrained sampling (100% valid permutations vs ~16%) is essential.

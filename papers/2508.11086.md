## Technical note

Watch time is the dominant implicit signal in short-video recommendation but is confounded by video duration, popularity, and user viewing habits, biasing preference estimation. The Relative Advantage Debiasing (RAD) framework maps each observed watch time to its empirical quantile within two reference distributions conditioned on video ID and on user ID, producing a bias-corrected, uniform preference signal that corrects both item- and user-side confounders beyond simple duration bias. It uses a two-stage architecture that decouples distribution estimation from preference learning and introduces distributional embeddings to parameterize watch-time quantiles without online sampling or storing history, then fuses user- and video-side signals via Bayesian evidence fusion. Offline benchmarks and live A/B tests show significant gains in recommendation accuracy and robustness over prior duration-debiasing baselines.

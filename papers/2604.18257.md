## Summary

1.  DocQAC addresses the challenge of query auto-completion specifically for searching within long documents (unlike web search), leveraging the document's unique context alongside user query history.
2.  To solve this, the authors propose a novel adaptive trie-guided decoding framework that uses user query prefixes to guide large language models towards high-quality completions, incorporating an adjustable trade-off between model confidence and guidance.
3.  This framework, combined with retrieval-augmented generation and document signals, significantly improves performance on seen queries across documents, outperforming strong baselines and larger instruction-tuned models, and is evaluated on a new DocQAC benchmark.

## Technical note

DocQAC formalizes in-document query auto-completion as closed-world conditional optimization over a specific document, contrasted with open-world WebQAC. The core method is an adaptive soft trie-guided decoding scheme that subtracts an annealed bias from logits of non-trie-conforming tokens, controlled by initial bias plus alpha (prefix-length decay) and beta (beam-depth decay), avoiding hard constraints. A subword-level BytesTrie with a [SEP_SPLIT] separator resolves the character-vs-subword tokenization mismatch. Guided T5-small and BART-base outperform LoRA-finetuned LLaMA-3.2-3B and Phi-3.5 on a new ORCAS-derived benchmark while being 15-52x smaller and adding only ~8-12ms latency. Document context is injected via title/URL, keyphrases (YAKE), summaries, and sparse/dense RAG.

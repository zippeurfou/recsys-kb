## Technical note

Short-term engagement signals (clicks, likes, dwell) are noisy, biased toward popular items, and poor predictors of long-term retention. The paper introduces Retentive Relevance, a content-level survey measure asking users how likely they are to return for similar content, capturing forward-looking behavioral intent rather than immediate satisfaction; it is validated psychometrically for convergent, discriminant, and behavioral validity. Large-scale offline modeling shows it outperforms both engagement signals and other survey measures at predicting next-day retention, especially for low-signal/cold-start users. A production-ready proxy model integrates the signal into the final stage of a multi-stage ranking system via calibrated score adjustments, and A/B experiments on a large social platform show improved engagement and retention while reducing exposure to low-quality content.

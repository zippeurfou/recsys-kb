## Summary

This paper evaluates how well Large Language Models (LLMs) align with human behavioral dispositions in social situations. The researchers developed a framework using adapted Situational Judgment Tests (SJTs) derived from human psychological questionnaires to assess LLMs' behavioral tendencies. Their analysis of 25 diverse LLMs revealed significant misalignments: models often overconfidently stick to single answers in ambiguous scenarios, deviate from human consensus in clear-cut situations, and exhibit inconsistent trait patterns, indicating substantial gaps between an LLM's stated values and its actual behavioral outputs.

## Technical note

This paper evaluates how well LLM behavioral dispositions align with humans by converting self-report psychological questionnaire statements into 2,500 Situational Judgment Tests, each a realistic scenario with two opposing actions. Human labels come from 10 of 550 annotators per SJT, and LLM free-form responses are mapped to reference actions via an LLM-as-a-judge over 20 samples each. Across 25 models, alignment is worst when human consensus is low, driven by LLM overconfidence in a single answer, while even frontier models pick the action opposite clear human consensus in 15-20% of cases. Mapping self-reports to behavior reveals large gaps between stated values and revealed behavior, indicating construct-validity issues in self-report questionnaires for LLMs.

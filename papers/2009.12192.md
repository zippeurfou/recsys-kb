## Summary

This paper investigates the use of Word2vec in recommendation systems, finding that default hyperparameters are inadequate. It demonstrates that hyperparameter optimization significantly boosts performance (221% hit rate improvement), but unconstrained optimization is computationally too expensive. To address this, the paper shows that optimizing hyperparameters on a small sample of data (10%) still yields substantial improvements (91%) when applied to the full dataset, and successfully increased follow rates on Twitter's Who To Follow service by 15%.

## Technical note

The paper shows word2vec's default hyperparameters are poor for recommender systems and systematically optimizes them for next-event prediction (HR@10/NDCG@10) across music, e-commerce, clickstream, and Twitter datasets. Unconstrained tuning of dimension, window size, negative-sampling exponent, number of negatives, learning rate, and downsampling yields an average 221% hit-rate lift but is too expensive; runtime-budget-constrained optimization still recovers 138% average lift, and tuning on only a 10% data sample generalizes to 91% lift on full data. Deployed on Twitter's Who-To-Follow service using hyperparameters learned from a 1/2000 sample, it increased follow rates by 15% in an online test.

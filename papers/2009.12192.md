## Summary

1.  Default Word2vec hyperparameters are inadequate for recommendation systems, which need careful tuning for better performance.
2.  While full hyperparameter optimization significantly boosts recommendation accuracy (over 200%), its high cost makes it impractical for large-scale systems; however, a budget-constrained optimization method achieves substantial gains (over 100%) and is shown to be effective even when trained on just a small sample of the data.
3.  This sample-based constrained optimization method was successfully applied to Twitter's Who To Follow system, resulting in a 15% increase in follow rates.

## Technical note

Shows that default word2vec hyperparameters are poorly suited to recommendation and that tuning them yields large gains: unconstrained optimization averages +221% HR@10, while runtime-constrained optimization still averages +138% without extra compute. To scale to datasets too large to search directly, hyperparameters learned on a 10% sample transfer well (+91% average HR@10 on full data), evaluated via next-event prediction with cosine-similarity ANN. Unlike NLP, uniform negative sampling (exponent alpha=0) is near-optimal for recommendation, and skip-gram generally beats CBOW. Deployed on Twitter's Who-To-Follow, sample-tuned embeddings raised follow rate by ~15% in an online test.

## Summary

1.  The paper addresses the challenge of training effective models for conversion rate prediction (CVR) in online advertising when dealing with incomplete and potentially skewed multi-label data, where advertisers often submit only a subset of user conversions.
2.  To overcome this, the authors propose KAML, a Knowledge transfer framework for Asymmetric Multi-Label data, incorporating an attribution-driven masking strategy (ADM) to utilize the available data more effectively, a hierarchical knowledge extraction mechanism (HKE) to handle data discrepancies, and a ranking loss strategy to leverage unlabeled data.
3.  KAML demonstrates significant improvements over standard multi-task learning baselines on both offline industry datasets and online A/B tests, effectively handling the complexities of incomplete and skewed training data for diverse CVR prediction tasks.

## Technical note

KAML targets conversion-rate (CVR) prediction in online advertising where advertisers submit only a subset of conversion actions, yielding incomplete and skewed asymmetric multi-label data with a train-serve distribution mismatch. It introduces an attribution-driven masking (ADM) strategy to identify negative vs unlabeled samples per task, a hierarchical knowledge extraction (HKE) mechanism that models target-advertiser vs other-advertiser samples with separate parameters within the task tower, and a ranking-based label utilization (RLU) loss to exploit unlabeled samples. Built on an MMoE-style base, KAML outperforms MTL baselines offline and, when deployed, lifts RPM by 12.11% and CVR by 0.92%.

## Summary

NetCause addresses the challenge of understanding how faults propagate through complex large-scale networks by using self-supervised learning on incident data, moving beyond traditional static rules and correlation-based methods. It models network incidents as dynamic graph processes and employs counterfactual simulations to rank potential root causes causally, offering an interpretable output that integrates with operational actions. Evaluated against a rule-based heuristic baseline, NetCause shows significant improvement (16.1% accuracy gain) in identifying the most likely root causes relevant for real-time operations, despite requiring substantial training resources but being efficient at inference time.

## Technical note

NetCause frames network root cause analysis as causal attribution over graph-temporal incident data, learning a self-supervised forward transition model P(X_{t+1}|X_t,G) that captures fault propagation across heterogeneous physical, logical, and hierarchical topologies. At inference it performs counterfactual simulation by removing candidate fault transitions from historical sequences and rolling the model forward, ranking causes via a Total Causal Influence (TCI) metric measuring divergence between factual and interventional outcomes. Trained on ~1,500 production cloud incidents without labeled root causes and evaluated on 31 expert-labeled incidents, it improves exact-match root cause identification by 16.1% over a rule-based heuristic. Inference is lightweight (sub-minute CPU-only p95), making it operationally practical despite computationally intensive training.

- Compact models generalize better. Smaller hidden dimensions (8-16) and fewer RNN layers (1) consistently outperformed larger architectures. This suggests that the fault propagation patterns in our data are relatively low-dimensional and that overparameterization leads to overfitting
- Low positive class weights are optimal. Weights of 0.5-2.0 achieved the best F1 scores, indicating that aggressive upweighting of rare events degrades performance. This likely reflects the fact that not all positive examples are equally informative
- Moderate spatial depth is sufficient. While 4-hop R-GCN models showed better overall performance and generalization, 2-hop models achieved higher peak F1 scores on some incidents. We selected 4 hops as the default to balance generalization and peak performance
- Simple RNN outperforms LSTM/GRU. Vanilla RNN cells performed comparably or better than gated variants, suggesting that the temporal dependencies in our data do not require complex gating mechanisms
- Bidirectional graphs are critical. Adding reverse edges for hierarchical relationships improved F1 scores substantially (from  0.5 to  0.87)

## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge in personalized search where relying on strong but unreliable behavioural statistics (Query Slice Stats, QSS) can cause models to neglect more generalizable patterns, leading to "shortcut learning." To overcome this, the authors introduce a training method where models learn on both examples with QSS included and those with it removed. Their experiments show that this dual-training approach preserves the ranking gains achieved with QSS while significantly improving performance when QSS is unavailable, effectively balancing the use of behavioural data with semantic robustness.

## Technical note

Spotify's personalised search reranker is a 0.6B PLUM-style LLM cross-encoder (vocabulary extended with Semantic IDs) that scores query-candidate pairs pointwise via the log-probability of a 'Y' token, with semantic, user-context and behavioural features rendered as prompt text, including Query Slice Stats (QSS), a query-item-country historical search-success rate bucketed into five percentile tiers. On 1M training and 10K eval sessions (514 candidates and 1.3 positives per session; 34.7% of eval queries unseen in training), QSS alone reaches NDCG@7 0.494 vs 0.105 for BM25 and 0.104 for monoT5, and always-present QSS training lifts full-prompt NDCG@7 by 12.6% but drops QSS-removed NDCG@7 below a no-QSS baseline (0.526 vs 0.550), i.e. shortcut learning. Dual-sample feature-dropout training (every example seen with and without QSS, mixture weight 0.5) keeps the gain (0.622, +13.3%) while holding QSS-removed performance at baseline (0.547), with its advantage concentrated on long-tail and cold-start traffic. In an online A/B/C test both QSS-aware variants lift search success by about 2% (2.05% dual-sample, 2.33% features-only, not separable), with a directional +0.75% for dual-sample on cold-start items.

## Summary

1.  Industrial recommendation systems face challenges handling diverse user interactions (Multi-Scenario Learning) and contexts (Multi-Task Learning) due to inefficient use of model parameters and difficulty unifying scenario/task modeling.
2.  The paper introduces MDL (Multi-Distribution Learning), inspired by large language models, which treats scenarios and tasks as specialized tokens, enabling deep interaction with the model's vast parameters through tokenization and three synergistic mechanisms.
3.  MDL significantly outperforms existing methods on real datasets and achieves notable online improvements (+0.0626% Lift30, -0.3267% change query reduction) on the Douyin Search platform, leading to its full deployment.

## Technical note

MDL unifies multi-scenario learning (MSL) and multi-task learning (MTL) by borrowing the LLM prompting paradigm: scenario and task priors are converted into specialized tokens rather than gating signals or auxiliary inputs, alongside feature tokens. A unified information tokenization module projects features, scenarios, and tasks into a common latent token space, then three interaction mechanisms operate on them: feature-token self-attention for high-order feature interactions, domain-feature attention where scenario/task tokens act as queries to adaptively activate feature subspaces, and a domain-fused aggregation for joint multi-distribution prediction. Stacking these layers lets distribution-specific tokens penetrate deep interaction modules and steer the large parameter space, better harvesting scaling-law gains than shallow shared-specific structures. Deployed on Douyin Search, a one-month A/B test showed +0.0626% LT30 and -0.3267% change-query rate.

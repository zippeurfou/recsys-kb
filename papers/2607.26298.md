## Summary

Based on the abstract:

1.  The paper found that relying on just one matching algorithm for entity resolution is ineffective because datasets vary too much; instead, they recommend training multiple algorithms per dataset and letting data automatically determine the best performer (a bake-off).
2.  Existing evaluation metrics often use a single threshold compromising both accuracy (precision) and finding rates (recall). The research suggests that precision requires strict vetoes to prevent bad matches, while improving recall needs more diverse candidate retrieval methods.
3.  A critical issue is how errors can cascade: once an incorrect merge occurs between two entities, assuming transitivity allows other unrelated records to be incorrectly linked together, potentially merging large groups of data points if not actively caught and verified during the process.

## Technical note

This is a practitioner's guide to a self-serve entity-resolution (block to match to cluster) pipeline evaluated on six deduplication benchmarks (864 to 5M records) at Walmart, distilling an SOP-conditioned frontier-LLM teacher into cheap matchers for about a 37.5x cost reduction ($12 vs $450 per 1M pairs). Three lessons: (1) no matcher family wins everywhere, so a per-dataset tournament over DeepMatcher, LightGBM, and GAT auto-selects the winner (DM 3/6, LGBM 3/6, GAT 0/6 on Pair-F1); (2) precision and recall break at different stages, so recall needs diverse retrieval (HNSW ensemble plus identifier blocking) and precision needs hard rule-based vetoes and sparsity-aware thresholds rather than a shared threshold; (3) transitive-closure clustering lets one false-positive bridge silently merge unrelated entities, so a verified-merge step (center assignment plus active cross-cluster re-verification) is needed, giving best Pair-F1 on 4/6 datasets. It is entity resolution, not a recommender ranking system.

## Summary

This paper introduces **Retrieval-Augmented Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RA-RFT)** as a post-training method for language models to improve analogical reasoning. It addresses limitations in standard retrieval methods that prioritize semantic similarity but can fail complex problems needing different solutions or sharing underlying patterns.

RA-RFT trains a specialized retriever using "gold-relevance distillation" that selects contexts based on their expected reasoning benefit, not just similarity, and then fine-tunes the model with these relevant demonstrations under outcome-based rewards. This approach retrieves diverse analogies crucial for effective analogy-based reasoning.

The method shows significant performance improvements on mathematical benchmarks like AIME 2025 compared to standard reinforcement techniques, demonstrating that reasoning-aware retrieval provides a complementary benefit orthogonal to other methods.

## Technical note

RA-RFT (Retrieval-Augmented Reinforcement Fine-Tuning) is a post-training framework that teaches LLMs to reason by analogy, addressing that conventional lexical/semantic retrieval correlates weakly with reasoning utility: similar problems may need different strategies and dissimilar problems may share a reasoning pattern. It defines reasoning relevance as how much conditioning on a candidate trace raises the probability of a correct answer, approximated by gold-relevance distillation using a GPT-4o judge over offline query-context pairs. These binary labels train a dense reasoning-aware retriever via contrastive learning, and the retrieved analogous reasoning traces are injected into RLVR (GRPO) rollouts so the policy learns to exploit analogies under verifiable outcome rewards rather than imitate them at test time. Analysis shows reasoning-aware retrieval surfaces structurally diverse, complementary solution strategies. On AIME 2025 average@32 it improves over GRPO by 7.1 and 2.8 points for Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B, an axis orthogonal to reward or curriculum design.

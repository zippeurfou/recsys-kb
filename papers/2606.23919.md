## Summary

This paper addresses the challenge in e-commerce of building a single relevance model for six different entity pair relationship tasks, as current separate models hinder knowledge transfer and consistency.

The authors investigated three task routing architectures (text prefix, multi-head classification, MHP Ensemble) applied to both LoRA-adapted LLMs and cross-encoders.

Their key findings show that the complex MHP architecture with private layers achieved the highest accuracy (89.96%) across all tasks; surprisingly, simpler text prefixes caused severe degradation in decoder-only models but were less critical for robust cross-encoder performance. They also demonstrated significant multi-task training benefits, achieving up to 14% improvement on low-resource tasks compared to single-task baselines.

## Technical note

This paper unifies six e-commerce entity-pair relevance tasks (query-ad, query-query, query-product-type, ad-PT, PT-PT, ad-ad) into one three-class (Irrelevant/Partial/Relevant) multi-task model with data-proportional loss weighting, comparing three task-routing schemes across LoRA LLMs (Gemma-2-2b, Llama-3-8b) and fully fine-tuned cross-encoders. It finds an encoder-decoder asymmetry: removing text prefixes barely hurts cross-encoders (CLS pooling, -1.02%) but collapses decoder-only LLMs (Gemma -31.6%) because last-token pooling loses task conditioning; adding two private transformer layers per task (MHP) fully recovers LLM performance. Multi-task training lifts low-resource tasks by up to +14% (T5 PT-PT) via positive transfer, with slight negative transfer on the largest task. A majority-vote MHP ensemble of Gemma, Llama, and ModernBERT reaches 89.96% on 453K test examples, beating the text-prefix ensemble by +1.92% because private-layer routing induces more diverse errors. The dataset is built from ~800K human-labeled query-ad seeds with derived-then-validated labels (Cohen's kappa >=0.76).
